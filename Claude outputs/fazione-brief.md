# `fazione` in Machiavelli's *Discorsi*: brief for an independent check

## Context

This brief comes from a bilingual reading edition: Machiavelli's *Discorsi* chapters that Guicciardini answered, set facing his *Considerazioni*, with Italian and English side by side. The English deliberately stays close to the Italian. Its standing rule is one Italian word, one English word, and a word may be split into two English senses only where the context forces it. The Italian is the 1824 edition of the *Discorsi* and Canestrini's 1857 edition of the *Considerazioni*.

## The principle under test

`fazione` takes two English words, decided by sense:

- **"faction"**: a party within a state (the glossary's standing rendering).
- **"action"**: a military operation or engagement. This was first ruled for *Discorsi* I.29 and held for review until a survey of Machiavelli's uses could be made.

The question is whether this split is right, whether "action" is the best English for the military sense, and whether any occurrence below has been assigned to the wrong sense.

## The occurrences

Status key:
- **E** — the Italian is established text in the edition, read twice from the page images.
- **P** — located through the scan's searchable text and then checked by eye on the page image.
- **T** — located through the scan's searchable text only. The chapter number comes from that text's headings and could be wrong by a chapter.

Where the edition has published an English translation, it is given.

### Sense 1: a party in the state ("faction")

1. ***Discorsi* I.7 (E).** The Senate had sent to Sicily for grain during a famine.
   - Italian: "Coriolano nimico alla fazione popolare, consigliò com'egli era venuto il tempo da poter gastigare la Plebe"
   - Edition's English: "Coriolanus, an enemy to the popular faction, …".

2. **Guicciardini, *Considerazioni* on I.10 (E).** On Sulla.
   - Italian: "perché el governo restò in mano degli uomini della sua fazione"
   - Edition's English: "because the government remained in the hands of the men of his faction".

3. ***Discorsi* I.37 (T).** On the strife over the agrarian law in the Gracchi's time.
   - Italian: "Talché non potendo i pubblici Magistrati rimediarvi, né sperando più alcuna delle fazioni in quelli, si ricorse ai rimedj privati, e ciascuna delle parti pensò di farsi un Capo che la difendesse."
   - Not yet translated.

4. ***Discorsi* I.54 (T).**
   - Italian: "Era, pochi anni sono, Firenze divisa in due fazioni, fratesche, e arrabbiate, che così si chiamavano"
   - Not yet translated.

5. ***Discorsi* II.2 (T).** Corcyra in the Peloponnesian War.
   - Italian: "dove sendo divisa quella Provincia in due fazioni, delle quali l'una seguitava gli Ateniesi, l'altra gli Spartani"
   - Not yet translated.

### Sense 2: a military operation or engagement ("action")

6. ***Discorsi* I.29 (E). This is the ruling under test.** A prince who suspects his victorious captain tries to show that the victory came from something other than the captain's *virtù*.
   - Italian: "con ogn'industria mostrare che quella vittoria è nata non per la virtù di quello, ma per fortuna, o per viltà dei nimici, o per prudenza degli altri Capitani che sono stati seco in tale fazione"
   - Edition's English: "…or through the prudence of the other captains who were with him in such an action".
   - Reasoning: `tale` points back to the expedition and victory, "sono stati seco in" means taking part with him, and all three alternatives explain who won. A "faction" would bring in a body the chapter never names.

7. ***Discorsi* II.18 (P, vol. 1 p. 286).** Roman cavalry dismounting at Sora.
   - Italian: "perché se nell'altre fazioni i Consoli facevano discendere i Cavalieri romani, era per soccorrere alle fanterie che pativano"
   - Not yet translated.

8. ***Discorsi* II.32 or III (T).** On Aratus of Sicyon. The chapter is uncertain: the text's headings place it at II.32, but it may sit in Book III.
   - Italian: "Né si trovò mai alcuno, che fusse più felice in queste espedizioni fraudolenti e notturne, che Arato Sicioneo, il quale quanto valeva in queste, tanto nelle diurne ed aperte fazioni era pusillanime."
   - Not yet translated.

9. ***Discorsi* III.32 or III.33 (T).** On auspices before battle.
   - Italian: "senz'aver fatto alcuna di queste cose non mai arebbe un buon Capitano e savio tentata alcuna fazione, giudicando d'averla potuta perdere facilmente, se i suoi soldati non avessero prima inteso gli Dii essere dalla parte loro."
   - Not yet translated.

10. ***Discorsi* III.39 (T).** Cyrus against the king of Armenia, after Xenophon.
    - Italian: "E Senofonte nella vita di Ciro mostra che andando Ciro ad assaltare il Re d'Armenia, nel divisare quella fazione ricordò a quelli suoi, che questa non era altro, ch'una di quelle cacce"
    - Not yet translated.

11. ***Discorsi* III.45 (P, vol. 2 p. 231).** Decius and Fabius against the Samnites and Etruscans (Sentinum).
    - Italian: "e venendo alla zuffa ed alla Giornata insieme, è da notare in tal fazione, quale di due diversi modi di procedere tenuti dai due Consoli sia migliore."
    - Not yet translated.
    - This is the same phrase as I.29, "in tal(e) fazione", and plainly refers to a battle.

## What I'd like you to check

1. **The split.** Is each of the 11 occurrences in the right sense? Flag any that is ambiguous. No. 3 (I.37) deserves a look: "né sperando più alcuna delle fazioni in quelli" could be read more than one way.

2. **The English for sense 2.** Is "action" the best choice for a plain, unornamented, slightly older English? Weigh it against "engagement", "operation", "enterprise", "exploit", "battle" and "feat of arms", and say why. Two words are already taken in this edition and should not be reused:
   - "engagement" is reserved for `zuffa`;
   - "enterprise" is reserved for `impresa`.

3. **Lexical evidence.** If you can reach them, check the historical dictionaries for the military sense of `fazione` in 16th-century Tuscan: the Crusca (*fatto d'arme*) and Battaglia's *Grande dizionario della lingua italiana*. Say whether either records it as current in Machiavelli's period.

4. **Published translations, as a calibration check only.** For as many of the 11 passages as you can, report how published English translations render `fazione`. Candidates:
   - Christian Detmold (1882);
   - Leslie J. Walker (1950);
   - Harvey C. Mansfield and Nathan Tarcov (University of Chicago Press, 1996);
   - Julia Conaway Bondanella and Peter Bondanella (Oxford World's Classics, 1997);
   - for the Guicciardini passage (No. 2), Margaret Grayson (1965) and James B. Atkinson and David Sices, *The Sweetness of Power* (2002).

   Quote only the rendering of the word or short phrase, with a citation, not whole passages. Say plainly where you can't confirm a translation's wording. Do not reconstruct one from memory and present it as a quotation.

5. **Verdict.** Should the edition confirm the split ("faction" for a party, "action" for an operation), change the English for the military sense, or do something else? Give one recommendation and your reasons.
