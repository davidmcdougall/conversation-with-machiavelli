# Register

## The decision

**Preserve.** On the Lattimore–Fitzgerald–Fagles–Wilson axis this sits at the
Lattimore end: hold the source's shape, and let the English be difficult where
the Italian is difficult.

The reason is structural, not a matter of taste. **The Italian is on the facing
page.** A reader who wants to check can check, which means this English does not
have to do the original's job — it has to be a true guide to it. That is a
different and lower-risk brief than a standalone literary translation, and it
buys the freedom to keep long periods intact.

One-line exemplar of the target voice:

> And because there are some of the opinion that the well-being of Italian
> affairs depends on the Church of Rome, I wish to discourse against it those
> reasons which occur to me.

Plain, unhurried, syntactically faithful, no ornament added and none removed.

## What this rules out

**Sentence-splitting for comfort.** Machiavelli's chapter is one 997-word block
in the witness, built of causal and consecutive links — `perché`, `adunque`,
`di qui`, `per conseguente`, `né`. The profile in `translation-drafts/` puts it
exactly: the risk is not long sentences, it is losing the hierarchy and
attachment of the relations. A clause may be split only where English syntax
genuinely breaks, never to make a passage easier.

The paragraph divisions already carry the readability load, and SCOPE §3
declares them editorial. That is the concession to the screen; the sentences are
not a second one.

**Maxim extraction.** A memorable clause that is subordinate in the Italian stays
subordinate in the English. Machiavelli is quotable and the temptation is
constant.

**Improving the author.** The characteristic AI failure named in the predecessor
protocol, and the one to watch hardest: producing English that is subtly clearer,
more coherent and better organised than the source. Where Machiavelli is
repetitive — `mantenere incorrotte ... mantenere incorrotte` — the English
repeats. Where Guicciardini trails off without a full stop, so does the English.

**Levelling the two voices.** Machiavelli accumulates towards demonstration;
Guicciardini qualifies, suspends and withholds. If both come out in the same
English, the pairing has failed and the edition has no reason to exist.

## Period flavour

Aim at a plain early-modern English that does not costume itself. No thee/thou,
no inversions for effect. But cognates that still work are kept rather than
modernised away — `province`, `prudent`, `credulity`, `conjecture`, `augment` —
because they hold the conceptual vocabulary in place and the facing text shows
the reader why they were chosen.
