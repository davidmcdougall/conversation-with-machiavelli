# Guicciardini — linguistic and voice profile for translation evaluation

**Status:** G2 evaluation profile; proposed under WI-000118

**Governing philosophy:** `translation-system/TRANSLATION-PHILOSOPHY-v0.9.md`

**Fixed source sample:** `FG-CONS.1.12`

**Stopping rule:** this profile identifies risks and testable features. It
creates no English rendering, term decision, source change, recension
equivalence, author-wide statistical claim, or protocol rule.

## 1. Evidence boundary

The direct corpus evidence is one accepted S2 evaluation passage: the
*Considerazione* responding to `NM-DISC.1.12`. Observations labelled
**sample-observed** refer only to that unit. Broader claims are attributed to
the cited historical-linguistic scholarship or marked **unknown**.

This narrow boundary must not be hidden. Fabrizio Franceschini documents
significant development across Guicciardini's works and redactions: living
Florentine usage persists, while colloquial and chancery features are selected
or reduced differently in the *Dialogo*, *Ricordi*, and *Storia d'Italia*;
reported speech and syntactic architecture also change by work and phase.
[GUIC-LINGUA] The fixed passage can establish risks for response prose, not a
universal Guicciardinian style.

## 2. Syntax and period profile

### 2.1 Qualification is structural

`FG-CONS.1.12` develops its judgment through causal, adversative,
counterfactual, concessive, and alternative constructions. Its central risk is
not length alone but the relation between a provisional claim and the
qualification that follows.

The passage begins by intensifying the condemnation of the Roman court, then
accepts as credible the causal claim that the Church helped prevent an Italian
monarchy. The next paragraph suspends judgment on whether that result was good
or bad. The third distinguishes republican domination from monarchy, admits
calamities under division, adduces a historical counterweight, considers
kingdom separately, proposes alternative explanations, and ends with limited
non-concurrence in Machiavelli's conclusion. [FG-CONS.1.12.01–03]

The movement depends on forms including:

- `perché` clauses that supply factual or argumentative grounds;
- `da uno canto ... da altro` coordination that distinguishes different
  capacities of the Church;
- `se ...`, `se bene`, and `benché` constructions that introduce
  counterfactuals or concessions;
- `nondimeno`, `al rincontro`, and `pure` as adversative redirectors, while
  `però` is consecutive (`per ciò`, “therefore”) and draws a conclusion;
- `o sia ... o per` alternatives that leave causation open; and
- long relative and participial structures that keep the compared political
  arrangements inside one judgment.

Franceschini anchors chiefly in the *Storia d'Italia* his discussion of
explicit causals as carriers of factual, psychological, and logical conditions,
concessives as instruments for reversing an apparently obvious judgment, and
cross-period linkage as a characteristic *legato*. [GUIC-LINGUA] These
findings therefore supply cross-genre hypotheses to test against response
prose, not oeuvre-wide corroboration or frequency claims.

### 2.2 Evaluation features

For each Guicciardini period sampled in G3, record:

- the proposition initially entertained;
- every causal condition and whether it is factual, psychological, or logical;
- each concession, its scope, and the judgment it modifies;
- alternative causes or outcomes left open;
- the point at which the author accepts, suspends, limits, or rejects a claim;
- how anaphora and connectors link the period to what precedes it; and
- whether sentence division turns a staged judgment into independent maxims.

No English division should be evaluated without reconstructing that movement.
Sentence-count identity is not evidence of fidelity.

### 2.3 Direct sample hypotheses

| Feature | Fixed-passage evidence | Evaluation risk |
|---|---|---|
| causal distinction | `perché` throughout; Church authority versus lack of arms in `.01` | several kinds of cause compressed into one generic explanation |
| balanced alternatives | `da uno canto ... da altro`; `o sia ... o per` | alternatives made falsely exhaustive or one selected without authority |
| concessive reversal | `se bene ... benché ... nondimeno` in `.03` | concession detached from the conclusion it limits |
| counterfactual comparison | republic, divided Italy, monarchy/kingdom | political forms collapsed or modernised; counterfactual asserted as history |
| judgment markers | `credo`, `non so`, `reputo`, `non concorro facilmente` | authorial degree of commitment erased |
| cross-period continuity | each paragraph takes up the preceding claim | fluent English breaks the response into aphorisms |

## 3. Hedge and qualification inventory

Hedges are not interchangeable tokens. The fixed passage uses different forms
to perform different epistemic and argumentative acts.

| Form | Locator | Sample-observed function | Translation risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| `credo sia vero` | `.01` | qualified assent to the causal proposition about the Church | plain assertion removes the speaker's evidentiary posture |
| `non so già se` | `.02` | suspends the value judgment between felicity and infelicity | converted into indecision, ignorance, or a rhetorical question with an implied answer |
| `se ... poteva` | `.02` | frames a counterfactual republican outcome | hypothesis converted into historical fact |
| `se bene` | `.03` | admits calamities under political division before reassessment | concessive force weakened to sequence or contrast |
| `forse` | `.03` | limits the claim that calamities would not have occurred under one dominion | hedge attached to the wrong proposition or dropped |
| `benché` | `.03` | introduces a historical counterweight concerning invasions under Rome | counterevidence made parenthetical or independent |
| `nondimeno` | `.03` | resumes and redirects the evaluation after concessions | conclusion detached from conceded evidence |
| `io reputo` | `.03` | marks a reasoned personal judgment about monarchy | converted into impersonal doctrine |
| `pure` | `.03` | reopens the inquiry after distinguishing kingdom from republic | flattened into mere emphasis or omitted |
| `o sia ... o per` | `.03` | offers alternative causal explanations without choosing | false resolution of causation |
| `né credo` | `.03` | limits an historical memory claim | absence-of-evidence turned into categorical historical denial |
| `non concorro facilmente` | `.03` | records limited, effortful non-assent to the inference | translated as total contradiction or mild stylistic disagreement |

The profile therefore requires **scope-aware hedge preservation**:

1. identify what proposition is qualified;
2. record the kind and degree of commitment;
3. preserve interaction among multiple qualifications;
4. distinguish evidentiary restraint from politeness or indecision; and
5. assess token screens only after contextual review.

TP-Q07 must seed defects that keep a hedge word while moving its attachment,
because token-count parity can conceal logical loss.

## 4. Voice and rhetorical movement

### 4.1 Responsive voice

The unit is a direct response, not a free-standing political maxim. Its voice
depends on partial agreement followed by discrimination among claims:

1. intensify Machiavelli's condemnation of the Roman court;
2. accept as credible one causal proposition;
3. suspend judgment on the alleged consequence;
4. distinguish republic from kingdom and domination from civic flourishing;
5. add contrary historical and dispositional considerations; and
6. withhold easy concurrence in the conclusion.

This movement must remain visible beside `NM-DISC.1.12`. Agreement on one
premise must not be expanded into agreement on the inference, while criticism
of that inference must not erase the initial agreement. [FG-CONS.1.12;
FG-CONS-DR-012]

Scholarly synthesis describes the *Considerazioni* as point-by-point work of
analysis and deconstruction born as marginal notes or glosses on
Machiavelli's theses. [GUIC-PENSIERO] This description supports a response
rubric but not a claim that every unit follows the same sequence.

### 4.2 Judging rather than systematising

The authorial first person (`credo`, `non so`, `reputo`, `non concorro`) marks
acts of judgment and their limits. In a discussion anchored chiefly in the
*Storia d'Italia*, Franceschini identifies predicates and syntactic
constructions as carriers of the author's judging function. [GUIC-LINGUA]
That observation is a cross-genre hypothesis to test against response prose,
not author-wide corroboration. Translation must not replace those acts with a
later abstract system or present “contextualism” as a slogan.

### 4.3 Rhetorical hazards

- **Qualification deletion:** a lucid English thesis replaces a staged
  judgment.
- **False indecision:** careful discrimination is made to sound hesitant or
  weak.
- **False system:** repeated distinctions become a comprehensive doctrine.
- **Response collapse:** Machiavelli's claim and Guicciardini's answer are
  paraphrased into a single modern debate proposition.
- **Retrospective consistency:** later concepts or later redactional style are
  projected uniformly onto every work and recension.

## 5. `discrezione` and adjacent-term risk inventory

This is a watchlist, not a glossary. It proposes no default English rendering.
`discrezione` itself does **not** occur in `FG-CONS.1.12`; any claim about its
local translation here would be unsupported.

Scholarly evidence describes `discrezione` as a mobile, empirical capacity for
judging the particular case, resistant to reduction into fixed rules and tied
to distinctions, exceptions, circumstance, reason, and experience.
[GUIC-PENSIERO] That is a sampling hypothesis, not permission to translate all
adjacent vocabulary through one concept.

| Source form or cluster | Evidence/status | Risk to test |
|---|---|---|
| `discrezione` | absent from fixed passage; broader scholarly profile only [GUIC-PENSIERO] | modern “discretion,” taste, politeness, or private choice imposed; flexible judgment turned into a fixed faculty |
| `particolare`, `circunstanzie` | not observed here; scholarly adjacent cluster [GUIC-PENSIERO] | “self-interest” or mere detail imposed without local evidence; particulars treated as noise under a rule |
| `esperienza`, `prudenza`; `ragione` | the first two are not observed here; `ragione` occurs at `.03` in `Questa ragione`, where it names the preceding argument; a broader relationship to judgment is documented [GUIC-PENSIERO] | the local argumentative reference collapsed with reason as a faculty; experiential, natural, rational, and practical capacities collapsed |
| `regola`, `distinzione`, `eccezione` | not observed here; broader Ricordi evidence [GUIC-PENSIERO] | rule/exception structure made more formal or systematic than the source |
| `credo`, `non so`, `reputo`, `non concorro` | dense in fixed passage | distinct acts of assent, suspension, judgment, and non-concurrence regularised into one hedge |
| `felicità`, `infelicità`, `calamità`, `glorioso` | fixed passage | private happiness or moral approval substituted for civic/political evaluation |
| `republica`, `regno`, `monarchia`, `imperio`, `dominio` | fixed passage | different political forms and relations collapsed into “state” or modern regime labels |
| `libertà`, `cittadini`, `sudditi` | fixed passage | modern liberal freedom, citizenship, or subjecthood imported without institutional context |
| `virtù`, `forze`, `armi proprie` | fixed passage | ethical virtue, generic strength, and military capacity collapsed |
| `fato`, `complessione`, `inclinazione`, `consuetudine` | fixed passage | alternative causal registers modernised into deterministic law, psychology, or national character |
| `facultà`, `grandezza`, `credito`, `autorità` | fixed passage | familiar cognates obscure capacity, political expansion, standing, or authority in context |

WI-000119 must test these clusters across multiple works, genres, and
recensions before proposing term-record fields or seed terms.

## 6. Recension and genre exceptions

### 6.1 *Considerazioni*

The fixed unit supports a profile of direct-response prose: quoted or assumed
claims from Machiavelli are distinguished, tested, qualified, and sometimes
partially accepted. It does not establish the handling of all 39 response
units, which require sampling for different degrees of agreement, textual
dependence, and argumentative completeness. [FG-CONS register; GUIC-PENSIERO]

### 6.2 *Ricordi*

The *Ricordi* have revision history and recension structure that are central
to both language and thought. Scholarship cited by Franceschini uses the
critical edition as evidence for Guicciardini's practice through 1530, while
the intellectual profile shows individual word substitutions sharpening a
distinction across redactions. [GUIC-LINGUA; GUIC-PENSIERO] Translation
evaluation must preserve revision as revision; no final-recension wording may
be back-projected into earlier states.

Exact translation consequences for each recension are **unknown** until fixed
source-ready samples exist.

### 6.3 *Dialogo del reggimento di Firenze*

Franceschini reports revision away from some colloquial or gergal expressions
and changes in pronominal usage. [GUIC-LINGUA] Dialogue, revision, speaker
identity, and institutional terminology therefore need separate cells. The
response profile above cannot govern them automatically.

### 6.4 Histories

The *Storia d'Italia* develops complex, linked syntax, causal and concessive
operations, reported discourse, and a more levelled high register; its
chaptering also has modern editorial features. [GUIC-LINGUA] Historical
narrative, speech representation, chronology, source attribution, and authorial
judgment need samples distinct from the *Considerazioni*.

### 6.5 Diplomatic, legal, administrative, and personal writing

Guicciardini's legal training and governmental roles do not make every text
institutionally voiced or every technical-looking term legal. Broader
scholarship reports selective removal of legal and chancery Latinisms alongside
stylistic Latinising choices. [GUIC-LINGUA] Sender, office, audience, document
function, and redactional layer must be recorded before voice or technicality
is inferred. The appropriate English register remains **unknown** pending
representative source-ready samples.

## 7. Evaluation consequences

The Guicciardini cells and rubrics should test:

1. attachment and cumulative force of hedges, concessives, and alternatives;
2. causal distinctions and the cross-period *legato*;
3. preservation of staged judgment without making it weak or indecisive;
4. separation of premise-level agreement from conclusion-level dissent;
5. non-collapse of political forms, actor categories, and causal registers;
6. resistance to making `discrezione` or adjacent terms a master key;
7. recension-sensitive treatment of revision; and
8. genre-sensitive voice across response, maxim, dialogue, history, and
   institutional writing.

The hedge screen should include at least three defect types: token deletion,
scope displacement, and strength change. A matching hedge count is never a
pass by itself.

## 8. Explicit unknowns

- No author-wide frequency, sentence-length, or hedge-density claim is
  established.
- One response unit cannot establish the voice of all *Considerazioni*, much
  less the whole oeuvre.
- `discrezione`, `particolare`, `esperienza`, and `regola` are not evidenced by
  the fixed passage; their local renderings remain wholly open.
- The distribution of colloquial, legal, chancery, Latinising, and elevated
  features by work and redaction requires source-ready sampling.
- Recension-specific translation rules for the *Ricordi* remain open.
- The appropriate English degree of syntactic foreignness and period division
  remains open under TP-Q03–TP-Q05.
- Analytic-pass visibility and model-family effects remain open under TP-Q01
  and TP-Q02.
- No term in this inventory has an adopted default rendering.
