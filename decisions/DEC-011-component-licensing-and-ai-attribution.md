# DEC-011 — Component-based licensing, AI-assisted translation credit, model provenance

## Issue

DEC-010 point 2 proposed CC BY 4.0 over translations, established text and
apparatus. `LICENSE` v0.3 (2026-09-21) departed from it, putting the
established text and apparatus data under CC0, without a decision record.
`LICENSE` v0.3 also credited the edition "Edited and translated by David
McDougall", which does not reflect the role of generative AI in producing the
English, and it combined licence grants with third-party rights assessments.

## Decision

1. **Component-based licensing.** Established Italian text: CC0 1.0. English
   translation: CC BY 4.0, to the extent protectable rights subsist and are
   held or controlled by the licensor. Original editorial commentary and
   apparatus: CC BY 4.0. Research, provenance and apparatus data, and
   operational specifications: CC0 1.0. Software and build scripts: MIT.
   Third-party materials: no grant. Each component keeps its licence inside
   mixed files and generated editions. Stated in `LICENSE`.
2. **Separation.** `LICENSE` states grants only. Third-party rights
   assessments move to `RIGHTS_AND_SOURCES.md`. Translation workflow and model
   provenance are in `TRANSLATION_METHOD.md`.
3. **Canonical credit.** "AI-assisted translation edited by David McDougall"
   for the English. "Edited by David McDougall" for the edition as a whole.
   David McDougall is not described as sole translator; no AI model is
   described as co-author or copyright holder.
4. **Model provenance.** Each translation and review step records the model
   and, where available, its exact API identifier. Unknown identities are
   recorded as unknown, not inferred. Existing records are partial: git
   commit trailers name session models up to 2026-09-29, and no API
   identifiers are recorded (`TRANSLATION_METHOD.md`).
5. **AI-generated text and human contribution.** No copyright is asserted
   over material solely because it was generated by AI. Editorial
   responsibility does not by itself establish copyright ownership. Licences
   apply only to rights that exist and are held or controlled by the licensor.
