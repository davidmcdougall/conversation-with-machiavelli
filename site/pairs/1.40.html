The creation of the decemvirate in Rome, and what is to be noted in it; where there is considered, among many other things, how one can save, by a like accident, or oppress a republic Discourses I.40, and Guicciardini's reply

Editor's summary Machiavelli holds that when a people gives a wise man reputation to beat those it hates, he becomes tyrant; Appius erred in leaving the people, whose favour keeps a tyrant more secure, for the nobles. Guicciardini is persuaded that the decemvirs' principal error was their persuasion that a tyranny could be founded in the Rome of those times, so well ordered and so ardent for liberty. He does not believe it would have been more stable had they beaten the nobility with the plebs' favour, because that people was too great a friend of the name of liberty. Between people and nobility the examples differ and each side has its reasons; no firm rule decides, and the conclusion is to be drawn from the city's humours and the condition of the times.

Capitolo XL

1.40.01
Volendo discorrere particolarmente sopra gli accidenti che nacquero in Roma per la creazione del Decemvirato, non mi pare superchio narrare prima tutto quello che seguì per simile creazione, e dipoi disputare quelle parti che sono in esse azioni notabili; le quali sono molte, e di grande considerazione, così per coloro che vogliono mantenere una Repubblica libera, come per quelli che disegnassero di sottometterla. Perché in tal discorso si vedranno molti errori fatti dal Senato e dalla Plebe in disfavore della libertà, e molti errori fatti da Appio, Capo del Decemvirato, in disfavore di quella Tirannide, che egli si aveva presupposto di stabilire in Roma.
Wishing to discourse particularly upon the accidents which arose in Rome through the creation of the decemvirate, it does not seem to me superfluous to narrate first all that followed through a like creation, and afterwards to dispute those parts which are notable in these proceedings; which are many, and of great consideration, as well for those who wish to maintain a republic free as for those who should design to subject it. For in such a discourse there will be seen many errors made by the senate and by the plebs in disfavour of liberty, and many errors made by Appius, head of the decemvirate, in disfavour of that tyranny which he had presupposed [he would] establish in Rome.
Commentary · 2

Argument. The chapter states its plan: first narrate what followed from the creation of the decemvirate, then dispute the parts of it that are notable. It promises lessons to two readers at once, those who wish to keep a republic free and those who should design to subject it, and names the errors on both sides: many made by the senate and the plebs against liberty, many made by Appius against the tyranny he had presupposed he would establish. The narrative runs to .06; the dispute begins at .07.

Cross-reference. Guicciardini is persuaded (Io mi persuado) that the principal error was none of those Machiavelli goes on to name (.08, .10): the decemvirs' persuading themselves that a tyranny could be founded in the Rome of those times Guicciardini .01. What Machiavelli gives here as Appius' presupposition, that he would establish a tyranny in Rome, is what Guicciardini judges the error.

1.40.02
Dopo molte disputazioni e contenzioni seguite tra il Popolo e la Nobiltà, per fermare nuove leggi in Roma, per le quali, e' si stabilisse più la libertà di quello Stato, mandarono d'accordo Spurio Postumio con due altri cittadini ad Atene, per gli esempj di quelle leggi che Solone dette a quella Città, acciocché sopra quelle potessero fondare le leggi romane. Andati e tornati costoro, si venne alla creazione degli uomini che avessero ad esaminare e fermare dette leggi; e crearono dieci cittadini per uno anno, intra i quali fu creato Appio Claudio, uomo sagace e inquieto. E perché e' potessero senz'alcun rispetto creare tali leggi, si levarono di Roma tutti gli altri Magistrati, ed in particolare i Tribuni e i Consoli, e levossi lo appello al Popolo; in modoché tal Magistrato veniva ad essere al tutto Principe di Roma. Appresso ad Appio si ridusse tutta l'autorità degli altri suoi compagni, per gli favori che gli faceva la Plebe, perché egli s'era fatto in modo popolare con le dimostrazioni, che pareva maraviglia ch'egli avesse preso sì presto una nuova natura e uno nuovo ingegno, essendo stato tenuto innanzi a questo tempo un crudele persecutore della Plebe.
After many disputations and contentions that followed between the people and the nobility, to fix new laws in Rome by which the liberty of that state might be more established, they sent by agreement Spurius Postumius with two other citizens to Athens, for the examples of those laws which Solon gave to that city, so that upon those they might found the Roman laws. These having gone and returned, it came to the creation of the men who were to examine and fix the said laws; and they created ten citizens for one year, among whom was created Appius Claudius, a man sagacious and restless. And so that they might create such laws without any respect, all the other magistracies were removed from Rome, and in particular the tribunes and the consuls, and the appeal to the people was removed; in such a way that such a magistracy came to be wholly prince of Rome. All the authority of his other companions was brought to Appius, through the favours which the plebs did him, because he had made himself popular in such a way with the demonstrations that it seemed a marvel that he should have taken so soon a new nature and a new mind, having been held before this time a cruel persecutor of the plebs.
Commentary · 2

Argument. The removal of every check is told here as fact; the verdict on it waits for .10.

1.40.03
Governaronsi questi Dieci assai civilmente, non tenendo più che dodici Littori, i quali andavano davanti a quello ch'era fra loro preposto. E benché egli avessero l'autorità assoluta nondimeno avendosi a punire un cittadino romano per omicidio, lo citarono nel cospetto del Popolo, e da quello lo fecero giudicare. Scrissero le loro leggi in dieci tavole, ed avanti che le confirmassero, le messero in pubblico, acciocché ciascuno le potesse leggere e disputarle, acciocché si conoscesse se vi era alcuno difetto, per poterlo innanzi alla confermazione loro emendare.
These Ten governed themselves very civilly, holding no more than twelve lictors, who went before the one who was appointed among them. And albeit they had absolute authority, nonetheless, a Roman citizen having to be punished for homicide, they cited him in the presence of the people, and had him judged by it. They wrote their laws on ten tables, and before they confirmed them they put them in public, so that each one could read them and dispute them, so that it might be known whether there was any defect, to be able to emend it before their confirmation.
Commentary · 2

Argument. The first year is told in one adverb, assai civilmente, very civilly: twelve lictors only, before the one of them who was appointed among them; a Roman citizen who had to be punished for homicide cited before the people and judged by it, though the Ten held absolute authority; the laws written on ten tables and put in public so that any defect might be found and emended before confirmation. 1.40.04
Fece in su questo Appio nascere un rumore per Roma, che se a queste dieci tavole se n'aggiugnessero due altre, si darebbe a quelle la loro perfezione; talché questa opinione dette occasione al Popolo di rifare i Dieci per un altro anno; a che il Popolo s'accordò volentieri, sì perché i Consoli non si rifacessero, sì perché speravano loro poter stare senza Tribuni, sendo loro giudici delle cause, come di sopra si disse. Preso adunque partito di rifarli, tutta la Nobiltà si mosse a cercare questi onori, e tra i primi era Appio; ed usava tanta umanità verso la Plebe nel domandarla, che la cominciò ad essere sospetta a' suoi compagni: Credebant enim haud gratuitam in tanta superbia comitatem fore. E dubitando d'opporsegli apertamente, deliberarono farlo con arte; e benché e' fusse minore di tempo di tutti, dettono a lui autorità di proporre i futuri Dieci al Popolo, credendo ch'egli osservasse i termini degli altri di non proporre sé medesimo, sendo cosa inusitata e ignominiosa in Roma. Ille vero impedimentum pro occasione arripuit; e nominò sé tra i primi, con maraviglia e dispiacere di tutti i Nobili; nominò poi altri nove al suo proposito. La qual nuova creazione fatta per un altro anno, cominciò a mostrare al Popolo e alla Nobiltà l'error suo.
Upon this Appius made a rumour arise through Rome that, if to these ten tables two others were added, their perfection would be given to them; so that this opinion gave occasion to the people to remake the Ten for another year; to which the people agreed willingly, both so that the consuls should not be remade, and because they hoped to be able to stay without tribunes, they being judges of the causes, as was said above. The course of remaking them taken, then, all the nobility moved to seek these honours, and among the first was Appius; and he used so much humanity towards the plebs in asking it, that it began to be suspect to his companions: Credebant enim haud gratuitam in tanta superbia comitatem fore. And, doubting whether to oppose him openly, they deliberated to do it with art; and albeit he was the least in time of all, they gave to him authority to propose the future Ten to the people, believing that he would observe the bearings of the others, of not proposing himself, it being a thing unused and ignominious in Rome. Ille vero impedimentum pro occasione arripuit; and he named himself among the first, to the marvel and displeasure of all the nobles; later he named nine others to his purpose. Which new creation, made for another year, began to show the people and the nobility their error.
Commentary · 2

Argument. Appius had it put about that two more tables would perfect the laws, and the people, wishing that the consuls should not be remade and hoping to do without tribunes, agreed to remake the Ten. The nobility sought the office, Appius among the first, and his humanity to the plebs grew suspect to his colleagues; doubting whether to oppose him openly, they chose to do it by art: albeit he was the youngest, they gave him the authority to propose the next Ten, believing he would observe the custom of not proposing himself. 1.40.05
Perché subito Appio: Finem fecit ferendae alienae personae; e cominciò a mostrare la innata sua superbia, e in pochi dì riempiè de' suoi costumi i suoi compagni. E per isbigottire il Popolo ed il Senato, in scambio di dodici Littori ne fecero cento venti. Stette la paura eguale qualche giorno; ma cominciarono poi ad intrattenere il Senato, e battere la Plebe; e se alcuno battuto dall'uno appellava all'altro, era peggio trattato nell'appellazione che nella prima causa. In modoché la Plebe, conosciuto l'error suo, cominciò piena d'afflizione a riguardar in viso i Nobili: Et inde libertatis captare auram, unde servitutem timendo, in eum statum rempublicam adduxerant. E alla Nobiltà era grata questa loro afflizione: Ut ipsi, taedio praesentium, consules desiderarent. Vennero i dì che terminavano l'anno; le due tavole delle leggi erano fatte, ma non pubblicate. Da questo i Dieci presero occasione di continuare nel Magistrato, e cominciarono a tenere con violenza lo Stato, e farsi satelliti della gioventù nobile, alla quale davano i beni di quelli che loro condannavano: Quibus donis juventus corrumpebatur, et malebat licentiam suam, quam omnium libertatem.
For at once Appius: Finem fecit ferendae alienae personae; and he began to show his innate pride, and in a few days filled his companions with his customs. And to frighten the people and the senate, in place of twelve lictors they made a hundred and twenty. The fear stood equal for some days; but later they began to entertain the senate and to beat the plebs; and if anyone beaten by the one appealed to the other, he was worse treated in the appeal than in the first cause. In such a way that the plebs, its error known, began, full of affliction, to look the nobles in the face: Et inde libertatis captare auram, unde servitutem timendo, in eum statum rempublicam adduxerant. And to the nobility was welcome this affliction of theirs: Ut ipsi, taedio praesentium, consules desiderarent. The days came that ended the year; the two tables of the laws were made, but not published. From this the Ten took occasion to continue in the magistracy, and began to hold the state with violence, and to make for themselves satellites of the noble youth, to whom they gave the goods of those whom they condemned: Quibus donis juventus corrumpebatur, et malebat licentiam suam, quam omnium libertatem.
Commentary · 2

Argument. Appius dropped the mask at once and filled his colleagues with his customs: a hundred and twenty lictors in place of twelve; the senate courted and the plebs beaten; an appeal from one decemvir to another worse than the first judgment. The plebs, its error known, looked to the nobles for liberty, and the nobles were pleased by its affliction, so that the people might come to wish for consuls. At the year's end the two tables were made but not published, and the Ten took occasion to stay in office, held the state by violence, and made the noble youth their satellites with the goods of those they condemned. 1.40.06
Nacque in questo tempo che i Sabini e i Volsci mossero guerra a' Romani, in su la qual paura cominciarono i Dieci a vedere la debolezza dello stato loro, perché senza il Senato non potevano ordinare la guerra, e ragunando il Senato pareva loro perdere lo Stato. Pure necessitati presero questo ultimo partito; e ragunati i Senatori insieme, molti de' Senatori parlarono contro alla superbia de' Dieci, e in particolare Valerio ed Orazio; e l'autorità loro si sarebbe al tutto spenta, se non che il Senato per invidia della Plebe non volle mostrare l'autorità sua, pensando che se i Dieci deponevano il Magistrato volontarj, che potesse essere che i Tribuni della Plebe non si rifacessero. Deliberossi adunque la guerra, uscissi fuori con due eserciti, guidati da parte di detti Dieci. Appio rimase a governare la città: donde nacque che si innamorò di Virginia, e che volendola torre per forza, il padre Virginio per liberarla l'ammazzò; donde seguirono i tumulti di Roma e degli eserciti, i quali ridottisi insieme con il rimanente della Plebe romana, se n'andarono nel Monte Sacro, dove stettero tanto che i Dieci deposero il Magistrato, e che furono creati i Tribuni ed i Consoli, e ridotta Roma nella forma dell'antica sua libertà.
It arose in this time that the Sabines and the Volsci moved war on the Romans, upon which fear the Ten began to see the weakness of their state, because without the senate they could not order the war, and, assembling the senate, it seemed to them [they would] lose the state. Yet, necessitated, they took this last course; and, the senators assembled together, many of the senators spoke against the pride of the Ten, and in particular Valerius and Horatius; and their authority would have been wholly extinguished, were it not that the senate, through envy of the plebs, did not wish to show its authority, thinking that, if the Ten laid down the magistracy voluntarily, that it could be that the tribunes of the plebs would not be remade. The war, then, was deliberated, [they] went out with two armies, led by part of the said Ten. Appius remained to govern the city: whence it arose that he fell in love with Virginia, and that, [he] wishing to take her away by force, the father, Virginius, to free her, killed her; whence followed the tumults of Rome and of the armies, which, having brought themselves together with the remainder of the Roman plebs, went off to the Sacred Mount, where they stayed until the Ten laid down the magistracy, and the tribunes and the consuls were created, and Rome brought back into the form of its ancient liberty.
Commentary · 2

Argument. War with the Sabines and Volsci showed the Ten their weakness: without the senate they could not order the war, and to assemble it seemed to them to lose the state. Necessitated, they assembled it; many senators, Valerius and Horatius in particular, spoke against the Ten's pride, and their authority would have been wholly extinguished had the senate, from envy of the plebs, not declined to show its own, thinking that if the Ten laid down office of their own will the tribunes might not be remade. The armies went out under some of the Ten; Appius stayed in Rome, and his attempt on Virginia, whom her father killed to free her, set off the tumults of city and armies, the withdrawal to the Sacred Mount, the Ten's abdication, and the return of tribunes, consuls and the ancient form of liberty. The senate's envy of the plebs is the hinge: senators spoke against the Ten, but the senate would not put them down, thinking a voluntary abdication might leave the tribunes unrestored.

History. Livy 3.38 has the Sabines and the Aequi as the enemies of this war; Machiavelli's Volsci are kept. The speakers in the senate are Lucius Valerius Potitus and Marcus Horatius Barbatus (Livy 3.39), and the father is Lucius Verginius (3.44).

1.40.07
Notasi adunque per questo testo, in prima esser nato in Roma questo inconveniente di creare questa Tirannide, per quelle medesime cagioni che nascono la maggior parte delle Tirannidi nelle Città; e questo è da troppo desiderio del Popolo d'esser libero, e da troppo desiderio de' Nobili di comandare. E quando e' non convengono a fare una legge in favore della libertà, ma gettasi qualcuna delle parti a favorire uno, allora è che subito la Tirannide surge. Convennero il Popolo e i Nobili di Roma a creare i Dieci, e crearli con tanta autorità, per desiderio che ciascuna delle parti aveva, l'una di spegnere il nome Consolare, l'altra il Tribunizio. Creati che furono, parendo alla Plebe che Appio fusse diventato popolare, e battesse la Nobiltà, si volse il Popolo a favorirlo. E quando un Popolo si conduce a far questo errore di dare riputazione ad uno, perché batta quelli che egli ha in odio, e che quello uno sia savio, sempre interverrà che diventerà Tiranno di quella Città. Perché egli attenderà insieme con il favore del Popolo a spegnere la Nobiltà, e non si volterà mai alla oppressione del Popolo, se non quando ei l'arà spenta; nel qual tempo conosciutosi il Popolo essere servo, non abbia dove rifuggire. Questo modo hanno tenuto tutti coloro che hanno fondato Tirannidi nelle Repubbliche; e se questo modo avesse tenuto Appio, quella sua Tirannide arebbe preso più vita, e non sarebbe mancata sì presto.
It is noted, then, by this text, in the first place, that this inconvenience of creating this tyranny arose in Rome through those same causes [through] which the greater part of tyrannies arise in cities; and this is from too much desire of the people to be free, and from too much desire of the nobles to command. And when they do not convene to make a law in favour of liberty, but one of the parties throws itself into favouring one [man], then it is that the tyranny at once rises. The people and the nobles of Rome convened to create the Ten, and to create them with so much authority, through the desire which each of the parties had, the one to extinguish the consular name, the other the tribunician. Once they were created, it seeming to the plebs that Appius had become popular and was beating the nobility, the people turned to favouring him. And when a people brings itself to make this error of giving reputation to one so that he may beat those whom it holds in hatred, and that one is wise, it will always befall that he will become tyrant of that city. For he will attend, together with the favour of the people, to extinguishing the nobility, and will never turn to the oppression of the people until he has extinguished it; at which time the people, having known itself to be a servant, has nowhere to flee for refuge. All those who have founded tyrannies in republics have held this way; and if Appius had held this way, that tyranny of his would have taken more life, and would not have failed so soon.
Commentary · 2

Argument. The dispute begins with the cause: this tyranny arose in Rome from the same causes as most tyrannies in cities, too much desire of the people to be free and too much desire of the nobles to command. When the parties do not agree on a law for liberty and one of them throws itself into favouring one man, tyranny rises at once. Rome's parties agreed to create the Ten with great authority, each wishing to extinguish the other's magistracy, the consular name or the tribunician. Once the Ten were created, the plebs, thinking Appius had become popular and was beating the nobility, turned to favouring him. Then a general rule with two conditions: when a people makes the error of giving reputation to one man so that he may beat those it hates, and that one is wise, he will always become tyrant, for he will attend, with the people's favour, to extinguishing the nobility, and will not turn to oppressing the people until he has extinguished it, at which time the people, knowing itself a servant, has nowhere to flee. All founders of tyrannies in republics have held this way; had Appius held it, his tyranny would have lived longer.

Cross-reference. Guicciardini does not believe the tyranny would have been more stable had the decemvirs turned to beating the nobility with the plebs' favour, because the Roman people loved the name of liberty too much; Manlius Capitolinus, who went against the senate by merely popular art, was put down by the people itself as soon as it was made able to grasp that he sought to occupy liberty Guicciardini .01.

1.40.08
Ma ei fece tutto il contrario, né si potette governare più imprudentemente, che per tener la Tirannide e' si fece inimico di coloro che glie l'avevano data, e che glie ne potevano mantenere, ed amico di quelli che non erano concorsi a dargliene, e che non gliene arebbono potuto mantenere, e perdessi coloro che gli erano amici; e cercò di avere amici quelli che non gli potevano essere amici. Perché ancoraché i Nobili desiderino tiranneggiare, quella parte della Nobiltà che si trova fuori della Tirannide, è sempre inimica al Tiranno; né quello se la può mai guadagnare tutta, per l'ambizione grande, e grande avarizia ch'è in lei, non potendo il Tiranno avere né tante ricchezze, né tanti onori, che a tutti satisfaccia. E così Appio lasciando il Popolo, ed accostandosi a' Nobili, fece uno errore evidentissimo, e per le ragioni dette di sopra, e perché a volere con violenza tenere una cosa, bisogna che sia più potente chi sforza, che chi è sforzato.
But he did wholly the contrary, nor could he have governed himself more imprudently, for, to hold the tyranny, he made himself the enemy of those who had given it to him, and who could maintain him in it, and the friend of those who had not concurred in giving it to him, and who would not have been able to maintain him in it, and he lost those who were friends to him; and sought to have as friends those who could not be friends to him. For even though the nobles desire to tyrannise, that part of the nobility which finds itself outside the tyranny is always enemy to the tyrant; nor can that one ever gain it all, through the great ambition and great avarice that is in it, the tyrant not being able to have either so many riches or so many honours that he satisfy all. And so Appius, leaving the people and drawing close to the nobles, made a most evident error, both for the reasons said above, and because, in wishing to hold a thing with violence, it is required that he who forces be more powerful than he who is forced.
Commentary · 1

Argument. Appius did the contrary, and could not have governed himself more imprudently: he made himself the enemy of those who gave him the tyranny and could keep him in it, and the friend of those who had not concurred in giving it and could not have kept him; he lost his friends and sought as friends those who could not be friends to him. The reason is general: though the nobles desire to tyrannise, the part of the nobility outside the tyranny is always the tyrant's enemy, and he cannot win it all, its ambition and avarice being greater than any riches or honours he could give. Turning from people to nobles was therefore a most evident error, for those reasons and because whoever holds a thing by violence must be more powerful than whoever is forced.

1.40.09
Donde nasce che quelli Tiranni che hanno amico l'universale, ed inimici i Grandi, sono più sicuri, per essere la loro violenza sostenuta da maggiori forze, che quella di coloro che hanno per inimico il Popolo ed amica la Nobiltà. Perché con quello favore bastano a conservarsi le forze intrinseche, come bastarono a Nabide Tiranno di Sparta, quando tutta Grecia ed il Popolo romano lo assaltò, il quale assicuratosi di pochi Nobili, avendo amico il Popolo, con quello si difese, il che non arebbe potuto fare avendolo inimico. In quell'altro grado, per aver pochi amici dentro, non bastano le forze intrinseche, ma gli conviene cercare di fuora. E hanno ad essere di tre sorte; l'una satelliti forestieri, che ti guardino la persona; l'altra armare il contado, che faccia quell'ufficio ch'arebbe a fare la Plebe; la terza aderirsi co' vicini potenti, che ti difendano. Chi tiene questi modi, e gli osserva bene, ancora ch'egli avesse per inimico il Popolo, potrebbe in qualche modo salvarsi. Ma Appio non poteva far questo di guadagnarsi il contado, sendo una medesima cosa il contado e Roma, e quel che poteva fare, non seppe; talmenteché rovinò nei primi principj suoi.
Whence it arises that those tyrants who have the generality as friend and the great as enemies are more secure, through their violence being sustained by greater forces than that of those who have the people as enemy and the nobility as friend. For with that favour the intrinsic forces are enough to conserve oneself, as they were enough for Nabis, tyrant of Sparta, when all Greece and the Roman people assailed him; who, having secured himself of a few nobles, having the people as friend, defended himself with it, which he would not have been able to do having it as enemy. In that other degree, through having few friends within, the intrinsic forces are not enough, but it is fitting for him to seek from outside. And they are to be of three sorts: the one, foreign satellites, who may guard your person; the other, to arm the countryside, that it may do that office which the plebs would have to do; the third, to adhere to powerful neighbours, who may defend you. Whoever holds these ways, and observes them well, even though he had the people as enemy, could in some way save himself. But Appius could not do this, of gaining the countryside for himself, the countryside and Rome being one same thing; and what he could do, he did not know how [to do]; so that he fell to ruin in his first beginnings.
Commentary · 3

Argument. The general rule follows: tyrants who have the generality as friend and the great as enemies are more secure, because their violence rests on greater forces. With the people's favour the forces within suffice, as they did for Nabis of Sparta against all Greece and Rome; it is fitting for the tyrant who has the people as enemy to seek forces outside, of three sorts, foreign guards, an armed countryside, powerful neighbours, and whoever holds these ways and observes them well might in some way save himself. Appius could not gain the countryside, Rome and its countryside being one, and what he could do he did not know how to do; so he fell to ruin in his first beginnings. What was closed to him is circumstance; the faults the chapter names are imprudence (.08) and not knowing how (.09).

Cross-reference. Guicciardini says examples are found on both sides and names two, both tyrannies made with the nobles, Sulla's and the Duke of Athens'; he gives reasons on both sides, the people's numbers and tolerance of greatness against the nobility's firmer foundation; and the conclusion, he says, is to be drawn from the humours of the city in question, the condition of the times and other occurrences, not from a firm rule Guicciardini .02.

History. How Nabis secured himself of the nobles Machiavelli does not say, and nothing is supplied.

The senate and the people made very great errors in this creation of the decemvirate; for, even though it be said above, in that discourse which is made of the dictator, that those magistracies which are made by themselves, not those which the people makes, are harmful to liberty, nonetheless the people must, when it orders the magistracies, make them in such a way that they are to have some respect to becoming wicked. And where a guard must be set over them to maintain them good, the Romans removed it, making it the sole magistracy in Rome, and annulling all the others, through the excessive longing, as we said above, which the senate had to extinguish the tribunes, and the plebs to extinguish the consuls; which blinded them in such a way that they concurred in such disorder. For men, as King Ferdinand used to say, often do as certain lesser birds of rapine, in which there is so much desire to attain their prey, to which nature incites them, that they do not sense another, greater bird which is above them to kill them. There is known, then, by this discourse, as I proposed in the beginning, the error of the Roman people, wishing to save liberty, and the errors of Appius, wishing to occupy the tyranny.
Commentary · 2

Argument. Senate and people made very great errors in the creation. Even granting what was said in the discourse on the dictator, that magistracies which make themselves, not those the people makes, are harmful to liberty, the people must order its magistracies so that they have some regard holding them back from becoming wicked; where a guard was needed, the Romans removed it, making the Ten the sole magistracy, blinded by the excessive longing of the senate to extinguish the tribunes and of the plebs to extinguish the consuls. The blindness is explained (Perché) by King Ferdinand's saying, which Machiavelli adopts: men often do as certain lesser birds of rapine, so intent on their prey that they do not sense the greater bird above them. The close narrows the opening's promise: the error, singular, of the Roman people wishing to save liberty, and the errors of Appius wishing to occupy the tyranny.

Cross-reference. The discourse on the dictator is earlier in Book I; Machiavelli gives no chapter number. Guicciardini does not take up the guard or the senate's and people's errors; he is persuaded that the principal error was the decemvirs' attempt itself Guicciardini .01.

donde nacque che si innamorò di Virginia, e che volendola torre per forza, il padre Virginio per liberarla l'ammazzò. "Whence it arose that he fell in love with Virginia, and that, [he] wishing to take her away by force, the father, Virginius, to free her, killed her": torre, take away (D131); ammazzare, killed (D262); "[he]" is supplied to hold the gerund on Appius, since the main clause's subject is the father (D53). "Which, having brought themselves together with the remainder of the Roman plebs, went off to the Sacred Mount": i quali, the armies; ridursi, bring oneself, as .02 si ridusse and ridotta Roma below; "draw together" is ristringersi's (D58).

e ridotta Roma nella forma dell'antica sua libertà. "And Rome brought back into the form of its ancient liberty": ridurre, bring, the chapter's third use of the verb; forma, form.

Notasi adunque per questo testo, in prima esser nato in Roma questo inconveniente di creare questa Tirannide. "It is noted, then, by this text, in the first place, that this inconvenience of creating this tyranny arose in Rome": the dispute begins. testo, text, the narrative just given; the accusative with infinitive takes "that" (D100); inconveniente, inconvenience (D133).

per quelle medesime cagioni che nascono la maggior parte delle Tirannidi nelle Città. "Through those same causes [through] which the greater part of tyrannies arise in cities": che without a preposition, kept loose in Italian and supplied in brackets in English (D53); cagione, cause (glossary).

e questo è da troppo desiderio del Popolo d'esser libero, e da troppo desiderio de' Nobili di comandare. "And this is from too much desire of the people to be free, and from too much desire of the nobles to command": desiderio, desire (D117), twice, as the Italian repeats it, and again in the birds of .10.

E quando e' non convengono a fare una legge in favore della libertà, ma gettasi qualcuna delle parti a favorire uno. "And when they do not convene to make a law in favour of liberty, but one of the parties throws itself into favouring one [man]": convenire with a personal subject, come together, agree, "convene" in its older sense (D358), and again at Convennero il Popolo e i Nobili; the impersonal conviene stays "it is fitting" (D85), as at .09. parte, party; favorire, favour (glossary).

allora è che subito la Tirannide surge. "Then it is that the tyranny at once rises": surgere, rise, as NM I.4 surga and I.30 surgeva; levarsi (NM I.39) shares the word, a recorded collapse (D358). subito, at once (D177).

Convennero il Popolo e i Nobili di Roma a creare i Dieci. "The people and the nobles of Rome convened to create the Ten": the predicate first in the Italian, answering non convengono just before (D263's contrast); English order, the contrast carried by the repeated verb, since a fronted "convened" would be costume.

l'una di spegnere il nome Consolare, l'altra il Tribunizio. "The one to extinguish the consular name, the other the tribunician": spegnere, extinguish (glossary), four times in the dispute; nome, name, as I.39's close.

Creati che furono, parendo alla Plebe che Appio fusse diventato popolare, e battesse la Nobiltà, si volse il Popolo a favorirlo. "Once they were created, it seeming to the plebs that Appius had become popular and was beating the nobility, the people turned to favouring him": the predicate first in the Italian, plain order in English (D263, exposition); Plebe and Popolo both in one sentence, kept apart (plebs, people).

E quando un Popolo si conduce a far questo errore di dare riputazione ad uno, perché batta quelli che egli ha in odio, e che quello uno sia savio. "And when a people brings itself to make this error of giving reputation to one so that he may beat those whom it holds in hatred, and that one is wise": riputazione, reputation (glossary); perché with the subjunctive, purpose; savio, wise. The second condition, e che quello uno sia savio, is part of the rule: without it the rule does not hold. Appius was called sagace at .02; the chapter's verdict on him is imprudence (.08) and not knowing how (.09), not the word savio.

Perché egli attenderà insieme con il favore del Popolo a spegnere la Nobiltà, e non si volterà mai alla oppressione del Popolo, se non quando ei l'arà spenta. "For he will attend, together with the favour of the people, to extinguishing the nobility, and will never turn to the oppression of the people until he has extinguished it": attendere a, attend to (D56); oppressione, with opprimere, oppress (glossary).

nel qual tempo conosciutosi il Popolo essere servo, non abbia dove rifuggire. "At which time the people, having known itself to be a servant, has nowhere to flee for refuge": servo, servant (glossary, I.16); rifuggire, flee for refuge (D178). The subjunctive is of the result the tyrant waits for, not of possibility, and "may have" would weaken it.

Questo modo hanno tenuto tutti coloro che hanno fondato Tirannidi nelle Repubbliche. "All those who have founded tyrannies in republics have held this way": plain order (D263, exposition); modo, way (glossary); tenere, hold; fondare, found.

quella sua Tirannide arebbe preso più vita, e non sarebbe mancata sì presto. "That tyranny of his would have taken more life, and would not have failed so soon": the counterfactual (se ... avesse tenuto ... arebbe preso) turns the rule on Appius; mancare, fail (D206).

Ma ei fece tutto il contrario, né si potette governare più imprudentemente, che per tener la Tirannide e' si fece inimico di coloro che glie l'avevano data. "But he did wholly the contrary, nor could he have governed himself more imprudently, for, to hold the tyranny, he made himself the enemy of those who had given it to him": che read as causal, "for"; it may also be the che of the comparison after più imprudentemente. potette, a judgment on what he did, "could he have". inimico, enemy (D334); governarsi, govern oneself.

e che glie ne potevano mantenere, ed amico di quelli che non erano concorsi a dargliene, e che non gliene arebbono potuto mantenere. "And who could maintain him in it, and the friend of those who had not concurred in giving it to him, and who would not have been able to maintain him in it": concorrere, concur (D01), here in its first sense, to run together to a deed; it returns at .10, concorsono in tale disordine. The 1554 edition reads & nemico, which makes Appius the enemy of both parties against the antithesis the period states (NM-DISC-I40-D006, D354), and potuta (NM-DISC-I40-D003, D353).

e perdessi coloro che gli erano amici; e cercò di avere amici quelli che non gli potevano essere amici. "And he lost those who were friends to him; and sought to have as friends those who could not be friends to him": the semicolon where the witness has it, so that the loss of friends closes the first antithesis and the seeking opens its restatement; amici three times, as the Italian repeats it.

né quello se la può mai guadagnare tutta, per l'ambizione grande, e grande avarizia ch'è in lei. "Nor can that one ever gain it all, through the great ambition and great avarice that is in it": quello, that one, the tyrant (D165); guadagnare, gain (glossary); avarizia, avarice (glossary). The Italian's chiasmus (ambizione grande, e grande avarizia) is not kept: English cannot postpose the first adjective without costume.

non potendo il Tiranno avere né tante ricchezze, né tanti onori, che a tutti satisfaccia. "The tyrant not being able to have either so many riches or so many honours that he satisfy all": satisfare, satisfy; the singular satisfaccia read with the tyrant; the riches and honours as its subject is possible, as assaltò with two subjects (.09). The 1554 edition reads a tutta, the whole nobility (NM-DISC-I40-D007, D354).

E così Appio lasciando il Popolo, ed accostandosi a' Nobili, fece uno errore evidentissimo. "And so Appius, leaving the people and drawing close to the nobles, made a most evident error": accostarsi, draw close.

a volere con violenza tenere una cosa, bisogna che sia più potente chi sforza, che chi è sforzato. "In wishing to hold a thing with violence, it is required that he who forces be more powerful than he who is forced": bisogna, "it is required", the edition's rendering of the impersonal bisognare in every tense (D260); the reasons are in the note on bisogni at Machiavelli I.2. sforzare, force; potente, powerful (D06). The maxim is the second of two reasons (e per le ragioni dette di sopra, e perché).

Perché con quello favore bastano a conservarsi le forze intrinseche, come bastarono a Nabide Tiranno di Sparta. "For with that favour the intrinsic forces are enough to conserve oneself, as they were enough for Nabis, tyrant of Sparta": bastare, be enough (D205), three times; conservare, conserve (D220); intrinseco, intrinsic, forces within the state. Nabis as NM I.10 (names.md).

quando tutta Grecia ed il Popolo romano lo assaltò, il quale assicuratosi di pochi Nobili, avendo amico il Popolo, con quello si difese. "When all Greece and the Roman people assailed him; who, having secured himself of a few nobles, having the people as friend, defended himself with it": assicurarsi di, secure oneself of (D219); assaltò is singular with its double subject, which English "assailed" does not show. How Nabis secured himself of the nobles, Machiavelli does not say.

In quell'altro grado, per aver pochi amici dentro, non bastano le forze intrinseche, ma gli conviene cercare di fuora. "In that other degree, through having few friends within, the intrinsic forces are not enough, but it is fitting for him to seek from outside": grado, degree (glossary), the condition of the tyrant who has the people as enemy; conviene, it is fitting (D85).

E hanno ad essere di tre sorte. "And they are to be of three sorts": avere a, destinative (D61, D261). The three sorts are listed in asyndeton, and the second and third are infinitives against the first's noun; both are kept.

l'una satelliti forestieri, che ti guardino la persona; l'altra armare il contado, che faccia quell'ufficio ch'arebbe a fare la Plebe. "The one, foreign satellites, who may guard your person; the other, to arm the countryside, that it may do that office which the plebs would have to do": forestiere, foreign (glossary); contado, countryside (D70); ufficio, office (D56); avere a in the conditional, "have to" (D261). The second person enters here and stays through ti difendano.

Ma Appio non poteva far questo di guadagnarsi il contado, sendo una medesima cosa il contado e Roma. "But Appius could not do this, of gaining the countryside for himself, the countryside and Rome being one same thing": the reason is Machiavelli's, that the countryside and the city were one, so that the remedy of arming the countryside against the plebs was closed to him; medesimo, same (glossary); contado, countryside (glossary).

e quel che poteva fare, non seppe; talmenteché rovinò nei primi principj suoi. "And what he could do, he did not know how [to do]; so that he fell to ruin in his first beginnings": sapere of capacity, "know how", the verb supplied (D53); talmente che, so that (glossary); rovinare, intransitive, fall to ruin (D256); principio, beginning (glossary).

perché ancoraché di sopra si dica in quel discorso che si fa del Dittatore. "For, even though it be said above, in that discourse which is made of the dictator": the subjunctive under ancoraché kept, "be said". The discourse on the dictator is earlier in Book I; Machiavelli gives no chapter number, and none is supplied.

che quelli Magistrati che si fanno da per loro, non quelli che fa il Popolo, sono nocivi alla libertà. "That those magistracies which are made by themselves, not those which the people makes, are harmful to liberty": da per loro, of themselves, magistracies that take authority without the people's grant; nocivo, harmful (D106). The 1554 edition prints dap loro (NM-DISC-I40-D011).

nondimeno il Popolo debbe, quando egli ordina i Magistrati, fargli in modo ch'egli abbiano ad avere qualche rispetto a diventare tristi. "Nonetheless the people must, when it orders the magistracies, make them in such a way that they are to have some respect to becoming wicked": dovere, must (D274); ordinare, order (glossary); avere a, destinative (D61); rispetto, respect (glossary), here regard that holds them back; tristo, wicked (D03).

E dove e' si debbe proporre loro guardia per mantenerli buoni, i Romani la levarono. "And where a guard must be set over them to maintain them good, the Romans removed it": proporre here is to set a guard over, not to propose a candidate (.04) or a subject (proposi, below); a scoped sense (D358), sharing "set" with porre (D115) because the root is shared. guardia, guard (glossary); levare, remove (D131).

facendolo solo Magistrato in Roma, ed annullando tutti gli altri. "Making it the sole magistracy in Rome, and annulling all the others": solo before its noun, "sole"; D172's "alone" is the predicative and adverbial solo, and "making it alone the magistracy" would misplace it (D358). annullare, annul.

per la eccessiva voglia, come di sopra dicemmo, che il Senato aveva di spegnere i Tribuni, e la Plebe di spegnere i Consoli. "Through the excessive longing, as we said above, which the senate had to extinguish the tribunes, and the plebs to extinguish the consuls": voglia, longing (D117); the "above" is .07, where the parties are the people and the nobles.

la quale gli accecò in modo, che concorsono in tale disordine. "Which blinded them in such a way that they concurred in such disorder": concorrere, concur (D01), the senate and people running together into the disorder, as the friends of .08 had not concurred in giving Appius the tyranny; disordine, disorder (glossary).

Perché gli uomini, come diceva il Re Ferrando, spesso fanno come certi minori uccelli di rapina. "For men, as King Ferdinand used to say, often do as certain lesser birds of rapine": the imperfect diceva, "used to say". names.md identifies Machiavelli's Ferdinand of I.29 as Ferdinand II of Aragon; whether this is the same king is not said here. rapina, rapine, so that preda, prey, keeps its own word in the same period.

ne' quali è tanto desiderio di conseguire la loro preda, a che la natura gli incita, che non sentono un altro maggior uccello, che sia loro sopra per ammazzarli. "In which there is so much desire to attain their prey, to which nature incites them, that they do not sense another, greater bird which is above them to kill them": conseguire, attain (glossary); sentire, sense; ammazzare, kill (D262).

Conoscesi adunque per questo discorso, come nel principio proposi, l'errore del Popolo romano, volendo salvare la libertà, e gli errori di Appio, volendo occupare la Tirannide. "There is known, then, by this discourse, as I proposed in the beginning, the error of the Roman people, wishing to save liberty, and the errors of Appius, wishing to occupy the tyranny": the subjects held to the end, where the chapter's two lessons land (D18); conoscere, know; proporre, propose, a third sense of the verb in the chapter, Machiavelli's own undertaking of .01; salvare, save, the rubric's verb; occupare la tirannide, occupy the tyranny (glossary, NM I.9). The close narrows .01: there, many errors of the senate and the plebs; at the opening of .10, very great errors of the senate and the people; here, the one error of the Roman people, against the errors of Appius. volendo twice, the two wills set side by side.

1.40.01
Io mi persuado che il principale errore che facessi Appio e i compagni fussi il persuadersi di potere fondare in quelli tempi una tirannide nella città di Roma, la quale era allora ordinata di ottime leggi, piena di santissimi costumi, e ardentissima del desiderio della libertà, e la quale, per essere il populo militare, era troppo difficile a violentare, e però durò quella tirannide mentre che con qualche colore, cioè dell'avere a finire le leggi, potettono allegare che il magistrato loro durassi, ma come questo inganno fu scoperto, il primo accidente benché piccolo distrusse la loro tirannide, la quale non credo fussi stata più stabile, se bene si fussino vòlti a battere col favore della plebe la nobiltà, perché quello populo era troppo amicissimo del nome della libertà. E si vede lo esemplo di Manlio Capitolino, il quale ancora che procedessi contro al senato e con arte meramente populare, pure fu oppresso dal populo medesimo, subito che fu fatto capace che lui cercava occupare la libertà.
I persuade myself that the principal error which Appius and the companions made was their persuading themselves that they could found in those times a tyranny in the city of Rome, which was then ordered with very good laws, full of most holy customs, and most ardent with the desire of liberty, and which, through the people's being military, was too difficult to do violence to; and therefore that tyranny lasted while, with some colour, that is, of having to finish the laws, they could allege that their magistracy should last, but once this deceit was discovered, the first accident, albeit small, destroyed their tyranny, which I do not believe would have been more stable, even had they turned to beating the nobility with the favour of the plebs, because that people was too exceedingly a friend of the name of liberty. And one sees the example of Manlius Capitolinus, who, even though he proceeded against the senate and with merely popular art, yet was oppressed by the people itself, as soon as it was made able to grasp that he was seeking to occupy liberty.
Commentary · 3

Argument. The reply opens on a conviction, Io mi persuado, and the principal error it judges is a persuasion too: the decemvirs persuaded themselves that a tyranny could be founded in the Rome of those times. The ground is the city as it then was: ordered with very good laws, full of most holy customs, most ardent for liberty, and, its people being military, too hard to do violence to. Therefore the tyranny lasted while a pretext held, the unfinished laws; once the deceit was discovered, the first accident, small as it was, destroyed it. Then a negated belief: he does not believe it would have been more stable even had they turned to beating the nobility with the plebs' favour, because that people was too great a friend of the name of liberty. Manlius Capitolinus is the example, not the ground: though he went against the senate by merely popular art, the people itself put him down as soon as it was made able to grasp that he sought to occupy liberty. The limit is of time and place, in quelli tempi, allora; he does not say a tyranny could never be founded at Rome, and his next paragraph names Sulla.

Cross-reference. Machiavelli lists many errors on both sides Machiavelli .01 and calls Appius' turn from the people to the nobles a most evident error Machiavelli .08; Guicciardini is persuaded that the principal error was the attempt itself. Machiavelli's counterfactual, that had Appius held the way of turning the people against the nobility his tyranny would have taken more life Machiavelli .07, is answered in its own verbs, turning and beating, and refused by a negated belief, not a proof. Machiavelli's cause of tyranny, too much desire of the people to be free, is not taken up; in Guicciardini the city's ardour for liberty is among the reasons the attempt could not succeed.

History. Manlius Capitolinus appears in Machiavelli's I.8 and I.24 and in Guicciardini's I.5, I.7 and I.8 (names.md); neither author dates his fall here.

1.40.02
E quanto alla dottrina generale, quale sia meglio a chi vuole occupare la tirannide, o procedere col favore del populo, o farsi amica la nobiltà, gli esempli si truovano diversi; perché e Silla occupò la tirannide a Roma, e la stabilì con le spalle della nobiltà, e a Firenze il Duca d'Atene fu fatto tiranno col favore de' nobili, e' quali per la sua imprudenza e levità non si seppe mantenere, il che fu causa di farnelo cadere presto. Così nell'una parte e nell'altra si trovano molti esempli, e anche ciascuna parte ha le sue ragioni, perché chi ha il populo dal suo, ha più numero di seguaci, e più facilmente comporta il populo una grandezza che non comportano e' nobili, e nondimeno chi ha seco la nobiltà, ha uno fondamento più nervoso, più efficace e più gagliardo, e che non varia di animo sì facilmente e spesso per cagioni leggieri, come fa il populo. Sono partiti che non si possono pigliare con una regola ferma, ma la conclusione s'ha a cavare dagli umori di quella città, dallo essere delle cose che si varia secondo la condizione de' tempi, e altre occorrenze che girano.
And as to the general doctrine, which is better for whoever wishes to occupy the tyranny, either to proceed with the favour of the people, or to make the nobility one's friend, the examples are found different; for both Sulla occupied the tyranny at Rome, and established it with the shoulders of the nobility, and at Florence the Duke of Athens was made tyrant with the favour of the nobles, whom, through his imprudence and levity, he did not know how to maintain [for himself], which was the cause of making him fall from it soon. So in the one party and in the other many examples are found, and also each party has its reasons, because whoever has the people on his side has a greater number of followers, and the people more readily bears a greatness than the nobles bear, and nonetheless whoever has the nobility with him has a foundation more sinewy, more efficacious and more hard, and one that does not vary in spirit so readily and often for light causes, as the people does. They are courses that cannot be taken by a firm rule, but the conclusion is to be drawn from the humours of that city, from the being of things, which varies according to the condition of the times, and other occurrences that go round.
Commentary · 3

Argument. On the general doctrine, whether a would-be tyrant does better with the people's favour or the nobility's friendship, the examples differ. Both of his are tyrannies made with the nobles: Sulla occupied the tyranny at Rome and established it on the nobility's shoulders; the Duke of Athens was made tyrant at Florence by the nobles and, through his own imprudence and levity, did not know how to keep them, which brought his quick fall. Each side has its reasons, set in parallel: whoever has the people has more followers, and the people bears a man's greatness more readily than the nobles do; nonetheless whoever has the nobility has a foundation more sinewy, more efficacious and more hard, which does not vary in spirit so readily and often, for light causes, as the people does. Then the refusal: these are courses not to be taken by a firm rule; the conclusion is to be drawn from the humours of the city in question, from the being of things that varies with the times, and from other occurrences. He does not say Machiavelli's rule is false, and he does not prefer the nobility.

Cross-reference. Machiavelli's rule is that tyrants with the generality as friend are more secure, their violence resting on greater forces, with Nabis as his example Machiavelli .09. Guicciardini grants the people its numbers and gives the nobility the firmer foundation, and answers a rule with the refusal of a rule. The Duke, made by the nobles and unable to keep them, may be set beside Appius' error with the parties reversed, an editorial observation: Appius lost the people who had made him and could maintain him Machiavelli .08.

History. Guicciardini gives no date for the Duke of Athens, and none is supplied; names.md records the usual identification, unchecked. Sulla is named by Machiavelli with Marius at I.28; Guicciardini gives no detail of how the nobility backed him.

la quale era allora ordinata di ottime leggi, piena di santissimi costumi, e ardentissima del desiderio della libertà. "Which was then ordered with very good laws, full of most holy customs, and most ardent with the desire of liberty": the three qualities in a row, three superlatives, the first the suppletive ottimo, very good, the elative of buono as pessimo of cattivo (glossary); santo, holy (D313); costume, custom (D41). Machiavelli made the people's troppo desiderio ... d'esser libero a cause of tyranny (Machiavelli .07); Guicciardini's desiderio della libertà stands in his praise of the city.

e la quale, per essere il populo militare, era troppo difficile a violentare. "And which, through the people's being military, was too difficult to do violence to": violentare in the root of violenza, Machiavelli's word for holding a thing by force (Machiavelli .08, .09). The two readers doubted the mark after libertà; both preferred a semicolon, and the comma is the 1933 edition's (D357).

e però durò quella tirannide mentre che con qualche colore, cioè dell'avere a finire le leggi, potettono allegare che il magistrato loro durassi. "And therefore that tyranny lasted while, with some colour, that is, of having to finish the laws, they could allege that their magistracy should last": però, therefore (D22), consecutive on the difficulty of doing violence to such a people; colore, colour, a pretext; avere a after the nominal infinitive, "having to" (D261). Machiavelli tells how the Ten stayed on when the two tables were made but not published (Machiavelli .05); Guicciardini gives the pretext a name.

ma come questo inganno fu scoperto, il primo accidente benché piccolo distrusse la loro tirannide. "But once this deceit was discovered, the first accident, albeit small, destroyed their tyranny": come, of time, "once"; inganno, deceit; benché, albeit (D31); accidente, accident (glossary), the rubric's word. Which accident is not said; Machiavelli's narrative has the Sabine and Volscian war and then Virginia (Machiavelli .06).

la quale non credo fussi stata più stabile, se bene si fussino vòlti a battere col favore della plebe la nobiltà. "Which I do not believe would have been more stable, even had they turned to beating the nobility with the favour of the plebs": a negated belief (credo, glossary), not a denial. It answers Machiavelli's counterfactual, that had Appius held the way of turning the people against the nobility his tyranny would have taken more life (Machiavelli .07). se bene with the pluperfect subjunctive is a supposition contrary to fact: they did not turn. D31's "although" would read as a fact in modern English, so the counterfactual takes "even had", a scoped exception (D363); volgersi, turn, as Machiavelli's si volse il Popolo a favorirlo; battere, beat (D120), as Machiavelli's battesse la Nobiltà; stabile, stable. Both readers doubted the mark after nobiltà and preferred a semicolon; the comma is the 1933 edition's (D357).

perché quello populo era troppo amicissimo del nome della libertà. "Because that people was too exceedingly a friend of the name of liberty": the double intensive kept, as molto lunghissima at FG I.16 (D227); amico, friend, the word of Machiavelli's friends and enemies (Machiavelli .08, .09); nome, name: compare the names Machiavelli's parties wished to extinguish (Machiavelli .07).

E si vede lo esemplo di Manlio Capitolino. "And one sees the example of Manlius Capitolinus": Manlius as FG I.5, I.7, I.8 and NM I.8, I.24 (names.md).

il quale ancora che procedessi contro al senato e con arte meramente populare, pure fu oppresso dal populo medesimo. "Who, even though he proceeded against the senate and with merely popular art, yet was oppressed by the people itself": ancora che, even though (glossary); meramente, merely (D298); pure, yet (D191), the concession answered; opprimere, oppress (glossary); arte without an article, "art", Machiavelli's word for the craft of Appius' companions against him (farlo con arte, Machiavelli .04).

subito che fu fatto capace che lui cercava occupare la libertà. "As soon as it was made able to grasp that he was seeking to occupy liberty": capace, able to grasp, the older sense (D350); occupare, occupy, as occupare la tirannide below and Machiavelli's last words (Machiavelli .10). The paragraph ends with a stop that both readers doubted and preferred; the 1933 edition prints it (D357).

E quanto alla dottrina generale, quale sia meglio a chi vuole occupare la tirannide, o procedere col favore del populo, o farsi amica la nobiltà. "And as to the general doctrine, which is better for whoever wishes to occupy the tyranny, either to proceed with the favour of the people, or to make the nobility one's friend": dottrina, doctrine, Machiavelli's rule of .07-.09, that tyrants with the generality as friend are more secure; procedere, proceed (glossary), as Manlius procedessi above.

gli esempli si truovano diversi. "The examples are found different": diverso, different (D348); si truovano and, below, si trovano, two spellings of one verb, "are found". The two readers doubted the mark after diversi and preferred a semicolon, which the 1933 edition prints (D357).

perché e Silla occupò la tirannide a Roma, e la stabilì con le spalle della nobiltà. "For both Sulla occupied the tyranny at Rome, and established it with the shoulders of the nobility": e ... e, both ... and; stabilire, establish, Machiavelli's verb for the tyranny Appius presupposed he would establish (Machiavelli .01); spalle, shoulders, backing, kept. Sulla as NM I.1, I.28 (names.md).

e a Firenze il Duca d'Atene fu fatto tiranno col favore de' nobili, e' quali per la sua imprudenza e levità non si seppe mantenere. "And at Florence the Duke of Athens was made tyrant with the favour of the nobles, whom, through his imprudence and levity, he did not know how to maintain [for himself]": e' quali is the object of mantenere, and si the dative of interest: he could not keep the nobles who had made him. mantenere, maintain (glossary), as Machiavelli's friends who glie ne potevano mantenere (Machiavelli .08). levità, levity, a recorded collapse with leggerezza (FG I.28; D358); imprudenza, imprudence, Machiavelli's imprudentemente of Appius (Machiavelli .08); sapere, know how, as Machiavelli's non seppe (Machiavelli .09). names.md records the usual identification of the Duke of Athens, Walter of Brienne, lord of Florence in 1342-43, as unchecked.

il che fu causa di farnelo cadere presto. "Which was the cause of making him fall from it soon": ne, from it, the tyranny; causa, cause (glossary). Both readers saw no stop before Così; it is supplied from the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-I40-C001, D357).

Così nell'una parte e nell'altra si trovano molti esempli, e anche ciascuna parte ha le sue ragioni. "So in the one party and in the other many examples are found, and also each party has its reasons": parte, party, as Machiavelli's qualcuna delle parti (Machiavelli .07) and FG I.23 nell'altra parte (D243); ragione, reason.

perché chi ha il populo dal suo, ha più numero di seguaci, e più facilmente comporta il populo una grandezza che non comportano e' nobili. "Because whoever has the people on his side has a greater number of followers, and the people more readily bears a greatness than the nobles bear": comportare, bear, as FG I.39 non comportava; facilmente, readily (D27); grandezza, greatness (glossary); the expletive non after the comparative not rendered.

e nondimeno chi ha seco la nobiltà, ha uno fondamento più nervoso, più efficace e più gagliardo. "And nonetheless whoever has the nobility with him has a foundation more sinewy, more efficacious and more hard": più three times, as the Italian repeats it; nondimeno, nonetheless (glossary); fondamento, foundation (glossary); nervoso, sinewy; gagliardo, hard (D313). Machiavelli's tyrant with the people as friend has maggiori forze, greater forces (Machiavelli .09); Guicciardini grants the people's number and gives the nobility the firmer foundation.

e che non varia di animo sì facilmente e spesso per cagioni leggieri, come fa il populo. "And one that does not vary in spirit so readily and often for light causes, as the people does": e che, the letter doubted by both readers, and sì, which both read without its accent, are read with the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-I40-D002, D356); sì facilmente, so readily. animo, spirit (glossary); cagione, cause (glossary), here beside causa in the same paragraph, a recorded collapse. Both readers saw no stop before Sono; it is supplied from the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-I40-C002, D357).

Sono partiti che non si possono pigliare con una regola ferma. "They are courses that cannot be taken by a firm rule": partito, course (D236); pigliare, take (D283), as FG I.26 non pigliando ... per regola assoluta; regola, rule. The refusal is of a rule, not of the question.

ma la conclusione s'ha a cavare dagli umori di quella città, dallo essere delle cose che si varia secondo la condizione de' tempi, e altre occorrenze che girano. "But the conclusion is to be drawn from the humours of that city, from the being of things, which varies according to the condition of the times, and other occurrences that turn": avere a, destinative, "is to be" (D61, D261); umore, humour (glossary), Machiavelli's word of I.39; condizione, condition (glossary); variare, vary, twice, the nobility that does not vary and the being of things that does. occorrenza, occurrence, from occorrere (D01): the reply ends in the currere family. girare, go round, perhaps as a wheel goes round; "turn" is kept for volgersi (vòlti, above) (D358). quella città is whatever city is in question.

What this is

A free, openly licensed, bilingual edition of Guicciardini's 39 Considerazioni and the Discorsi chapters they answer — 78 text units, Italian and English facing, every reading traceable to a named public-domain witness.

It is a reading edition with a transparent apparatus, not a text of record. It does not adjudicate the critical text against Bausi 2001 or the manuscript tradition, and does not claim to.

On the title

Machiavelli had been dead two years when Guicciardini wrote; the Discorsi were unpublished, and the Considerazioni stayed unprinted until 1857. Machiavelli never answers.

“Conversation” is a quotation, not a description. It invokes Machiavelli's letter to Francesco Vettori of 10 December 1513: at evening he puts on royal and curial garments (panni reali e curiali), enters the ancient courts of ancient men (le antiche corti degli antichi uomini), asks them the reason for their actions, and quelli per loro umanità mi rispondono, “they, for their humanity, answer me”. Guicciardini does to Machiavelli what Machiavelli did to Livy — and is denied the reply Machiavelli claimed. The letter is quoted from the 1813 Opere, vol. 8, p. 96, read twice from the page image.

Method

No image-derived reading enters the text on a single reading. Each unit is transcribed twice, independently, by readers who have not seen each other's work; disagreements are adjudicated against the page images and recorded.

Existing English translations of the Considerazioni — Grayson (1965), Atkinson & Sices (2002) — are never used as translation inputs.
