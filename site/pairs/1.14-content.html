A Conversation with Machiavelli · Pair 13 of 39

Craft or belief Discourses I.14, and Guicciardini's reply

Machiavelli argues that the Romans bent the auspices to necessity while keeping up the show of obeying them, and takes the consul Papirius as his proof. Guicciardini does not hold it for certain that the captains were so calculating, believes that in the first times their minds were taken up with the religion, and finds that the same story does not tell against him.

NM-DISC.1.14

Capitolo XIV

I Romani interpretavano gli auspizj secondo la necessità, e con la prudenza mostravano di osservare la Religione, quando forzati non l'osservavano, e se alcuno temerariamente la dispregiava, lo punivano.

The Romans interpreted the auspices according to necessity, and with prudence made a show of observing religion when, forced, they did not observe it, and if anyone rashly despised it, they punished him.

Italian · 1824
English
1.14.01
Non solamente gli augurj, come di sopra si è discorso, erano il fondamento in buona parte dell'antica Religione de' Gentili, ma ancora erano quelli, ch'erano cagione del bene essere della Repubblica romana. Donde i Romani ne avevano più cura che di alcuno altro ordine di quella, ed usavangli ne' Comizj consolari, nel principiare le imprese, nel trar fuori gli eserciti, nel fare le giornate, e in ogni azione loro importante o civile o militare; né mai sarebbono iti ad una espedizione, che non avessero persuaso ai soldati che gli Dii promettevano loro la vittoria. E fra gli altri aruspizj, avevano negli eserciti certi ordini di auspizj che e' chiamavano Pollarj. E qualunque volta eglino ordinavano di fare la giornata col nimico, volevano che i Pollarj facessero i loro auspizj; e beccando i polli, combattevano con buono augurio, non beccando, si astenevano dalla zuffa.
Not only were the auguries, as has been discoursed above, in good part the foundation of the ancient religion of the Gentiles, but they were also those that were the cause of the well-being of the Roman republic. Whence the Romans had more care of them than of any other order of it, and used them in the consular comitia, in beginning enterprises, in leading out the armies, in giving battle, and in every important action of theirs, whether civil or military; nor would they ever have gone on an expedition without having persuaded the soldiers that the gods promised them the victory. And among the other haruspicies they had in the armies certain orders of auspices, which they called pullarii. And whenever they ordered battle to be given with the enemy, they wished the pullarii to make their auspices; and, the chickens pecking, they fought with good augury; not pecking, they abstained from the engagement.
Commentary · 2

Argument. The chapter continues Machiavelli's run on Roman religion. The rubric states the thesis before the example, in three parts: the Romans read the auspices by necessity, kept up the show of observing them when they did not, and punished anyone who despised them rashly. The chapter opens by restating how much the auguries mattered, so that what follows reads as the management of something important, not contempt for something trivial.

History. The pullarii kept the sacred chickens that went with a Roman army. Before battle the birds were offered food; if they ate so greedily that grain fell from their beaks, the sign was favourable (Cicero, De divinatione 2.72).

1.14.02
Nondimeno quando la ragione mostrava loro una cosa doversi fare, non ostante che gli auspizj fussero avversi, la facevano in ogni modo; ma rivoltavanla con termini e modi tanto attentamente, che non paresse che la facessero con dispregio della Religione: il quale termine fu usato da Papirio Consolo in una zuffa che fece importantissima coi Sanniti, dopo la quale restorno in tutto deboli ed afflitti.
Nonetheless, when reason showed them a thing ought to be done, notwithstanding that the auspices were adverse, they did it in any case; but they turned it about with terms and modes so attentively that it should not appear they did it in contempt of religion: which term was used by Papirius the consul in a most important engagement that he had with the Samnites, after which they remained altogether weak and afflicted.
Commentary · 1

Argument. The thesis in small: when reason said a thing should be done and the auspices were against it, the Romans did it anyway, but dressed the doing in "terms and modes" that kept it from looking like contempt of religion. Papirius is introduced as the man who used this "term" before his story is told.

1.14.03
Perché sendo Papirio in su i campi rincontro ai Sanniti, e parendogli avere nella zuffa la vittoria certa, e volendo per questo fare la giornata, comandò ai Pollarj che facessero i loro auspizj; ma non beccando i polli, e veggendo il Principe de' Pollarj la gran disposizione dello esercito di combattere, e la opinione ch'era nel Capitano e in tutt' i soldati di vincere, per non torre occasione di bene operare a quello esercito, riferì al Consolo come gli auspizj procedevano bene; talché Papirio ordinando le squadre, ed essendo da alcuno de' Pollarj detto a certi soldati i polli non avere beccato, quelli lo dissono a Spurio Papirio nipote del Consolo, e quello riferendolo al Consolo, rispose subito ch'egli attendesse a fare l'ufficio suo bene, e che quanto a lui e allo esercito gli auspizj erano retti, e se il Pollario aveva detto le bugie, ritornerebbono in pregiudizio suo. E perché lo effetto corrispondesse al pronostico, comandò ai Legati che costituissero i Pollarj nella prima fronte della zuffa. Onde nacque che andando contro ai nemici, sendo da un soldato romano tratto un dardo, a caso ammazzò il Principe de' Pollarj; la qual cosa udita il Consolo, disse come ogni cosa procedeva bene, e col favore degli Dii, perché lo esercito con la morte di quel bugiardo si era purgato da ogni colpa, e da ogni ira che quelli avessero preso contro di lui. E così col sapere bene accomodare i disegni suoi agli auspizj, prese partito di azzuffarsi, senza che quello esercito si avvedesse, che in alcuna parte quello avesse negletti gli ordini della loro Religione.
For, Papirius being in the field over against the Samnites, and it seeming to him that victory in the engagement was certain, and wishing for this reason to give battle, he commanded the pullarii to make their auspices; but, the chickens not pecking, and the prince of the pullarii seeing the great disposition of the army to fight, and the opinion that was in the captain and in all the soldiers of winning, so as not to take from that army the occasion of acting well, he reported to the consul that the auspices were proceeding well; so that, Papirius ordering the squadrons, and it being said by some of the pullarii to certain soldiers that the chickens had not pecked, they told it to Spurius Papirius, nephew of the consul, and he reporting it to the consul, [the consul] answered at once that he should attend to doing his office well, and that as for himself and the army the auspices were right, and if the pullarius had told lies, they would turn back to his prejudice. And so that the effect should correspond to the prognostic, he commanded the legates to station the pullarii in the front line of the engagement. Whence it arose that, as they went against the enemy, a dart thrown by a Roman soldier by chance killed the prince of the pullarii; which thing being heard, the consul said that everything was proceeding well, and with the favour of the gods, for the army by the death of that liar had purged itself of every fault, and of every anger that they might have taken against it. And so, by knowing well how to accommodate his designs to the auspices, he took the decision to engage, without that army perceiving that in any part he had neglected the orders of their religion.
Commentary · 2

History. The story is Livy's (10.40), from the Third Samnite War: the battle of Aquilonia, 293 BC, under the consul Lucius Papirius Cursor. In Livy the consul tells his nephew that if the keeper has lied, the omen falls on the keeper's own head; the keeper, posted in the front line, is struck down by a stray javelin.

Argument. Machiavelli tells it as a demonstration. The "prince of the pullarii" who reports a favourable sign is the liar the dart later kills: the consul turns the lie into a true sign by putting the liar where the gods can punish him, and fights with his army's piety intact. Every step is a management of appearances. The reply reads the same steps as ordinary procedure Guicciardini .01.

1.14.04
Al contrario fece Appio Pulcro in Sicilia nella prima guerra Punica, che volendo azzuffarsi con l'esercito Cartaginese, fece fare gli auspizj a' Pollarj, e riferendogli quelli come i Polli non beccavano, disse, veggiamo se volessero bere, e gli fece gittare in mare, dondeché azzuffandosi perdette la giornata, di che egli ne fu a Roma condannato, e Papirio onorato, non tanto per aver l'uno perduto e l'altro vinto, quanto per aver l'uno fatto contro agli auspizj prudentemente, e l'altro temerariamente.
Appius Pulcher did the contrary in Sicily in the first Punic war, who, wishing to engage the Carthaginian army, had the auspices made by the pullarii, and, they reporting to him that the chickens were not pecking, said, let us see whether they would drink, and had them thrown into the sea; whereupon, engaging, he lost the battle, for which he was condemned at Rome, and Papirius honoured, not so much for the one having lost and the other won, as for the one having acted against the auspices prudently, and the other rashly.
Commentary · 2

History. "Appius Pulcher" is Publius Claudius Pulcher, consul in 249 BC, who had the chickens thrown into the sea before the sea-fight off Drepana and lost his fleet (Cicero, De natura deorum 2.7). Machiavelli gives him his father's name, Appius.

Argument. The counter-example, and the third clause of the rubric: rash contempt, punished. Machiavelli says the two men were judged not so much for losing and winning as for going against the auspices "prudently" and "rashly". Both went against them. Read strictly, the second "the one ... the other" would make Appius the prudent one, since he was "the one" who lost; the sense requires the reverse, and the English keeps the Italian's looseness.

1.14.05
Né ad altro fine tendeva questo modo dello aruspicare, che di fare i soldati confidentemente ire alla zuffa, dalla qual confidenza quasi sempre nasce la vittoria. La qual cosa fu non solamente usata dai Romani, ma dagli esterni; di che mi pare di addurre uno esempio nel seguente capitolo.
Nor did this mode of haruspicating tend to any other end than to make the soldiers go confidently to the engagement, from which confidence victory almost always arises. Which thing was used not only by the Romans but by foreigners; of which I think fit to adduce an example in the following chapter.
Commentary · 1

Argument. The conclusion is functional: this way of taking the auspices existed to send soldiers confidently into battle, and confidence almost always wins. Guicciardini does not dispute that; what he doubts is the rubric's picture of captains working the auspices knowingly Guicciardini .01.

Variants

E fra gli altri aruspizj — 1824. The 1554 witness reads Et tra gli altri auspici. The 1824 text names haruspicy, the reading of entrails, where the 1554 has auspices, the reading of birds. The chickens that follow are auspices in either case.

tanto attentamente — 1824. The 1554 witness reads tanto attamente. 1824: so carefully. 1554: so aptly. Either way the Romans dressed up the decision so that it did not look like contempt of religion.

che costituissero i Pollarj — 1824. The 1554 witness reads che ponessero i Pollarj. Same sense: the keepers of the chickens were to be stationed in the front line.

nella prima guerra Punica, che volendo azzuffarsi con l'esercito Cartaginese — 1824. The 1554 witness reads nella prima guerra Carthaginese, che uolendo azzuffarsi con l'essercito de nemici. The 1554 calls the war Carthaginian and the army simply the enemy's. Same war, same battle.

Orthography, capitalization, word division and punctuation follow the governing witness throughout; no reading is hybridized between witnesses.

Notes

auspizj, augurj, aruspizj. Three different words, kept as three: auspices, the taking of signs from birds; auguries, the art and its readings; haruspicies, properly the reading of entrails. Machiavelli uses them loosely: he counts the chicken-auspices among the "haruspicies" in .01, and calls the whole practice "haruspicating" in .05. The English keeps his words rather than correcting his Roman antiquities.

pullarii. The keepers of the sacred chickens, whose feeding was read as an auspice: if the birds ate eagerly, the gods approved. Left in Latin, as the Italian keeps the office's name (Pollarj); Guicciardini calls them Pullarii.

il Principe de' Pollarj. "The prince of the pullarii": the chief of the college. The word is kept because it is the word Machiavelli uses of rulers; here it has its Latin sense of first man.

termine. "Term" in the older sense of a manner of proceeding, not a word: termini e modi is close to "ways and means", and "which term" picks up the device Papirius used, not anything he said.

.03, rispose. The Italian gives the verb no subject, and the last man named is the nephew. It is the consul who answers; the English supplies him in brackets.

.03, contro di lui. "Against him" could be the liar, but the sense is the anger the gods might have taken against the army, which by the liar's death has purged itself of it. The English says "it", for the army.

zuffa, azzuffarsi, combattere, giornata. Engagement, engage, fight, battle: four words, kept apart. Zuffa is used of Aquilonia, a pitched battle, so it is not a skirmish.

.03, in pregiudizio suo. "To his prejudice", in the old sense of harm: if the chicken-keeper lied, the lie falls on his own head, as it does.

The sources. Livy 10.40, for Lucius Papirius Cursor, son of the dictator of the same name, before Aquilonia (293 BC). The chickens thrown into the sea belong to Publius Claudius Pulcher, before the sea-fight off Drepana in Sicily (249 BC), in Cicero, De natura deorum 2.7, and Livy, Periochae 19. Machiavelli calls him Appius, his father's name.

FG-CONS.1.14

Considerazione sul capitolo XIV

I Romani interpretavano gli auspicii secondo la necessità, e con la prudenza mostravano di osservare la religione, quando forzati non l'osservavano, e se alcuno temerariamente la dispregiava, lo punivano

The Romans interpreted the auspices according to necessity, and with prudence made a show of observing religion when, forced, they did not observe it, and if anyone rashly despised it, they punished him Canestrini sets Machiavelli's chapter title over the reply, in his own spelling, with lower-case religione and no full stop.

Italian · 1857
English
1.14.01
Non ho per certo che e' capitani degli eserciti usassino astutamente la autorità degli auspicii e degli augurii, ma credo che massime ne' primi tempi fussino gli animi loro occupati da questa religione, né mi repugna lo esemplo di Papirio, il quale, avendo avuto la relazione da Pullarii di chi [e]ra lo officio, non aveva a attendere a quello che gli fussi referito da terze persone.
I do not hold it for certain that the captains of the armies used the authority of the auspices and of the auguries craftily, but I believe that, especially in the first times, their minds were occupied by this religion; nor is repugnant to me the example of Papirius, who, having had the report from the pullarii whose office it was, did not have to attend to what was reported to him by third persons.
Commentary · 3

Argument. Three acts in one sentence. He withholds certainty — "I do not hold it for certain" — that the captains used the auspices craftily, as Machiavelli's rubric and his account of Papirius suppose Machiavelli .02. He states his own belief against Machiavelli's: that, especially in the first times, their minds were held by the religion. And he finds that Machiavelli's own example does not tell against him.

Argument. Papirius, in Guicciardini's reading, did nothing irreligious. He had the report from the pullarii whose office it was to make it, and it was favourable; what some of them told certain soldiers, who told his nephew, who told him, came from third persons, and he had no need to act on it Machiavelli .03. Where Machiavelli sees a general turning a lie into a sign, Guicciardini sees a consul following procedure. He says nothing of Appius Machiavelli .04.

Term. Gli animi loro occupati da questa religione: their minds taken up, occupied, by the religion — held by it, not using it.

Notes

di chi [e]ra lo officio. Canestrini's page, in the only scan of it available, prints ora, "now", which leaves the clause without a verb. The Italian here is emended from Palmarocchi's 1933 edition, era, "whose office it was", and the bracket marks the emended letter.

astutamente. "Craftily", not "astutely", which in modern English praises. Guicciardini's question is whether the captains worked a religion they did not believe in.

né mi repugna. "Repugnant" in its older sense: contrary, inconsistent. The example of Papirius does not tell against Guicciardini's view. Machiavelli uses the noun two chapters earlier, of his two reasons against the Church, which "have no repugnance".

relazione ... referito. "Report ... reported". The pullarii's report is the one Papirius had to act on; what the soldiers told his nephew, who told him, came from third persons. Machiavelli's riferì, riferendolo, riferendogli are the same verb and take the same English.

attendere. "Attend to": Machiavelli's consul tells the nephew to attend to his own office, and Guicciardini says the consul did not have to attend to hearsay.

It is a reading edition with a transparent apparatus, not a text of record. It does not adjudicate the critical text against Bausi 2001 or the manuscript tradition, and does not claim to.

On the title

Machiavelli had been dead two years when Guicciardini wrote; the Discorsi were unpublished, and the Considerazioni stayed unprinted until 1857. Machiavelli never answers.

“Conversation” is a quotation, not a description. It invokes Machiavelli's letter to Vettori of 10 December 1513, where he changes into court dress at evening, enters the antique courts of ancient men, and reports that they, in their humanity, answer me. Guicciardini does to Machiavelli what Machiavelli did to Livy — and is denied the reply Machiavelli claimed.

Method

No image-derived reading enters the text on a single reading. Each unit is transcribed twice, independently, by readers who have not seen each other's work; disagreements are adjudicated against the page images and recorded.
