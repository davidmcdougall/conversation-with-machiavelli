The multitude is wiser and more constant than a prince Discourses I.58, and Guicciardini's reply

Editor's summary Machiavelli argues that a people regulated by the laws is more prudent, more stable and of better judgment than a prince, and errs less when both are unbound. Guicciardini answers that the Roman example is not to the purpose, since Rome's most important deliberations were made by the senate, consuls and principal magistrates, and believes without doubt that a prince matched in virtù would proceed with more prudence and constancy. Between the unbound, he grants the prince perhaps more vices of other kinds, but commonly more prudence and constancy. He grants that popular growth goes on more continuously, since successors are not always alike, and that popular government can well be a city's better fortune, a point outside the dispute; yet ordinarily, he says, a people has more imprudence and inconstancy.

That nothing is more vain and more inconstant than the multitude, both our Livy and all the other historians affirm. Because it often occurs, in narrating the proceedings of men, to see the multitude to have condemned someone to death, and afterwards to have wept for and supremely desired that same [man]; as one sees the Roman people to have done with Manlius Capitolinus, whom, having condemned to death, it supremely afterwards desired. And the words of the author are these: Populum brevi, posteaquam ab eo periculum nullum erat, desiderium ejus tenuit. And elsewhere, when he shows the accidents that arose in Syracuse after the death of Hieronymus, grandson of Hiero, he says: Haec natura multitudinis est, aut humiliter servit, aut superbe dominatur. I do not know whether I shall take upon myself a hard province, and one full of so much difficulty that it may be fitting for me either to abandon it with shame or to follow it with charge, wishing to defend a thing which, as I have said, is accused by all the writers. But however it may be, I do not judge, nor shall I ever judge, it to be a defect to defend some opinions with reasons, without wishing to use therein either authority or force.
Commentary · 2

Argument. The chapter opens by stating the case against its thesis: Livy and all the other historians affirm that nothing is more vain and inconstant than the multitude, and Livy supplies two texts, the Roman people desiring Manlius after it had condemned him, and the nature of the multitude at Syracuse, either to serve humbly or to dominate proudly. Machiavelli's undertaking is announced as a doubt: he does not know whether he is taking upon himself a hard province, one he may have to abandon with shame or follow with charge. What he stakes is method: he does not judge it a defect to defend an opinion with reasons, without using authority or force.

History. Manlius Capitolinus, who had saved the Capitol from the Gauls, was accused of aiming at kingship and condemned in the year conventionally dated 384 BC (Livy 6.20). Hieronymus, grandson of Hiero II, reigned at Syracuse in 215-214 BC; after his murder the Syracusan crowd killed the women of the royal house, and Livy's remark on the nature of the multitude comes in that account (24.25). Livy goes on to say that liberty, the mean between the two extremes, the multitude knows neither how to build with measure nor how to hold; Machiavelli quotes only the extremes.

Commentary · 2

Argument. The first move extends the defect from the multitude to men taken one by one: each one not regulated by the laws would make the errors of the unbound multitude, and princes above all. The princes in question are those who have been able to break the rein that can correct them, of whom many have been and few good and wise. The kings of Egypt, of Sparta and of France were held by laws, and so they are not to be counted among the princes whose nature is to be weighed against the multitude; against them one must set a multitude likewise regulated. Such a multitude will show the same goodness, and Livy's two verbs come back denied: it will neither dominate proudly nor serve humbly. Rome is the instance, while the republic lasted uncorrupted.

Cross-reference. France as a kingdom moderated by laws is also Machiavelli's example in I.16, where its kings had bound themselves to infinite laws. Guicciardini sets the French kings among the unbound and says the Author calls them legati Guicciardini .03; Machiavelli's word here is moderato, and at .10 obbligato alle leggi.

Ma l'opinione contro ai Popoli nasce, perché de' Popoli ciascun dice male senza paura, e liberamente ancora mentre che regnano; de' Principi si parla sempre con mille paure, e mille rispetti. Né mi par fuor di proposito, poiché questa materia mi vi tira, disputare nel seguente capitolo di quali confederazioni altri si possa più fidare, o di quelle fatte con una Repubblica, o di quelle fatte con un Principe.
But the opinion against peoples arises because, of peoples, each one speaks ill without fear and freely, even while they reign; of princes one speaks always with a thousand fears and a thousand respects. Nor does it seem to me out of purpose, since this matter draws me to it, to dispute in the following chapter which confederations one can trust more, whether those made with a republic, or those made with a prince.
Commentary · 2

Argument. The last paragraph gives the cause of the common opinion the chapter began by contradicting: of peoples men speak ill without fear, even while peoples reign; of princes only with a thousand fears and respects. The question of which confederations are more to be trusted is handed to the next chapter.

Cross-reference. Machiavelli's account of where the opinion against peoples arises (nasce) has its counterpart in Guicciardini's claim that the examples meritamente hanno partorito the writers' opinion Guicciardini .02: one says the opinion comes from fear of speaking of princes, the other that it was deserved. Guicciardini does not answer I.59.

Notes

¶ 01 Nessuna cosa esser più vana e più incostante che la moltitudine, così Tito Livio nostro, come tutti gli altri Istorici affermano. "That nothing is more vain and more inconstant than the multitude, both our Livy and all the other historians affirm": the infinitive clause stands first as the object of affermano, and the English keeps it first, so that the authorities the chapter will contradict come last (D263, a landing); così ... come, both ... and. Tito Livio, Livy (names.md); nostro, our, Machiavelli's habit with Livy; Istorici, historians, as Istoria, history (glossary).

¶ 01 Perché spesso occorre nel narrare le azioni degli uomini. "Because it often occurs, in narrating the proceedings of men": occorrere, occur (D01), the currere family in the chapter's second sentence; azioni of narrated doings, "proceedings" (D358). sommamente, supremely (D190).

¶ 01 il quale avendo condannato a morte, sommamente dipoi desiderava. "Whom, having condemned to death, it supremely afterwards desired": the relative is the object of both verbs. dipoi, afterwards (D187). The 1554 prints lo desideraua, with the pronoun (NM-DISC-I58-D005). In the sentence before, vedere la moltitudine avere condannato ... e quel medesimo dipoi pianto e sommamente desiderato keeps the multitude as the subject of all three participles, and the English supplies "[man]" for quel medesimo (D53).

¶ 01 Populum brevi, posteaquam ab eo periculum nullum erat, desiderium ejus tenuit. Livy 6.20: "In a short time, once there was no danger from him, desire for him took hold of the people." The Latin Library text (thelatinlibrary.com/livy/liv.6.shtml, consulted 2026-11-12) reads Populum brevi, postquam periculum ab eo nullum erat, per se ipsas recordantem virtutes desiderium eius tenuit, with postquam and a different order, and with the people "recalling his virtues in themselves". The quotation as printed in the 1824, and in the 1554, lacks that phrase; Machiavelli's own explanation below, e' desiderava le sue virtù (.03), gives its sense in his words. Printed in italic in the 1824; the 1554 follows it with an Italian rendering, Cioè, d'indi a poco ..., which the 1824 does not print (NM-DISC-I58-D004).

¶ 01 gli accidenti che nacquero in Siracusa dopo la morte di Girolamo nipote di Jerone. "The accidents that arose in Syracuse after the death of Hieronymus, grandson of Hiero": accidente, accident (glossary); nascere, arise (D04). Hieronymus succeeded his grandfather Hiero II as king of Syracuse in 215 BC and was killed the next year; nipote is grandson here. The tumults after his death, in which the people killed the royal women, are Livy's 24.21-26.

¶ 01 Haec natura multitudinis est, aut humiliter servit, aut superbe dominatur. Livy 24.25: "This is the nature of the multitude: either it serves humbly, or it dominates proudly." The Latin Library text (thelatinlibrary.com/livy/liv.24.shtml, consulted 2026-11-12) reads ea natura multitudinis est: aut servit humiliter aut superbe dominatur, and goes on libertatem, quae media est, nec struere modice nec habere sciunt: liberty, which lies between, they know neither how to build with measure nor how to hold. Machiavelli quotes the two extremes and not the mean; he takes up the extremes in the same verbs, denied of Rome, né superbamente dominare, né umilmente servire (.02). The 1554 adds an Italian rendering here too (NM-DISC-I58-D004).

¶ 01 Io non so se io mi prenderò una provincia dura. "I do not know whether I shall take upon myself a hard province": provincia, province (glossary), in the sense of a charge or task undertaken, which the English cognate still carries. convenire impersonal, be fitting (D85); carico, charge (glossary), the blame that follows the task; vergogna, shame. Guicciardini answers with Difficile impresa ... piglia, sanza dubio (Guicciardini .01).

¶ 01 io non giudico, né giudicherò mai essere difetto difendere alcune opinioni con le ragioni, senza volervi usare o l'autorità o la forza. "I do not judge, nor shall I ever judge, it to be a defect to defend some opinions with reasons, without wishing to use therein either authority or force": giudicare, judge (D374); difetto, defect (glossary), which the next sentence turns on the accusers; vi, therein, in the defence.

¶ 02 Dico adunque come di quello difetto, di che accusano gli scrittori la moltitudine, se ne possono accusare gli uomini particolarmente, e massime i Principi. "I say, then, that of that defect of which the writers accuse the multitude men can be accused particularly, and above all princes": adunque, then (D74); dico come, "I say that", here and at .07, come introducing what is said (at .10 Come ... hanno durato is "as"); accusare, accuse (glossary); particolarmente, particularly, man by man against the multitude together (D374); massime, above all (glossary). The 1554 reads tutti gli huomini, all men (NM-DISC-I58-D005).

¶ 02 ciascuno che non sia regolato dalle leggi farebbe quelli medesimi errori, che la moltitudine sciolta. "Each one who is not regulated by the laws would make those same errors as the unbound multitude": the chapter's two participles, regolato, regulated, and sciolto, unbound, hold through both units, one English word each (glossary, D402; FG I.10 autorità sciolta); incatenato, chained (.10), is the strong form of the first, and obbligato (.10) is "obliged" (D401).

¶ 02 perché e' sono e sono stati assai Principi; e de' buoni, e de' savj ne sono stati pochi. "Because there are and have been many princes, and of good and wise ones there have been few": e', the impersonal subject; assai of number, many (glossary). The 1554 reads perche de cattiui sono, & sono stati assai Prencipi, "of bad ones there are and have been many princes", which makes the antithesis explicit; the 1824 construes and is kept (NM-DISC-I58-D003, D394). savj, wise, as the rubric's savia.

¶ 02 quel freno che li può correggere. "That rein that can correct them": freno, rein (D389). correggere, correct.

¶ 02 intra i quali non sono quelli Re che nascevano in Egitto. "Among whom are not those kings who were born in Egypt": intra, among; nascere of persons, be born, the root of nascere, arise (D04), which English cannot keep in one verb. il quale Regno è moderato più dalle leggi: moderare, moderate (glossary); France's kingdom moderated by laws is NM I.16's Regno di Francia (names.md). notizia, notice (D373).

¶ 02 non sono da mettere in quel numero, donde si abbia a considerare la natura di ciascun uomo per sé. "Are not to be put in that number from which one is to consider the nature of each man by himself": avere a destinative, "be to" (D261); considerare, consider (glossary); per sé, by himself. costituzioni, constitutions, as costituire, constitute (glossary).

¶ 02 perché all'incontro loro si debbe porre una moltitudine medesimamente regolata dalle leggi. "Because over against them one must set a multitude likewise regulated by the laws": all'incontro of position, "over against", set opposite for comparison; D313's "in return" is scoped to requital, and D24's "on the other side" belongs to al rincontro, which Guicciardini uses to reverse this comparison, setting a prince against the Roman people (Guicciardini .03). (D401) si debbe, must (D274); porre, set (D115).

¶ 02 e vedrassi quella né superbamente dominare, né umilmente servire. "And it will be seen neither proudly to dominate nor humbly to serve": Livy's superbe dominatur and humiliter servit turned into Italian and denied. dominare, dominate (glossary). anzi, reversing after the negatives, "on the contrary" (D135); grado, degree (glossary); Magistrati, magistracies, the offices (D93).

¶ 03 E quando era necessario insurgere contro a un potente, lo faceva, come si vede in Manlio, ne' Dieci, ed in altri che cercarono opprimerla. "And when it was necessary to rise up against a powerful one, it did it, as one sees in Manlius, in the Ten, and in others who sought to oppress [the republic]": la is feminine, the republic of .02, not the people; the English names it in brackets (D53). insurgere, rise up, the root of surgere, rise (D358); un potente, a powerful one (D06). i Dieci, the Ten, the decemvirs of 451-449 BC (names.md, as NM I.40). salute pubblica, the public safety.

¶ 03 E se il Popolo romano desiderava Manlio Capitolino morto, non è maraviglia; perché e' desiderava le sue virtù. "And if the Roman people desired Manlius Capitolinus [when] dead, it is no marvel; because it desired his virtù": the people longed for him after his death, the case of .01; "desired him dead" would say the opposite, and the English supplies "[when]" (D53). virtù untranslated (D16), here plural, his qualities, le quali erano state tali; "which ... of them" keeps the plural in the pronouns. This is the sense Livy gives and the printed quotation lacks (.01 note).

¶ 03 perché l'è sentenza di tutti gli scrittori, come la virtù si lauda e si ammira ancora negli inimici suoi. "Because it is the sentence of all the writers that virtù is praised and admired even in one's enemies": l', the expletive pronoun, "it"; the 1554 omits it (NM-DISC-I58-D005). sentenza, sentence (glossary); suoi refers to the impersonal subject of si lauda, the one who praises: "one's".

¶ 03 e se Manlio infra tanto desiderio fusse resuscitato. "And if Manlius, amid so much desire, had risen again": resuscitato with essere, intransitive, "risen again" rather than "resuscitated", now medical. tratto che lo ebbe di prigione: in Livy the senate releases Manlius by decree in the face of the plebs' agitation (6.17, ex senatus consulto Manlius vinculis liberatur, the Latin Library text, consulted 2026-11-12), and the people condemn him later (6.20); Machiavelli gives both acts to the people.

¶ 03 come Alessandro, Clito, ed altri suoi amici, ed Erode, Marianne. "As Alexander [did] Clitus and other friends of his, and Herod Mariamne": the verb is understood from hanno fatto morire, and the English supplies it once (D53). Alexander the Great killed Clitus in a drunken quarrel and mourned him; Herod the Great had his wife Mariamne put to death and grieved for her, as Josephus tells. Marianne is Machiavelli's form of the name (names.md, added with this pair).

¶ 04 Però non è più da incolpare la natura della moltitudine che de' Principi. "Therefore the nature of the multitude is not more to be inculpated than that of princes": però, therefore (D22). incolpare, inculpate, new here (D402), kept apart from accusare, accuse (glossary), and biasimo, blame (D173). rispetto, respect (glossary): without regard to anything that restrains them.

¶ 04 e tra gl'Imperatori romani, e tra gli altri Tiranni e Principi. "Both among the Roman emperors and among the other tyrants and princes": Imperatori, emperors, as Imperadore (glossary); Tiranni, tyrants (glossary). incostanza, inconstancy, answering incostante in the first line; variazione di vita, variation of life, as varj below.

¶ 05 Conchiudo adunque contro alla comune opinione. "I conclude, then, against the common opinion": the 1554 reads oltre alla commune oppenione, "beyond", or "besides", the common opinion, which would make the conclusion an addition rather than a contradiction; the 1824 contro construes, answers the provincia dura of .01, and is kept (NM-DISC-I58-D003, D394). comune, common.

¶ 05 i Popoli, quando sono Principi, sono varj, mutabili, ingrati. "Peoples, when they are princes, are various, mutable, ungrateful": Principi here of peoples that rule. vario, various, kept as the root of variazione and of Guicciardini's varietà (Guicciardini .02); the sense is changeable. ingrato, ungrateful (glossary). affermando, affirming, Machiavelli's own act, governed by Conchiudo; the next period, E che la variazione ... nasce, depends on affermando or on Conchiudo, and the English supplies "[I affirm]" (D53), the nearer verb; "[I conclude]" would serve the other reading. peccati, sins.

¶ 05 ma traendone i Principi, s'inganna. "But taking the princes out of it, he deceives himself": ingannarsi, deceive oneself (D374). comanda, commands; stimato, esteemed; eziandio, even; dall'altra parte, on the other hand (D24).

¶ 05 e se vi è vantaggio di bene è nel Popolo. "And if there is advantage of good it is in the people": a conditional aside, not the thesis. vantaggio, advantage, new here; it shares the English with utile as a noun (D283), a recorded collapse (D401). procedere, proceeding (glossary).

¶ 06 E chi considererà il Popolo romano. "And whoever will consider the Roman people": the future, kept; the 1554 reads considera (NM-DISC-I58-D003, D394). nome Regio, royal name; patria, fatherland (glossary); usare of examples, and of ingratitude below, "use" (D313).

¶ 06 rispondo quello che di sopra lungamente si discorse in questa materia; dove si mostrò i Popoli essere meno ingrati dei Principi. "I answer what was discoursed long above on this matter, where peoples were shown to be less ungrateful than princes": discorrere, discourse (D01); lungamente, long (D140). The place is I.29, where Scipio's case is weighed and the people judged less ungrateful.

¶ 07 E non senza cagione si assomiglia la voce d'un Popolo a quella di Dio. "And not without cause is the voice of a people likened to that of God": the proverb vox populi vox Dei. The fronted non senza cagione is kept: a plain English writer fronts a negative adverbial for emphasis (D263); Guicciardini answers it in nearly the same words, non sanza cagione è assomigliata la moltitudine alle onde del mare (Guicciardini .01). assomigliare, liken; pronostichi, prognostications; talché, so that (glossary); occulta virtù, an occult virtù (D16), a hidden capacity.

¶ 07 quando egli ode due concionanti che tendano in diverse parti. "When it hears two haranguers who tend in different directions": concionanti, those who make concioni, harangues (glossary); diverso, different (glossary). rarissime volte, very seldom (D97). capace of understanding a truth heard, "able to grasp" (D350, D373).

¶ 07 E se nelle cose gagliarde, o che pajano utili, come di sopra si dice, egli erra. "And if in hard things, or those that seem useful, as is said above, it errs": gagliardo, hard (D313), here bold, vigorous, not difficult; the place above is presumably I.53, on the people deceived by a false appearance of good. pajano, subjunctive; the 1554 reads paiono, at a letter the scan breaks, and the 1824 is kept (NM-DISC-I58-D002, D393).

¶ 08 Vedesi ancora nelle sue elezioni ai Magistrati fare di lunga migliore elezione che un Principe. "One sees it also, in its choices to magistracies, make far better choice than a prince": elezione, choice (glossary), the word repeated; di lunga, by far. tirare alla dignità, draw to dignity (D374); infame, infamous (glossary); via, path (D131). si persuaderà ad un Popolo ... si persuade ad un Principe, "will it be persuaded to a people ... is persuaded to a prince": the thing persuaded is the subject both times, as the Italian has it, so that the people and the prince stand in the same place; corrotti, corrupted (glossary).

¶ 08 E dell'una e dell'altra di queste due cose voglio mi basti per testimone il Popolo romano. "And for the one and the other of these two things I wish the Roman people to be enough for me as witness": volere, wish (D117); bastare, be enough (D205). di che quello si avesse a pentire, "of which it had to repent": avere a of necessity, "have to" (D261).

¶ 08 che nessuno obbligo di alcuno suo cittadino, che tentasse quel nome, potette fargli fuggire le debite pene. "That no debt to any citizen of its who attempted that name could make him flee the due penalties": obbligo, debt (D17), what the people owed a citizen for his services. The Italian says alcuno; Manlius, who had saved the Capitol, is the instance in this chapter (and I.24). fuggire, flee (D325); pene, penalties (glossary).

¶ 08 fare in brevissimo tempo augumenti eccessivi. "Make in very brief time excessive augmentations": augumento, augmentation (D69). cacciata de' Re, the expulsion of the kings; Pisistrato, Pisistratus (names.md), as I.2 and I.28: Pisistratus died in power, and Athens was freed from his son Hippias in 510 BC; Machiavelli names the house by its founder.

¶ 09 Né voglio che si opponga a questa mia opinione tutto quello che lo Istorico nostro ne dice nel preallegato testo. "Nor do I wish all that our historian says of it, in the above-alleged text and in any other, to be opposed to this opinion of mine": the order is normalised, as exposition (D263). preallegato, above-alleged, the two Livy passages of .01; allegare, allege, as se alcuno mi allegasse (.06).

¶ 09 perché se si discorreranno tutti i disordini de' Popoli. "Because if one will discourse all the disorders of peoples": discorrere transitive, discourse over, run through (D01), future as the Italian; Guicciardini uses the verb so, of the Author (Guicciardini .03).

¶ 09 i Popoli sono tanto superiori nel mantenere le cose ordinate, ch'egli aggiungono senza dubbio alla gloria di coloro che l'ordinano. "Peoples are so much superior in maintaining the things ordered that they add without doubt to the glory of those who order them": mantenere, maintain (glossary); ordinare, order (glossary), throughout the period. egli, they, the plural form; the 1554 essi (NM-DISC-I58-D005).

¶ 10 Ed in somma, per epilogare questa materia, dico. "And in sum, to epilogize this matter, I say": epilogare, epilogize, sum up in an epilogue. Come hanno durato assai gli Stati dei Principi, hanno durato assai gli Stati delle Repubbliche: Come, as; assai with a verb, "much" (D348); Stato, state (D265). The second hanno is printed with a damaged sort, read hanne by one reader; it is read hanno, as the 1554 has it (NM-DISC-I58-D001, D392). pazzo, mad; savio, wise, the rubric's word.

¶ 10 Se adunque si ragionerà d'un Principe obbligato alle leggi, e d'un Popolo incatenato da quelle. "If, then, one will reason of a prince obliged to the laws, and of a people chained by them": obbligato here is held by the laws, not indebted to them; D204's "indebted" would make the laws a creditor. The nearest precedent is D229, essersi quelli Re obbligati ad infinite leggi, "having bound themselves to infinite laws" (NM I.16), of the same kings of France; "bound" is kept here for Guicciardini's legati (Guicciardini .03), which he puts in Machiavelli's mouth. (D401) incatenato, chained.

¶ 10 ad un Popolo licenzioso e tumultuario, gli può da un uomo buono essere parlato. "A licentious and tumultuary people can be spoken to by a good man": the Italian is impersonal and passive, with ad un Popolo resumed by gli; the English keeps the people the subject of both passives, against the prince to whom non è alcuno che possa parlare. licenzioso, licentious, with licenza, licence; tumultuario, tumultuary (glossary); ridurre, bring (D314); cattivo, bad (D03); ferro, the steel, the sword.

¶ 10 Da che si può far coniettura della importanza della malattia dell'uno e dell'altro. "From which one can make conjecture of the importance of the illness of the one and of the other": malattia, illness, both times, which curare, cura and the steel continue as a physician's figure. The 1554 reads malitia, malice, both times, which would make it a judgment of character; the 1824 is kept (NM-DISC-I58-D003, D394). coniettura, conjecture.

¶ 10 a quella del Principe bisogna il ferro. "For that of the prince the steel is required": bisogna, it is required (D260); see the note on bisogni at Machiavelli I.2. Again in dove bisogna maggior cura, "where greater cure is required": cura is the treatment, keeping the figure.

¶ 11 Quando un Popolo è bene sciolto. "When a people is well unbound": bene intensifying, fully unbound. pazzie, madnesses, the noun of pazzo (.10); paura, fear (D313). potendo nascere fra tanta confusione un Tiranno: "since a tyrant can arise amid so much confusion".

¶ 11 Ma ne' Principi tristi interviene il contrario. "But with wicked princes the contrary befalls": tristo, wicked (D03); intervenire, befall (D298); persuadersi, persuade oneself (D358); surgere, rise (D358).

¶ 11 la quale è quanto dalle cose che sono a quelle che hanno ad essere. "Which is as much as from the things that are to those that are to be": avere a destinative, "be to" (D261).

¶ 11 Le crudeltà della moltitudine sono contro a chi ei temono che occupi il ben comune; quelle d'un Principe sono contro a chi ei temono che occupi il ben proprio. "Even though it had been regulated by good laws, full of customs holy and most careful of its liberty": ancora che, even though (glossary); di of the agent, "by"; costumi, customs (D41); santo, holy (D313). accuratissimi agrees with costumi, and the English sets both adjectives after the noun so that the care for liberty stays with the customs, not with the multitude. The 1933 edition reads amantissimi, most loving; Canestrini construes and is kept (FG-CONS-I58-D003, D400). riprendere, reprove (D173).

¶ 03 Di poi quando bene noi chiamassimo le deliberazioni de' Romani deliberazioni della moltitudine. "Afterwards, even if we called the deliberations of the Romans deliberations of the multitude": di poi, afterwards (D187), here the next step of the argument; quando bene, even if (glossary); grado, degree (glossary). piglisi al rincontro uno principe, "let there be taken on the other side a prince": al rincontro, on the other side (D24), reverses Machiavelli's all'incontro loro si debbe porre una moltitudine (Machiavelli .02), where a multitude was set against the bound kings; here a prince is set against the Roman people. The glossary keeps the two words apart, so the English shows the answer only in this note.

¶ 03 credo sanza dubio procederà in tutte le sue cose con maggiore prudenza e con maggiore constanza che non procederia il populo romano. "I believe without doubt he will proceed in all his things with greater prudence and with greater constancy than the Roman people would proceed": credo, I believe (glossary), the reply's only credo, strengthened by sanza dubio, without doubt: belief, not certainty. procederia, conditional, "would proceed"; the 1933 edition reads procedeva, "proceeded" (FG-CONS-I58-D003, D400). The semicolon before credo is Canestrini's.

¶ 03 dove e' termini siano pari, è più ordine, più distinzione, più resoluzione, più fermezza in uno che in molti. "Where the terms are even, there is more order, more distinction, more resolution, more firmness in one than in many": termini, terms, the conditions compared, as FG I.3's data paritate terminorum; D325's "bearings" is the sense of conduct. (D401) pari, even (D262). The comma after ordine was doubtful to both readers and is read with the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-I58-D004, D110).

¶ 03 E pel contrario se si piglia uno populo sciolto dalle leggi e uno principe libero e sciolto. "And on the contrary, if one takes a people unbound from the laws and a prince free and unbound": sciolto, unbound, Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .02, .10); pel contrario, on the contrary, which shares the English with anzi reversing (D135, Machiavelli .02), a recorded collapse (D401).

¶ 03 e quegli di Francia ancora, che lo Autore chiama legati. "And those of France too, whom the Author calls bound": Machiavelli did not use legati; he said France's kingdom was moderato più dalle leggi and set its kings among the princes held by laws (Machiavelli .02). Guicciardini's word for Machiavelli's claim is the one he contests. in potestà de' quali è nel regno suo fare ciò che vogliono: "in whose power it is in their kingdom to do what they wish"; potestà, power (D78); suo, their, with the plural vogliono.

¶ 03 dico che in uno principe si potrà trovare forse più altri vizii che in uno populo. "I say that in a prince there may be found perhaps more other vices than in a people": forse, perhaps, a real concession, limited at once to vices other than imprudence and inconstancy; vizio, vice (glossary); prontezza, promptness, with pronto, prompt (D298); eseguire, execute (glossary).

¶ 03 e' quali quando lo Autore discorre si parte da' termini della sua quistione. "Which when the Author discourses, he departs from the terms of his question": e' quali, the vices, is the object of discorre, transitive as Machiavelli's se si discorreranno tutti i disordini (Machiavelli .09), and the subject changes to lo Autore; the English keeps the shape. discorrere, discourse (D01); quistione, question.

¶ 03 ma comunemente si troveria più prudenza e più constanza, che è proprio il titulo dello Autore, che non si troveria in una moltitudine. "But commonly there would be found more prudence and more constancy, which is precisely the title of the Author, than would be found in a multitude": comunemente, commonly, a limit on the claim; troveria, conditional, here and twice below; the 1933 edition reads troverrà, will be found, each time (FG-CONS-I58-D003, D400). il titulo dello Autore is Machiavelli's rubric, which names wisdom and constancy; Guicciardini keeps to it. facultà, substance (D198); appetito, appetite (D117); invidia, envy (glossary).

¶ 03 E se bene de' principi se ne truova imprudentissimi. "And although among princes some are found most imprudent": se bene, although (D31); ne partitive, "some". quella ultima spezie, that last species (glossary): either the unbound prince just set against the unbound people, or the extreme of imprudence named in imprudentissimi; the English leaves it as open as the Italian. pernizioso, pernicious (glossary); verbi gratia, Latin, "for example", literally "for the sake of a word" (latin.md). de' re prudenti e imprudenti: kings both prudent and imprudent.

¶ 03 perché lo assunto non è disputare degli altri vizii, ma solo se ne' populi è più imprudenza e inconstanza che ne' principi. "Because the assumption is not to dispute of the other vices, but only whether in peoples there is more imprudence and inconstancy than in princes": assunto, the assumption in its older sense, the proposition taken up to be proved, as the impresa taken up in the first line; disputare, dispute, the verb of Machiavelli's last sentence (Machiavelli .12).

¶ 03 Così è impertinente il dire che più augumento fa una città sotto uno governo populare che sotto uno principe. "So too it is impertinent to say that a city makes more augmentation under a popular government than under a prince": Così comparative, as with the examples of other vices, not consecutive (that is però's, D22). impertinente, not pertaining to the question; augumento, augmentation (D69), Machiavelli's augumenti eccessivi (Machiavelli .08). perché nasce da altre cagioni, "because that arises from other causes", answers Machiavelli's Il che non può nascere da altro (Machiavelli .08) in his verb, nascere, arise (D04). altanti, as many. non dubito, "I do not doubt", his first person, under the hypothesis of fifty good years each.

¶ 03 Ma non essere poi sempre e' successori simili, fa che lo augumento del governo populare va più continuando che quello di uno principato. "But the successors not being, later, always alike makes the augmentation of the popular government go on more continuously than that of a principate": the infinitive clause is the subject of fa; poi, later (D250), the successors after a good first prince. successori, successors (glossary); simile, alike (glossary); principato, principate (glossary).

¶ 03 e può molto bene stare insieme, che sia migliore fortuna di una città a cadere in governo populare che sotto a principi. "And it can very well stand together that it is a better fortune for a city to fall into popular government than under princes": a principi was read by both readers without the apostrophe; the 1933 edition reads e' principi; kept (FG-CONS-I58-D003, D399). fuora della disputa nostra, outside our dispute: he does not argue the point.

¶ 03 e nondimeno, che ordinariamente sia più imprudenza e più inconstanza in uno populo che in uno principe. "And nonetheless that there is ordinarily more imprudence and more inconstancy in a people than in a prince": the second thing that can stand together with the first, after Canestrini's semicolon, which keeps it apart from what is fuora della disputa nostra. nondimeno, nonetheless (D45); ordinariamente, ordinarily, after the verb (D151), the limit on the conclusion. The reply ends without a stop, and so does the English (D40).
