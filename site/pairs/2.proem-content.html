A Conversation with Machiavelli · Pair 29 of 39

Book Two Discourses II, proem, and Guicciardini's reply

Editor's summary Machiavelli argues that men praise past times, mostly wrongly, since the past's faults are hidden and the present's known; but, judging the world always equal in good and bad, its virtù moving between provinces, he holds that one born in Italy or Greece may rightly blame his times. Guicciardini grants that past times are often praised beyond their due, but does not agree that every age has as much good as another: by the heavens or some other hidden cause, certain ages at times have more virtù or vice the world over, or at least an art or discipline flourishes more. The past, he concludes, is not always to be preferred, but it is not true to deny that one age is sometimes more corrupt or more virtuous than others.

2.proem.014 notes1 variant
Laudano sempre gli uomini, ma non sempre ragionevolmente, gli antichi tempi, e gli presenti accusano; e in modo sono delle cose passate partigiani, che non solamente celebrano quelle etadi, che da loro sono state per la memoria che ne hanno lasciata gli scrittori conosciute, ma quelle ancora che, sendo già vecchi, si ricordano nella loro giovanezza avere vedute. E quando questa loro opinione sia falsa, come il più delle volte è, mi persuado varie essere le cagioni, che a questo inganno li conducono.
Men always praise, but not always reasonably, the ancient times, and the present [ones] they accuse; and they are partisans of past things in such a way that they celebrate not only those ages which have been known by them through the memory of them that the writers have left, but those too which, being already old, they remember having seen in their youth. And when this opinion of theirs is false, as most times it is, I persuade myself that the causes which lead them to this deceit are various.
Commentary · 1

Argument. The proem to Book II opens on a habit and a doubt about it. Men always praise the ancient times and accuse the present, not always reasonably, and they are partisans of the past both where they know it from writers and where they remember it from their own youth. Machiavelli does not say the opinion is always false: when it is false, as most times it is, he persuades himself that the causes of the deceit are various. The first is given in .02, and further causes follow there and in .06; the qualification non sempre governs everything until he turns the case in .03.

2.proem.028 notes2 variants
E la prima credo sia che delle cose antiche non s'intenda al tutto la verità, e che di quelle il più delle volte si nasconda quelle cose, che recherebbono a quelli tempi infamia, e quelle altre che possono partorire loro gloria, si rendano magnifiche e amplissime. Però che i più degli scrittori in modo alla fortuna dei vincitori ubbidiscono, che per fare le loro vittorie gloriose, non solamente accrescono quello che da loro è virtuosamente operato, ma ancora le azioni de' nimici in modo illustrano, che qualunque nasce dipoi in qualunque delle due Provincie, o nella vittoriosa o nella vinta, ha cagione di maravigliarsi di quelli uomini e di quelli tempi, ed è forzato sommamente laudarli ed amarli. Oltra di questo, odiando gli uomini le cose o per timore o per invidia, vengono ad essere spente due potentissime cagioni dell'odio nelle cose passate, non ti potendo quelle offendere, e non ti dando cagione d'invidiarle. Ma al contrario interviene di quelle cose che si maneggiano e veggono, le quali per la intera cognizione di esse, non ti essendo in alcuna parte nascoste, e conoscendo in quelle insieme con il bene molte altre cose che ti dispiacciono, sei forzato giudicarle alle antiche molto inferiori, ancoraché in verità le presenti molto più di quelle di gloria e di fama meritassero; ragionando non delle cose pertinenti alle arti, le quali hanno tanta chiarezza in sé, che i tempi possono torre o dare loro poco più gloria che per loro medesime si meritino, ma parlando di quelle pertinenti alla vita e costumi degli uomini, delle quali non se ne veggono sì chiari testimonj.
And the first, I believe, is that of ancient things the truth is not wholly understood, and that of them most times those things are hidden that would bring infamy to those times, and those others that can bring forth glory for them are rendered magnificent and most ample. For most of the writers obey the fortune of the victors in such a way that, to make their victories glorious, they not only increase what is virtuously worked by them, but also make the deeds of the enemies illustrious in such a way that whoever is born afterwards in whichever of the two provinces, either in the victorious or in the vanquished, has cause to marvel at those men and at those times, and is forced supremely to praise and to love them. Beyond this, men hating things either from fear or from envy, two most powerful causes of hatred come to be extinguished in past things, those not being able to offend you, and not giving you cause to envy them. But the contrary befalls with those things that are handled and seen, which, through the entire knowledge of them, [they] not being hidden from you in any part, and [you] knowing in them, together with the good, many other things that displease you, you are forced to judge them much inferior to the ancient [ones], even though in truth the present [ones] might merit much more of glory and of fame than those; reasoning not of the things pertaining to the arts, which have so much clearness in themselves that the times can take away from them or give them little more glory than they merit of themselves, but speaking of those pertaining to the life and customs of men, of which such clear witnesses are not seen.
Commentary · 2

Argument. The causes of the deceit about ancient things. First, the truth of the past is not wholly understood: most times what would shame those times is hidden and what brings them glory is magnified, because most writers obey the fortune of the victors and make even the enemies' deeds illustrious, so that whoever is born afterwards on either side has cause to marvel at those men and is forced to praise and love them. Further, men hate from fear or envy, and past things can neither offend nor be envied, so two most powerful causes of hatred are extinguished in them; present things, known whole, with their faults, are judged much inferior even where they might merit more glory. The argument is limited at once: it concerns the life and customs of men, not the arts, whose clearness is such that the times can give or take little glory from them.

Cross-reference. Guicciardini calls these reasons well considered and passes over the others he could add Guicciardini .01; he then takes the arts that Machiavelli sets aside here as his first evidence that ages differ.

Commentary · 2

Argument. The habit is real, but erring in it is not always so. It is sometimes necessary that men judge the truth, because human things are always in motion, going up or going down. A city or province ordered to public living by an excellent man may go for a time in augmentation under his virtù: whoever is born there at that time and praises the past deceives himself, for the causes just given. Whoever is born in that city when its time has come to go down towards the worse does not, at that time, deceive himself. The judgment of present against past now depends on where in its course a city stands.

Cross-reference. Guicciardini's last words take up this paragraph's non essere già sempre vero and turn them against the next one Guicciardini .01.

2.proem.047 notes
E pensando io come queste cose procedino, giudico il Mondo sempre essere stato ad un medesimo modo, ed in quello essere stato tanto di buono, quanto di tristo; ma variare questo tristo e questo buono di Provincia in Provincia, come si vede per quello si ha notizia di quelli Regni antichi, che variavano dall'uno all'altro per la variazione de' costumi, ma il Mondo restava quel medesimo; solo vi era differenza, che dove quello aveva prima collocata la sua virtù in Assiria, la collocò in Media, dipoi in Persia, tanto che la ne venne in Italia e a Roma; e se dopo l'Imperio romano non è seguìto Imperio che sia durato, né dove il Mondo abbia ritenuta la sua virtù insieme, si vede nondimeno essere sparsa in di molte nazioni, dove si viveva virtuosamente; come era il Regno de' Franchi, il Regno de' Turchi, quel del Soldano, ed oggi i Popoli della Magna, e prima quella Setta saracina che fece tante gran cose, ed occupò tanto mondo, poi che la distrusse l'Imperio romano orientale. In tutte queste Provincie adunque, poi che i Romani rovinarono, e in tutte queste Sette è stata quella virtù, ed è ancora in alcuna parte d'esse, che si desidera, e che con vera laude si lauda.
And I, thinking how these things proceed, judge the world always to have been in one same way, and in it to have been as much of good as of wicked; but this wicked and this good to vary from province to province, as one sees by what notice one has of those ancient kingdoms, which varied from the one to the other through the variation of customs, but the world remained that same; only there was a difference, that where it had earlier placed its virtù in Assyria, it placed it in Media, afterwards in Persia, so far that it came from there into Italy and to Rome; and if after the Roman empire there has not followed an empire that has lasted, nor [one] where the world has retained its virtù together, one sees it nonetheless scattered among many nations, where one lived virtuously; as was the kingdom of the Franks, the kingdom of the Turks, that of the Sultan, and today the peoples of Germany, and earlier that Saracen sect which did so many great things, and occupied so much of the world, after it destroyed the eastern Roman empire. In all these provinces, then, after the Romans fell into ruin, and in all these sects, there has been that virtù, and there still is in some part of them, which is desired, and which is praised with true praise.
Commentary · 3

Argument. The general claim behind the turn, given as Machiavelli's judgment (giudico). The world has always been in one same way, with as much good as wicked in it; what varies is where the good and the wicked are. Virtù was placed in Assyria, then Media, then Persia, and came into Italy and to Rome; after the Roman empire no lasting empire held it together, but it is scattered among many nations where men lived virtuously, the Franks, the Turks, the Sultan, the peoples of Germany, and earlier the Saracen sect. In those provinces and sects, since Rome fell, there has been virtù, and some of it remains. The claim is about the world as a whole; it is the one Guicciardini answers.

History. The succession of Assyria, Media and Persia before Rome recalls the old scheme of the world empires, carried from antiquity through the medieval chroniclers; Machiavelli names no source, and the comparison is the editor's. On the editor's identifications, the kingdom of the Franks is presumably Charlemagne's; the Turks are the Ottomans; the Sultan is presumably the Mamluk sultan of Egypt and Syria, whose state the Ottomans ended in 1517; the Saracen sect is the religion of the Arabs and those who carried it, from the conquests of the seventh century.

Cross-reference. Guicciardini restates this paragraph as tanto del buono in una età quanto in una altra, leaving out the wicked, and does not accord with it Guicciardini .01. On the editor's reading, Machiavelli's world keeps its good and wicked while their seat moves; Guicciardini's answer is that certain ages at times have more virtù or more vice universally, in all the world, or at least flourish more in an art or a discipline.

Commentary · 2

Argument. The consequence for the reader. One born in the provinces where virtù has been, and still is in some part, might deceive himself in praising the past; one born in Italy or in Greece, who has not become ultramontane in Italy or Turk in Greece, has reason to blame his own times, because nothing redeems them: no observance of religion, not of laws, not of soldiery, and every kind of filth, the more detestable in those who sit in judgment, command each one and wish to be adored. On the editor's reading, Machiavelli's own praise of Rome is here placed among the cases where the habit is justified, as .07 then claims.

History. The editor takes coloro che seggono pro tribunali, comandano a ciascuno, e vogliono essere adorati to point at the rulers of Italy, perhaps including the prelates; the text names no one.

2.proem.068 notes
Ma tornando al ragionamento nostro dico, che se il giudizio degli uomini è corrotto in giudicare qual sia migliore, o il secolo presente o l'antico, in quelle cose dove per l'antichità ei non ha possuto avere perfetta cognizione come egli ha de' suoi tempi, non dovrebbe corrompersi ne' vecchi nel giudicare i tempi della gioventù e vecchiezza loro, avendo quelli e questi egualmente conosciuti e visti. La qual cosa sarebbe vera, se gli uomini per tutt'i tempi della lor vita fussero del medesimo giudizio, ed avessero quelli medesimi appetiti. Ma variando quelli, ancoraché i tempi non varino, non possono parere agli uomini quelli medesimi, avendo altri appetiti, altri diletti, altre considerazioni nella vecchiezza, che nella gioventù. Perché mancando gli uomini, quando egli invecchiano, di forze, e crescendo di giudizio e di prudenza, è necessario che quelle cose che in gioventù parevano loro sopportabili e buone, riescano poi invecchiando insopportabili e cattive, e dove quelli ne dovrebbono accusare il giudizio loro, ne accusano i tempi. Sendo oltra di questo gli appetiti umani insaziabili, perché hanno dalla natura di potere e voler desiderare ogni cosa, e dalla fortuna di potere conseguirne poche, ne risulta continuamente una mala contentezza nelle menti umane, ed un fastidio delle cose che si posseggono; il che fa biasimare i presenti tempi, laudare i passati, e desiderare i futuri, ancorché a far questo non fussero mossi da alcuna ragionevole cagione.
But returning to our reasoning, I say that if the judgment of men is corrupted in judging which is better, either the present century or the ancient, in those things where through antiquity he has not been able to have perfect knowledge as he has of his own times, it should not be corrupted in old men in judging the times of their youth and old age, [they] having known and seen those and these equally. Which thing would be true if men through all the times of their life were of the same judgment and had those same appetites. But those varying, even though the times do not vary, they cannot seem those same [times] to men, [men] having other appetites, other delights, other considerations in old age than in youth. Because, men lacking force when they grow old, and growing in judgment and in prudence, it is necessary that those things which in youth seemed to them supportable and good turn out later, as they grow old, insupportable and bad, and where they should accuse their judgment for it, they accuse the times. Human appetites being, beyond this, insatiable, because they have it from nature to be able and to wish to desire every thing, and from fortune to be able to attain few of them, there results from it continually an ill contentment in human minds, and a vexation with the things that are possessed; which makes [men] blame the present times, praise the past, and desire the future, even though they were not moved to do this by any reasonable cause.
Commentary · 1

Argument. Further causes, from men themselves; the count is the editor's. If judgment errs about antiquity it should not err in old men judging their own youth and age, which they have known and seen alike; but men vary. Their force fails and their judgment and prudence grow, so that what seemed supportable and good in youth turns out insupportable and bad in age, and they accuse the times instead of their judgment. And human appetites are insatiable: nature gives the power and the wish to desire everything, fortune the means to attain few, so a continual ill contentment makes men blame the present, praise the past and desire the future, even though no reasonable cause moves them to do it.

2.proem.077 notes
Non so adunque se io meriterò di essere numerato tra quelli che s'ingannano, se in questi miei discorsi io lauderò troppo i tempi degli antichi Romani, e biasimerò i nostri. E veramente se la virtù che allora regnava, e il vizio che ora regna, non fussero più chiari che il Sole, andrei col parlare più rattenuto, dubitando non incorrere in quello inganno di che io accuso alcuni. Ma essendo la cosa sì manifesta che ciascuno la vede, sarò animoso in dire manifestamente quello che intenderò di quelli e di questi tempi, acciocché gli animi de' giovani, che questi miei scritti leggeranno, possano fuggire questi, e prepararsi ad imitar quelli, qualunque volta la fortuna ne desse loro occasione. Perché gli è ufficio d'uomo buono, quel bene che per la malignità dei tempi e della fortuna tu non hai potuto operare, insegnarlo ad altri, acciocché sendone molti capaci, alcuno di quelli più amati dal Cielo possa operarlo. Ed avendo ne' discorsi del superior libro parlato delle deliberazioni fatte dai Romani pertinenti al di dentro della Città, in questo parleremo di quelle, che il Popolo romano fece pertinenti allo augumento dello Imperio suo.
I do not know, then, whether I shall merit being numbered among those who deceive themselves, if in these discourses of mine I shall praise too much the times of the ancient Romans and blame ours. And truly, if the virtù that at that time reigned and the vice that now reigns were not clearer than the sun, I would go with speaking more held back, doubting lest I incur that deceit of which I accuse some. But, the thing being so manifest that each one sees it, I shall be spirited in saying manifestly what I shall understand of those and of these times, in order that the spirits of the young who will read these writings of mine may flee these and prepare themselves to imitate those, whatever time fortune gave them occasion for it. Because it is the office of a good man, that good which through the malignity of the times and of fortune you have not been able to work, to teach it to others, in order that, many being capable of it, someone of those more loved by Heaven may work it. And having spoken, in the discourses of the book above, of the deliberations made by the Romans pertaining to the inside of the city, in this [one] we shall speak of those which the Roman people made pertaining to the augmentation of its empire.
Commentary · 2

Argument. Machiavelli applies the doubt to himself and resolves it on a condition. He does not know whether he will be numbered among the deceived if he praises the times of the Romans too much and blames his own; but if the virtù of then and the vice of now were not clearer than the sun he would speak more held back, and since they are manifest he will speak openly, that the young may flee the one and prepare themselves to imitate the other whenever fortune gives them occasion. It is the office of a good man to teach the good he could not work, through the malignity of the times and of fortune, so that someone more loved by Heaven may work it. Book I spoke of Rome's deliberations pertaining to the inside of the city; Book II will speak of those pertaining to the augmentation of its empire.

Cross-reference. Guicciardini concludes that the ancient times are not always to be preferred, which Machiavelli has said in .01 and .03, and that it is not true to deny that an age is sometimes more corrupt or more virtuous, which, on the editor's reading, is the ground Machiavelli's praise of Rome needs Guicciardini .01. On that reading the two agree on Machiavelli's conclusion for Italy and differ on its ground: for Machiavelli the good has moved elsewhere in a world that keeps it; for Guicciardini an age can be worse everywhere.

Notes

The heading. The 1824 prints no rubric over the proem, only the half-title of Book II: DISCORSI / SOPRA / LA PRIMA DECA DI T. LIVIO, and LIBRO SECONDO. The page is headed as the witness heads it, "Book Two", and nothing is imported. The 1554 sets over it the work's title, the dedication to Zanobi Buondelmonti and Cosimo Rucellai, and LIBRO SECONDO. PREFATIONE.; Canestrini calls it the proem (NM-DISC-IIproem-D004, D421).

¶ 01 Laudano sempre gli uomini, ma non sempre ragionevolmente, gli antichi tempi, e gli presenti accusano. "Men always praise, but not always reasonably, the ancient times, and the present [ones] they accuse": laudare, praise (glossary), against biasimare, blame, below; accusare, accuse (glossary). The second member keeps its fronted object, since it answers the first (D263, a contrast). sempre ... non sempre is held word for word.

¶ 01 e in modo sono delle cose passate partigiani, che. "And they are partisans of past things in such a way that": partigiani, partisans (D218); in modo ... che, in such a way that (glossary), three times in .01-.02 and held each time; the fronted complement is exposition and goes into plain order (D263).

¶ 01 che da loro sono state per la memoria che ne hanno lasciata gli scrittori conosciute. "Which have been known by them through the memory of them that the writers have left": the participle conosciute is held back to the end of the clause in the Italian; the English cannot hold it so far.

¶ 01 mi persuado varie essere le cagioni. "I persuade myself that the causes ... are various": persuadersi, persuade oneself (D358); cagione, cause (glossary); quando with the subjunctive sia, "when ... is". Only the first cause is numbered (E la prima, .02); further causes follow at Oltra di questo (.02), in the change of judgment with age (.06), and at a second oltra di questo (.06), the insatiability of appetites. The count is the editor's.

¶ 02 Però che i più degli scrittori in modo alla fortuna dei vincitori ubbidiscono. "For most of the writers obey the fortune of the victors in such a way": però che is causal, per ciò che, "for", and not the consecutive però, "therefore" (D22), which would make the writers' flattery a consequence of the hidden truth instead of its reason.

¶ 02 le azioni de' nimici in modo illustrano, che. "Make the deeds of the enemies illustrious in such a way that": azioni, things done by men, "deeds" (D410); "action" stays fazione's (D341). illustrare, in its sense of making illustrious.

¶ 02 qualunque nasce dipoi in qualunque delle due Provincie, o nella vittoriosa o nella vinta. "Whoever is born afterwards in whichever of the two provinces, either in the victorious or in the vanquished": qualunque twice, whoever, whichever; dipoi, afterwards (D187); provincia, province (glossary), here the two countries at war. The 1554 reads delle ſue prouincie, "of its provinces" (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

¶ 02 non ti potendo quelle offendere, e non ti dando cagione d'invidiarle. "Those not being able to offend you, and not giving you cause to envy them": offendere, offend, the older sense, injure (glossary). The second person enters here and runs to sei forzato; the English keeps it, bracketing the subjects of the gerunds that follow, "[they] not being hidden", "[you] knowing". The 1554 has non potendo and non gli dando (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

¶ 02 le quali ... sei forzato giudicarle alle antiche molto inferiori. "Which ... you are forced to judge them much inferior to the ancient [ones]": the relative le quali is left hanging and resumed by giudicarle, "judge them", as in the Italian. molto, adverbial, "much"; the 1554 reads molte, agreeing it with antiche (NM-DISC-IIproem-D002, D420).

¶ 02 ancoraché in verità le presenti molto più di quelle di gloria e di fama meritassero. "Even though in truth the present [ones] might merit much more of glory and of fame than those": ancoraché, even though (glossary, with ancora che); the subjunctive as "might", since "should" is dovere's in this unit (dovrebbe, .06; D348).

¶ 02 ragionando non delle cose pertinenti alle arti. "Reasoning not of the things pertaining to the arts": ragionare, reason, with ragionamento, reasoning, in .06. The 1554 reads dalle coſe (NM-DISC-IIproem-D002, D420). The arts are what Guicciardini takes up first (Guicciardini .01).

¶ 02 delle quali non se ne veggono sì chiari testimonj. "Of which such clear witnesses are not seen": testimonj, witnesses, the evidence a thing gives, as testimone in NM I.60.

¶ 03 Replico pertanto essere vera quella consuetudine del laudare e biasimare soprascritta. "I repeat, on that account, that that above-written usage of praising and blaming is true": replicare, here to say again what .01 said, not to answer anything; pertanto, on that account (D98); consuetudine, usage (D41); soprascritto, above-written (glossary). "Usage" is the habit just described, men praising the past.

¶ 03 ma non essere già sempre vero, che si erri nel farlo. "But that it is not always true that one errs in doing it": già after the negative is left unrendered, as in non so già, non accetto già (D374). Guicciardini's last clause turns on the same words (ma non è già vero il negare, Guicciardini .01), rendered the same way.

¶ 03 o le salgono o le scendono. "Either they go up or they go down": salire, scendere, go up, go down; scendere again at the end of the paragraph, of the city. "Rise" stays levarsi's and surgere's (D358), "descend" descendere's (D374).

¶ 03 essere ordinata al vivere pubblico da qualche uomo eccellente. "Ordered to public living by some excellent man": vivere pubblico, public living, with vivere politico, vivere civile, vivere libero (glossary); ordinatore, orderer (glossary). The 1554 reads al uiuere politico, "to political living" (NM-DISC-IIproem-D001, D420).

¶ 03 Chi nasce allora in tale stato, ed ei laudi più gli antichi tempi che i moderni, s'inganna. "Whoever is born at that time in such a state, and [if] he praises the ancient times more than the modern, deceives himself": allora, "at that time", since "then" is adunque's (D74, D187); stato, state, here a condition; ed ei laudi, the pleonastic ei kept as "he", the subjunctive's condition carried by "[if]"; ingannarsi, deceive oneself, inganno, deceit (D374). ed è causato il suo inganno is exposition and goes into plain order (D263).

¶ 03 in quella Città o Provincia, che gli è venuto il tempo, che la scende verso la parte più rea. "In that city or province for which the time has come that it goes down towards the more evil part": che gli is the loose relative, "for which"; la scende, indicative, la the city as subject, as la ne venne (.04); reo, evil (D03). The 1554 reads di cui è uenuto il tempo (NM-DISC-IIproem-D001, D420).

¶ 04 giudico il Mondo sempre essere stato ad un medesimo modo, ed in quello essere stato tanto di buono, quanto di tristo. "Judge the world always to have been in one same way, and in it to have been as much of good as of wicked": giudicare, judge (D374); tristo, wicked (D03). This is the claim Guicciardini answers (Guicciardini .01), restated of ages, not provinces.

¶ 04 come si vede per quello si ha notizia di quelli Regni antichi. "As one sees by what notice one has of those ancient kingdoms": notizia, notice (D373); che is not printed before si ha, which the 1554 supplies (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

¶ 04 solo vi era differenza, che dove quello aveva prima collocata la sua virtù in Assiria. "Only there was a difference, that where it had earlier placed its virtù in Assyria": quello is the world; prima, earlier (D334); collocare, place (D115). The succession recalls the old order of the world empires; the comparison is the editor's. tanto che, "so far that", until. The 1554 has queſta differenza for differenza (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

¶ 04 si vede nondimeno essere sparsa in di molte nazioni, dove si viveva virtuosamente. "One sees it nonetheless scattered among many nations, where one lived virtuously": in di molte, the partitive after in, as printed; the 1554 reads in molte (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420). virtuosamente, the adverb of virtù, as virtuosamente operato in .02.

¶ 04 come era il Regno de' Franchi, il Regno de' Turchi, quel del Soldano, ed oggi i Popoli della Magna, e prima quella Setta saracina. "As was the kingdom of the Franks, the kingdom of the Turks, that of the Sultan, and today the peoples of Germany, and earlier that Saracen sect": era, singular, governs the catalogue as printed; prima, earlier (D334). The Franks presumably of Charlemagne's kingdom; the Turks, the Ottomans; the Sultan, presumably the Mamluk sultan of Egypt and Syria; la Magna, Germany (Alemagna); the Saracen sect, the religion of the Arabs and those who carried it. Setta, sect, and Sette below. The identifications are the editor's. The 1554 adds et before the second kingdom (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

¶ 04 poi che la distrusse l'Imperio romano orientale. "After it destroyed the eastern Roman empire": la is the subject, the sect, as la scende (.03) and la ne venne (.04); the empire is the object. Imperio, empire, dominion over peoples (D124), lower case throughout this paragraph.

¶ 04 poi che i Romani rovinarono. "After the Romans fell into ruin": rovinare, intransitive, from rovina, ruin (glossary).

¶ 05 chi nasce in Italia ed in Grecia, e non sia divenuto, o in Italia Oltramontano o in Grecia Turco. "Whoever is born in Italy and in Greece, and has not become either in Italy ultramontane or in Greece Turk": Oltramontano, from beyond the mountains, a foreigner from north of the Alps; the witness's capital is not carried (names.md, Barbari). The 1554 reads in Italia, & non in Grecia, which would take Greece out of the case the clause goes on to make (NM-DISC-IIproem-D001, D420).

¶ 05 ha ragione di biasimare i tempi suoi, e laudare gli altri; perché in quelli vi sono assai cose. "Has reason to blame his own times and to praise the others; because in those there are many things": biasimare, blame (glossary, biasimo); assai of number, "many" (glossary). The 1554 reads gli altrui and in quella (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

¶ 05 in questi non è cos'alcuna che li ricomperi d'ogni estrema miseria, infamia e vituperio. "In these there is not anything that redeems them from every extreme misery, infamy and disgrace": ricomperare, buy back, redeem; vituperio, disgrace (D26); infamia, infamy (glossary).

¶ 05 non di milizia, ma sono maculati d'ogni ragione bruttura. "Not of soldiery, but they are maculate with every kind of filth": the triple non kept, "no ... not ... not"; milizia, soldiery (glossary); maculati, stained, the cognate kept; ragione, kind, as in d'ogni ragione. The 1554 reads macchiate d'ogni ragione di bruttura, feminine plural (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

¶ 05 in coloro che seggono pro tribunali. "In those who sit pro tribunali": the Latin of the bench, those who sit in judgment; left in Latin, as printed.

¶ 06 se il giudizio degli uomini è corrotto in giudicare qual sia migliore, o il secolo presente o l'antico. "If the judgment of men is corrupted in judging which is better, either the present century or the ancient": giudizio, judgment, giudicare, judge (D374); corrompere, corrupt (glossary); secolo, century, Guicciardini's secoli (Guicciardini .01); "age" stays età's (D410).

¶ 06 in quelle cose dove per l'antichità ei non ha possuto avere perfetta cognizione come egli ha de' suoi tempi. "In those things where through antiquity he has not been able to have perfect knowledge as he has of his own times": ei, egli, taken as the man judging, singular after degli uomini; it may also be read as the judgment. cognizione, knowledge (D374).

¶ 06 non dovrebbe corrompersi ne' vecchi. "It should not be corrupted in old men": dovere, conditional, "should" (D348); "must" stays the indicative's (D274).

¶ 06 Perché mancando gli uomini, quando egli invecchiano, di forze. "Because, men lacking force when they grow old": mancare di + noun, lack (D312); forze, force (glossary); invecchiare, grow old, and again invecchiando. The 1554 has no egli (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

¶ 06 riescano poi invecchiando insopportabili e cattive. "Turn out later, as they grow old, insupportable and bad": riuscire, here turn out; "succeed" (D332) is the sense of a thing that comes off; poi, later (D250), "afterwards" being dipoi's (D187); sopportabile, insopportabile, supportable, insupportable, the cognate, "bear" being comportare's; cattivo, bad (D03). The bare subjunctive takes no "should", which is dovrebbono's in the next clause (D348).

¶ 06 perché hanno dalla natura di potere e voler desiderare ogni cosa, e dalla fortuna di potere conseguirne poche. "Because they have it from nature to be able and to wish to desire every thing, and from fortune to be able to attain few of them": conseguire, attain (glossary); volere, wish (D117). The 1554 reads perche hauendo and conſeguire poche, without ne (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

¶ 06 ne risulta continuamente una mala contentezza nelle menti umane, ed un fastidio delle cose che si posseggono. "There results from it continually an ill contentment in human minds, and a vexation with the things that are possessed": male, ill (glossary), here the adjective; fastidio, vexation (glossary).

¶ 06 il che fa biasimare i presenti tempi, laudare i passati, e desiderare i futuri. "Which makes [men] blame the present times, praise the past, and desire the future": three verbs, three tenses. ancorché a far questo non fussero mossi, the fronted infinitive put in plain order (D263). The 1554 reads laudare i futuri, repeating the verb of the past (NM-DISC-IIproem-D001, D420). mossi, moved; the 1554 has meßi (NM-DISC-IIproem-D002, D420).

¶ 07 Non so adunque se io meriterò di essere numerato tra quelli che s'ingannano. "I do not know, then, whether I shall merit being numbered among those who deceive themselves": adunque, then (D74). The doubt is Machiavelli's own, before the claim it qualifies.

¶ 07 andrei col parlare più rattenuto, dubitando non incorrere in quello inganno. "I would go with speaking more held back, doubting lest I incur that deceit": rattenuto, the form of ritenuto, held back (D312), with the speaking; allora, "at that time", against ora, now; dubitare ... non, doubt lest, apprehension (D205); incorrere, incur (D192), a currere compound.

¶ 07 sarò animoso in dire manifestamente quello che intenderò. "I shall be spirited in saying manifestly what I shall understand": animoso, spirited, with animo, spirit (glossary), and gli animi de' giovani after it; intendere, understand (D374).

¶ 07 Perché gli è ufficio d'uomo buono. "Because it is the office of a good man": ufficio, office (D56); gli, the impersonal subject. The tu of non hai potuto is general. acciocché, in order that, twice in the paragraph, purpose, kept apart from talché, so that, result (glossary).

¶ 07 alcuno di quelli più amati dal Cielo possa operarlo. "Someone of those more loved by Heaven may work it": operare, work (D83), twice, as virtuosamente operato in .02. The 1554 reads amato, agreeing with alcuno (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

¶ 07 ne' discorsi del superior libro. "In the discourses of the book above": Book I; superiore, above, of what precedes. discorsi lower case, Machiavelli's word for his chapters.

¶ 07 pertinenti allo augumento dello Imperio suo. "Pertaining to the augmentation of its empire": augumento, augmentation (D69), as andare ... in augumento in .03; Imperio, empire (D124). The 1554 reads allo accreſcimẽto dell'Imperio ſuo, "the increase" (NM-DISC-IIproem-D001, D420).

Considerazioni II, proem

English

La conclusione è verissima, che spesso e' tempi antichi sono laudati più che il debito, e le ragioni sono bene considerate dallo Scrittore, alle quali se ne potrebbe aggiugnere qualche una altra, ma le pretermetto. Non concordo già seco in quello che dice, che sempre nel mondo fu tanto del buono in una età quanto in una altra, benché si variino e' luoghi, perché si vede essere verissimo che o per influsso de' cieli o per altra occulta disposizione corrono talvolta certe età, nelle quali non solo in una provincia, ma universalmente in tutto il mondo, è più virtù o più vizio che non è stato in una altra età, o almanco fiorisce più una arte o una disciplina che non è fiorita in qualunque parte del mondo in altro tempo. E per cominciare a quelle meccaniche di che fa menzione lo Scrittore, chi non sa in quanta eccellenza fussino a tempo de' Greci e poi de' Romani la pittura e la scultura, e quanto di poi restassino oscure in tutto il mondo, e come dopo essere state sepolte per molti secoli siano da cento cinquanta o dugento anni in qua ritornate in luce? Chi non sa quanto a' tempi antichi fiorì non solo appresso a' Romani, ma in molte provincie la disciplina militare, della quale e' tempi nostri e quelli de' nostri padri e avoli non hanno veduto in qualunque parte del mondo se non piccoli e oscuri vestigii? Il medesimo si può dire delle lettere, della religione, che sanza dubio in alcune età sono state sepolte per tutto, in altre sono state in molti luoghi eccellenti e in sommo prezzo. Ha visto qualche età il mondo pieno di guerre, un'altra ha sentito e goduto la pace; dalle quali variazioni delle arti, della religione, de' movimenti delle cose umane, non è maraviglia siano anche variati e' costumi degli uomini, e' quali spesso pigliano il moto suo dalla instituzione, dalle occasioni, dalla necessità. [È] adunque vera conclusione che non sempre e' tempi antichi sono da essere preferiti a' presenti, ma non è già vero il negare che una età sia qualche volta più corrotta o più virtuosa che l'altre
The conclusion is most true, that the ancient times are often praised more than is due, and the reasons are well considered by the Writer, to which one could add some other, but I pass them over. I do not accord with him in what he says, that there was always in the world as much of the good in one age as in another, albeit the places vary, because it is seen to be most true that, either by influence of the heavens or by some other occult disposition, there run at times certain ages in which, not only in one province but universally in all the world, there is more virtù or more vice than there has been in another age, or at least an art or a discipline flourishes more than it has flourished in any part of the world in another time. And to begin with those mechanical [arts] of which the Writer makes mention, who does not know in how great excellence painting and sculpture were in the time of the Greeks and then of the Romans, and how much afterwards they remained obscure in all the world, and how, after having been buried for many centuries, they have, in the last hundred and fifty or two hundred years, returned to light? Who does not know how much in ancient times military discipline flourished, not only among the Romans but in many provinces, of which our times and those of our fathers and grandfathers have not seen in any part of the world but small and obscure vestiges? The same can be said of letters, of religion, which without doubt in some ages have been buried everywhere, in others have been in many places excellent and of supreme price. Some age has seen the world full of wars, another has felt and enjoyed peace; from which variations of the arts, of religion, of the movements of human things, it is no marvel that the customs of men too have varied, which often take their motion from institution, from occasions, from necessity. It is then a true conclusion that the ancient times are not always to be preferred to the present, but it is not true to deny that one age is sometimes more corrupt or more virtuous than the others
Commentary · 3

Argument. Guicciardini grants the conclusion and refuses one claim. The conclusion, that the ancient times are often praised more than is due, is most true, and the Writer's reasons for it are well considered; he could add others and passes over them. He does not accord with the claim that there was always as much good in one age as in another, the places varying; he leaves out Machiavelli's wicked and does not deny that the good moves among places, only that its sum is constant across ages. By the influence of the heavens or by some other occult disposition, there run at times certain ages in which, not only in one province but universally in all the world, there is more virtù or more vice than in another age, or at least an art or a discipline flourishes more than it has in any part of the world at another time. The evidence is put as questions nobody could answer otherwise: painting and sculpture excellent under the Greeks and Romans, then obscure in all the world and buried for many centuries, and returned to light within the last one hundred and fifty or two hundred years; military discipline flourishing in antiquity, of which his own and his fathers' times have seen in any part of the world only small and obscure vestiges. The same, it can be said, of letters and of religion. Ages of war and of peace, and the variations of arts, religion and the movements of human things, make it no marvel that the customs of men vary too, which often take their motion from institution, occasions and necessity. His close weighs both sides: the ancient times are not always to be preferred, but it is not true to deny that one age is sometimes more corrupt or more virtuous than the others.

History. The editor puts the Considerazioni about 1530; counted from then, da cento cinquanta o dugento anni in qua sets the revival of painting and sculpture in the fourteenth century, and the longer figure reaches the age the Florentine writers associated with Giotto. Both the date and the identification are the editor's. The two causes he gives for the ages, the heavens' influence and an occult disposition, are kept as alternatives; he does not choose between them.

Cross-reference. The conclusion he grants is Machiavelli's opening, ma non sempre ragionevolmente Machiavelli .01, with the reasons of Machiavelli .02 and Machiavelli .06. The claim he answers is Machiavelli .04, in his own restatement. The arts that Machiavelli set aside as clear in themselves Machiavelli .02 are Guicciardini's first proof that ages differ; le cose umane and moto are Machiavelli's words from Machiavelli .03, and the last clause, ma non è già vero, answers his ma non essere già sempre vero there. He does not take up Machiavelli's use of the argument for Italy and Greece Machiavelli .05, though military discipline and religion, two of his proofs, are two of the three things Machiavelli says Italy lacks; nor does he take up Machiavelli's own justification for praising Rome Machiavelli .07. On the editor's reading his conclusion supports that justification on another ground.

Notes

The heading. Canestrini's: CONSIDERAZIONE SUL PROEMIO DEL LIBRO II, in small capitals. Unlike the replies above and below it, this one has no rubric under the heading; none is supplied (D421, D32). The 1933 edition heads it NEL PROEMIO DEL SECONDO LIBRO.

¶ 01 La conclusione è verissima, che spesso e' tempi antichi sono laudati più che il debito. "The conclusion is most true, that the ancient times are often praised more than is due": verissimo, most true, the concession's word, turned in the next period to the ground of the refusal (si vede essere verissimo); spesso, often, against Machiavelli's il più delle volte, most times (Machiavelli .01); laudare, praise (glossary); il debito, what is owed, "is due". The stop both readers half saw after verissima is read as a comma with the 1933 edition, since the che clause states the conclusion (D110, D419). The conclusion Guicciardini grants is Machiavelli's non sempre ragionevolmente (Machiavelli .01), not the claim of .04.

¶ 01 e le ragioni sono bene considerate dallo Scrittore, alle quali se ne potrebbe aggiugnere qualche una altra, ma le pretermetto. "And the reasons are well considered by the Writer, to which one could add some other, but I pass them over": lo Scrittore, the Writer (glossary), Machiavelli; considerare, consider (glossary); pretermettere, pass over, as FG I.58. The reasons are Machiavelli's causes of the deceit (.02, .06). Canestrini's note here, his summary of those reasons, is his and not carried.

¶ 01 Non concordo già seco in quello che dice. "I do not accord with him in what he says": concordare, accord, kept apart from concorrere, concur (D01): this is not his formula of non-concurrence, and it carries no facilmente. già after the negative is left unrendered (D374), as in his last clause. It is a disagreement on the claim, stated without qualifier.

¶ 01 che sempre nel mondo fu tanto del buono in una età quanto in una altra, benché si variino e' luoghi. "That there was always in the world as much of the good in one age as in another, albeit the places vary": Guicciardini's restatement of Machiavelli's tanto di buono, quanto di tristo (Machiavelli .04), with tristo left out and the provinces kept in a concessive: he does not deny that the good moves among places, only that its sum is constant across ages; benché, albeit (D31); età, age (D410).

¶ 01 o per influsso de' cieli o per altra occulta disposizione corrono talvolta certe età. "Either by influence of the heavens or by some other occult disposition, there run at times certain ages": the two causes left open, as o ... o; occulto, occult, hidden; correre, run, the root of the currere family (D01), kept literal. talvolta, at times.

¶ 01 è più virtù o più vizio. "There is more virtù or more vice": virtù (D16) and vizio, vice (glossary), Machiavelli's pair (Machiavelli .07).

¶ 01 o almanco fiorisce più una arte o una disciplina. "Or at least an art or a discipline flourishes more": almanco, at least (glossary); fiorire, flourish, twice here and again with military discipline; the predicate-first order is exposition and goes into plain order (D263).

¶ 01 E per cominciare a quelle meccaniche di che fa menzione lo Scrittore. "And to begin with those mechanical [arts] of which the Writer makes mention": Machiavelli names le arti (Machiavelli .02) only to set them aside from the deceit, since their excellence is evident in itself; Guicciardini's first proof starts from that exception. meccaniche is his word for them, the arts of the hand. Both readers doubted fa, the scan's a/u; read with the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-IIproem-D002, D417). Canestrini's comma after Scrittore was doubtful to both and is read with the 1933 edition (D110, D419).

¶ 01 in quanta eccellenza fussino a tempo de' Greci e poi de' Romani la pittura e la scultura. "In how great excellence painting and sculpture were in the time of the Greeks and then of the Romans": Canestrini's scan shows o la scultura, and both readers preferred the o while naming e; the 1933 edition has e, and the plural verbs that follow (restassino, state sepolte, ritornate) take both arts together (FG-CONS-IIproem-D002, D416).

¶ 01 siano da cento cinquanta o dugento anni in qua ritornate in luce. "They have, in the last hundred and fifty or two hundred years, returned to light": da ... in qua, from then until now. Counted from the date of writing, which the editor puts about 1530, the revival falls in the fourteenth century; the longer figure reaches the age of Giotto. Both the date and the identification are the editor's.

¶ 01 Chi non sa quanto a' tempi antichi fiorì non solo appresso a' Romani, ma in molte provincie la disciplina militare. "Who does not know how much in ancient times military discipline flourished, not only among the Romans but in many provinces": appresso, among; provincia, province (glossary). At appresso ⟨n'|a'⟩ Romani one reader printed the non-word n'; read a' with the other reader and the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-IIproem-D002, D417). Machiavelli's milizia (Machiavelli .05) is "soldiery"; disciplina militare, military discipline, is Guicciardini's term.

¶ 01 non hanno veduto in qualunque parte del mondo se non piccoli e oscuri vestigii. "Have not seen in any part of the world but small and obscure vestiges": qualunque after the negative, "any".

¶ 01 Il medesimo si può dire delle lettere, della religione. "The same can be said of letters, of religion": si può dire, it can be said; the two named without a conjunction, as printed.

¶ 01 in altre sono state in molti luoghi eccellenti e in sommo prezzo. "In others have been in many places excellent and of supreme price": sommo, supreme (D190); in prezzo, prized, held in esteem.

¶ 01 dalle quali variazioni delle arti, della religione, de' movimenti delle cose umane. "From which variations of the arts, of religion, of the movements of human things": variazione, variation (D402), Machiavelli's la variazione de' costumi (Machiavelli .04); cose umane, human things, his le cose umane sempre in moto (Machiavelli .03).

¶ 01 non è maraviglia siano anche variati e' costumi degli uomini, e' quali spesso pigliano il moto suo. "It is no marvel that the customs of men too have varied, which often take their motion": non è maraviglia, it is no marvel (glossary); costumi, customs (D41); pigliare, take (glossary); moto, motion, Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .03); suo for loro, as printed. e' quali may be the men or their customs; the English takes the customs, which are what is said to vary.

¶ 01 dalla instituzione, dalle occasioni, dalla necessità. "From institution, from occasions, from necessity": instituzione, institution (glossary): the word can name the ordering of a people or a man's upbringing, and the English, like the Italian, does not choose; occasione, occasion (glossary).

¶ 01 [È] adunque vera conclusione che non sempre e' tempi antichi sono da essere preferiti a' presenti. "It is then a true conclusion that the ancient times are not always to be preferred to the present": Canestrini prints E, leaving the period without a verb; emended from the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-IIproem-D001, D418, as D114). adunque, then (D74). The reply closes on its first words, conclusione, vera.

¶ 01 ma non è già vero il negare che una età sia qualche volta più corrotta o più virtuosa che l'altre. "But it is not true to deny that one age is sometimes more corrupt or more virtuous than the others": the words of Machiavelli's non essere già sempre vero (Machiavelli .03), rendered the same way, già unrendered (D374), turned on his claim of .04; qualche volta, sometimes, limits the counter-claim. The reply ends without a stop (D40, D419).
