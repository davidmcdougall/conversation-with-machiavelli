## NM-DISC.2.13 — 1824, pp. 260-262

605 words (pass A: p. 260 188, p. 261 259, p. 262 158). Both passes find
CAPITOLO XIII on p. 260 below the end of II.12 (`... ciascuno nel suo
grado, si difenderà meglio.`), the two-line italic rubric `Che si viene di
bassa a gran fortuna più con la / fraude, che con la forza.`, an enlarged
initial I, one printed block with no indents and no notes, the Latin of
Livy 8.4 in italic (p. 262 ll. 12-14), and the end at p. 262 l. 19 `come
fu questa de'Romani.`, CAPITOLO XIV following on the same page.

**The sample (D302).** A fifth of 605 is 121; every page is eligible.
Word-level differences from the 1554, after spelling normalisation: p. 260
none; p. 261 four (`constituito`, `Bernabò`, `all'intorno`, `longinqui`);
p. 262 six (`essi`, `poco` added, `questa guerra`, the Italian gloss added,
`etiam`, `di` added). p. 262 is the densest per word and was chosen and
recorded before it went out, with five line crops of p. 261: the four
differences, and pass A's one doubt between words (`invidia ⟨e|è⟩
questo`, D203(a)).

**Result.** The sample agrees with pass A word for word (158 to 158) but
for the Latin's `⟨ctiam|etiam⟩`, where pass A saw a c-shaped first letter
and named `etiam`, and pass B read `etiam` without doubt: one word, a
damaged sort, not a disagreement of word (as D392, D406). Four crops agree
with pass A. The fifth, `invidia ⟨è|e⟩ questo`, is a common doubt, the same
mark and the same two alternatives, but with the other preference. Rather
than rule on whether that is a disagreement under D302, pp. 260-261 went to
a second, separate pass B whole (447 words: 188, 259), which agrees with
pass A word for word and prefers `e` at the same mark. The chapter is read
twice in full (**D448**). Whether the fifth crop triggers D426's revisit is
put to David (**O47**), with the recommendation that it does not.

What remains is apostrophe spacing (`d' infima`, `ne'principj`,
`de'Romani`), the gaps inside `Camp ani`, `n ome`, `Ro mani`, thin spaces
before punctuation, and a stray dot outside the measure after `furono i`
(p. 261 l. 29, pass A only), all Class A.

**`invidia e questo timore`** (p. 261 l. 30, .04): a dot over the e, not a
grave, doubted by all three readers; only the conjunction construes, and
the 1554 prints `et`. Read `e`, as D303: **D449**.

**Against the 1554 (ruled by precedent).** No difference at a D203(b)
letter class. The sense-bearing whole words, each read by both 1824
readers without doubt: `constituito` / `poſto` (.02); `messer Bernabò` /
`M.Bernardo` (.02); `all'intorno` / `a l'incontro` (.03); `longinqui` /

D438, with translator's notes on `Bernabò`, `all'intorno` and `etiam`:

Recorded as forms, without ruling: `vengano` / `uenghino`, `Senofonte` /

## FG-CONS.2.13 — Canestrini, pp. 66-67

318 / 319 words (A: p. 66 187, p. 67 130; B: 188, 131; A counts the
unspaced `d'in-dustria` as one). Both passes find the heading
**CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO XIII** at the head of p. 66, with no `DEL
LIBRO II` (the L of CAPITOLO broken; the stop after XIII a faint trace,
split); a two-line rubric in smaller type, `Che si viena di bassa a gran
fortuna piu con la fraude. / che con la forza`; one indented paragraph; the
end at p. 67 l. 14 `di conseguire gli intenti suoi`, only a faint trace
after it; and CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO XIV following on p. 67.
Canestrini's note, keyed after `fraude.` on p. 66, on Xenophon's
Cyropaedia, Cyaxares, and Giovanni Galeazzo and Bernabò, is his commentary
and is not carried. `paragraph_divisions: witness` (D66). Pass A marks 30
loci, pass B 39, each 6 in the heading and rubric.

**Body: two splits**, each where both readers see the printed letters:

- p. 66 l. 2 `che si usa ⟨eltam|etiam⟩`: A prefers the printed `eltam`,
  B `etiam`, reading e-l-t-a-m on the page. Not a word; the 1933 edition
  prints `etiam` in italic. Emended `e[ti]am` under D68, as D439.
- p. 66 l. 11 `la ambizione ⟨sna|sua⟩`: A prefers the printed n, B `sua`,
  seeing an n. Emended `s[u]a`, as D439.

**Non-words both readers prefer as printed**, each naming the word: `cha`
×2 (p. 66 ll. 7, 16), `ragni` (l. 8), `feciona` (l. 15), `tonto` (p. 67 l.
1), `potera` (l. 5). None construes in its place; the 1933 edition reads
`che`, `regni`, `feciono`, `tanto`, `potere`. Emended `ch[e]`, `r[e]gni`,
`fecion[o]`, `t[a]nto`, `poter[e]` under D68, as D381, D396.

**Doubts where both readers prefer the word** (`cognosciuto`, `può`,
`buono`, `toglie`, `instruisca`, `imperii`, and B's `essere`, `bassa`, `si`,
`sia`, `instrumento`, `simulazione`, `dissimulazione`): the scan's s/a,
u/n and li/h breaks; readings, as printed, as D398.

All of the above by precedent: **D450**.

**`fraudolenta`** (p. 67 l. 14): A reads it without doubt, B doubts it

`fraudulento`. A word both read that does not construe is borderline under
D68 and comes to David: **O46**, with the recommendation `fraudolent[o]`,
in Canestrini's spelling. Applied provisionally.

**Against the 1933 edition.** `Fu anche prudenza` against `Fu adunche

imperii` / `ed imperi`, `arti` / `arte`, `prudenza` / `prudenzia` ×2, `ai` /
`a'`, `populo` / `popolo`). Pointing: `fortuna.`, `servitù;`, `romano.`

**D450**.

**Witness independence (SCOPE §7).** Neither pass carries a 1933

**Heading and rubric.** The heading as printed, without `del libro II`,
