# Pair I.23 — cold passes and collation

NM pass A, FG passes A and B and the Giglio read were dispatched together,
before the collator had seen any page. The NM sample page was chosen after
pass A came back and given to a fifth reader alone. The 1824 pages went at
350 ppi in thirds; the Canestrini pages at 1200 ppi (native 600) in halves
enlarged 1.6x, with quarter-page crops enlarged 2.2x; the Giglio leaves at
500 ppi in thirds. The collator read the 1933 pages against the passes.

## NM-DISC.1.23 — 1824, pp. 95–98

720 words (pass A: p. 95 101, p. 96 261, p. 97 256, p. 98 102). Pass A finds
CAPITOLO XXIII on p. 95 after line 13, the last line of XXII (`parleremo
solo degli altri due ne' seguenti duoi capitoli.`), the three-line italic
rubric `Che non si debbe mettere a pericolo tutta la fortuna, e non tutte le
forze; e per questo spesso il guardare i passi è dannoso.`, a large initial
N, one printed block, no notes, and the end at p. 98 line 12 `in su i
monti.`, with CAPITOLO XXIV following.

**The sample (D199, D203(c)).** Pass A marked two loci in the whole chapter:
one in the rubric on p. 95 (`fortuna` or `fortunà`, a speck), one in the
body on p. 96 (`è`, a worn sort). P. 96 carries 261 words of the chapter
(36%), and is the page with most pass-A body doubts that is at least a
fifth of the chapter. The collator chose p. 96 and recorded the choice before
giving it out. Pass B read only that page.

**Result: no word, clause or sense disagreement.** The 292 tokens of the
page (words and pointing) are identical between the passes after Class A.
Both readers doubt the same worn `è` (`tale partito è da prendere`) and read
it `è`. Pass B's two other marks are a damaged letter in `questo`, twice. The
full pass B is not triggered, and the sample rule stands for the next
chapter.

**Outside the sample (D203(a)).** Pass A's one other doubt is in the rubric,
where the alternative is not a word. Nothing to raise.

**Against the 1554 (D203(b)).** Word-level differences at letters the scan
breaks, found on the word diff, whether or not anyone doubted:

| .04 `sendo i monti come ___` (p. 97) | `campagna` | `campagne` | kept, D235 |

## Comparison witnesses

**Witness independence (SCOPE §7).** The FG passes split at six loci (three
words, three marks). Pass B sides with Palmarocchi at five, pass A at one,
each on a damaged letter or mark that the reader doubted. That is the
pattern the rule warns of, so the decisive test was run: neither pass carries
a Palmarocchi word-reading where Canestrini differs. Both have `o il
guadagno`, `restorono così combattendo` (no `potenti`), `Flaminio Quinto`,
`danno suo`, `ripreso`, `possono militare`, `combattuto`, `parato`, `delle
sue forze`, against the 1933 `ed el guadagno`, `così potenti combattendo`,
`Tito Quinzio`, `danno tuo`, `riprese`, `può militare`, `combattuti`,
`partito`, `delle tue forze`. The passes read Canestrini.

**FG, 1933 edition.** Word differences from Canestrini, for the variant
notes: `ed el guadagno` (D231); `restorono così potenti combattendo`;
`Tito Quinzio` for `Flaminio Quinto`; `danno tuo`; `riprese` (D232); `può
militare`; `combattuti`; `partito` (D233); `delle tue forze`. The rest is
Palmarocchi's orthographic profile (`el`, `popolo`, `imprudenzia`,
`verbigrazia`, `Alpe`, `costringa`, `uccisione`, `condizione`, `occasione`,
`ragione` plurals, `adunche`, `apresso`, `circumiacente`, `resistenzia`) and
his pointing, and is not enumerated.

**NM, 1554 Giglio.** A third reader transcribed fol. 38r–39r cold
(`NM-DISC.1.23-1554.md`); the transcript was collated by word against the
established 1824 text. The readings cited below were checked on the leaf

(.03) and the two .05 readings, which rest on the transcript. The

  di essi`.

- .05 `si accostarono alle genti Franciose` for `si aderirono alle Genti
