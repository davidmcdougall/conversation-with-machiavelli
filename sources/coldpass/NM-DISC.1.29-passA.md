# Reader's notes

- p. 107, l. 3, "Principe⟨.|:⟩": the final stop after "Principe" has a small extra speck above/beside it, so it could be read as a colon; the next line begins with capital "E", so a full stop is preferred.
- p. 108, l. 25, "⟨e|e’⟩ virtuosissi-": a small raised mark like an apostrophe sits between "e" and "virtuosissi-". It may be a stray mark or a printed elision; plain "e" is preferred.
- p. 109, l. 2, "⟨in|m⟩ Roma": the first word looks like "m" (the dot of the i is not visible and the two strokes look joined). "in" is preferred from the letter spacing and sense.
- p. 111, l. 1, "⟨E|E’⟩ parve": a small raised mark sits before "parve" ("E 'parve"), similar to the p. 108 mark. It is probably a stray mark, so plain "E" is preferred.
- Printed features read silently: p. 107 l. 17-19 the Latin quotation "Proclivius ... habetur." is in italic (marked *...*; the italic v has a cursive form). p. 108 l. 22 "dal suo": the s is damaged and l. 17 "suoi" has a broken o; both words are clear. p. 109 l. 13 "a Napoli" is printed with almost no space ("aNapoli"); a space is kept. p. 109 l. 15 "menò seco" has specks under the n and the o, and l. 17 "possono" has a speck above the final o; these are not diacritics. p. 109 l. 16 "È" carries a grave accent on the capital. p. 110 l. 10 "Vero": the V is slightly damaged. p. 110 l. 11 "discorse" has a speck over the s. p. 110 l. 22 "il quale" has a stray dot. The spacing after the apostrophe varies (e.g. "d’ Antonio", "l’ uno l’ acquistare, l’altro", "nell’ una", "all’ uno" against "l’offende", "ogn’industria", "ch’egli"); it is given as best judged, and spacing after an apostrophe is not counted as uncertain.
