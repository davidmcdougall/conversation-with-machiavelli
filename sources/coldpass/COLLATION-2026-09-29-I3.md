# Pair I.3 — pass A against pass B

Both B passes were dispatched before pass A was made, in separate
directories, and returned only locations, counts and paths.

## NM-DISC.1.3 — 1824, pp. 24–25

387 words. **No token difference after Class A.** Heading and rubric
identical; both passes find the same boundaries (drop capital on p. 24 to
`de' Nobili.` on p. 25; CAPITOLO IV opens p. 26). Both independently mark
the same point-like mark after `loro` (`quella loro. superbia`, p. 24) as a
probable blemish: mid-clause, no capital follows. Not carried.

## FG-CONS.1.3 — Canestrini, pp. 10–12

441 words. **No clause or sense disagreement.** Both passes find two printed
paragraphs at the same point (`La causa dello eleggere`), the same two
editor's notes (not part of the unit), a printed dash before `Le quali`, and
a terminal stop after `plebe`.

The scan loses the bar of e (`republico`, `por`, `vivera`, `coma`,
`ambiziona`, `poriculo`, `pone`, `ara` ×2, `chiamaro`) and breaks the bowl of
u into n (`nomini`, `qnesto`, `snoi`, `fnrono`). Both passes read every one
of these the same way.

**O17.** Same word, same sense; the English is "capacity" (glossary) on
either spelling. The letter is an o or a broken u. The same unit prints
`facultà del fare male` clearly on p. 11, and the scan breaks u into n-shapes
throughout. Recommend `facultà`.

**O18.** The printed shape is an a, but so are `ara` for `era` twice in the
same paragraph. `e'` (the plural article) is what the unit prints two
sentences later, `che e tribuni fussino`, and `eleggere` takes a direct
object everywhere else in this edition. `a'` would read "to the tribunes",
which is not a construction the chapter uses. Recommend `e'`. English:
"electing the tribunes".

**The stop after `cattivi`.** Canestrini's note reference stands where the
stop would be. If pass B's faint point is real, it is printed; if pass A is
right, it is displaced by the reference, which is D40's case. Either way the
text carries a stop, and it is declared as C001.

**O19 — not a split, a borderline emendation.** Both passes read the opening
as `E posto troppo assolutamente che ...`, with no accent; Canestrini prints
`È vero` and `È adunque` accented in the same unit. As printed the first
period has no finite verb: a participle (`posto`), two `che` clauses, and a
`perché` giving the reason for a claim never made. With `È` it is Guicciardini's
opening judgment: "It is laid down too absolutely that ...". The 1933 edition
prints `È posto`. D68 emends a clause without a verb; D68 also sends
borderline cases to David, and this one is borderline because a reader can
supply an elliptical copula after the conjunction, as in speech. Recommend
emending to `[È]`, the printed `E` in the variant note. Carried
provisionally.

**Stops.** One sentence break with a capital and no visible point, before
Canestrini's dash at `di loro — Le quali`; the 1933 edition prints a stop
and no dash. Supplied as witness correction C002 (D67); the dash is
Canestrini's and is kept.

Class A (accents, apostrophes, the plural article `e'` at `e' savii`,
`e' patrizii`, `e' comodi`, `e' tribuni`, `tra 'l`) applied by rule and not
enumerated.

## Comparison witnesses

Witness independence (SCOPE §7): at the three FG split loci pass A sides with
Palmarocchi at none. Pass B sides with it at all three. That is the pattern
the rule tells us to check, so it is recorded rather than passed over. It is
not a provenance finding: pass B had three Canestrini page images and nothing
else, in a directory of its own, and at each locus the Canestrini page is
damaged in the way the scan is damaged everywhere, so a reader must choose
the letter from sense; sense and Palmarocchi agree. Pass A's divergences are
the literal letter-shapes. No other locus in the unit shows B nearer the 1933
text than A.
