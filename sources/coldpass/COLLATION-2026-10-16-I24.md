# Pair I.24 — cold passes and collation

NM pass A, FG passes A and B and the Giglio read were dispatched together,
before the collator had seen any page. The NM sample page was chosen after
pass A came back and given to a fifth reader alone. The 1824 pages went at
350 ppi in thirds; the Canestrini pages at 1200 ppi (native 600) in halves
enlarged 1.6x, with quarter-page crops enlarged 2.2x; the Giglio leaves at
500 ppi in thirds. The collator read the 1933 page against the passes. The
NM pass B reader reports writing a scratch draft outside its directory and
deleting it; it read nothing but its four images.

## NM-DISC.1.24 — 1824, pp. 98–100

434 words (pass A: p. 98 82, p. 99 249, p. 100 103). Pass A finds CAPITOLO
XXIV on p. 98 after line 12, the last line of XXIII (`che i Francesi
dovessero essere tenuti in su i monti.`), the three-line italic rubric `Le
Repubbliche bene ordinate constituiscono premj e pene a' loro cittadini, nè
compensano mai l' uno con l' altro.`, a large initial E, one printed block,
no notes, and the end at p. 100 line 14 `aveva salvato.`, with CAPITOLO XXV
following.

**The sample (D199, D203(c)).** Pass A marked one locus in the whole
chapter, on p. 99 (`premiato`, a worn m). P. 99 carries 249 words of the
chapter (57%), and is the only page with a pass-A doubt. The collator chose
p. 99 and recorded the choice before giving it out. Pass B read only that
page.

**Result: no word, clause or sense disagreement.** The 291 tokens of the
page (words and pointing) are identical between the passes after Class A,
but for the page-foot hyphen of `Pub-`. Both readers doubt the same worn m
in `premiato` and read it `premiato`. Both read `ancora che grande` as
printed and flag it for sense; the 1554 prints it too. The full pass B is
not triggered, and the sample rule stands for the next chapter.

**Outside the sample (D203(a)).** No pass-A doubt on pp. 98 or 100.

**Against the 1554 (D203(b)).** Word-level differences at letters the scans
break, found on the word diff, whether or not anyone doubted:

| .03 `per ___ di bene` (p. 99) | `ricompensa` | `ricompenso` | kept, D248 |

Also at e/a, but spellings of one word, not other words: `confidenza`/

## Comparison witnesses

**Witness independence (SCOPE §7).** The FG passes split at two loci. Pass
B sides with Palmarocchi at both, each on a letter both readers doubted.
That is the pattern the rule warns of, so the decisive test was run:
neither pass carries a Palmarocchi word-reading where Canestrini differs.
Both have `pensatamente`, `via provocata`, `andata`, `il populo` (B) or
`poputo` (A), and `cagioni`, against the 1933 `pesatamente`, `ira
provocata`, `andato`, `el popolo` and `cagione`. The passes read
Canestrini.

**FG, 1933 edition.** Word differences from Canestrini, for the variant
notes: `pesatamente`; `ira` (D245); `andato` (D247). The rest is
Palmarocchi's orthographic profile (`el`, `popolo`, `amazzare`,
`circunstanzie`, `cagione`, `ed`) and his pointing, and is not enumerated.

**NM, 1554 Giglio.** A third reader transcribed fol. 39v–40r cold
(`NM-DISC.1.24-1554.md`); the transcript was collated by word against the
established 1824 text. The readings cited below were checked on fol. 40r
by the collator; `assolto` and `condennare` (.02) rest on the transcript.
The differences that bear on the English:

- .03 `debbe di di quel poco astenersi`, without `non`: the 1554 says the
  opposite of the 1824.
- .03 `per ricompenso di bene` (D248); `si uide` for `si vede`.
- .04 `Mallio` for `Manlio`, twice.
- Both witnesses print `ancora che grande`; not a variant.
