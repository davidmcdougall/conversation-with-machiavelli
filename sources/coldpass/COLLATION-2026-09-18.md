# Dual cold pass — FG-CONS.1.12 and NM-DISC.1.12

Pass A is the text already in `corpus/`, made 2026-07-17 by a single agent.
Pass B is an independent cold transcription made 2026-09-18 by a reader given
only the witness page images, with no access to pass A. Neither reader saw the
other's work. Full pass B texts are alongside this file.

Per SCOPE §7 these disagreements are **not resolved here**. They are for David.

## Result in one line

**Machiavelli: 15 differences in 997 words, of which one is a reading.**
**Guicciardini: 29 differences in 422 words, of which roughly eight are readings.**

The rule is not ceremony. It is also not uniformly necessary — the two units
behaved very differently, and the difference is instructive.

---

## A. Systematic conventions — one decision each, not one per instance

These are not misreadings by either pass. They are undeclared editorial
conventions in pass A. Each needs a single ruling that then applies to all 78
units, and each ruling belongs in DEC-009 where it can be cited.

**A1 · Space after an elided apostrophe.** 10 instances in Machiavelli, 1 in
Guicciardini. Pass A sets `de' Potenti`, `ne' Popoli`, `a' Viniziani`,
`da' quali`; pass B sets them solid — `de'Potenti` — and notes that the print's
spacing varies line by line with justification, so neither is "what the page
says". Pass A's spaced form is a modern reading convention. Fine, but it should
be declared.

**A2 · Accent direction on -ché / né.** Pass A prints `perché`, `benché`, `né`;
pass B reads grave throughout — `perchè`, `benchè`, `nè`. Pass A is internally
inconsistent about this: it keeps grave in the Machiavelli `Perchè` but uses
acute `Né` in the same file. Nineteenth-century Italian printing normally sets
grave. Worth looking at the page, then ruling once.

**A3 · Euphonic d.** Three instances, all one direction: pass A `Ed anche`,
`libertà ed imperio`, `consuetudine ed inclinazione`; pass B `E anche`,
`e imperio`, `e inclinazione`. Either Canestrini prints the d and pass B dropped
it three times, or pass A supplied it three times. One look at the page settles
all three.

**A4 · Semicolon or comma.** Eight instances in Guicciardini, every one in the
same direction: pass A semicolon, pass B comma. Either the scan is losing the
upper dot — plausible, pass B reports punctuation dropping out on this bitonal
scan — or pass A is pointing the text more heavily than the witness does. This
is the largest single block of disagreement in the unit and it is systematic,
so it is one decision.

## B. Readings — these need eyes on the page, one at a time

**B1 · `[ar]ebbe` or `ebbe`.** *This is the important one.* Pass A reads
`non [ar]ebbe patito`, with Canestrini's supplied letters bracketed, and the
whole thing is recorded as disagreement D001 with a rationale about preserving
the bracketing under DEC-009. Pass B read `non ebbe patito` — no brackets, no
`ar` — and did not flag it as doubtful. Both cannot be right, and the most
carefully documented reading in the unit is the one they split on.

**B2 · `eziandio` or `eziamdio`.** Pass A `eziandio`. Pass B `eziamdio`, and it
did not guess: it counted strokes against the `n` of `quando` on the same line
and reports three.

**B3 · `soggiogarono` or `soggiogorono`.** Pass A `soggiogarono`. Pass B
`soggiogorono`, again checked against a control `a` two words later, and flagged
as a probable compositor's error transcribed as printed. If pass B is right,
pass A silently corrected the witness — which DEC-009 does not permit.

**B4 · `e'` or `e`.** Four instances: `e' vituperii`, `e' frutti`, `e' sudditi`,
`e' quali`. Pass A prints the apostrophe. Pass B magnified all four and reports
no apostrophe on the page in any of them. If pass B is right, pass A supplied
four editorial apostrophes without recording it.

**B5 · `violenzia` or `violenza`.** Pass A `violenzia`, pass B `violenza`.

**B6 · Capitalisation, three places.** `de' barbari` / `de' Barbari` and
`de' romani` / `de' Romani` in Guicciardini; and in Machiavelli
`hanno meno Religione` / `hanno meno religione` — pass B flags this last as the
single lower-case instance in a text that capitalises the word everywhere else,
which is the kind of detail a careless pass regularises away.

**B7 · `più` twice.** Pass B sees no accent at `furono piu a tempo` and
`che è piu secondo`, while `più` prints clearly four other times in the same
unit. It transcribed what it saw and flagged it. Probably dropped accents in the
impression; confirm and move on.

**B8 · Final stop.** Pass B reports the Guicciardini unit ends at
`inclinazione sua` with no full stop printed. Pass A closes with one.

**B9 · One convergence worth noting.** At `sotto uno re; pure` pass B found an
anomalously wide blank — about two word-spaces — inspected it at 18× and found
no ink, and flagged a probable dropped point. Pass A prints a semicolon there.
Two readers independently found something at the same spot. That is the process
working.

## C. Structure — the finding neither pass was looking for

Pass B measured the left edge of every line in both units and reports that
**neither unit contains a single indented line after its opening**. Both are
printed as one paragraph.

Pass A divides Guicciardini into three paragraphs and Machiavelli into seven.

This matters more than any single reading, because the paragraph numbers are
load-bearing: `FG-CONS.1.12.01`, `.02`, `.03` are the unit's internal addresses,
the collation table's `locator` values point at them, and the site's navigation
will be built on them. If the divisions are editorial, they are an undeclared
editorial layer sitting underneath the addressing scheme of the whole edition —
and the decision should be taken deliberately now rather than inherited by 78
units.

There is a respectable case for keeping them: an unbroken 1,000-word block is
punishing on screen, and a reading edition may reasonably break it. But then say
so, mark them as editorial, and do not let the collation apparatus imply the
witness supports them.

**Related:** pass A carries Machiavelli's three-line italic rubric as the first
line of body paragraph `.01`; pass B reads it as heading apparatus. One of those
is wrong.

## E. What this says about the rule

On Machiavelli the pass found one reading in a thousand words. On Guicciardini
it found eight in four hundred, plus a structural finding that affects the whole
edition. The difference is the witness, not the reader: the Canestrini scan is
bitonal, small-type and drops hairlines, accents and points; the 1824 Discorsi is
a clean, well-inked, generously set page.

So the rule earns its keep, but not evenly. A defensible refinement, once more
units are done: require the second pass where the witness is poor, and sample it
where the witness is clean. That is a decision to take on evidence at unit ten or
so, not now on a sample of two.

**Cost of this run:** two readers, about fourteen minutes wall clock.

## F. Process note

Both readers initially shared one scratch directory and overwrote each other's
page renders. The Guicciardini reader detected the collision itself, moved to a
private directory, re-rendered from the witness and reports that no foreign file
was ever read. No transcription is believed to be contaminated, but parallel cold
passes must be given separate scratch space. This has been fixed for future runs.
