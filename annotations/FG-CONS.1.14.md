## FG-CONS.1.14.01

**Argument.** Three acts in one sentence. He withholds certainty — "I do not hold it for certain" — that the captains used the auspices craftily, as Machiavelli's rubric and his account of Papirius suppose [[NM-DISC.1.14.02]]. He states his own belief against Machiavelli's: that, especially in the first times, their minds were held by the religion. And he finds that Machiavelli's own example does not tell against him.

**Argument.** Papirius, in Guicciardini's reading, did nothing irreligious. He had the report from the *pullarii* whose office it was to make it, and it was favourable; what some of them told certain soldiers, who told his nephew, who told him, came from third persons, and he had no need to act on it [[NM-DISC.1.14.03]]. Where Machiavelli sees a general turning a lie into a sign, Guicciardini sees a consul following procedure. He says nothing of Appius [[NM-DISC.1.14.04]].

**Term.** *Gli animi loro occupati da questa religione*: their minds taken up, occupied, by the religion — held by it, not using it.
