## FG-CONS.1.32.01

**Argument.** A reply of 103 words in three periods. The first divides the men Machiavelli spoke of in general by what they already are to the giver. To seek, with new benefits in a time of necessity, to make one who is ordinarily your friend more your friend is one thing; to seek to gain one who is wholly your enemy is another. The second weighs them: the first much easier, as it was for the Romans, the second most difficult; and even in the first case it is without comparison more useful to have done it before the need. That much of Machiavelli's precept he grants: providing beforehand is beyond comparison more useful, even in the easier case. He grants it as a comparison, not as a rule. The third turns: in either case he does not blame the man who has been imprudent in not providing earlier, if, brought to the necessity, he attempts this remedy, a remedy which, although it has little hope of helping, carries no danger of doing harm. He calls the man imprudent, limits the withholding of blame to that condition, and does not recommend the remedy.

**Cross-reference.** The last clause answers the last clause of the chapter. Machiavelli's man who has governed himself otherwise and, when danger comes, believes he can regain men with benefits "accelerates his ruin" [[NM-DISC.1.32.03]]; Guicciardini says the remedy carries no danger of doing harm. He takes up Machiavelli's own words for it, "necessity", "remedy", "help" and "need" [[NM-DISC.1.32.01]] [[NM-DISC.1.32.02]] [[NM-DISC.1.32.03]], and with "little hope" concedes Machiavelli's conclusion that such remedies seldom help, without taking up the ground he gave for it, that like causes happen seldom. He does not take up the grounds Machiavelli gave in his first period for the late benefit's failure, that the generality credits it to your adversaries and, fearing it will be taken back, has no debt with you [[NM-DISC.1.32.01]]; for the ruin itself Machiavelli gives no separate reason.

**Cross-reference.** Machiavelli's subject was "the people" and "men" in general, and his example the Roman plebs. Guicciardini's "as befell the Romans" puts that example in his easier case, among friends, without saying why. Machiavelli had the senate doubting whether the plebs would rather accept the kings [[NM-DISC.1.32.01]], though he then gives the people reasons to credit the senate's disposition [[NM-DISC.1.32.02]].
