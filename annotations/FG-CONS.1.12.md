## FG-CONS.1.12.01

**Argument.** Guicciardini passes over Machiavelli's whole theory of religion [[NM-DISC.1.12.01]]–[[NM-DISC.1.12.04]] in silence and begins with the court. There he goes further than Machiavelli: nothing said against it can be enough. Then, with *credo* — assent — he grants the causal claim of the second charge and restates its mechanism, with two shifts [[NM-DISC.1.12.06]].

**History.** He had spent a decade in the service of the Medici popes: governor of Modena and Reggio under Leo X, then president of the Romagna and lieutenant-general of the papal army under Clement VII. In the *Ricordi* (C 28) he voices the same loathing in his own person, and adds what this page leaves out: no one dislikes the ambition, avarice and softness of priests more than he, but his standing with several popes obliged him, for his own interest, to love their greatness — and but for that he would have loved Martin Luther as himself.

## FG-CONS.1.12.02

**Argument.** *Non so già* — doubt, pressed. Machiavelli wrote "one republic or one prince" as if they were the same remedy [[NM-DISC.1.12.06]]. Guicciardini splits them. Had a single republic ruled Italy, it would have been glory to the name of Italy and happiness to the ruling city, and calamity to every other, because republics do not share the fruits of their liberty with their subjects. The paragraph is contrary to fact: no republic had ruled Italy since Rome.

**Term.** *Felicità* and *infelicità* are civic conditions, of a city or of a province, not private states of mind.

## FG-CONS.1.12.03

**Argument.** The counterweights, in order. Division brought calamities, but the barbarian invasions came more under the Roman empire than since — compare Machiavelli's "prey … of powerful barbarians" [[NM-DISC.1.12.07]]. Division also brought flourishing cities that a single ruling republic would not have allowed. A kingdom is different — "more common to all the subjects" — which grants Machiavelli's France [[NM-DISC.1.12.06]] and denies it to Italy for reasons older than the Church: fate, the complexion of the people, a natural appetite for liberty. No power but Rome, he believes, ever held all of Italy, and Rome held it by *virtù* and violence together; compare the *virtù* Machiavelli says the Church lacked [[NM-DISC.1.12.06]].

**Term.** *Complessione* is humoral: the temperament of a people as a mixture of qualities, "tempered" so that they have wit and force.

**Argument.** The close. If the Church opposed monarchy, Guicciardini does "not readily concur" that this was Italy's unhappiness, since it kept Italy in the way of living most in keeping with its most ancient usage and inclination. He declines Machiavelli's conclusion; his own is hedged. For *concorrere* against Machiavelli's *discorrere* and *occorrere*, see [[NM-DISC.1.12.05]].
