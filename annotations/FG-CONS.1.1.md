## FG-CONS.1.1.01

**Argument.** He accepts the distinction — "is true" — and then places Rome in it on his own grounds. Athens and Venice were built by their dwellers out of necessity; Rome was built without necessity, as a colony of Alba, by men who loved the place or wanted to govern themselves [[NM-DISC.1.1.03]]. Machiavelli's Aeneas option [[NM-DISC.1.1.08]] he dismisses as looking for origins too far back.

## FG-CONS.1.1.02

**Argument.** A correction to what Machiavelli says of cities built by strangers [[NM-DISC.1.1.04]]. Colonies sent out to relieve a country do not always depend on those who sent them: the Gauls and Cimbri sent colonies that owed nothing to home. The truer distinction would be between colonies built to govern themselves and colonies built to acknowledge a lord, and even the second can grow great when accidents free them. He grants that such cities are slower to begin, but thinks, "perhaps", that as many of them have risen to power as of those born free. Florence, which Machiavelli set among the cities born dependent, is his example.

**History.** Livy (5.34) has the Gauls send out a surplus population under Bellovesus and Segovesus to seek new lands; the Cimbri were a whole people on the move rather than a colony sent out.

## FG-CONS.1.1.03

**Argument.** A large people is the foundation of power, so a fertile site is better, because inhabitants "concur" there — flock there, in the verb's first sense of running together. But if a site not fat could be peopled, the necessity of providing for oneself would make men more virtuous than good laws, because laws can be changed at will and necessity cannot. Machiavelli had made the laws supply the necessity a fertile site lacks [[NM-DISC.1.1.06]]; Guicciardini ranks necessity above them.

**Argument.** Rome, fertile but without a countryside and ringed by strong peoples, was forced outward by exactly that necessity. The close limits the whole argument to cities that live by "the common use of the world", not to a city that would live "in the philosophical manner". It turns back on Machiavelli his own argument that the weak are oppressed [[NM-DISC.1.1.06]].
