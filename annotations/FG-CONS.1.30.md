## FG-CONS.1.30.01

**Argument.** A reply of 92 words, which answers the prince's part of the chapter only. Guicciardini begins with praise: a prince does well to go on his expeditions himself. His reasons are not Machiavelli's. Machiavelli's prince goes so that the glory and the acquisition stay his and he has no captain to suspect; Guicciardini's goes because the expeditions proceed with a different reputation, and because his men serve him otherwise than when he works through captains. Both reasons concern the conduct of the war, not gratitude.

Then a belief, hedged inside itself: the counsel of the Discourse is perhaps necessary to a tyrant, or to whoever does not hold his state well and firmly, but of little profit to a great and natural king. The evidence is what is seen every day: the princes he calls ours commonly make war through captains, and do not, for that reason, suffer these mishaps, or very seldom. He limits the scope of Machiavelli's remedy rather than denying it, and the limit comes from experience, not from an argument about suspicion.

**Cross-reference.** Machiavelli gives no precept to the prince who stays at home through negligence or little prudence and sends a captain [[NM-DISC.1.30.02]]. Guicciardini's princes commonly make their wars through captains; he gives them no such cause, and reports that the mishaps do not, for that reason, follow, or very seldom.

**Cross-reference.** The captain's two ways, the republic, Rome and the citizen [[NM-DISC.1.30.02]] [[NM-DISC.1.30.04]] are not taken up, except as "these mishaps". Canestrini's footnote to the reply, not carried here, paraphrases the sentence of the chapter the reply praises [[NM-DISC.1.30.01]].

**History.** Which princes Guicciardini means is not said. In his lifetime the kings of France and Spain both made war in person and through captains: Charles VIII and Louis XII crossed into Italy themselves, and Ferdinand of Aragon conquered Naples through Gonzalo Fernández de Córdoba, the Consalvo of I.29. When the reply was written, around 1530, the kings were Francis I and Charles V.
