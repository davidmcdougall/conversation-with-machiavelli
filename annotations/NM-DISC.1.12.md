## NM-DISC.1.12.01

**Argument.** The chapter's premise, and it is functional before it is pious: a religion lives by "some principal order of its own", and the proof is what happened when that order failed. The pagan system hung on the oracles; once they were seen to speak for the powerful, belief went, and with it the disposition to keep "every good order". The same test is applied to the Christian religion [[NM-DISC.1.12.04]] and underlies the first charge against the Roman court [[NM-DISC.1.12.05]].

**History.** The charge that the oracles spoke for the powerful is ancient. Cicero reports Demosthenes' complaint that the Pythia — the oracle of Delphi — "philippized", prophesying to suit Philip of Macedon (*De divinatione* II.118). The governing witness names Delos here, the 1554 printing Delphi; see the translator's notes.

## NM-DISC.1.12.02

**Argument.** The prescription: princes should favour and increase whatever arises in religion's favour "even though they should judge them false", and the more so the more prudent and the more "knowing in natural things" they are. Understanding nature is a reason to sponsor miracles, not to expose them. Machiavelli states it without apology, and calls those who practise it wise.

## NM-DISC.1.12.03

**History.** Veii, Rome's Etruscan neighbour, fell to Camillus, traditionally in 396 BC. Livy tells the story of Juno's image (V.22): young men chosen to carry the goddess to Rome entered her temple with reverence, and when one asked — whether moved by the god or in youthful jest — *visne Romam ire, Iuno?*, the others cried that she had nodded; a voice saying she was willing was, he adds, a later addition to the tale. Machiavelli's Latin is not Livy's, and he gives the scene to soldiers in the act of sacking. The reverence he keeps, because it is his evidence.

**Argument.** The explanation is psychological: men full of religion hear the answer they had "perhaps presupposed". Camillus and the princes did not invent the miracle; they did to it what [[NM-DISC.1.12.02]] prescribes, in the same words — favoured and increased it.

## NM-DISC.1.12.04

**Term.** "The Christian republic" is Christendom — the *respublica Christiana* of medieval usage — not a state; "the Christian states and republics" in the same sentence are its members. The Giver is presumably Christ.

**Argument.** The turn. The rule of [[NM-DISC.1.12.01]] is applied to Christianity: measure the present use against the foundations, and the result is a correlation — the nearer a people is to the Roman Church, the less religion it has.

**Term.** "The scourge" (*il flagello*) echoes the Florentine prophetic preaching of the 1490s, Savonarola's above all, which announced a scourge coming upon Italy before the Church's renewal.

**Cross-reference.** Guicciardini answers none of this; see [[FG-CONS.1.12.01]].

## NM-DISC.1.12.05

**Argument.** Two charges, numbered and of different kinds. The first is moral: the court's example has destroyed devotion, and without religion "the contrary" of every good is presupposed. The second, "greater still", is political: the Church has kept Italy divided. The debt Italians owe the Church is ironic both times.

**Term.** "I wish to discourse … those reasons which occur to me": *discorrere* and *occorrere*, two compounds of *currere*, to run. Guicciardini's reply turns on a third, *concorrere* [[FG-CONS.1.12.03]]. Whether he meant the echo cannot be shown; the words share a root, and this edition keeps all three in English so that the reader can hear it.

## NM-DISC.1.12.06

**Argument.** The mechanism of the second charge, in two halves that must be read together: the Church was not powerful enough, nor of enough *virtù*, to unify Italy itself, and not so weak that it could not summon "a powerful one" to defend it against anyone in Italy who had grown too powerful. The claim that no province was ever "united or happy" except under one republic or one prince is the premise Guicciardini takes apart [[FG-CONS.1.12.02]].

**History.** Charlemagne, at the call of Pope Adrian I, conquered the Lombard kingdom in 773–74. In Machiavelli's own time the League of Cambrai (December 1508), which Julius II joined in 1509, broke Venetian power on the mainland at Agnadello; in 1512 the French were driven out of Lombardy with Swiss arms.

**Cross-reference.** Guicciardini restates the mechanism and grants it [[FG-CONS.1.12.01]], with two shifts: the Church's strength becomes the credit religion gave it, its weakness the lack of arms of its own; and it summons foreign princes against whoever would oppress Italy, not to defend itself.

## NM-DISC.1.12.07

**Argument.** The experiment that cannot be run, proposed anyway: move the Roman court to Switzerland, and its customs would disorder that province faster than any other accident could. The Swiss are chosen because they alone, today, live "according to the ancients" in religion and in military orders.

**Cross-reference.** "Prey … of powerful barbarians" is answered in effect: the barbarian inundations came more in the time of the Roman empire than otherwise [[FG-CONS.1.12.03]].
