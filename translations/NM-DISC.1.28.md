# Discourses I.28

*For what cause the Romans were less ungrateful to their citizens than the Athenians.*

## NM-DISC.1.28.01

Whoever reads the things done by republics will find in all of them some species of ingratitude against their citizens, but he will find less of it in Rome than in Athens, and, it may be, [than] in any other republic. And seeking the cause of this, speaking of Rome and of Athens, I believe it happened because the Romans had less cause to suspect their citizens than the Athenians [had].

## NM-DISC.1.28.02

For at Rome, reasoning of it from the expulsion of the kings as far as Sulla and Marius, liberty was never taken away by any citizen of its own; in such a way that in it there was no great cause to suspect them, and consequently to offend them inconsiderately.

## NM-DISC.1.28.03

At Athens indeed the contrary befell; for, liberty being taken away from it by Pisistratus in its most flourishing time, and under a deceit of goodness, as soon as it later became free, remembering the injuries received and the past servitude, it became a most sharp avenger not only of the errors, but of the shadow of the errors of its citizens. Hence arose the exile and the death of so many excellent men; hence the order of ostracism, and every other violence which at various times was done against its optimates by that city. And it is most true, what these writers of civility say, that peoples bite more fiercely after they have recovered liberty than after they have conserved it.

## NM-DISC.1.28.04

Whoever will consider, then, what has been said will not blame Athens in this, nor praise Rome; but will accuse necessity alone for it, through the diversity of the accidents that arose in this city. For it will be seen, whoever will consider things subtly, that if liberty had been taken away at Rome as at Athens, Rome would not have been more pious towards its citizens than that one was.

## NM-DISC.1.28.05

Of which a most true conjecture can be made, through what occurred after the expulsion of the kings against Collatinus and Publius Valerius; of whom the first, even though he had taken part in freeing Rome, was sent into exile for no other cause than for holding the name of the Tarquins; the other, having only given suspicion of himself by building a house on the Caelian Hill, was also about to be made an exile. So that one can esteem, seeing how suspicious and severe Rome was in these two, that it would have used ingratitude as Athens [did], if by its citizens, as that one in its first times, and before its augmentation, it had been injured. And so as not to have to return any more to this matter of ingratitude, I will say of it what will occur [to me] of it in the following chapter.

## Notes

**The rubric.** `Per qual cagione i Romani furono meno ingrati ai loro cittadini che gli Ateniesi`. "For what cause the Romans were less ungrateful to their citizens than the Athenians": `cagione`, cause (glossary). The chapter opens Machiavelli's two chapters on ingratitude, I.28 and I.29; Guicciardini answers each in a separate consideration.

**`qualche specie d'ingratitudine contro a' suoi cittadini`.** "Some species of ingratitude against their citizens": `specie`, species (glossary). `suoi` refers to the republics, `suo` for `loro` as often in both authors (D211).

**`e per avventura`.** "And, it may be": as elsewhere in the edition, kept apart from `forse`, "perhaps". The comparison carries over without a second `che`; the English supplies "[than]" (D53).

**`che gli Ateniesi`.** "Than the Athenians [had]": `gli Ateniesi` is a subject, not the object of `sospettare`; the verb is supplied in brackets (D53).

**`credo accadesse`.** "I believe it happened": `accadere`, happen (D273). Machiavelli marks his own judgment; the English keeps it a belief, not a finding.

**`sospettare di`.** "To suspect": `sospettare`, `sospetto`, `sospettosa`, suspect, suspicion, suspicious, one root through the chapter and into Guicciardini's reply.

**`ragionando di lei dalla cacciata de' Re insino a Silla e Mario`.** "Reasoning of it from the expulsion of the kings as far as Sulla and Marius": `ragionare`, reason (D178); `insino a`, as far as (D166). The kings were expelled in 509 BC on the traditional reckoning; Sulla's march on Rome was in 88 BC, his dictatorship from 82; Marius, with Cinna, took Rome in 87. Machiavelli names Sulla first. `cacciata`, expulsion, as in Guicciardini's first line.

**`non fu mai tolta la libertà`.** "Liberty was never taken away": `tolta`, of `togliere`, take away (D131); `libertà`, liberty (glossary). `tolta la libertà` comes three times (.02, .03, .04), and the English keeps one phrase, "liberty taken away", for Rome's never, Athens' once, and the counterfactual.

**`e per conseguente d'offendergli inconsideratamente`.** "And consequently to offend them inconsiderately": `offendere`, offend, in the older sense of injure, as at NM I.7; `inconsideratamente`, inconsiderately (D104), the adverb of `considerare`, which the chapter takes up twice in .04.

**`Intervenne bene ad Atene il contrario`.** "At Athens indeed the contrary befell": `intervenire`, befall, kept apart from `accadere`, happen (.01); "intervene" is a false friend. `bene` concedes and contrasts, "indeed". The Italian puts the verb first; plain English order (D263).

**`sendole tolta la libertà da Pisistrato nel suo più florido tempo, e sotto uno inganno di bontà`.** "Liberty being taken away from it by Pisistratus in its most flourishing time, and under a deceit of goodness": the absolute construction kept. Pisistratus seized the tyranny of Athens in 561/60 BC and held it, with two expulsions, until his death in 528/7; his son Hippias held it until 510. "Under a deceit of goodness": Herodotus (1.59) and Plutarch (*Solon* 29-30) tell how he won the people as their champion. The 1554 edition reads `fiorito`, "flowering", for `florido` (NM-DISC-I28-D004).

**`come prima la diventò poi libera`.** "As soon as it later became free": `come prima`, as soon as; `la` is the subject pronoun, Athens.

**`diventò acerrima vendicatrice`.** "It became a most sharp avenger": `acerrima`, the Latin superlative of `acre`, sharp; "bitter" is kept for `acerbo` (glossary, I.16), and "fierce" for `fieramente` two sentences on. The 1554 edition reads `aspra`, "harsh" (NM-DISC-I28-D004).

**`non solamente degli errori, ma dell'ombra degli errori`.** "Not only of the errors, but of the shadow of the errors": the repetition kept; the shadow of an error is the suspicion of one.

**`Di qui nacque ... di qui`.** "Hence arose ... hence": `nascere`, arise (D04); the anaphora kept.

**`lo esilio e la morte di tanti eccellenti uomini`.** "The exile and the death of so many excellent men": Machiavelli names none. Aristides, Themistocles and Cimon were ostracised; Miltiades was fined and died soon after of a wound; the generals of Arginusae were put to death in 406 BC.

**`l'ordine dello Ostracismo`.** "The order of ostracism": `ordine`, order (glossary), here an institution. Ostracism, the Athenian vote by potsherds that exiled a citizen for ten years without charge, introduced after the fall of the tyranny; Machiavelli capitalises it, the English does not.

**`ogni altra violenza che contra i suoi Ottimati in varj tempi da quella Città fu fatt[a]`.** "Every other violence which at various times was done against its optimates by that city": `Ottimati`, optimates (D76). The 1824 prints `fatto`, which does not agree with `violenza`; emended `fatt[a]` from the 1554 edition (D295; NM-DISC-I28-D001). The English is the same either way.

**`Ed è verissimo quello che dicono questi scrittori della civiltà`.** "And it is most true, what these writers of civility say": the English extraposes, as the Italian does, so that the maxim stays attached to what the writers say, and Machiavelli endorses it rather than asserting it as his own. `verissimo` returns in `verissima coniettura` (.05), and both are "most true". `civiltà`, civility (glossary, I.2): civil life under laws, so "writers of civility" are writers on the civil life of cities. Machiavelli names none, and "these" points to no one named before.

**`i popoli mordono più fieramente poi ch'egli hanno ricuperata la libertà, che poi che l'hanno conservata`.** "Peoples bite more fiercely after they have recovered liberty than after they have conserved it": `egli`, the pleonastic subject, not rendered; `conservare`, conserve (D220). The double `poi che`, after, is kept as "after ... after".

**`Chi considererà adunque quanto è detto`.** "Whoever will consider, then, what has been said": `adunque`, then (D74). The 1554 edition has the present, `considera` (NM-DISC-I28-D005).

**`non biasimerà in questo Atene, né lauderà Roma; ma ne accuserà solo la necessità`.** "Will not blame Athens in this, nor praise Rome; but will accuse necessity alone for it": `biasimare`, blame (D173); `laudare`, praise (glossary); `accusare`, accuse (D159); `solo`, alone (D172). `ne`, for it, the ingratitude.

**`per la diversità degli accidenti che in questa Città nacquero`.** "Through the diversity of the accidents that arose in this city": `accidente`, accident (glossary), what befalls a city. The last city named is Rome, and in this chapter `quella` is always Athens (.03, .04, .05), so `questa` reads most naturally as Rome; but the accidents that made the difference befell Athens, and the diversity lies between the two. The English "this city" leaves it as open as the Italian. The 1554 edition reads the plural, `in queste città`, "in these cities"; escalated as a word at a letter the scans break and kept (D296; NM-DISC-I28-D002).

**`Perché si vedrà, chi considererà le cose sottilmente, che`.** "For it will be seen, whoever will consider things subtly, that": the impersonal `si vedrà` is joined to a personal `chi considererà`, and the English keeps the loose join rather than choose "he will see". The 1554 edition prints `si uerra`, most likely a misprint for `si uedra` (NM-DISC-I28-D005).

**`se a Roma fusse suta tolta la libertà come ad Atene`.** "If liberty had been taken away at Rome as at Athens": `suta`, the old participle of `essere`, "been".

**`non sarebbe stata Roma più pia verso i suoi cittadini, che si fusse quella`.** "Rome would not have been more pious towards its citizens than that one was": `pio`, pious, of duty owed, as in Latin *pietas*; `quella`, that one, Athens.

**`Di che si può fare verissima coniettura`.** "Of which a most true conjecture can be made": `coniettura`, conjecture, the cognate kept (register.md).

**`per quello che occorse`.** "Through what occurred": `occorrere`, occur (D01), the *currere* verb. It returns in the chapter's last words, of what will occur to Machiavelli.

**`contro a Collatino ed a Publio Valerio`.** "Against Collatinus and Publius Valerius": Lucius Tarquinius Collatinus, Lucretia's husband and consul with Brutus in the first year, who was made to abdicate and leave Rome because he bore the name of the Tarquins; and Publius Valerius, later Publicola, who was suspected of aiming at kingship when he built a house on the Velia and pulled it down. Livy 2.2 and 2.7. Livy's hill is the Velia; Machiavelli says the Caelian, a slip or another source. Livy records the suspicion, not a threat of exile: `fu ancora per essere fatto esule` sharpens the parallel with Collatinus.

**`ancora che si trovasse a liberare Roma`.** "Even though he had taken part in freeing Rome": `trovarsi a`, to be among those doing a thing; `ancora che`, even though (glossary).

**`per tenere il nome de' Tarquinj`.** "For holding the name of the Tarquins": `tenere`, hold, extended from the glossary's holding of a place or forces. Livy's Brutus asks Collatinus to take away the royal name with himself (2.2).

**`avendo solo dato di sé sospetto`.** "Having only given suspicion of himself": `solo` here is the adverb, "only"; D172's "alone" is for `solo` of a person or a cause, as `solo la necessità` (.04).

**`fu ancora per essere fatto esule`.** "Was also about to be made an exile": `essere per`, be about to (D45); `ancora`, also.

**`Talché si può stimare`.** "So that one can esteem": `talché`, so that (glossary); `stimare`, esteem, judge by estimate (D259 keeps "esteem" for it).

**`veduto quanto Roma fu in questi due sospettosa e severa`.** "Seeing how suspicious and severe Rome was in these two": the absolute participle; `in questi due`, in the cases of these two men.

**`che ella arebbe usata l'ingratitudine come Atene`.** "That it would have used ingratitude as Athens [did]": `arebbe`, the old conditional of `avere`; the verb of comparison is supplied in brackets (D53).

**`se dai suoi cittadini, come quella ne' primi tempi, ed innanzi allo augumento suo, fusse stata ingiuriata`.** "If by its citizens, as that one in its first times, and before its augmentation, it had been injured": the Italian holds the verb to the end, after the condition, the comparison and the time; the English keeps the order and the commas, and supplies no verb to `come quella`. `augumento`, augmentation (glossary); `ingiuriare`, injure, the verb of `ingiuria`. Whether `ne' primi tempi` belongs to Rome or to `quella`, Athens, the Italian does not settle; the commas set it with `come quella`, but Athens lost its liberty `nel suo più florido tempo` (.03), not in its first times, and Rome's first times are what Collatinus and Valerius have just shown, so the sense leans to Rome. `suo` is most naturally Rome's.

**`E per non avere a tornare più sopra questa materia della ingratitudine`.** "And so as not to have to return any more to this matter of ingratitude": `avere a` after "so as" is "have to" (D222, D261).

**`ne dirò quello ne occorrerà nel seguente capitolo`.** "I will say of it what will occur [to me] of it in the following chapter": the first `ne` is "of it", the matter; the second stands where a relative is wanted, with the relative understood. The 1554 edition reads `quello che occorrerà`, "what will occur"; the 1824 was read by one reader, construes as an elliptical relative and is kept (D297; NM-DISC-I28-D003). `occorrere`, occur (D01). The following chapter is I.29, `Quale sia più ingrato, o un Popolo, o un Principe`.
