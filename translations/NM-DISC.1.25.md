# Discourses I.25

*Whoever wishes to reform an ancient state in a free city, let him retain at least the shadow of the ancient ways.*

## NM-DISC.1.25.01

He who desires or who wishes to reform a state of a city, if he wishes it to be accepted, and to be able to maintain it with the satisfaction of each, is necessitated to retain at least the shadow of the ancient ways, so that it may not seem to the peoples to have changed order, even though in fact the new orders were altogether alien from the past ones: for the generality of men feeds itself as much on what seems as on what is; indeed many times they are moved more by the things that seem than by those that are.

## NM-DISC.1.25.02

For this cause the Romans, knowing this necessity in the beginning of their free living, having created in place of one king the two consuls, did not wish them to have more than twelve lictors, so as not to pass the number of those who ministered to the kings. Besides this, there being made in Rome an anniversary sacrifice, which could not be made except by the person of the king, and the Romans wishing that that people should not have to desire, through the absence of the kings, any thing of the ancient ones, [they] created a head of the said sacrifice, whom they called king *sacrificulus*, and subjected him to the supreme priest. So that that people by this path came to satisfy itself of that sacrifice, and never to have cause, through the failing of it, to desire the return of the kings.

## NM-DISC.1.25.03

And this must be observed by all those who wish to cancel an ancient living in a city, and reduce it to a new and free living. For, new things altering the minds of men, you must contrive that those alterations retain as much of the ancient as is possible; and if the magistracies vary from the ancient ones both in number and in authority and in time, that at least they retain the name. And this, as I have said, he must observe who wishes to order an absolute power, either by the path of a republic or of a kingdom; but he who wishes to make an absolute power, which is called tyranny by the authors, must renew every thing, as will be said in the following chapter.

## Notes

**The rubric.** `Chi vuole riformare uno Stato antico in una Città libera, ritenga almeno l'ombra de' modi antichi`. "Whoever wishes to reform an ancient state in a free city, let him retain at least the shadow of the ancient ways": `volere`, wish (D117); `stato`, state (D265); `modi`, ways (glossary), against `ordini`, orders, in the body. The rubric has `almeno`, the body `almanco`; both are "at least", a recorded collapse.

**`Colui che desidera o che vuole`.** "He who desires or who wishes": `desiderare`, desire, and `volere`, wish, kept apart (D117).

**`riformare uno stato d'una Città`.** "To reform a state of a city": the `stato` is the city's order of government, as in the rubric. `riformare`, reform: the word Guicciardini makes the hinge of his reply, against `mutare`, change.

**`a volere che sia accetto`.** "If he wishes it to be accepted": the infinitive `a volere` is conditional. `accetto`, accepted, of the new state.

**`con satisfazione di ciascuno`.** "With the satisfaction of each": `ciascuno`, each (D161). `satisfarsi`, satisfy itself, at .02, keeps the root.

**`è necessitato a ritenere`.** "Is necessitated to retain": `necessitato`, the participle of necessity, kept apart from `è necessario`, "it is necessary", and from `bisogna`, "it is required" (D260).

**`l'ombra almanco de' modi antichi`.** "At least the shadow of the ancient ways": `almanco` sits after `l'ombra` in the Italian and applies to it; the English puts "at least" before "the shadow", where its scope is the same.

**`acciò che ai Popoli non paja avere mutato ordine`.** "So that it may not seem to the peoples to have changed order": the subject of `avere mutato` is unexpressed: either the reformed state (the `lo` of `poterlo`), or, taking `paja` as impersonal, the peoples themselves, as in `mi pare avere`. The English "it" holds both. `ordine`, order (glossary), singular, then `gli ordini nuovi`, "the new orders".

**`ancora che in fatto gli ordini nuovi fussero al tutto alieni dai passati`.** "Even though in fact the new orders were altogether alien from the past ones": `ancora che`, even though (glossary). The 1554 edition reads `diversi`, "different", for `alieni` (NM-DISC-I25-D002).

**`l'universale degli uomini`.** "The generality of men": `universale` as a noun, the generality (glossary, I.16).

**`si pasce così di quel che pare, come di quello che è`.** "Feeds itself as much on what seems as on what is": `pascersi`, to feed oneself. The singular `l'universale` takes a plural verb in the next clause (`si muovono`), and the English follows: "itself", then "they". `parere`, seem, held through `pajono`, "seem", in the next clause.

**`anzi molte volte`.** "Indeed many times": `anzi` correcting upward after an affirmative, "indeed" (D147); not "on the contrary" (D135), which would reverse.

**`nel principio del loro vivere libero`.** "In the beginning of their free living": `vivere libero`, free living, as `vivere civile`, `vivere politico`.

**`avendo in cambio d'un Re creati i duoi Consoli`.** "Having created in place of one king the two consuls": the numerals are Machiavelli's point, one king, two consuls, and the twelve lictors that one king had; the Italian order is kept for that contrast (D263). `creare`, create, of magistrates, as FG I.3 (D89).

**`non vollono ch'egli avessero più che dodici Littori`.** "Did not wish them to have more than twelve lictors": `egli`, plural, "they", the consuls. Livy (2.1.8) has only one of the first consuls hold the fasces, Brutus first by his colleague's leave, so that the terror should not seem doubled; Machiavelli gives the twelve lictors and the reason in his own terms.

**`ministravano`.** "Ministered": attended, as the lictors attended the kings.

**`non avesse a desiderare`.** "Should not have to desire": `avere a` after a negative, "have to" (D261).

**`alcuna cosa dell'antiche`.** "Any thing of the ancient ones": the ancient things, the institutions of the kings.

**`crearono un Capo`.** "[They] created a head": the subject, the Romans, stands in the absolute phrase before it; the English supplies the pronoun in brackets (D53).

**`Re sacrificolo`.** "King *sacrificulus*": the *rex sacrificulus* or *rex sacrorum*, the priest created after the expulsion of the kings to perform the royal sacrifices, and set under the *pontifex maximus* so that the name of king should carry no power (Livy 2.2). The English translates `Re`, because the name of king is what the Romans kept, and leaves the Latin title in italic; Guicciardini's `re Sacrificulo` is rendered the same way.

**`lo sottomessono al sommo Sacerdote`.** "Subjected him to the supreme priest": `sommo`, supreme (D190); the *pontifex maximus*. The 1554 edition reads `al Sacerdote`, "to the priest", without `sommo` (NM-DISC-I25-D003).

**`Talmenteché`.** "So that": `talmente che`, as FG I.10 (glossary).

**`per questa via`.** "By this path": `via`, path (D131), and again at .03, `per via di Repubblica`.

**`venne a satisfarsi di quel sacrifizio`.** "Came to satisfy itself of that sacrifice": to have its fill of it.

**`per mancamento di esso`.** "Through the failing of it": `mancamento`, the noun of `mancare`, fail (D206).

**`la tornata de' Re`.** "The return of the kings".

**`si debbe osservare`.** "Must be observed": `dovere`, must, as `ti debbi`, `debbe osservare` and `debbe rinnovare` below.

**`scancellare uno antico vivere`.** "Cancel an ancient living": `scancellare`, the intensive of `cancellare`, cancel (D249); the English keeps one root.

**`Perché alterando le cose nuove le menti degli uomini`.** "For, new things altering the minds of men": the gerund, with its own subject, gives the cause of the precept; `Perché` links the precept to the sentence before. The English keeps the absolute construction, as at .02. `ridurre`, reduce, in its older sense, bring to, as FG I.10 `ridursi privato`.

**`ti debbi ingegnare`.** "You must contrive": the second person enters for the precept, as at NM I.23. `ingegnarsi`, contrive, as NM I.9.

**`ritengano più dell'antico sia possibile`.** "Retain as much of the ancient as is possible": the 1824 prints no `che` before `sia`, and both the 1824 reader and the comparative read it as elliptical; the 1554 edition has `che sia possibile`. Put to David and kept as printed (D270; NM-DISC-I25-D004). The sense is the same either way.

**`i Magistrati`.** "The magistracies": the offices, which vary in number, authority and time (D93).

**`che almeno ritengano il nome`.** "That at least they retain the name": the `che` resumes the precept after the condition. The Romans kept the name of king in the *rex sacrificulus*.

**`colui che vuole ordinare una potenza assoluta, o per via di Repubblica, o di Regno; ma quello che vuol fare una potestà assoluta`.** "He who wishes to order an absolute power, either by the path of a republic or of a kingdom; but he who wishes to make an absolute power": the Italian sets `ordinare` against `fare` and `potenza` against `potestà`. The edition renders both nouns "power" (D78, a recorded collapse), so in English the contrast is carried by order against make, and by republic or kingdom against tyranny; the facing Italian shows the second noun change. Whether to split the two here is an open question (O28). The 1554 edition omits the words from the first `assoluta` to the second, by an eye-skip, so that renewal of every thing is prescribed to whoever orders any absolute power (NM-DISC-I25-D001).

**`la quale dagli autori è chiamata Tirannide`.** "Which is called tyranny by the authors": `autori`, the writers, probably the ancient writers on forms of government; `tirannide`, tyranny (glossary). The Italian puts the agent first; the order does no work here, so English order (D263).

**`E questo, come ho detto, debbe osservare colui che vuole`.** "And this, as I have said, he must observe who wishes": the object first and the subject last, as the Italian, because the two subjects are the contrast the sentence turns on, "he who wishes to order ... but he who wishes to make" (D263).

**`come nel seguente capitolo si dirà`.** "As will be said in the following chapter": I.26, whose rubric is that a new prince must make every thing new. Guicciardini replies to it too.
