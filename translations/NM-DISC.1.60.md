# Discourses I.60

*How the consulship and any other magistracy in Rome was given without respect of age.*

## NM-DISC.1.60.01

It is seen by the order of the history that the Roman republic, after the consulship came into the plebs, granted it to its citizens without respect of age or of blood; even though respect of age never was in Rome, but one always went to find *virtù*, whether it were in [a] young [man] or in [an] old [one]. Which is seen by the witness of Valerius Corvinus, who was made consul at twenty-three years; and the said Valerius, speaking to his soldiers, said that the consulship *erat praemium virtutis non sanguinis*. Which thing, whether it was well considered or not, would be [matter] for much disputing.

## NM-DISC.1.60.02

And as to blood, this was granted by necessity, and that necessity which was in Rome would be in every city that wished to make the effects that Rome made, as has been said another time; because one cannot give men hardship without reward, nor can one take away the hope of attaining the reward without danger. And therefore early it was fitting that the plebs should have hope of having the consulship, and on this hope it nourished itself [for] a time without having it. Afterwards the hope was not enough, so that it was fitting that one should come to the effect. But the city that does not employ its plebs in any glorious thing can treat it after its own way, as was disputed elsewhere; but that [city] which wishes to make what Rome made is not to make this distinction.

## NM-DISC.1.60.03

And granted that it be so, that of time has no reply, but rather is necessary; because in choosing a young man to a degree that has need of a prudence of [an] old [man], it is fitting, the multitude having to choose him, that some most noble deed of his make him arrive at that degree. And when a young man is of so much *virtù* that he has made himself known in some notable thing, it would be a most damaging thing that the city could not avail itself of him then, and that it should have to wait until there had grown old with him that vigour of spirit and that promptness of which at that age his fatherland could avail itself; as Rome availed itself of Valerius Corvinus, of Scipio, of Pompey, and of many others who triumphed most young.

## Notes

**The rubric.** Two lines in italic: `Consolato`, consulship, the office; `Consolo`, consul, the man (.01), as `Consoli`, consuls (names.md); Guicciardini's forms are `consulato`, `consule` (glossary). `qualunque altro Magistrato`, any other magistracy, the office (D93); the chapter speaks only of the consulship. `rispetto di età`, respect of age (glossary, `rispetto`). The reply's heading in the witness repeats this rubric; it is Canestrini's setting, not Guicciardini's (D264). The chapter closes Book I; the 1824 prints FINE DEL LIBRO PRIMO below it.

**`E' si vede per l'ordine della istoria, come la Repubblica romana, poiché il Consolato venne nella Plebe`.** "It is seen by the order of the history that the Roman republic, after the consulship came into the plebs": `E'`, the impersonal subject; `come`, "that", introducing what is seen, as `dico come` (NM I.58); `istoria`, history (glossary); `poiché` temporal, after. `Plebe`, plebs (glossary). The 1554 reads `nelle plebe` (NM-DISC-I60-D002, D409); the 1824's singular agrees with `Plebe`. The plebs won the consulship by the Licinian-Sextian laws, conventionally dated 367 BC (Livy 6.42).

**`concesse quello ai suoi cittadini senza rispetto di età o di sangue`.** "Granted it to its citizens without respect of age or of blood": `concedere`, grant, as `fu concesso` (.02). `sangue`, blood, birth: the chapter's two respects, taken in turn, blood at .02 and age at .03.

**`ancora che il rispetto della età mai non fusse in Roma, ma sempre si andò a trovare la virtù, o in giovane o in vecchio che la fusse`.** "Even though respect of age never was in Rome, but one always went to find *virtù*, whether it were in [a] young [man] or in [an] old [one]": `ancora che`, even though (glossary); it corrects the scope of `poiché`: the disregard of age is not dated from the plebs' consulship but was always Rome's, and only blood waited. `mai non`, never; `si andò a trovare`, impersonal, one went to find. *virtù* untranslated (D16). `la` is *virtù*. Guicciardini answers this clause: the age ban was in Rome, at least later (Guicciardini .01).

**`Il che si vede per il testimone di Valerio Corvino, che fu fatto Consolo nelli ventitré anni`.** "Which is seen by the witness of Valerius Corvinus, who was made consul at twenty-three years": `testimone`, witness, here the evidence his case gives, not a witness in the edition's sense of a printed source; the 1554 reads `testimonio`. Machiavelli's Valerius Corvinus is Livy's Marcus Valerius Corvus, consul in the year conventionally dated 348 BC; the Latin Library text of Livy (thelatinlibrary.com/livy/liv.7.shtml, consulted 2026-11-19) gives his age at the election as twenty-three (7.26) and in his own speech (7.40). The 1554 prints the age as a numeral, `XXIII.` (NM-DISC-I60-D003).

**`e Valerio detto parlando ai suoi soldati disse, come il Consolato erat praemium virtutis non sanguinis`.** "And the said Valerius, speaking to his soldiers, said that the consulship *erat praemium virtutis non sanguinis*": "was the reward of *virtù*, not of blood". The editor compares Livy 7.32, where Corvus tells his soldiers that the consulship is now the reward not of birth but of *virtus*: the Latin Library text reads `nec generis, ut ante, sed virtutis est praemium`, with `generis`, birth, and the present tense. Printed in italic in the 1824; the 1554 sets marginal inverted commas beside it. `praemium` answers the Italian `premio`, reward (glossary), twice in .02.

**`La qual cosa se fu bene considerata, o no, sarebbe da disputare assai`.** "Which thing, whether it was well considered or not, would be [matter] for much disputing": `essere da` + infinitive, the matter for a doing (D53 for the bracket). `disputare`, dispute, as `si disputò` (.02). The question is taken up at once, blood at .02 and age at .03; `La qual cosa` may be the Roman practice or Valerius' saying, and the Italian does not say which.

**`fu concesso questo per necessità, e quella necessità che fu in Roma, sarebbe in ogni Città che volesse fare gli effetti che fece Roma`.** "This was granted by necessity, and that necessity which was in Rome would be in every city that wished to make the effects that Rome made": `fare gli effetti`, make the effects (glossary, `fare`), kept with its object, as NM I.58 "make that same effect", and held in `quel che fece Roma`, "what Rome made", below, so that the paragraph's two conditions keep one verb; `effetto` again in `si venisse allo effetto`, came to the effect, the hope made actual. The 1554 reads `che fece in Roma` (NM-DISC-I60-D002, D409). `come altra volta si è detto`: as has been said another time; the editor takes the reference to be NM I.6, where a republic that would grow as Rome grew must arm and employ its plebs.

**`perché e' non si può dare agli uomini disagio senza premio, né si può torre la speranza di conseguire il premio senza pericolo`.** "Because one cannot give men hardship without reward, nor can one take away the hope of attaining the reward without danger": `disagio`, hardship; the clause is general, and the editor reads it here of the plebs' service in war; `torre`, take away (D131); `conseguire`, attain (glossary); `pericolo`, danger (glossary).

**`E però a buona ora convenne che la Plebe avesse speranza di avere il Consolato, e di questa speranza si nutrì un tempo senza averlo`.** "And therefore early it was fitting that the plebs should have hope of having the consulship, and on this hope it nourished itself [for] a time without having it": `però`, therefore (D22); `a buona ora`, early, in good time; `convenne`, it was fitting (D85), and again `che e' convenne` in the next sentence. The 1554 omits the article before `Consolato` and the reflexive `si` (NM-DISC-I60-D002, D409). The plebs could stand for the consular tribunate from the year conventionally dated 445 BC, before the consulship itself (Livy 4.6), which the editor suggests is the hope that fed them; NM I.47 treats the episode.

**`Dipoi non bastò la speranza, che e' convenne che si venisse allo effetto`.** "Afterwards the hope was not enough, so that it was fitting that one should come to the effect": `dipoi`, afterwards (D187); `bastare`, be enough (D205); `si venisse`, subjunctive under `convenne`, "should come", as `avesse` "should have" before it. `che` is taken as consecutive; after the negative it can also be read explicatively, the second clause saying what replaced hope.

**`Ma la Città che non adopera la sua Plebe ad alcuna cosa gloriosa, la può trattare a suo modo, come altrove si disputò`.** "But the city that does not employ its plebs in any glorious thing can treat it after its own way, as was disputed elsewhere": `adoperare`, employ; `trattare`, treat; `a suo modo`, as it pleases, "after its own way" (glossary, `modo`). The dispute is presumably NM I.6, Venice and Sparta against Rome.

**`ma quella che vuol fare quel che fece Roma, non ha a fare questa distinzione`.** "But that which wishes to do what Rome did is not to make this distinction": `avere a` with the negative. D261 gives "have to" after a negative, but "does not have to make" would turn the sentence into a permission; the sense is a prohibition, what such a city may not do, and the destinative "is not to" is kept, with the amendment of D261 put to David (O40). "This distinction" is of blood. The 1554 reads `ha a fare`, without the negative, which reverses the sense: such a city would have to make the distinction. The 1824 construes with the argument and is kept (NM-DISC-I60-D001, D409).

**`E dato che così sia, quella del tempo non ha replica, anzi è necessaria`.** "And granted that it be so, that of time has no reply, but rather is necessary": `quella del tempo`, that of time, age: `quella` has no antecedent that makes the sense run. Taken with `questa distinzione`, it would make the distinction of age necessary, against what follows; the sense needs the disregard of age, as in .01 `concesse ... senza rispetto di età`. The English leaves it as open as the Italian. `replica`, reply, an answer that could be made against it; `anzi` after a negative, "but rather" (D90).

**`perché nello eleggere un giovane in un grado che abbia bisogno d'una prudenza di vecchio, conviene, avendolo ad eleggere la moltitudine`.** "Because in choosing a young man to a degree that has need of a prudence of [an] old [man], it is fitting, the multitude having to choose him": `eleggere`, choose (glossary), the multitude's choice of magistrates, as NM I.58 .08; `grado`, degree (glossary), here of office; `bisogno`, need (glossary); `prudenza`, prudence (glossary); `conviene`, it is fitting (D85); `avere a` after a gerund, "having to" (D237): the gerund gives the reason, that the choice lies with the multitude, so a deed it can see must carry the young man; it does not mean the multitude is bound to choose him. The 1554 reads `hauendouelo` (NM-DISC-I60-D002, D409).

**`che a quel grado lo faccia pervenire qualche sua nobilissima azione`.** "That some most noble deed of his make him arrive at that degree": the order normalised, as exposition (D263). `azione` of a single deed done by a man, "deed": "action" is `fazione`'s (D341), D358's "proceedings" is scoped to narrated doings, D348's "functions" and D389's "public business" to offices.

**`che si sia fatto in qualche cosa notabile conoscere`.** "That he has made himself known in some notable thing": `farsi conoscere`, make oneself known, its parts split in the Italian by the phrase. `dannosissima`, most damaging (D106).

**`che la Città non se ne potesse valere allora, e che l'avesse ad aspettare che fusse invecchiato con lui quel vigore dell'animo, e quella prontezza`.** "That the city could not avail itself of him then, and that it should have to wait until there had grown old with him that vigour of spirit and that promptness": `l'avesse`: `la`, the city, as subject (as .01 `che la fusse`), not "wait for him". The delayed subject is kept, since it is heavy and carries its relative, `della quale`, which agrees with `prontezza`. `valersi di`, avail oneself of (D162), twice more in the sentence; `avere a` with necessity, "have to" (D261); `animo`, spirit (glossary); `prontezza`, promptness, as FG I.58. `in quella età`, at that age, the young man's, not an era. `patria`, fatherland (glossary). The 1554 reads `ella citta` for `la Città` (NM-DISC-I60-D003).

**`come si valse Roma di Valerio Corvino, di Scipione, di Pompeo, e di molti altri che trionfarono giovanissimi`.** "As Rome availed itself of Valerius Corvinus, of Scipio, of Pompey, and of many others who triumphed most young": `Scipione`, by the editor's identification Scipio Africanus, as NM I.58 (names.md); `Pompeo`, Pompey (names.md). The triumphs are Machiavelli's claim; the commentary gives the record. `giovanissimi`, most young, the superlative kept, as `nobilissima`, `dannosissima`.
