# Discourses III.24

## NM-DISC.3.24.01

If one considers well the proceeding of the Roman republic, one will see two things to have been the cause of the dissolution of that republic; the one was the contentions that arose from the Agrarian law; the other the prolongation of commands: which things, if they had been known well from the beginning, and due remedies made for them, free living would have been longer, and, it may be, more quiet.

## NM-DISC.3.24.02

And albeit, as to the prolongation of command, one does not see that any tumult ever arose in Rome, nonetheless one sees in fact how much that authority which the citizens took through such deliberations did harm to the city. And if the other citizens to whom the magistracy was prorogued had been wise and good, as L. Quinctius was, one would not have incurred this inconvenience.

## NM-DISC.3.24.03

His goodness is of a notable example, because, a convention of accord having been made between the plebs and the Senate, and the plebs having prolonged the command to the tribunes for a year, judging them apt to be able to resist the ambition of the nobles, the Senate, out of rivalry with the plebs, and so as not to seem less than it, wished to prolong the consulship to L. Quinctius; who wholly denied this deliberation, saying that bad examples wanted [one] to seek to extinguish them, not to increase them with another worse example; and he wished that new consuls be made. Which goodness and prudence, if it had been in all the Roman citizens, would not have let that usage of prolonging the magistracies be introduced, and from that one would not have come to the prolongation of commands: which thing in time ruined that republic.

## NM-DISC.3.24.04

The first to whom command was prorogued was P. Philo, who, being in camp at the city of Palaeopolis, and the end of his consulship coming, and it seeming to the Senate that he had that victory in hand, they did not send him the successor, but made him proconsul; so that he was the first proconsul. Which thing, even though moved by the Senate for public utility, was that which in time made Rome servile.

## NM-DISC.3.24.05

For the more the Romans went to a distance with arms, the more such prorogation seemed necessary to them, and the more they used it. Which thing made two inconveniences. The one, that a lesser number of men were exercised in commands, and one came through this to restrict reputation to few; the other, that a citizen, staying much time commander of an army, gained it for himself, and made it his partisan; because that army in time forgot the Senate, and acknowledged that head. Through this Sulla and Marius could find soldiers who would follow them against the public good; through this Caesar could occupy the fatherland.

## NM-DISC.3.24.06

That if the Romans had never prolonged the magistracies and the commands, if they had not come so soon to so much power, and if their acquisitions had been later, they would also have come later into servitude.

## Notes

**The rubric.** `La prolungazione degli Imperj fece serva Roma.` "The prolongation of commands made Rome servile": `prolungazione`, prolongation, the chapter's noun, with `prolungare`, prolong (.03, .06), kept apart from `prorogare`, `prorogazione`, prorogue, prorogation (.02, .04, .05), the older English sense of the Latin *prorogare*, to extend a term (D512); `Imperj`, commands (D124), the magistrate's *imperium*, here the command of armies; `serva`, servile (D388), in the old sense, in servitude, as `servitù`, servitude, at the chapter's close (.06). The 1554 sets the same sentence as its heading; Canestrini sets it over Guicciardini's reply with `degl'imperii` (NM-DISC-III24-D005; D511).

**`Se si considera bene il procedere della Repubblica romana, si vedrà due cose essere state cagione della risoluzione di quella Repubblica`.** "If one considers well the proceeding of the Roman republic, one will see two things to have been the cause of the dissolution of that republic": `procedere`, proceeding (glossary), the verbal noun; `si vedrà` with the infinitive, "one will see two things to have been", the accusative and infinitive kept; `cagione`, cause (glossary), singular for the two things, as printed; `risoluzione`, dissolution (D512), a sense split from the glossary's `resoluzione`, resolution (FG II.12): the breaking up of the republic, not a decision.

**`l'una furono le contenzioni che nacquero dalla legge Agraria; l'altra la prolungazione degl'Imperj`.** "The one was the contentions that arose from the Agrarian law; the other the prolongation of commands": `furono`, plural with its complement, "was" in English; `contenzioni`, contentions (D512); `nascere`, arise (D04); `legge Agraria`, the Agrarian law, the subject of I.37 (the editor's cross-reference).

**`le quali cose se fussero state conosciute bene da principio, e fattivi debiti rimedj`.** "Which things, if they had been known well from the beginning, and due remedies made for them": the relative is the subject of the protasis, and the apodosis passes to a new subject, `il vivere libero`, as printed; `da principio`, from the beginning (D454): both cold readers saw a round dot over the `a`, not an accent, and preferred `da`; only `da` construes, and the 1554 prints it (D398, D508); `fattivi`, made for them, `vi` the things; `debiti rimedj`, due remedies, without the article; the 1554 has `i debiti rimedij`, the due remedies (NM-DISC-III24-D001; D509); `rimedio`, remedy (glossary).

**`sarebbe stato il vivere libero più lungo, e per avventura più quieto`.** "Free living would have been longer, and, it may be, more quiet": the fronted verb set in plain order (D263); `vivere libero`, free living (glossary); `per avventura`, it may be (D512), kept apart from `forse`, perhaps.

**`E benché quanto alla prolungazione dell'Imperio, non si vegga che in Roma nascesse mai alcuno tumulto`.** "And albeit, as to the prolongation of command, one does not see that any tumult ever arose in Rome": `benché`, albeit (D31); `quanto a`, as to; `Imperio`, command, singular, as the rubric's plural; `tumulto`, tumult (glossary), the word of I.4 for the contentions of plebs and senate.

**`nondimeno si vede in fatto, quanto nuocé alla Città quella autorità che i cittadini per tali deliberazioni presero`.** "Nonetheless one sees in fact how much that authority which the citizens took through such deliberations did harm to the city": the fronted verb set in plain order (D263), which keeps the relative beside `autorità`; `nondimeno`, nonetheless (D45); `in fatto`, in fact, in the deed, against the want of tumult; `nuocere`, do harm (D106); `nuocé` is printed with a grave accent, which both cold readers saw clearly, `nuocè`; the accented form is a past, `nocé`, `nocque`, as `presero` requires, set acute by DEC-009 clause 1; the English takes the past, "did harm"; the 1554 prints `nuoce`; `autorità`, authority (glossary); `deliberazioni`, deliberations (glossary), the decisions that prorogued commands.

**`E se gli altri cittadini, a chi era prorogato il Magistrato, fussero stati savj e buoni, come fu L. Quinzio, non si sarebbe incorso in questo inconveniente`.** "And if the other citizens to whom the magistracy was prorogued had been wise and good, as L. Quinctius was, one would not have incurred this inconvenience": `a chi`, to whom; `prorogare`, prorogue (D512); `Magistrato`, magistracy (D93), the office; `savj`, wise (glossary `savio`); `come fu`, as ... was; the 1554 omits `fu` (NM-DISC-III24-D002; D509); `incorrere`, incur (D192), a *currere* compound; `inconveniente`, inconvenience (D133), the older sense, a drawback that follows. Lucius Quinctius Cincinnatus, consul in 460 BC, whose poverty is the subject of the next chapter, III.25 (Livy 3.19-21, the editor's reference; names.md); not III.19's Titus Quinctius Capitolinus.

**`La bontà del quale è d'uno esempio notabile`.** "His goodness is of a notable example": `bontà`, goodness (D512), with `buoni`, good (.02); `esempio`, example (glossary); `notabile`, notable.

**`perché sendosi fatto tra la Plebe ed il Senato convenzione d'accordo`.** "Because, a convention of accord having been made between the plebs and the Senate": `perché`, because; `convenzione`, convention (D512), with `accordo`, accord (D452); `Plebe`, plebs (glossary); `Senato`, the Senate (names.md, D118).

**`ed avendo la Plebe prolungato in un anno l'Imperio ai Tribuni, giudicandogli atti a poter resistere all'ambizione dei Nobili`.** "And the plebs having prolonged the command to the tribunes for a year, judging them apt to be able to resist the ambition of the nobles": `in un anno`, for a year, a further year; `ai Tribuni`, to the tribunes, the dative kept, as `a L. Quinzio` below, the two prolongations built alike; `Imperio`, command, Machiavelli's one word for the office prolonged, though the tribunes of the plebs held no *imperium* in the Roman sense (the editor's note); `Tribuni`, tribunes (names.md); `giudicare`, judge (D374); `atti`, apt (D512); `ambizione`, ambition; `Nobili`, nobles (glossary).

**`volle il Senato per gara della Plebe, e per non parere da meno di lei, prolungare il Consolato a L. Quinzio`.** "The Senate, out of rivalry with the plebs, and so as not to seem less than it, wished to prolong the consulship to L. Quinctius": the fronted verb set in plain order (D263); `per gara di`, out of rivalry with (D512), the first `per` causal, the second final; `da meno di`, less than; `lei`, it, the plebs; `Consolato`, consulship (D410); `prolungare ... a`, prolong to, the dative kept.

**`il quale al tutto negò questa deliberazione`.** "Who wholly denied this deliberation": `al tutto`, wholly; `negare`, deny (D512), kept apart from `recusare`, refuse (D496).

**`dicendo che i cattivi esempj si volevano cercare di spegnerli, non di accrescerli con un altro più cattivo esempio`.** "Saying that bad examples wanted [one] to seek to extinguish them, not to increase them with another worse example": `volere` of a requirement, want (D148), the subject the bad examples, resumed by `li`; "[one]" supplied (D53); `cattivo`, bad (D03), `più cattivo`, worse; `spegnere`, extinguish (glossary); `accrescere`, increase (D69).

**`e volle si facessero nuovi Consoli`.** "And he wished that new consuls be made": `volere`, wish (D117); the subjunctive without "should" (D348).

**`La qual bontà e prudenza se fusse stata in tutt'i cittadini romani, non arebbe lasciata introdurre quella consuetudine di prolungare i Magistrati`.** "Which goodness and prudence, if it had been in all the Roman citizens, would not have let that usage of prolonging the magistracies be introduced": the singular verb for the pair, as printed; `prudenza`, prudence (glossary); `arebbe`, would have, the printed form of `avrebbe`, read by both cold readers; `lasciare`, let, with the infinitive; `introdurre`, introduce (glossary); `consuetudine`, usage (D41).

**`e da quella non si sarebbe venuto alla prolungazione degl'Imperj: la qual cosa col tempo rovinò quella Repubblica`.** "And from that one would not have come to the prolongation of commands: which thing in time ruined that republic": `rovinare`, ruin (D488).

**`Il primo a chi fu prorogato l'Imperio fu P. Filone`.** "The first to whom command was prorogued was P. Philo": `prorogare`, prorogue; the 1554 reads `allongato`, lengthened (NM-DISC-III24-D003; D509). Quintus Publilius Philo, consul in 327 BC, whose command before Palaeopolis was prorogued in 326 BC so that he could finish the war as proconsul (Livy 8.23 and 8.26, the editor's reference); Machiavelli's `P.` is printed in both 1824 and 1554, where Livy has `Q.` (names.md).

**`il quale essendo a campo alla Città di Palepoli, e venendo la fine del suo Consolato, e parendo al Senato ch'egli avesse in mano quella vittoria, non gli mandarono il successore, ma lo fecero Proconsolo`.** "Who, being in camp at the city of Palaeopolis, and the end of his consulship coming, and it seeming to the Senate that he had that victory in hand, they did not send him the successor, but made him proconsul": the relative `il quale` is not resumed, and the subject passes to an unnamed "they", as printed; `essere a campo a`, be in camp at, besieging; `Palepoli`, Palaeopolis (names.md), the old city beside Naples; `avere in mano`, have in hand; `Proconsolo`, proconsul (D512), lower case as an office.

**`talché fu il primo Proconsolo. La qual cosa, ancora che mossa dal Senato per utilità pubblica, fu quella che con il tempo fece serva Roma`.** "So that he was the first proconsul. Which thing, even though moved by the Senate for public utility, was that which in time made Rome servile": `talché`, so that (glossary); `ancora che`, even though (D31); `muovere`, move (D452); `utilità`, utility (D472); `serva`, servile, the rubric's word.

**`Perché quanto più i Romani si discostarono con le armi, tanto più pareva loro tale prorogazione necessaria, e più l'usarono`.** "For the more the Romans went to a distance with arms, the more such prorogation seemed necessary to them, and the more they used it": `Perché`, for, opening the period, `perché`, because, within it (below); `quanto più ... tanto più`, the more ... the more; `discostarsi`, go to a distance (D444); `armi`, arms; `prorogazione`, prorogation (D512); `usare`, use (D313).

**`La qual cosa fece due inconvenienti`.** "Which thing made two inconveniences": `fare`, make (D410).

**`L'uno, che meno numero di uomini si esercitarono negl'Imperj, e si venne per questo a ristringere la riputazione in pochi`.** "The one, that a lesser number of men were exercised in commands, and one came through this to restrict reputation to few": `meno numero`, a lesser number; `esercitarsi`, be exercised (D512), the reflexive taken as the passive; `ristringere ... in`, restrict ... to (D480, D512), transitive here, `ristringersi`, draw together, staying D58's; `riputazione`, reputation (glossary).

**`l'altro, che stando un cittadino assai tempo Comandatore d'uno esercito, se lo guadagnava, e facevaselo partigiano`.** "The other, that a citizen, staying much time commander of an army, gained it for himself, and made it his partisan": `assai tempo`, much time (D312); `Comandatore`, commander (D512), lower case; `guadagnare`, gain (glossary); `partigiano`, partisan (D218), of the army.

**`perché quello esercito col tempo dimenticava il Senato, e riconosceva quello Capo`.** "Because that army in time forgot the Senate, and acknowledged that head": `riconoscere`, acknowledge (D283); `Capo`, head (glossary).

**`Per questo Silla e Mario poterono trovare soldati che contro al bene pubblico li seguitassero; per questo Cesare potette occupare la patria`.** "Through this Sulla and Marius could find soldiers who would follow them against the public good; through this Caesar could occupy the fatherland": `per questo` ×2, through this, the anaphora kept; `seguitare`, follow (glossary), the subjunctive of the purpose and kind, "who would follow"; `bene pubblico`, public good; `occupare`, occupy (glossary); `patria`, fatherland (glossary). Sulla, Marius and Caesar as names.md.

**`Che se mai i Romani non avessero prolungati li Magistrati e gl'Imperj, se non venivano sì tosto a tanta potenza, e se fussero stati più tardi gli acquisti loro, sarebbero ancora venuti più tardi nella servitù`.** "That if the Romans had never prolonged the magistracies and the commands, if they had not come so soon to so much power, and if their acquisitions had been later, they would also have come later into servitude": `Che`, that, opening the period, as printed; `se mai ... non`, if ... never; three conditions, the second in the imperfect indicative, the ordinary Tuscan counterfactual, rendered with the other two as past unreal, and one apodosis. The 1554 has no second `se`, so that `non venivano` is the apodosis of the first condition, "they would not have come so soon to so much power", and the second condition begins a new member (NM-DISC-III24-D004; D509); the 1824 is kept and translated as printed. `sì tosto`, so soon; `potenza`, power (D06); `acquisti`, acquisitions (glossary); `ancora`, also (D472); `servitù`, servitude (glossary).
