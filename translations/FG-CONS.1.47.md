# Consideration on chapter XLVII of Book I

*Men, even though they deceive themselves in generals, in particulars do not deceive themselves.*

## FG-CONS.1.47.01

What the Discourse says, that men more readily deceive themselves in generals than in particulars, can be said in another way, that experience many times undeceives men of what they have imagined before they put a hand into the wound; because it is no marvel that whoever did not know the particulars of things should change sentence when later he has known them and seen them in the face; and the example of the Florentines tends to this, who, not having in the piazzas that notice, nor seeing those advices which later they saw in the palace, were readily of an opinion different from the truth. One can also consider in the example of the Romans that it seemed to the people a thing unworthy and disgraceful that generally all should be incapable of the honours, and that, it seeming to them that they had acquired much in attaining to be able to be eligible for the magistracy of consular power, they remained in part vented, and abstained from choosing those not fit, as those who had not fought for the particular ambition of ascending to that degree, but only to remove from themselves that infamy, that the whole plebs should be prohibited by the laws from partaking of the honours; and therefore Livy says well: “*contenta eo quod sui ratio habita esset.*”

## FG-CONS.1.47.02

The other conclusion of the Discourse, that the people deceives itself less in the distribution of honours and of magistracies than in other things, I believe it is true, and the reason is at hand, because it is a matter that is more readily known; and in this case the judgment of the people is founded not upon the notice it may have of itself of the worth of a citizen, but upon that universal opinion which arises from the length of time and from the experience which this and that particular [man] have had of him. I do not accept that in this the people does not deceive itself, or at least [deceives itself] at rarer times than the few do, because the people governs itself in this judgment not with particular notice, but with universal opinions, nor does it examine or distinguish subtly, in such a way that it deceives itself often, above all in those choices of which few are capable; it believes false rumours, it is moved by light foundations, and in effect, as to ignorance, it is much more dangerous than the judgment of few.

## Notes

**The rubric.** Canestrini's setting of the chapter title: Machiavelli's rubric with `si ingannino` and `si ingannano` for `s'ingannino`, `s'ingannano`, and `nei` for `ne'`. It is heading apparatus, not Guicciardini's (D32). The two readers split at `ne'`/`nei`, read with the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-I47-D001, D366); both spellings are the same word.

**`Quello che dice il Discorso, che più facilmente gli uomini si ingannano ne' generali che ne' particulari`.** "What the Discourse says, that men more readily deceive themselves in generals than in particulars": `il Discorso`, the Discourse, Guicciardini's name for the chapter he answers; `facilmente`, readily (D27); `ingannarsi`, deceive oneself (D334), Machiavelli's verb. He restates the rubric as a comparison (`più facilmente ... che`), which is Machiavelli's own form in the body (`assai ... non tanto`, Machiavelli .02), not the rubric's absolute. `generali`, `particulari`, the rubric's nouns, "generals" and "particulars".

**`si può dire in uno altro modo, che la esperienza sganna molte volte gli uomini di quello che s'hanno immaginato innanzi mettino mano nella piaga`.** "Can be said in another way, that experience many times undeceives men of what they have imagined before they put a hand into the wound": `si può dire`, it can be said, the impersonal of the restatement; `modo`, way (glossary); `sgannare`, undeceive, Machiavelli's `isgannare` of the chapter's last line; `molte volte`, many times; `innanzi`, before (D334). The image is kept as printed; no source is named, and none is supplied here. The two readers doubted the mark after `piaga` and preferred a semicolon, which the 1933 edition prints (D369).

**`perché non è maraviglia che chi non sapeva e' particulari delle cose, muti sentenza quando poi gli ha saputi e veduti in viso`.** "Because it is no marvel that whoever did not know the particulars of things should change sentence when later he has known them and seen them in the face": `sapere`, know, here and `saputi`, a recorded collapse with `conoscere`, `cognoscere` (D374). `sentenza`, sentence, an opinion pronounced (glossary); `poi`, later (D250); `in viso`, in the face. Both readers read a colon after `viso` with doubt; the semicolon is the 1933 edition's (D369).

**`e a questo tende lo esemplo de' Fiorentini, e' quali non avendo nelle piazze quella notizia, né vedendo quegli avvisi che poi vedevano in palazzo, erano facilmente di opinione diversa dalla verità`.** "And the example of the Florentines tends to this, who, not having in the piazzas that notice, nor seeing those advices which later they saw in the palace, were readily of an opinion different from the truth": Machiavelli's Florentine case (Machiavelli .05), whose piazza and palace come from its proverb. `notizia`, notice, in the older sense of information held, three times in the reply, kept apart from Machiavelli's `cognizione`, knowledge (Machiavelli .05) (D373); `avvisi`, advices, reports; `diverso`, different (D348). Canestrini's note at `verità`, on the palace and the proverb, is his and is not carried.

**`Si può anche nello esemplo de' Romani considerare`.** "One can also consider in the example of the Romans": `considerare`, consider (glossary); the act is offered (`si può`), not asserted. The example is Machiavelli's tribunes with consular power (Machiavelli .01-.02).

**`che al populo pareva cosa indegna e vituperosa che generalmente tutti fussino incapaci degli onori`.** "That it seemed to the people a thing unworthy and disgraceful that generally all should be incapable of the honours": `indegno`, unworthy, Machiavelli's word for the men the Capuans named (Machiavelli .04), here the exclusion; `vituperoso`, disgraceful, in the root of `vituperio`, disgrace (D26, D373); `generalmente`, generally: the grievance is the general exclusion; `incapace`, incapable, answered by `capaci` in .02.

**`e che parendogli avere acquistato assai a conseguire di potere essere abili al magistrato di potestà consulare, restassino in parte sfogati`.** "And that, it seeming to them that they had acquired much in attaining to be able to be eligible for the magistracy of consular power, they remained in part vented": `assai` after a verb, "much" (D348); `abile`, eligible (D374); `potestà`, power (D78), the tribunes `con potestà Consolare` of Machiavelli .01; `sfogare`, vent (D149).

**`e si astenessino da eleggere e' non idonei`.** "And abstained from choosing those not fit": `eleggere`, choose (glossary), not "elect"; `e'`, the plural article (D35); `idoneo`, fit (D374).

**`come quelli che non avessino combattuto per la ambizione particulare di ascendere a quello grado, ma solo per levarsi quella infamia`.** "As those who had not fought for the particular ambition of ascending to that degree, but only to remove from themselves that infamy": `particulare`, particular, here of private ambition against the plebs as a whole; `ascendere`, ascend, Machiavelli's verb for the popular man raised to the supreme magistracy (Machiavelli .05); `solo`, only (D298); `levarsi` with an object, remove from oneself, where D348's "rise" is the intransitive (D373).

**`che la plebe tutta fussi proibita dalle leggi di participare degli onori`.** "That the whole plebs should be prohibited by the laws from partaking of the honours": `participare di`, partake of: D28's "impart" is the verb with a direct object, whatever its spelling (D373).

**`e però bene dice Livio: « contenta eo quod sui ratio habita esset. »`** "And therefore Livy says well": `però`, therefore (D22), consecutive on the account just given. The Latin is left as Canestrini prints it, in italic within guillemets: "content with this, that account had been taken of it." The Latin Library text of Livy 4.6 (thelatinlibrary.com/livy/liv.4.shtml, consulted 2026-10-22) reads `contentus eo quod ratio habita plebeiorum esset`, of the people (`populus`) that created the tribunes all patricians; Guicciardini's `contenta` and `sui` make the plebs the subject. Machiavelli's `Fu contenta a questo la Plebe` (Machiavelli .01) is the same verb. Both readers doubted the mark after `Livio` and preferred a colon, which the 1933 edition prints (D369).

**`L'altra conclusione del Discorso, che manco si inganni il populo nella distribuzione degli onori e de' magistrati che nell'altre cose, credo sia vero`.** "The other conclusion of the Discourse, that the people deceives itself less in the distribution of honours and of magistracies than in other things, I believe it is true": `credo`, I believe (glossary), his assent; `manco`, less. The conclusion as he states it is comparative, the people against itself in other things; Machiavelli's comparison with the few is taken up separately below. `vero` is neuter: what he believes true is the proposition, "it", not the conclusion as such, with which the 1933 edition's `vera` would agree (FG-CONS-I47-D003, D368).

**`e la ragione è in pronto, perché è materia che più facilmente si cognosce`.** "And the reason is at hand, because it is a matter that is more readily known": `in pronto`, at hand, `pronto` prompt (D298), so that "ready" stays off it; `cognoscere` and `sapere` (.01) are both "know", with Machiavelli's `conoscere`, a recorded collapse (D374). The readers split at the mark after `cognosce`; the semicolon is the 1933 edition's (D369).

**`e in questo caso il giudicio del populo è fondato non in sulla notizia che abbia per sé stesso del valore di uno cittadino, ma in su quella opinione universale che nasce dalla lunghezza del tempo e dalla esperienza che ne hanno avuto questo e quello particulare`.** "And in this case the judgment of the people is founded not upon the notice it may have of itself of the worth of a citizen, but upon that universal opinion which arises from the length of time and from the experience which this and that particular [man] have had of him": `per sé stesso`, of itself, first-hand, against what others report; `valore`, worth; `universale`, universal, the adjective; `esperienza`, experience, as in .01; `particulare`, particular [man] (D93). The reason he grants is not Machiavelli's: the people does not know the particular citizen, it trusts a long common opinion. Both readers doubted the stop after `particulare` before `Non`; it is supplied from the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-I47-C001, D369).

**`Non accetto già che in questo il populo non si inganni, o almanco più rare volte che non fanno e' pochi`.** "I do not accept that in this the people does not deceive itself, or at least [deceives itself] at rarer times than the few do": `non accetto`, I do not accept, a fifth act beside `credo`, `non so`, `reputo` and `non concorro facilmente`: a refusal of a proposition, not a suspension (D374); `già` intensifies it and is not rendered, as in `non so già` (glossary). `in questo` limits the refusal to the distributions. After `almanco` the verb is understood in the positive, and the bracket supplies it, so that the second proposition refused, that the people deceives itself more rarely than the few, cannot be read as negated. `rare volte`, at rare times (D90, D373), so that `volte` answers Machiavelli's `più volte`; the expletive `non` after the comparative is not rendered. `e' pochi`, the few (D35): Machiavelli's `i pochi uomini` (Machiavelli .06). He refuses both of Machiavelli's claims for the people in this matter, its not being deceived and its being deceived more rarely than the few.

**`perché il populo si governa in questo giudicio non con la notizia particulare, ma con le opinioni universali`.** "Because the people governs itself in this judgment not with particular notice, but with universal opinions": `governare`, govern (D64); the same pair as above, `notizia` against `opinione universale`, turned: the ground that made the conclusion true now makes the people's judgment fallible.

**`né esamina o distingue sottilmente, in modo che si inganna spesso, massime in quelle elezioni delle quali pochi sono capaci`.** "Nor does it examine or distinguish subtly, in such a way that it deceives itself often, above all in those choices of which few are capable": `o distingue`, `quelle`, `delle`, letters doubtful to both readers, read with the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-I47-D002, D367); `elezione`, choice (glossary). `capace` answers `incapaci degli onori` in .01, and the Italian leaves open whether the few are capable of judging the choice or of filling the place; "capable" keeps both, where D350's "able to grasp" would settle it (D373). `spesso`, often, answers Machiavelli's `fia sì raro`.

**`crede a' romori falsi, muovesi per fondamenti leggieri`.** "It believes false rumours, it is moved by light foundations": `romore`, rumour, talk, as D362, since a false `romore` is a report and not a tumult; `fondamento`, foundation (glossary), Machiavelli's word for the nobility's firmer foundation at I.40; `leggieri`, light, as FG I.40 `cagioni leggieri`. The readers doubted the mark after `capaci` and preferred a semicolon, which the 1933 edition prints (D369).

**`e in effetto quanto alla ignoranza è molto più pericoloso che il giudicio di pochi`.** "And in effect, as to ignorance, it is much more dangerous than the judgment of few": `in effetto`, in effect; `ignoranza`, ignorance (FG I.29); `pericoloso`, dangerous, in the root of `pericolo`, danger (glossary), the word on which the readers split with `periculoso` (FG-CONS-I47-D001, D366). `di pochi`, without the article, against `e' pochi` above. The subject is the people, or its judgment; the Italian does not say which, and "it" keeps both. The reply ends on the comparison with the few on which Machiavelli's argument closes (Machiavelli .06), turned against the people.
