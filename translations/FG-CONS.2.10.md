# Consideration on chapter X of Book II

## FG-CONS.2.10.01

He who was the author of that sentence, that money was the sinew of war, and whoever has since followed it, did not understand [by it] that money alone was enough to make war, nor that it was more necessary than soldiers, because that would have been an opinion not only false but also very ridiculous; but understood [by it] that whoever made war had very great need of money, and that without it it was impossible to sustain it, because it is necessary not only for paying the soldiers but for providing the arms, the victuals, the spies, the munitions and so many instruments that are employed in war, which call for so great a flood of it that it is impossible for whoever has not tried it to imagine it. And although sometimes an army in dearth of money provides for it by its *virtù* and by the favour of victories, nonetheless in our times above all these are most rare examples; and in every case and at every time money does not run after armies, except after they have won. I confess that whoever has soldiers of his own makes war with less money than does whoever has mercenary soldiers; nonetheless money too is required by whoever makes war with soldiers of his own, and not everyone has soldiers of his own, and it is much easier with money to find soldiers than with soldiers to find money. Whoever, then, will interpret that sentence according to the sense of him who said it, and as it is commonly understood, will not marvel at it, nor condemn it in any way

## Notes

**The heading and the rubric.** Canestrini's heading, CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO X DEL LIBRO II., with `del libro II` as he prints it for every Book II reply (D421). His rubric, `I danari non sono il nervo della guerra, secondo che è la comune oppinione`, is Machiavelli's word for word but for the spelling `oppinione`, and is rendered as Machiavelli's (Machiavelli, rubric). Both readers doubted the accent of `è` and the stop after `oppinione`; the verb is read, as the 1824 rubric has it, and no stop is set (D430). Canestrini's note on `provede`, gathering Machiavelli's sentences on steel and gold with a French proverb, is his commentary and is not carried.

**`Chi fu autore di quella sentenza, che e' danari furno il nervo della guerra`.** "He who was the author of that sentence, that money was the sinew of war": `sentenza`, sentence (glossary), Machiavelli's word for the maxim (Machiavelli .02); `furno`, were, puts the maxim in the past, as once pronounced. The 1933 edition reads `siano`, "is", a standing claim; both readers read `furno` without doubt, and Canestrini is kept (FG-CONS-II10-D002, D429). Guicciardini does not name Curtius.

**`e chi l'ha poi seguitata, non intese`.** "And whoever has since followed it, did not understand [by it]": `seguitare`, follow, Machiavelli's verb for the princes who follow it (Machiavelli .02); `intendere`, understand (D374), here and at `ma intese` and `comunemente è intesa`: the reply is about what the sentence was taken to mean, and the bracket keeps "did not understand that" from reading as a failure to grasp a truth. `Chi`, the author, is "he who" here and at `chi la disse`; `chi` of anyone, "whoever".

**`né che e' fussino più necessarii che e' soldati`.** "Nor that it was more necessary than soldiers": `danari`, money (glossary), takes the English singular throughout; `necessario`, necessary, Machiavelli's `Sono ben necessari i danari in secondo luogo` (Machiavelli .06), which Guicciardini's first clauses do not contest.

**`perché sarebbe stata opinione non solo falsa, ma ancora molto ridicula`.** "Because that would have been an opinion not only false but also very ridiculous": the counterfactual answers Machiavelli's `Né può essere più falsa quella comune opinione` (Machiavelli .02) in his own adjective: Guicciardini agrees that such an opinion would be false, and says no one held it. `ridicula`, ridiculous; `grandissimo`, below, "very great", as elsewhere in the edition.

**`aveva bisogno grandissimo di danari, e che sanza quelli era impossibil[e] a sostenerla`.** "Had very great need of money, and that without it it was impossible to sustain it": `bisogno`, need (glossary); `sanza`, without; `sostenere`, sustain, of a war; "detain" stays for custody (FG I.10). Canestrini's impression prints `impossibila`; one reader read the a, the other preferred e; emended from the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-II10-D001, D429). The first `impossibile` answers Machiavelli's `è impossibile che a' buoni soldati manchino i danari` (Machiavelli .06): what is impossible without money is the war.

**`le armi, le vettovaglie, le spie, le munizioni e tanti instrumenti`.** "The arms, the victuals, the spies, the munitions and so many instruments": the catalogue as printed, the articles kept. `vettovaglie`, victuals, Machiavelli's word for the army's food (Machiavelli .05).

**`e' quali ne ricercano tanto profluvio`.** "Which call for so great a flood of it": `profluvio`, a flowing forth, rendered by the image and not by the medical "profluvium".

**`E se bene qualche volta uno esercito carestioso di danari con la virtù sua e col favore delle vittorie gli provede`.** "And although sometimes an army in dearth of money provides for it by its *virtù* and by the favour of victories": `se bene`, although (D31); `qualche volta`, sometimes, as Machiavelli's `qualche volta ... la carestia dei danari` (Machiavelli .05); `carestioso`, from `carestia`, dearth, Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .04, .05); `virtù` (D16); `gli provede`, provides for it, the want of money. Granted as possible, sometimes.

**`nondimeno a' tempi nostri massime sono esempli rarissimi`.** "Nonetheless in our times above all these are most rare examples": `nondimeno`, nonetheless (D45); `massime`, above all (glossary). The limit is to frequency, not to possibility.

**`non corrono e' danari dietro agli eserciti, se non da poi che hanno vinto`.** "Money does not run after armies, except after they have won": `correre`, run, the root of the *currere* family (D01), kept literal; the subject of `hanno vinto` is the armies; `vincere` without an object, "win" (D313). This is the reply to Machiavelli's Romans, to whom gold was carried into their camps (Machiavelli .04), and it holds `in ogni caso e in ogni tempo`, without exception.

**`Confesso`.** "I confess": the reply's one concession, and a narrow one: soldiers of one's own make war with less money than mercenaries. It does not grant that arms of one's own are the measure of a prince's forces (Machiavelli .01). An act beside `credo`, `non so`, `reputo`, `non concorro facilmente` and `non accetto` (D374); in an editor's summary, "grants" (D431).

**`chi ha soldati proprii fa la guerra con manco danari che non fa chi ha soldati mercenarii`.** "Whoever has soldiers of his own makes war with less money than does whoever has mercenary soldiers": `soldati proprii`, Machiavelli's `armi proprie` (Machiavelli .01) put in his own term; `manco`, less; `non` after the comparative is not rendered.

**`nondimeno e anche danari bisognano a chi fa guerra co' soldati proprii`.** "Nonetheless money too is required by whoever makes war with soldiers of his own": `bisognare`, be required (D260), here personal, with `danari` its subject. The full note on `bisognare` is at Machiavelli I.2.

**`e ognuno non ha soldati proprii`.** "And not everyone has soldiers of his own": the Italian negates the verb, not `ognuno`, and means that some lack them, not that no one has them.

**`ed è molto più facile co' danari trovare soldati, che co' soldati trovare danari`.** "And it is much easier with money to find soldiers than with soldiers to find money": Machiavelli's chiasmus reversed (`l'oro non è sufficiente a trovare i buoni soldati, ma i buoni soldati sono ben sufficienti a trovar l'oro`, Machiavelli .04), in his verb, `trovare`, find, with `facile`, easy (D27), in place of his `sufficiente`; and `soldati`, not his `buoni soldati`.

**`Chi adunque interpreterà quella sentenza secondo il senso di chi la disse, e secondo che comunemente è intesa`.** "Whoever, then, will interpret that sentence according to the sense of him who said it, and as it is commonly understood": `adunque`, then (D74); `secondo che`, as, as in the rubric; `comunemente`, commonly, answers Machiavelli's `comune opinione` (Machiavelli .02, .04).

**`non se ne maraviglierà, né la dannerà in modo alcuno`.** "Will not marvel at it, nor condemn it in any way": `maravigliarsi`, marvel (glossary); `dannare`, condemn (D99). He does not call the sentence true in so many words; he defends what it was meant to say with reasons of his own, and the close is a prediction under a condition (`Chi ... interpreterà`). The reply ends without a stop (D40, D429).
