# Discourses I.39

*In different peoples the same accidents are often seen.*

## NM-DISC.1.39.01

It is readily known, by whoever considers the things present and the ancient, that in all cities and in all peoples there are those same desires and those same humours, and that they were always there. In such a way that it is an easy thing for whoever examines with diligence the things past to foresee in every republic the future ones, and to apply there those remedies which have been used by the ancients, or, not finding any used, to think of new ones, through the likeness of the accidents. But because these considerations are neglected, or not understood by whoever reads, or, if they are understood, are not known by whoever governs, it follows from it that there are always the same scandals in every time.

## NM-DISC.1.39.02

Having lost, after [14]94, part of its empire, such as Pisa and other towns, the city of Florence was necessitated to make war on those who occupied them: and because whoever occupied them was powerful, it followed from it that much was spent on the war without any fruit: from spending much, there resulted many burdens, from the burdens infinite complaints of the people: and because this war was administered by a magistracy of ten citizens, who were called the Ten of War, the generality began to take it in despite, as being the cause both of the war and of the expenses of it, and began to persuade itself that, the said magistracy taken away, the war was taken away; so much so that, it having to be remade, its replacements were not made; and, it having been let expire, its functions were committed to the Signoria. Which deliberation was so pernicious that not only did it not remove the war, as the generality persuaded itself, but, those men taken away who administered it with prudence, there followed from it so much disorder that, besides Pisa, Arezzo and many other places were lost; in such a way that the people, having perceived its error, and that the cause of the ill was the fever and not the physician, remade the magistracy of the Ten.

## NM-DISC.1.39.03

This same humour rose in Rome against the name of the consuls, because, that people seeing one war arise from the other, and [itself] never able to rest, where they should have thought that it arose from the ambition of the neighbours who wished to oppress them, they thought it arose from the ambition of the nobles, who, not being able within Rome to chastise the plebs defended by the tribunician power, wished to bring it out of Rome under the consuls, to oppress it where it had no aid whatever. And they thought for this that it was necessary either to remove the consuls, or to regulate their power in such a way that they would have authority over the people neither outside nor at home.

## NM-DISC.1.39.04

The first who attempted this law was one Terentilius, a tribune, who proposed that five men should be created, who should consider the power of the consuls, and limit it. Which altered the nobility much, it seeming to them that the majesty of the command had wholly declined, so that no degree any longer remained to the nobility in that republic. The obstinacy of the tribunes was nonetheless so great that the consular name was extinguished; and they were in the end content, after some other order, rather to create the tribunes with consular power than the consuls: so much more did they hold in hatred the name than the authority of them. And so they continued a long time, until, their error known, as the Florentines returned to the Ten, so they re-created the consuls.

## Notes

**The rubric.** `In diversi Popoli si veggono spesso i medesimi accidenti`. "In different peoples the same accidents are often seen": `diverso`, different, as at NM I.25; `accidente`, accident (glossary), what befalls, not chance; `medesimo`, same, the chapter's word (`quelli medesimi desiderj`, `i medesimi scandali`, `Questo medesimo umore`). Guicciardini's reply carries the same rubric (Guicciardini, the rubric).

**`E' si conosce facilmente per chi considera le cose presenti e le antiche`.** "It is readily known, by whoever considers the things present and the ancient": `E'`, the impersonal pronoun, not the verb; `facilmente`, readily (D27); `considerare`, consider (glossary), which returns in the five men who are to `considerare la potenza de' Consoli` (.04); `conoscere`, know, held through the chapter (`non sono conosciute`, .01; `conosciuto l'error loro`, .04), so that the knowledge the chapter opens with is the knowledge Rome reached late.

**`come in tutte le Città e in tutt'i Popoli sono quelli medesimi desiderj e quelli medesimi umori, e come vi furono sempre`.** "That in all cities and in all peoples there are those same desires and those same humours, and that they were always there": `come` after a verb of knowing, "that" (D334); `desiderio`, desire (D117); `umore`, humour (glossary, NM I.4), the word the Roman case takes up (`Questo medesimo umore`, .03). `quelli` twice is kept, as the Italian repeats it.

**`In modo ch'egli è facil cosa a chi esamina con diligenza le cose passate, prevedere in ogni Repubblica le future`.** "In such a way that it is an easy thing for whoever examines with diligence the things past to foresee in every republic the future ones": `in modo che`, in such a way that (glossary); `facile`, easy (D27); `prevedere`, foresee. The 1554 edition reads `ogni Republiche`, the plural (NM-DISC-I39-D002, D343). The claim is conditional on the examination, and its next clause makes the remedy depend on the likeness of the accidents.

**`e farvi quelli rimedj che dagli antichi sono stati usati, o non ne trovando degli usati, pensarne de' nuovi, per la similitudine degli accidenti`.** "And to apply there those remedies which have been used by the ancients, or, not finding any used, to think of new ones, through the likeness of the accidents": `farvi`, literally to make there, of remedies "apply"; `vi`, there, in the republic or among the future things, and "there" keeps both; `rimedio`, remedy (glossary); `similitudine`, likeness (glossary). The likeness is the ground of the remedies, old and new, and perhaps of the foresight too; the phrase closes the period and its scope is left open.

**`Ma perché queste considerazioni sono neglette, o non intese da chi legge, o se le son intese, non sono conosciute da chi governa`.** "But because these considerations are neglected, or not understood by whoever reads, or, if they are understood, are not known by whoever governs": three failures: the considerations are neglected, or not understood by whoever reads, or, understood, not known by whoever governs. `intendere`, understand, and `conoscere`, know, are kept apart; the reader and the governor are distinguished, and the failure that remains is the governor's. Whether `da chi legge` also governs `neglette` is left as open as the Italian leaves it.

**`ne seguita che sempre sono i medesimi scandali in ogni tempo`.** "It follows from it that there are always the same scandals in every time": `scandalo`, scandal, in the older sense of public disturbance; `ne` is kept, "from it". The paragraph ends on the sameness that the rubric announced.

**`Avendo la Città di Firenze dopo il 94 perduta parte dello Imperio suo, come Pisa e altre terre`.** "Having lost, after [14]94, part of its empire, such as Pisa and other towns, the city of Florence": the subject of the gerund put first in the main clause, plain order (D263, exposition), with no pronoun added; `imperio`, empire, dominion over other peoples (D124); `terra`, a walled town, the Florentine sense; the year supplied as at NM I.2 (names.md). Machiavelli dates the losses only `dopo il 94`. The 1554 edition prints the year in roman figures and reads `perduto`, unagreed (NM-DISC-I39-D003, D343).

**`fu necessitata a fare guerra a coloro, che le occupavano`.** "Was necessitated to make war on those who occupied them": `necessitare`, necessitate, as `necessitato` at NM I.30, I.32.

**`ne seguiva che si spendeva assai nella guerra senza alcun frutto: dallo spendere assai, ne risultavano assai gravezze`.** "It followed from it that much was spent on the war without any fruit: from spending much, there resulted many burdens": `assai` three times. After a verb, "much" (D348), as D312's "much" with a noun; D333's "enough" stays for the claim of sufficiency it was ruled on (NM I.32); so also `alterò assai` (.04). With a plural noun, of number, "many" (glossary, NM I.7). `frutto`, fruit; `gravezza`, burden (D334). The chain of causes runs in colons, as the witness points it, and is kept so: war, spending, burdens, complaints.

**`dalle gravezze infinite querele del Popolo`.** "From the burdens infinite complaints of the people": the verb carried over; `querela`, complaint, the word Guicciardini's reply opens on (`la querela de' Fiorentini`, Guicciardini .01), and held in the same English (D348).

**`da un Magistrato di dieci cittadini, che si chiamavano i Dieci della guerra`.** "By a magistracy of ten citizens, who were called the Ten of War": `Magistrato`, magistracy, the office (D93); the Ten as names.md. Guicciardini names them Ten of Balìa, and later of Liberty and Peace (Guicciardini .01).

**`l'universale cominciò a recarselo in dispetto, come quello che fusse cagione e della guerra e delle spese di essa`.** "The generality began to take it in despite, as being the cause both of the war and of the expenses of it": `l'universale`, the generality (glossary, NM I.16); `recarsi in dispetto`, to take in despite, to conceive a grudge against; "despite" is kept apart from `dispregio`, contempt (glossary). `fusse` is the subjunctive of the generality's belief, and "as being" keeps it a belief; `cagione`, cause (glossary).

**`e cominciò a persuadersi che, tolto via detto Magistrato, fusse tolto via la guerra`.** "And began to persuade itself that, the said magistracy taken away, the war was taken away": `togliere via`, take away (D131), twice, as the Italian repeats it, the two removals one act in the generality's mind; `detto`, said (D140); `persuadersi`, persuade itself, which returns at `come l'universale si persuadeva`. The 1554 edition agrees the second participle, `tolta` (NM-DISC-I39-D001, D342).

**`tantoché avendosi a rifare, non se gli fecero gli scambj`.** "So much so that, it having to be remade, its replacements were not made": `tantoché`, consecutive here, "so much so that"; `avere a` after a gerund, "have to" (D237, D261): the office had to be made anew; `rifare`, remake, the verb that returns when the people `rifece il Magistrato` (.02); `scambio`, the replacement, the next board elected in place of the old.

**`e lasciatosi spirare, si commisero le azioni sue alla Signoria`.** "And, it having been let expire, its functions were committed to the Signoria": `spirare`, expire; `azioni`, the office's business and powers, "functions", so that "action" stays for `fazione` in its military sense (D341, D348); the Signoria, Florence's chief magistracy (names.md).

**`La qual deliberazione fu tanto perniziosa, che non solamente non levò la guerra`.** "Which deliberation was so pernicious that not only did it not remove the war": `deliberazione`, deliberation, the word Guicciardini uses of the Ten's re-creation (`E fu questa deliberazione tale`, Guicciardini .01); `levare`, remove (D131).

**`ma tolti via quelli uomini, che con prudenza l'amministravano, ne seguì tanto disordine, che oltre a Pisa, si perdé Arezzo e molti altri luoghi`.** "But, those men taken away who administered it with prudence, there followed from it so much disorder that, besides Pisa, Arezzo and many other places were lost": `prudenza`, prudence (glossary `prudente`); `disordine`, disorder (glossary); the singular verb with its compound subject following it, put in English order (D263, exposition). Arezzo had been subject to Florence; Machiavelli gives no date for its loss.

**`in modoché ravvedutosi il Popolo dell'error suo, e come la cagione del male era la febbre, e non il medico`.** "In such a way that the people, having perceived its error, and that the cause of the ill was the fever and not the physician": `ravvedersi di`, perceive, come to see; `come`, that, a second object of the same participle; `male`, ill (glossary). The figure is the people's own recognition: it had blamed the physician for the fever.

**`rifece il Magistrato de' Dieci`.** "Remade the magistracy of the Ten": `rifare`, remake, as above.

**`Questo medesimo umore si levò in Roma contro al nome de' Consoli`.** "This same humour rose in Rome against the name of the consuls": `levarsi`, rise, the reflexive of `levare`, remove; English cannot hold the two in one word. `nome`, name: the chapter closes on the name hated more than the authority (.04).

**`perché veggendo quello Popolo nascere l'una guerra dall'altra, e non poter mai riposarsi`.** "Because, that people seeing one war arise from the other, and [itself] never able to rest": both infinitives hang on `veggendo`, the second thing seen, and the supplied word keeps them there (D53); `nascere`, arise (D04), three times in the period and one English word.

**`dove e' dovevano pensare che la nascesse dall'ambizione dei vicini che gli volevano opprimere, pensavano nascesse dall'ambizione de' Nobili`.** "Where they should have thought that it arose from the ambition of the neighbours who wished to oppress them, they thought it arose from the ambition of the nobles": `dovere` in the imperfect of an obligation not met, "should have"; "must" (D274) cannot carry the unfulfilled sense (D348). `e'`, they; `la`, it, the war. The nobles' design that follows is what the people thought, not what Machiavelli asserts. `dall'ambizione` twice, "from the ambition"; the 1554 edition reads `dell'ambitione` the second time (NM-DISC-I39-D004, D343). `ambizione`, ambition; `opprimere`, oppress (glossary); `Nobili`, nobles (glossary).

**`che non potendo dentro in Roma gastigare la Plebe difesa dalla potestà Tribunizia`.** "Who, not being able within Rome to chastise the plebs defended by the tribunician power": `gastigare`, chastise (glossary); `potestà`, power (D78); the tribunes' power to protect the plebs within the city.

**`la volevano condurre fuori di Roma sotto i Consoli, per opprimerla dove la non aveva ajuto alcuno`.** "Wished to bring it out of Rome under the consuls, to oppress it where it had no aid whatever": `condurre`, bring, as FG I.32 `condotto`; `ajuto`, aid, kept apart from `giovare`, help (D334); `alcuno` after a negative and after its noun, "whatever" (D195), as NM I.32 `obbligo alcuno`; before its noun it is "any" (`senza alcun frutto`, .02). Outside the city, Machiavelli's people thought, the plebs had no aid; the tribunes' protection was confined to the city (history, not checked here).

**`E pensarono per questo, che fusse necessario, o levar via i Consoli, o regolare in modo la loro potestà`.** "And they thought for this that it was necessary either to remove the consuls, or to regulate their power": `levare via`, remove (D131); `regolare`, regulate.

**`ch'e' non avessero autorità sopra il Popolo, né fuori, né in casa`.** "That they would have authority over the people neither outside nor at home": the subjunctive of result, "would", so that "should" stays the mark of `dovere` in this chapter, and English takes one negative where the Italian has three; `autorità`, authority (glossary); `fuori` and `in casa`, in the field and in the city, the Roman *domi militiaeque*.

**`Il primo che tentò questa legge, fu uno Terentillo Tribuno`.** "The first who attempted this law was one Terentilius, a tribune": `tentare`, attempt (D334); Gaius Terentilius Harsa, tribune of the plebs, whom Livy brings in at 3.9 (names.md).

**`il quale proponeva che si dovessero creare cinque uomini, che dovessero considerare la potenza de' Consoli, e limitarla`.** "Who proposed that five men should be created, who should consider the power of the consuls, and limit it": `dovere` in the subjunctive of a proposal, "should", twice; "must" (D274) would make the proposal a command, and "be to" is `avere a`'s (D61) (D348). `potenza`, power (D78), the same English as `potestà` in the sentences around it, a recorded collapse; `considerare`, consider, the chapter's opening verb. In Livy the five were to write down laws on the consuls' power (3.9); Machiavelli says consider and limit.

**`Il che alterò assai la Nobiltà, parendogli che la maestà dell'Imperio fusse al tutto declinata`.** "Which altered the nobility much, it seeming to them that the majesty of the command had wholly declined": `alterare`, alter, in its older sense, disturb, as NM I.25 (`alterando le cose nuove le menti degli uomini`); `assai` with the verb, "much" (D348); `parendogli`, "it seeming to them", the nobles, after the singular `la Nobiltà`; the 1554 edition reads `parendole`, "to it" (NM-DISC-I39-D005, which records the 1554's readings in .04 together). `Imperio` here is the consuls' *imperium*, "command" (D124); `al tutto`, wholly (D284); `declinare`, decline.

**`talché alla Nobiltà non restasse più alcun grado in quella Repubblica`.** "So that no degree any longer remained to the nobility in that republic": `talché`, so that (glossary); `grado`, degree (glossary).

**`Fu nondimeno tanta l'ostinazione de' Tribuni, che il nome Consolare si spense`.** "The obstinacy of the tribunes was nonetheless so great that the consular name was extinguished": predicate first in the Italian; English order (D263, exposition). `nondimeno`, nonetheless (glossary); `spegnere`, extinguish (glossary).

**`e furono in fine contenti dopo qualche altro ordine, piuttosto creare i Tribuni con potestà Consolare, che i Consoli`.** "And they were in the end content, after some other order, rather to create the tribunes with consular power than the consuls": `ordine`, order (glossary), here an arrangement made before the consular tribunes, which Machiavelli does not describe; `piuttosto ... che`, rather ... than. Who were content is not said; the English keeps "they". The tribunes with consular power are Livy's military tribunes with consular power, who replaced the consuls in many years until the consulship was restored (the dates are not given by Machiavelli and are not checked here).

**`tanto avevano più in odio il nome, che l'autorità loro`.** "So much more did they hold in hatred the name than the authority of them": the landing keeps the name first and the authority last (D18). `loro`, of them, the consuls; "their" would attach it to the haters.

**`E così seguirono lungo tempo, infino che conosciuto l'error loro, come i Fiorentini tornarono ai Dieci, così loro ricrearono i Consoli`.** "And so they continued a long time, until, their error known, as the Florentines returned to the Ten, so they re-created the consuls": `seguire` without an object, continue (the 1554 edition `seguitorno`, NM-DISC-I39-D005); `lungo tempo`, a long time (D262); `conoscere`, know, closing the chain of .01; `come ... così`, as ... so, the two cases set side by side to the end. `ricreare`, re-create; Guicciardini's `creorono di nuovo`, "created anew", tells the Florentine half (Guicciardini .01). The 1554 edition reads `l'errore`, without `loro`, and `ritornorno a` (NM-DISC-I39-D005).
