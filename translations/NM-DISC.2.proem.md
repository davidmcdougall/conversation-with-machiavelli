# Discourses II, proem

## NM-DISC.2.proem.01

Men always praise, but not always reasonably, the ancient times, and the present [ones] they accuse; and they are partisans of past things in such a way that they celebrate not only those ages which have been known by them through the memory of them that the writers have left, but those too which, being already old, they remember having seen in their youth. And when this opinion of theirs is false, as most times it is, I persuade myself that the causes which lead them to this deceit are various.

## NM-DISC.2.proem.02

And the first, I believe, is that of ancient things the truth is not wholly understood, and that of them most times those things are hidden that would bring infamy to those times, and those others that can bring forth glory for them are rendered magnificent and most ample. For most of the writers obey the fortune of the victors in such a way that, to make their victories glorious, they not only increase what is virtuously worked by them, but also make the deeds of the enemies illustrious in such a way that whoever is born afterwards in whichever of the two provinces, either in the victorious or in the vanquished, has cause to marvel at those men and at those times, and is forced supremely to praise and to love them. Beyond this, men hating things either from fear or from envy, two most powerful causes of hatred come to be extinguished in past things, those not being able to offend you, and not giving you cause to envy them. But the contrary befalls with those things that are handled and seen, which, through the entire knowledge of them, [they] not being hidden from you in any part, and [you] knowing in them, together with the good, many other things that displease you, you are forced to judge them much inferior to the ancient [ones], even though in truth the present [ones] might merit much more of glory and of fame than those; reasoning not of the things pertaining to the arts, which have so much clearness in themselves that the times can take away from them or give them little more glory than they merit of themselves, but speaking of those pertaining to the life and customs of men, of which such clear witnesses are not seen.

## NM-DISC.2.proem.03

I repeat, on that account, that that above-written usage of praising and blaming is true, but that it is not always true that one errs in doing it. Because it is sometimes necessary that they judge the truth; because, human things being always in motion, either they go up or they go down. And one sees a city or a province ordered to public living by some excellent man, and for a time, through the *virtù* of that orderer, go always in augmentation towards the better. Whoever is born at that time in such a state, and [if] he praises the ancient times more than the modern, deceives himself; and his deceit is caused by those things that have been said above. But those who are born afterwards in that city or province, for which the time has come that it goes down towards the more evil part, at that time do not deceive themselves.

## NM-DISC.2.proem.04

And I, thinking how these things proceed, judge the world always to have been in one same way, and in it to have been as much of good as of wicked; but this wicked and this good to vary from province to province, as one sees by what notice one has of those ancient kingdoms, which varied from the one to the other through the variation of customs, but the world remained that same; only there was a difference, that where it had earlier placed its *virtù* in Assyria, it placed it in Media, afterwards in Persia, so far that it came from there into Italy and to Rome; and if after the Roman empire there has not followed an empire that has lasted, nor [one] where the world has retained its *virtù* together, one sees it nonetheless scattered among many nations, where one lived virtuously; as was the kingdom of the Franks, the kingdom of the Turks, that of the Sultan, and today the peoples of Germany, and earlier that Saracen sect which did so many great things, and occupied so much of the world, after it destroyed the eastern Roman empire. In all these provinces, then, after the Romans fell into ruin, and in all these sects, there has been that *virtù*, and there still is in some part of them, which is desired, and which is praised with true praise.

## NM-DISC.2.proem.05

And whoever is born in those, and praises the past times more than the present, might deceive himself; but whoever is born in Italy and in Greece, and has not become either in Italy ultramontane or in Greece Turk, has reason to blame his own times and to praise the others; because in those there are many things that make them marvellous; in these there is not anything that redeems them from every extreme misery, infamy and disgrace, where there is no observance of religion, not of laws, not of soldiery, but they are maculate with every kind of filth. And these vices are so much the more detestable as they are more in those who sit *pro tribunali*, command each one, and wish to be adored.

## NM-DISC.2.proem.06

But returning to our reasoning, I say that if the judgment of men is corrupted in judging which is better, either the present century or the ancient, in those things where through antiquity he has not been able to have perfect knowledge as he has of his own times, it should not be corrupted in old men in judging the times of their youth and old age, [they] having known and seen those and these equally. Which thing would be true if men through all the times of their life were of the same judgment and had those same appetites. But those varying, even though the times do not vary, they cannot seem those same [times] to men, [men] having other appetites, other delights, other considerations in old age than in youth. Because, men lacking force when they grow old, and growing in judgment and in prudence, it is necessary that those things which in youth seemed to them supportable and good turn out later, as they grow old, insupportable and bad, and where they should accuse their judgment for it, they accuse the times. Human appetites being, beyond this, insatiable, because they have it from nature to be able and to wish to desire every thing, and from fortune to be able to attain few of them, there results from it continually an ill contentment in human minds, and a vexation with the things that are possessed; which makes [men] blame the present times, praise the past, and desire the future, even though they were not moved to do this by any reasonable cause.

## NM-DISC.2.proem.07

I do not know, then, whether I shall merit being numbered among those who deceive themselves, if in these discourses of mine I shall praise too much the times of the ancient Romans and blame ours. And truly, if the *virtù* that at that time reigned and the vice that now reigns were not clearer than the sun, I would go with speaking more held back, doubting lest I incur that deceit of which I accuse some. But, the thing being so manifest that each one sees it, I shall be spirited in saying manifestly what I shall understand of those and of these times, in order that the spirits of the young who will read these writings of mine may flee these and prepare themselves to imitate those, whatever time fortune gave them occasion for it. Because it is the office of a good man, that good which through the malignity of the times and of fortune you have not been able to work, to teach it to others, in order that, many being capable of it, someone of those more loved by Heaven may work it. And having spoken, in the discourses of the book above, of the deliberations made by the Romans pertaining to the inside of the city, in this [one] we shall speak of those which the Roman people made pertaining to the augmentation of its empire.

## Notes

**The heading.** The 1824 prints no rubric over the proem, only the half-title of Book II: DISCORSI / SOPRA / LA PRIMA DECA DI T. LIVIO, and LIBRO SECONDO. The page is headed as the witness heads it, "Book Two", and nothing is imported. The 1554 sets over it the work's title, the dedication to Zanobi Buondelmonti and Cosimo Rucellai, and `LIBRO SECONDO. PREFATIONE.`; Canestrini calls it the proem (NM-DISC-IIproem-D004, D421).

**`Laudano sempre gli uomini, ma non sempre ragionevolmente, gli antichi tempi, e gli presenti accusano`.** "Men always praise, but not always reasonably, the ancient times, and the present [ones] they accuse": `laudare`, praise (glossary), against `biasimare`, blame, below; `accusare`, accuse (glossary). The second member keeps its fronted object, since it answers the first (D263, a contrast). `sempre ... non sempre` is held word for word.

**`e in modo sono delle cose passate partigiani, che`.** "And they are partisans of past things in such a way that": `partigiani`, partisans (D218); `in modo ... che`, in such a way that (glossary), three times in .01-.02 and held each time; the fronted complement is exposition and goes into plain order (D263).

**`che da loro sono state per la memoria che ne hanno lasciata gli scrittori conosciute`.** "Which have been known by them through the memory of them that the writers have left": the participle `conosciute` is held back to the end of the clause in the Italian; the English cannot hold it so far.

**`mi persuado varie essere le cagioni`.** "I persuade myself that the causes ... are various": `persuadersi`, persuade oneself (D358); `cagione`, cause (glossary); `quando` with the subjunctive `sia`, "when ... is". Only the first cause is numbered (`E la prima`, .02); further causes follow at `Oltra di questo` (.02), in the change of judgment with age (.06), and at a second `oltra di questo` (.06), the insatiability of appetites. The count is the editor's.

**`Però che i più degli scrittori in modo alla fortuna dei vincitori ubbidiscono`.** "For most of the writers obey the fortune of the victors in such a way": `però che` is causal, *per ciò che*, "for", and not the consecutive `però`, "therefore" (D22), which would make the writers' flattery a consequence of the hidden truth instead of its reason.

**`le azioni de' nimici in modo illustrano, che`.** "Make the deeds of the enemies illustrious in such a way that": `azioni`, things done by men, "deeds" (D410); "action" stays `fazione`'s (D341). `illustrare`, in its sense of making illustrious.

**`qualunque nasce dipoi in qualunque delle due Provincie, o nella vittoriosa o nella vinta`.** "Whoever is born afterwards in whichever of the two provinces, either in the victorious or in the vanquished": `qualunque` twice, whoever, whichever; `dipoi`, afterwards (D187); `provincia`, province (glossary), here the two countries at war. The 1554 reads `delle ſue prouincie`, "of its provinces" (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

**`non ti potendo quelle offendere, e non ti dando cagione d'invidiarle`.** "Those not being able to offend you, and not giving you cause to envy them": `offendere`, offend, the older sense, injure (glossary). The second person enters here and runs to `sei forzato`; the English keeps it, bracketing the subjects of the gerunds that follow, "[they] not being hidden", "[you] knowing". The 1554 has `non potendo` and `non gli dando` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

**`le quali ... sei forzato giudicarle alle antiche molto inferiori`.** "Which ... you are forced to judge them much inferior to the ancient [ones]": the relative `le quali` is left hanging and resumed by `giudicarle`, "judge them", as in the Italian. `molto`, adverbial, "much"; the 1554 reads `molte`, agreeing it with `antiche` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D002, D420).

**`ancoraché in verità le presenti molto più di quelle di gloria e di fama meritassero`.** "Even though in truth the present [ones] might merit much more of glory and of fame than those": `ancoraché`, even though (glossary, with `ancora che`); the subjunctive as "might", since "should" is `dovere`'s in this unit (`dovrebbe`, .06; D348).

**`ragionando non delle cose pertinenti alle arti`.** "Reasoning not of the things pertaining to the arts": `ragionare`, reason, with `ragionamento`, reasoning, in .06. The 1554 reads `dalle coſe` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D002, D420). The arts are what Guicciardini takes up first (Guicciardini .01).

**`delle quali non se ne veggono sì chiari testimonj`.** "Of which such clear witnesses are not seen": `testimonj`, witnesses, the evidence a thing gives, as `testimone` in NM I.60.

**`Replico pertanto essere vera quella consuetudine del laudare e biasimare soprascritta`.** "I repeat, on that account, that that above-written usage of praising and blaming is true": `replicare`, here to say again what .01 said, not to answer anything; `pertanto`, on that account (D98); `consuetudine`, usage (D41); `soprascritto`, above-written (glossary). "Usage" is the habit just described, men praising the past.

**`ma non essere già sempre vero, che si erri nel farlo`.** "But that it is not always true that one errs in doing it": `già` after the negative is left unrendered, as in `non so già`, `non accetto già` (D374). Guicciardini's last clause turns on the same words (`ma non è già vero il negare`, Guicciardini .01), rendered the same way.

**`o le salgono o le scendono`.** "Either they go up or they go down": `salire`, `scendere`, go up, go down; `scendere` again at the end of the paragraph, of the city. "Rise" stays `levarsi`'s and `surgere`'s (D358), "descend" `descendere`'s (D374).

**`essere ordinata al vivere pubblico da qualche uomo eccellente`.** "Ordered to public living by some excellent man": `vivere pubblico`, public living, with `vivere politico`, `vivere civile`, `vivere libero` (glossary); `ordinatore`, orderer (glossary). The 1554 reads `al uiuere politico`, "to political living" (NM-DISC-IIproem-D001, D420).

**`Chi nasce allora in tale stato, ed ei laudi più gli antichi tempi che i moderni, s'inganna`.** "Whoever is born at that time in such a state, and [if] he praises the ancient times more than the modern, deceives himself": `allora`, "at that time", since "then" is `adunque`'s (D74, D187); `stato`, state, here a condition; `ed ei laudi`, the pleonastic `ei` kept as "he", the subjunctive's condition carried by "[if]"; `ingannarsi`, deceive oneself, `inganno`, deceit (D374). `ed è causato il suo inganno` is exposition and goes into plain order (D263).

**`in quella Città o Provincia, che gli è venuto il tempo, che la scende verso la parte più rea`.** "In that city or province for which the time has come that it goes down towards the more evil part": `che gli` is the loose relative, "for which"; `la scende`, indicative, `la` the city as subject, as `la ne venne` (.04); `reo`, evil (D03). The 1554 reads `di cui è uenuto il tempo` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D001, D420).

**`giudico il Mondo sempre essere stato ad un medesimo modo, ed in quello essere stato tanto di buono, quanto di tristo`.** "Judge the world always to have been in one same way, and in it to have been as much of good as of wicked": `giudicare`, judge (D374); `tristo`, wicked (D03). This is the claim Guicciardini answers (Guicciardini .01), restated of ages, not provinces.

**`come si vede per quello si ha notizia di quelli Regni antichi`.** "As one sees by what notice one has of those ancient kingdoms": `notizia`, notice (D373); `che` is not printed before `si ha`, which the 1554 supplies (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

**`solo vi era differenza, che dove quello aveva prima collocata la sua virtù in Assiria`.** "Only there was a difference, that where it had earlier placed its *virtù* in Assyria": `quello` is the world; `prima`, earlier (D334); `collocare`, place (D115). The succession recalls the old order of the world empires; the comparison is the editor's. `tanto che`, "so far that", until. The 1554 has `queſta differenza` for `differenza` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

**`si vede nondimeno essere sparsa in di molte nazioni, dove si viveva virtuosamente`.** "One sees it nonetheless scattered among many nations, where one lived virtuously": `in di molte`, the partitive after `in`, as printed; the 1554 reads `in molte` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420). `virtuosamente`, the adverb of *virtù*, as `virtuosamente operato` in .02.

**`come era il Regno de' Franchi, il Regno de' Turchi, quel del Soldano, ed oggi i Popoli della Magna, e prima quella Setta saracina`.** "As was the kingdom of the Franks, the kingdom of the Turks, that of the Sultan, and today the peoples of Germany, and earlier that Saracen sect": `era`, singular, governs the catalogue as printed; `prima`, earlier (D334). The Franks presumably of Charlemagne's kingdom; the Turks, the Ottomans; the Sultan, presumably the Mamluk sultan of Egypt and Syria; `la Magna`, Germany (Alemagna); the Saracen sect, the religion of the Arabs and those who carried it. `Setta`, sect, and `Sette` below. The identifications are the editor's. The 1554 adds `et` before the second kingdom (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

**`poi che la distrusse l'Imperio romano orientale`.** "After it destroyed the eastern Roman empire": `la` is the subject, the sect, as `la scende` (.03) and `la ne venne` (.04); the empire is the object. `Imperio`, empire, dominion over peoples (D124), lower case throughout this paragraph.

**`poi che i Romani rovinarono`.** "After the Romans fell into ruin": `rovinare`, intransitive, from `rovina`, ruin (glossary).

**`chi nasce in Italia ed in Grecia, e non sia divenuto, o in Italia Oltramontano o in Grecia Turco`.** "Whoever is born in Italy and in Greece, and has not become either in Italy ultramontane or in Greece Turk": `Oltramontano`, from beyond the mountains, a foreigner from north of the Alps; the witness's capital is not carried (names.md, `Barbari`). The 1554 reads `in Italia, & non in Grecia`, which would take Greece out of the case the clause goes on to make (NM-DISC-IIproem-D001, D420).

**`ha ragione di biasimare i tempi suoi, e laudare gli altri; perché in quelli vi sono assai cose`.** "Has reason to blame his own times and to praise the others; because in those there are many things": `biasimare`, blame (glossary, `biasimo`); `assai` of number, "many" (glossary). The 1554 reads `gli altrui` and `in quella` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

**`in questi non è cos'alcuna che li ricomperi d'ogni estrema miseria, infamia e vituperio`.** "In these there is not anything that redeems them from every extreme misery, infamy and disgrace": `ricomperare`, buy back, redeem; `vituperio`, disgrace (D26); `infamia`, infamy (glossary).

**`non di milizia, ma sono maculati d'ogni ragione bruttura`.** "Not of soldiery, but they are maculate with every kind of filth": the triple `non` kept, "no ... not ... not"; `milizia`, soldiery (glossary); `maculati`, stained, the cognate kept; `ragione`, kind, as in `d'ogni ragione`. The 1554 reads `macchiate d'ogni ragione di bruttura`, feminine plural (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

**`in coloro che seggono pro tribunali`.** "In those who sit *pro tribunali*": the Latin of the bench, those who sit in judgment; left in Latin, as printed.

**`se il giudizio degli uomini è corrotto in giudicare qual sia migliore, o il secolo presente o l'antico`.** "If the judgment of men is corrupted in judging which is better, either the present century or the ancient": `giudizio`, judgment, `giudicare`, judge (D374); `corrompere`, corrupt (glossary); `secolo`, century, Guicciardini's `secoli` (Guicciardini .01); "age" stays `età`'s (D410).

**`in quelle cose dove per l'antichità ei non ha possuto avere perfetta cognizione come egli ha de' suoi tempi`.** "In those things where through antiquity he has not been able to have perfect knowledge as he has of his own times": `ei`, `egli`, taken as the man judging, singular after `degli uomini`; it may also be read as the judgment. `cognizione`, knowledge (D374).

**`non dovrebbe corrompersi ne' vecchi`.** "It should not be corrupted in old men": `dovere`, conditional, "should" (D348); "must" stays the indicative's (D274).

**`Perché mancando gli uomini, quando egli invecchiano, di forze`.** "Because, men lacking force when they grow old": `mancare di` + noun, lack (D312); `forze`, force (glossary); `invecchiare`, grow old, and again `invecchiando`. The 1554 has no `egli` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

**`riescano poi invecchiando insopportabili e cattive`.** "Turn out later, as they grow old, insupportable and bad": `riuscire`, here turn out; "succeed" (D332) is the sense of a thing that comes off; `poi`, later (D250), "afterwards" being `dipoi`'s (D187); `sopportabile`, `insopportabile`, supportable, insupportable, the cognate, "bear" being `comportare`'s; `cattivo`, bad (D03). The bare subjunctive takes no "should", which is `dovrebbono`'s in the next clause (D348).

**`perché hanno dalla natura di potere e voler desiderare ogni cosa, e dalla fortuna di potere conseguirne poche`.** "Because they have it from nature to be able and to wish to desire every thing, and from fortune to be able to attain few of them": `conseguire`, attain (glossary); `volere`, wish (D117). The 1554 reads `perche hauendo` and `conſeguire poche`, without `ne` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

**`ne risulta continuamente una mala contentezza nelle menti umane, ed un fastidio delle cose che si posseggono`.** "There results from it continually an ill contentment in human minds, and a vexation with the things that are possessed": `male`, ill (glossary), here the adjective; `fastidio`, vexation (glossary).

**`il che fa biasimare i presenti tempi, laudare i passati, e desiderare i futuri`.** "Which makes [men] blame the present times, praise the past, and desire the future": three verbs, three tenses. `ancorché a far questo non fussero mossi`, the fronted infinitive put in plain order (D263). The 1554 reads `laudare i futuri`, repeating the verb of the past (NM-DISC-IIproem-D001, D420). `mossi`, moved; the 1554 has `meßi` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D002, D420).

**`Non so adunque se io meriterò di essere numerato tra quelli che s'ingannano`.** "I do not know, then, whether I shall merit being numbered among those who deceive themselves": `adunque`, then (D74). The doubt is Machiavelli's own, before the claim it qualifies.

**`andrei col parlare più rattenuto, dubitando non incorrere in quello inganno`.** "I would go with speaking more held back, doubting lest I incur that deceit": `rattenuto`, the form of `ritenuto`, held back (D312), with the speaking; `allora`, "at that time", against `ora`, now; `dubitare ... non`, doubt lest, apprehension (D205); `incorrere`, incur (D192), a *currere* compound.

**`sarò animoso in dire manifestamente quello che intenderò`.** "I shall be spirited in saying manifestly what I shall understand": `animoso`, spirited, with `animo`, spirit (glossary), and `gli animi de' giovani` after it; `intendere`, understand (D374).

**`Perché gli è ufficio d'uomo buono`.** "Because it is the office of a good man": `ufficio`, office (D56); `gli`, the impersonal subject. The `tu` of `non hai potuto` is general. `acciocché`, in order that, twice in the paragraph, purpose, kept apart from `talché`, so that, result (glossary).

**`alcuno di quelli più amati dal Cielo possa operarlo`.** "Someone of those more loved by Heaven may work it": `operare`, work (D83), twice, as `virtuosamente operato` in .02. The 1554 reads `amato`, agreeing with `alcuno` (NM-DISC-IIproem-D003, D420).

**`ne' discorsi del superior libro`.** "In the discourses of the book above": Book I; `superiore`, above, of what precedes. `discorsi` lower case, Machiavelli's word for his chapters.

**`pertinenti allo augumento dello Imperio suo`.** "Pertaining to the augmentation of its empire": `augumento`, augmentation (D69), as `andare ... in augumento` in .03; `Imperio`, empire (D124). The 1554 reads `allo accreſcimẽto dell'Imperio ſuo`, "the increase" (NM-DISC-IIproem-D001, D420).

**The 1554 leaf.** The 1554's fol. 84v has lost the left of its last thirteen lines, answering this text from `etadi, che da loro sono state` (.01) to `per fare le loro vittorie` (.02); there the 1824 was read by both its readers, and they agree (NM-DISC-IIproem-D005, D415).
