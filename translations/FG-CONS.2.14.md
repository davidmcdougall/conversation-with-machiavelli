# Consideration on chapter XIV

## FG-CONS.2.14.01

The conclusion of the Discourse is in part contrary to that which he said in another place, that it is more prudence to temporize in dangerous cases than to strike, and therefore it is required to distinguish that, when your forces are not even with those of the enemy, it is better to come to [an] accord, even leaving something, than to pull ruin upon yourself at once; because time can bring accidents that are enough to provide against your future danger. But when you have forces even or almost even with the enemy, even though entering into war is with danger and with difficulty, the beginning to take away your reputation from you, to make you base, the enemy insolent, matters so much that unwillingly one must yield. Which reason Thucydides discourses at large in the person of Pericles, when he counselled the Athenians rather to take the war with the Lacedaemonians, albeit difficult and dangerous, than to accept the conditions proposed by them, even though in themselves they seemed of little moment.

## Notes

**The heading and the rubric.** Canestrini's heading, CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO XIV, without `del libro II`, as both readers saw it; the column is headed as the page is (D443, D463). His rubric is Machiavelli's sentence (Machiavelli, rubric), printed with broken letters and read with the 1824 rubric; the 1933 edition prints `umilitá` (FG-CONS-II14-D004).

**`La conclusione del Discorso è in parte contraria a quello che lui disse in altro luogo`.** "The conclusion of the Discourse is in part contrary to that which he said in another place": `conclusione`, conclusion (glossary), Machiavelli's counsel never to leave a thing by accord (Machiavelli .02); `Discorso`, the Discourse, capitalised as printed; `in parte`, in part, the measure of the objection; `in altro luogo`, in another place (glossary). Canestrini's footnote names the place, Book I, chapter 33; it is his commentary and is not carried (D463). The objection is that Machiavelli contradicts himself, and only in part.

**`che è più prudenza temporeggiarsi ne' casi periculosi che urtare`.** "That it is more prudence to temporize in dangerous cases than to strike": Guicciardini's own summary of Machiavelli's earlier counsel, not a quotation. `prudenza`, prudence (glossary), a noun where English would want an adjective, kept; `temporeggiarsi`, temporize (D444), here reflexive; `urtare`, strike (glossary), as at I.23; `periculoso`, dangerous, with `periculo`, danger (glossary), held in the reply's four uses (dangerous ×2, danger ×2). Canestrini's letter is broken; read `periculosi`, his own spelling (D462; FG-CONS-II14-D002).

**`e però bisogna distinguere`.** "And therefore it is required to distinguish": `però`, therefore (D22): the distinction is drawn because of the contradiction, not against it. `bisogna`, it is required (D260; see the note on `bisogni` at Machiavelli I.2). `distinguere`, distinguish: the reply is a distinction of two cases, not a rejection.

**`quando le forze tue non sono pari a quell[e] dello inimico`.** "When your forces are not even with those of the enemy": `pari`, even (D262), held at `forze pari o quasi pari` below; `forze`, forces (glossary); `inimico`, enemy (D334). Canestrini prints `quello`, which agrees with nothing; emended `quell[e]` with the 1933 edition (D461; FG-CONS-II14-E001, D001).

**`meglio sia accordare, etiam lasciando qualche cosa`.** "It is better to come to [an] accord, even leaving something": `accordare`, come to [an] accord (D464), the verb of Machiavelli's `d'accordo` (Machiavelli .02), kept apart from `concordare`, accord (D422); `etiam`, even (latin.md; glossary, D453); `lasciare`, leave, Machiavelli's verb. The subjunctive `sia` follows `distinguere che`; the English is indicative, which keeps the echo of Machiavelli's `egli è meglio ... che`, "it is better ... than" (Machiavelli .02). This is the case Machiavelli met by arming `ancora che elle siano inferiori`, even though the forces are inferior (Machiavelli .03), and Guicciardini answers it otherwise.

**`che tirarsi subito addosso la ruina`.** "Than to pull ruin upon yourself at once": `tirarsi addosso`, pull upon yourself (D464); `subito`, at once (D177), Machiavelli's word for arming (Machiavelli .03); `ruina`, ruin (glossary, `rovina`).

**`perché il tempo può portare degli accidenti che bastino a provedere al tuo futuro periculo`.** "Because time can bring accidents that are enough to provide against your future danger": `portare`, bring, of time (D464; D374's "bear" stays for danger borne); `accidente`, accident (glossary); `bastare`, be enough (D205); `provedere`, provide (glossary), with `a`, against.

**`Ma quando tu hai forze pari o quasi pari allo inimico`.** "But when you have forces even or almost even with the enemy": the second case, set against the first by `Ma`.

**`ancora che lo entrare in guerra sia con periculo e con difficultà`.** "Even though entering into war is with danger and with difficulty": `ancora che`, even though (D31 extended); `difficultà`, difficulty (D464). A concession inside the second case: war is dangerous here too.

**`importa tanto il cominciare a tòrti la riputazione, a fare vile te, insolente lo inimico, che mal volentieri si debbe cedere`.** "The beginning to take away your reputation from you, to make you base, the enemy insolent, matters so much that unwillingly one must yield": `importare`, matter (D464); `il cominciare`, the beginning, the first step, as Machiavelli's adversary `ti comincia a stimare` (Machiavelli .03); `torre`, take away (D131), with `ti`, from you; the two infinitives are active and name no agent, and the English keeps them so, with the reader as their object; `riputazione`, reputation (glossary); `vile`, base, Machiavelli's word (`o debole o vile`, Machiavelli .02); the third member, `insolente lo inimico`, has no verb, and the English keeps it so; `insolente`, insolent, Machiavelli's (`gli uomini insolenti`, Machiavelli .01). `dovere`, must (D274); `cedere`, yield (glossary). `mal volentieri`, unwillingly (D464), governs the obligation: one must be loath to yield; it is not a counsel to yield, and it is not Machiavelli's `mai`, never (Machiavelli .02). The English sets the adverb first, as the Italian does, so that it reads over `must`. The Italian fronts `importa tanto`; the English is in plain order (D263). The stop after `cedere` is supplied (FG-CONS-II14-C001, D67), as the 1933 edition prints it.

**`La quale ragione largamente discorre Tucidide nella persona di Pericle`.** "Which reason Thucydides discourses at large in the person of Pericles": `Tucidide`, Thucydides (names.md); `discorrere`, discourse (glossary, the *currere* family, SCOPE §3), Guicciardini's verb for Thucydides; `largamente`, at large; `nella persona di`, in the person of, speaking through him (D464). Guicciardini cites him for the reason, not for the measure of forces. The speech is Pericles' first, Thucydides 1.140-144 (the editor's reference).

**`quando consigliò agli Ateniesi più presto il pigliare la guerra co' Lacedemonii ... che accettare le condizioni proposte da loro`.** "When he counselled the Athenians rather to take the war with the Lacedaemonians ... than to accept the conditions proposed by them": `consigliare`, counsel (D444); `più presto ... che`, rather ... than (D135); `pigliare`, take (glossary); `condizione`, condition (glossary). Names as names.md.

**`benché difficile e periculosa`.** "Albeit difficult and dangerous": `benché`, albeit (D31).

**`ancora che per sé stesse le paressino di poco momento`.** "Even though in themselves they seemed of little moment": `le`, the conditions, the subject (the old pronoun, as Machiavelli's `la` at II.13 .03); `di momento`, of moment (glossary). The reply ends with Canestrini's stop.
