# Discourses I.49

*If those cities that have had a free beginning, like Rome, have difficulty in finding laws that may maintain them, those that have it immediately servile have almost an impossibility of it.*

## NM-DISC.1.49.01

How difficult it is, in ordering a republic, to provide for all those laws that may maintain it free, the process of the Roman republic demonstrates it very well, where, notwithstanding that many laws were ordered, by Romulus first, afterwards by Numa, by Tullus Hostilius and Servius, and lastly by the Ten citizens created for a like work, nonetheless always, in handling that city, new necessities were discovered, and it was necessary to create new orders; as befell when they created the censors, who were one of those provisions that aided in holding Rome free [for] that time that it lived in liberty. Because, become arbiters of the customs of Rome, they were the chief cause that the Romans deferred longer corrupting themselves.

## NM-DISC.1.49.02

They indeed made an error in the beginning of the creation of such a magistracy, creating it for five years; but not much time afterwards it was corrected by the prudence of Mamercus the dictator, who by a new law brought the said magistracy to eighteen months; which the censors who were [then] in office took so ill that they deprived Mamercus of the senate; which thing was much blamed both by the plebs and by the Fathers: and because the history does not show that Mamercus could defend himself from it, it is fitting either that the historian be defective, or the orders of Rome in this part not good; because it is not good that a republic be ordered in such a way that a citizen, for promulgating a law conformable to free living, can be offended for it without any remedy.

## NM-DISC.1.49.03

But, returning to the beginning of this discourse, I say that one must consider, through the creation of this new magistracy, that if those cities that have had a free beginning, and which by itself has governed itself, like Rome, have great difficulty in finding good laws to maintain them free, it is no marvel that those cities that have had their beginning immediately servile have, not merely difficulty, but impossibility of ever ordering themselves in such a way that they can live civilly and quietly: as one sees [it] has befallen the city of Florence, which, through having had its beginning subjected to the Roman empire, and having lived always under the government of others, stayed a time subject, and without thinking of itself; afterwards, the occasion of breathing having come, it began to make its orders, which, being mixed with the ancient ones, which were wicked, could not be good; and so it has gone on handling itself for two hundred years, of which there is true memory, without ever having had a state by which it can truly be called a republic. And these difficulties that have been in it have been always in all those cities that have had beginnings like it. And albeit many times, by public and free suffrages, ample authority has been given to a few citizens of being able to reform it, they have for all that never ordered it to common utility, but always to the purpose of their party; which has made not order, but greater disorder in that city.

## NM-DISC.1.49.04

And, to come to some particular example, I say that among the other things that are to be considered by an orderer of a republic is to examine into the hands of which men he sets the authority of blood against his citizens: this was well ordered at Rome, because one could appeal to the people ordinarily; and if yet some important thing had occurred, where the deferring of the execution by means of the appeal were dangerous, they had the refuge of the dictator, who executed immediately, tn which remedy they never took refuge, except through necessity.

## NM-DISC.1.49.05

But Florence, and the other cities born in the way of it, being servile, had this authority placed in a stranger, who, sent by the prince, did such office. When afterwards they came into liberty, they maintained this authority in a stranger, whom they called the Captain. Which, for being able to be readily corrupted by powerful citizens, was a most pernicious thing. But afterwards, this order changing through the change of states, they created eight citizens who should do the office of that Captain. Which order from bad became very bad, for the causes that have been said at other times, that the few were always ministers of the few, and of the most powerful. From which the city of Venice has guarded itself, which has ten citizens who can punish every citizen without appeal. And because they would not be enough to punish the powerful, even though they had authority for it, they have constituted the Quarantie there; and moreover they have wished that the council of the Pregadi, which is the greater council, can chastise them. In such a way that, the accuser not failing there, the judge does not fail there to hold powerful men reined in.

## NM-DISC.1.49.06

It is no marvel, then (seeing how at Rome, ordered by itself and by so many prudent men, new causes rose every day, for which new orders had to be made in favour of free living), if in the other cities, which have a more disordered beginning, such difficulties rise there that they can never be reordered.

## Notes

**The rubric.** Machiavelli's rubric: a conditional, the first case as its protasis and the second as its apodosis, joined by a semicolon. `principio`, beginning (glossary); `libero`, free; `immediate`, immediately, the Latin adverb, without any intermediate state: their beginning was servile from the first. `servo` as an adjective, "servile" (D388): the glossary's "servant" is the noun (NM I.16), and English cannot set it after "beginning". `lo hanno` is present, "have it", where the first case is perfect; `ne hanno quasi una impossibilità`, "have almost an impossibility of it", `ne` the difficulty of finding laws. The body states the second case more strongly, `non che difficultà, ma impossibilità` (.03), and the close more strongly still, `non si possano riordinare mai` (.06).

**`Quanto sia difficile nello ordinare una Repubblica provvedere a tutte quelle leggi che la mantengano libera, lo dimostra assai bene il processo della Repubblica romana`.** "How difficult it is, in ordering a republic, to provide for all those laws that may maintain it free, the process of the Roman republic demonstrates it very well": the question is fronted and resumed by `lo`, and the English keeps the resumptive "it". `ordinare`, order (glossary, with `ordine`, `ordinatore`, `riordinare`); `provvedere`, provide, and `provvedimenti`, provisions, below, one root; `mantenere`, maintain (glossary); `assai` of degree, "very" (D254); `processo`, process, the course of the republic's history, the cognate kept.

**`non ostante che fussero ordinate di molte leggi da Romolo prima, dipoi da Numa, da Tullo Ostilio e Servio, e ultimamente dai Dieci cittadini creati a simile opera`.** "Notwithstanding that many laws were ordered, by Romulus first, afterwards by Numa, by Tullus Hostilius and Servius, and lastly by the Ten citizens created for a like work": `non ostante che`, notwithstanding that (glossary); `dipoi`, afterwards (D187); `simile`, like (glossary); `opera`, work (D253). The kings are Romulus, Numa Pompilius, Tullus Hostilius and Servius Tullius; Ancus Marcius and the two Tarquins are not named. `i Dieci cittadini` are the decemvirs of 451-449 BC, who drew up the Twelve Tables, the Ten of I.40 (names.md); the 1554 edition prints `i x. cittadini`.

**`nondimeno sempre nel maneggiare quella Città si scoprivano nuove necessità, ed era necessario creare nuovi ordini`.** "Nonetheless always, in handling that city, new necessities were discovered, and it was necessary to create new orders": `nondimeno`, nonetheless (D45); `maneggiare`, handle (glossary), which returns of Florence, `è ita maneggiandosi` (.03); `scoprire`, discover. `necessità` and `necessario` are one root, as in the Italian; `creare`, create (glossary), here and of the censors.

**`come intervenne quando crearono i Censori, i quali furono uno di quelli provvedimenti che ajutarono a tenere Roma libera quel tempo che la visse in libertà`.** "As befell when they created the censors, who were one of those provisions that aided in holding Rome free [for] that time that it lived in liberty": `intervenire`, befall (D298); `ajutare`, aid, the verb of `ajuto` (D348), kept off "help", which is `giovare`'s (D334); `tenere`, hold (D242). `la` is the pronoun, Rome, as the 1554 `ella`. The 1554 prints `aiutarono tenere`, without `a` (NM-DISC-I49-D003). Livy dates the beginning of the censorship to 443 BC (4.8, `censurae initium`).

**`Perché diventati arbitri de' costumi di Roma, furono cagione potissima che i Romani differissero più a corrompersi`.** "Because, become arbiters of the customs of Rome, they were the chief cause that the Romans deferred longer corrupting themselves": `costumi`, customs (D41); `cagione`, cause (glossary); `potissima`, the Latin superlative *potissimus*: "chief", since English has no cognate and "principal" is `principale`'s (FG I.40) (D389); `differire`, defer (D334), the verb that returns at .04 of the appeal; `corrompersi`, corrupt themselves (glossary).

**`Fecero bene nel principio della creazione di tal Magistrato uno errore, creando quello per cinque anni`.** "They indeed made an error in the beginning of the creation of such a magistracy, creating it for five years": `bene` concessive, "indeed"; the object is not held back in the English, which is exposition (D263): the institution was good and its first form an error. `principio`, beginning, the chapter's word, here the start of the office; `Magistrato`, magistracy, the office (D93); `errore`, error (glossary), the word Guicciardini takes up, `Fu anche [e]rrore` (Guicciardini .01).

**`ma dipoi non molto tempo, fu corretto dalla prudenza di Mamerco Dittatore, il qual per nuova legge ridusse detto Magistrato a diciotto mesi`.** "But not much time afterwards it was corrected by the prudence of Mamercus the dictator, who by a new law brought the said magistracy to eighteen months": `molto tempo`, much time (D262); `prudenza`, prudence, the noun of `prudente` (glossary); `ridurre`, bring (D358); `detto`, said (D140). Mamercus Aemilius, dictator in 434 BC (Livy 4.24), Guicciardini's `Mamerco Emilio`. The 1554 prints the months as `XVIII`.

**`il che i Censori che vegghiavano ebbero tanto per male, che privarono Mamerco del Senato`.** "Which the censors who were [then] in office took so ill that they deprived Mamercus of the senate": `vegghiare`, to watch, keep awake, said of a magistracy or a law, to be in being or in force; "watching" would read as onlookers (D389); `avere per male`, take ill, `male` ill (glossary). In Livy the censors, angered that he had diminished a magistracy of the Roman people, `tribu moverunt octiplicatoque censu aerarium fecerunt`, moved him from his tribe and made him an *aerarius* with an eightfold assessment (4.24, the Latin Library text, thelatinlibrary.com/livy/liv.4.shtml, consulted 2026-11-02); "deprived of the senate" is Machiavelli's, and Guicciardini's after him. Livy has the leading senators offended at the censors' harshness and the people's indignation great, Machiavelli's blame of plebs and fathers.

**`la qual cosa e dalla Plebe, e dai Padri fu assai biasimata`.** "Which thing was much blamed both by the plebs and by the Fathers": `assai` with a verb, "much" (D348); `biasimare`, blame (D173); `Padri`, the Fathers, the senators (names.md, NM I.8), set beside the plebs.

**`e perché la Istoria non mostra che Mamerco se ne potesse difendere, conviene o che lo Istorico sia difettivo, o gli ordini di Roma in questa parte non buoni`.** "And because the history does not show that Mamercus could defend himself from it, it is fitting either that the historian be defective, or the orders of Rome in this part not good": `Istoria`, history (glossary), Livy's, and `Istorico`, historian; `conviene`, it is fitting (D85): the inference is put as what must follow, and the alternatives are left as alternatives. `difettivo`, defective, the word Guicciardini answers with `defettivo` of the Roman government (Guicciardini .01). The verb is not repeated in the second member, and the English does not supply it.

**`perché non è bene che una Repubblica sia in modo ordinata, che un cittadino per promulgare una legge conforme al viver libero, ne possa essere senza alcun rimedio offeso`.** "Because it is not good that a republic be ordered in such a way that a citizen, for promulgating a law conformable to free living, can be offended for it without any remedy": `in modo ... che`, in such a way that (glossary); `vivere libero`, free living (glossary); `rimedio`, remedy (glossary), the word Guicciardini answers twice, `non vi era remedio alcuno`, `altro remedio`; `offendere`, offend, in the older sense, injure (glossary). The 1554 prints `uiuere libero` and `alcuno rimedio`.

**`Ma tornando al principio di questo discorso, dico che si debbe per la creazione di questo nuovo Magistrato considerare`.** "But, returning to the beginning of this discourse, I say that one must consider, through the creation of this new magistracy": `principio`, beginning, now of the discourse; `discorso`, discourse (glossary); `si debbe`, one must (D274); `considerare`, consider (glossary). `per` is "through", by way of the example.

**`che se quelle Città che hanno avuto il principio libero, e che per sé medesimo si è r[e]tto, come Roma`.** "That if those cities that have had a free beginning, and which by itself has governed itself, like Rome": the 1824 prints `si è rotto`, "has broken", read by both readers without doubt; the 1554 `si è retto`, "has governed itself", which answers Florence `sempre sotto governo d'altri` below. Emended from the 1554 (NM-DISC-I49-D001, D379). The verb and `medesimo` are singular, after the plural `quelle Città`; whether the antecedent is the city or the beginning is left open, and "which by itself has governed itself" keeps the singular. `reggere`, govern, with `governare`, a recorded collapse (D64). The 1554 also reads `il principio loro libero` (NM-DISC-I49-D003).

**`hanno difficultà grande a trovar leggi buone per mantenerle libere, non è maraviglia che quelle Città, che hanno avuto il principio loro immediate servo, abbiano, non che difficultà, ma impossibilità ad ordinarsi mai in modo che le possano vivere civilmente e quietamente`.** "Have great difficulty in finding good laws to maintain them free, it is no marvel that those cities that have had their beginning immediately servile have, not merely difficulty, but impossibility of ever ordering themselves in such a way that they can live civilly and quietly": the rubric's propositions return, the second raised from `quasi una impossibilità` to `impossibilità`; `non che ... ma`, not merely ... but; `civilmente`, civilly, with `vivere civile` (glossary). `le` is the pronoun, the cities.

**`come si vede che è intervenuto alla Città di Firenze, la quale per aver avuto il principio suo sottoposto all'Imperio romano, ed essendo vivuta sempre sotto governo d'altri, stette un tempo soggetta, e senza pensare a sé medesima`.** "As one sees [it] has befallen the city of Florence, which, through having had its beginning subjected to the Roman empire, and having lived always under the government of others, stayed a time subject, and without thinking of itself": `intervenire`, befall (D298); `Imperio romano`, the Roman empire (D124); `sottoposto`, subjected, kept apart from `soggetta`, subject; `governo`, government (D262). Machiavelli's account of Florence's beginning is I.1.

**`dipoi, venuta l'occasione di respirare, cominciò a fare i suoi ordini, i quali sendo mescolati con gli antichi, ch'erano tristi, non poterono esser buoni`.** "Afterwards, the occasion of breathing having come, it began to make its orders, which, being mixed with the ancient ones, which were wicked, could not be good": `occasione`, occasion (glossary); `tristi`, wicked (D03).

**`e così è ita maneggiandosi per dugento anni, che si ha di vera memoria, senza avere mai avuto Stato, per il quale ella possa veramente essere chiamata Repubblica`.** "And so it has gone on handling itself for two hundred years, of which there is true memory, without ever having had a state by which it can truly be called a republic": `maneggiarsi`, handle itself, the verb of Rome at .01; `Stato`, state (D265), here a form of government, set against `Repubblica`; `vera memoria`, true memory, and `veramente`, truly. The 1554 prints the years as `CC`.

**`E benché molte volte per suffragj pubblici e liberi si sia dato ampia autorità a pochi cittadini di potere riformarla, non pertanto mai l'hanno ordinata a comune utilità, ma sempre a proposito della parte loro`.** "And albeit many times, by public and free suffrages, ample authority has been given to a few citizens of being able to reform it, they have for all that never ordered it to common utility, but always to the purpose of their party": `benché`, albeit (D31); `autorità`, authority (glossary); `riformare`, reform (D272); `pochi cittadini`, "a few citizens", since bare "few" would deny that there were any to speak of; `non pertanto`, the set adversative, "for all that", kept apart from `pertanto`, on that account (D98), `nondimeno`, nonetheless, and `pure`, yet; `parte`, of a faction, "party" (D389), where `in questa parte` (.02) stays "in this part". The 1554 prints `ampla`. Florence called such grants balìe; Machiavelli uses neither the word nor a date.

**`il che ha fatto non ordine, ma maggiore disordine in quella Città`.** "Which has made not order, but greater disorder in that city": `ordine` / `disordine`, order / disorder (glossary).

**`E per venire a qualche esempio particolare, dico come tra l'altre cose che si hanno a considerare da uno ordinatore d'una Repubblica, è, esaminare nelle mani di quali uomini ei ponga l'autorità del sangue contro de' suoi cittadini`.** "And, to come to some particular example, I say that among the other things that are to be considered by an orderer of a republic is to examine into the hands of which men he sets the authority of blood against his citizens": `particolare`, particular (D374); `avere a`, be to (D261), destinative; `ordinatore`, orderer (glossary); `porre`, set (D115), kept apart from `collocata`, placed, at .05; `l'autorità del sangue`, the authority of blood, the power of capital judgment.

**`questo era bene ordinato in Roma, perché ei si poteva appellare al Popolo ordinariamente`.** "This was well ordered at Rome, because one could appeal to the people ordinarily": `appellare`, appeal (glossary); `ordinariamente`, ordinarily, by the orders (D151). The appeal to the plebs is mentioned at I.32 and is at issue with the Ten at I.40.

**`e se pure fusse occorsa cosa importante, dove il differire la esecuzione mediante l'appellazione fusse pericoloso, avevano il rifugio del Dittatore, il quale eseguiva immediate`.** "And if yet some important thing had occurred, where the deferring of the execution by means of the appeal were dangerous, they had the refuge of the dictator, who executed immediately": `pure`, yet (D191); `occorrere`, occur (glossary, the *currere* family); `differire`, defer (D334); `appellazione`, appeal (D348); `pericoloso`, dangerous, in the root of `pericolo`, danger, agreeing with `il differire`: the 1554 has `pericolosa`, agreeing with `la esecuzione`, and the 1824 is kept (NM-DISC-I49-D002, D380). `immediate`, immediately, the rubric's adverb; `eseguire`, execute, the verb of `esecuzione`: he carried out the sentence.

**`al qual rimedio non rifuggivano mai, se non per necessità`.** "Tn which remedy they never took refuge, except through necessity": `rifuggire`, take refuge, the verb of `rifugio`, refuge, just before (D389); "have recourse" stays `ricorrere`'s (D209).

**`Ma Firenze, e l'altre Città nate nel modo di lei, sendo serve, avevano questa autorità collocata in un forestiero, il quale mandato dal Principe faceva tale ufficio`.** "But Florence, and the other cities born in the way of it, being servile, had this authority placed in a stranger, who, sent by the prince, did such office": `modo`, way (glossary); `serve`, servile, the rubric's word (D388), in the old sense, in servitude, not of manner; `collocare`, place (D115); `forestiero`, stranger (D57); `Principe`, prince (glossary), the ruler who sent the foreign official, not named; `ufficio`, office (D56). The 1554 prints `posta` for `collocata` (NM-DISC-I49-D003).

**`Quando dipoi vennero in libertà, mantennero questa autorità in un forestiero, il quale chiamavano Capitano`.** "When afterwards they came into liberty, they maintained this authority in a stranger, whom they called the Captain": `mantenere`, maintain (glossary); `Capitano`, the Captain, a Florentine magistrate with criminal jurisdiction (names.md, as NM I.8), not the captain of an army (NM I.29). Machiavelli does not say which magistracy he means, and the note does not settle it.

**`Il che, per potere essere facilmente corrotto da' cittadini potenti, era cosa perniciosissima`.** "Which, for being able to be readily corrupted by powerful citizens, was a most pernicious thing": `facilmente`, readily (D27); `corrompere`, corrupt (glossary), the verb of `corrompersi` (.01); `potente`, powerful (D06); `pernizioso`, pernicious (glossary). The infinitive's subject is unexpressed, the Captain or the arrangement, and the English leaves it so.

**`Ma dipoi mutandosi per la mutazione degli Stati questo ordine, crearono otto cittadini che facessero l'uffizio di quel Capitano`.** "But afterwards, this order changing through the change of states, they created eight citizens who should do the office of that Captain": `mutare`, `mutazione`, change (D272), one root; `Stati`, states (D265), changes of government; `otto cittadini`, eight citizens, presumably the *Otto di Guardia*, the Eight (names.md, as FG I.10); Machiavelli does not name the magistracy.

**`Il quale ordine di cattivo diventò pessimo, per le cagioni che altre volte sono dette, che i pochi furono sempre ministri de' pochi, e de' più potenti`.** "Which order from bad became very bad, for the causes that have been said at other times, that the few were always ministers of the few, and of the most powerful": `cattivo` / `pessimo`, bad / very bad (D03, glossary); `ministri`, ministers, servants; `de' più potenti`, the relative superlative, "the most powerful". The 1554 prints `ministri di pochi` (NM-DISC-I49-D003). The cause is said at I.7, of accusing a powerful man before eight judges: `pochi sempre fanno a modo de' pochi`.

**`Da che si è guardata la Città di Vinegia, la quale ha dieci cittadini, che senza appello possono punire ogni cittadino`.** "From which the city of Venice has guarded itself, which has ten citizens who can punish every citizen without appeal": `guardarsi`, guard oneself; `dieci cittadini`, ten citizens, the Venetian Council of Ten (names.md), not the Roman Ten of .01 or the Florentine Ten of I.39; `appello`, appeal (glossary).

**`E perché ei non basterebbono a punire i potenti, ancora che n'avessero autorità, v'hanno constituito le Quarantie`.** "And because they would not be enough to punish the powerful, even though they had authority for it, they have constituted the Quarantie there": `bastare`, be enough (D205); `ancora che`, even though (glossary); `costituire`, constitute (glossary). `le Quarantie`, the Venetian courts of forty (names.md), kept in Italian; the Florentine Quarantia of FG I.7 shares the name.

**`e di più hanno voluto che il Consiglio de' Pregadi, che è il Consiglio maggiore, possa gastigarli`.** "And moreover they have wished that the council of the Pregadi, which is the greater council, can chastise them": `volere`, wish (D117); `Pregadi`, the Venetian senate, kept in Italian; `il Consiglio maggiore`, the greater council: perhaps comparative, greater than the Ten and the Forty; Venice's *Maggior Consiglio*, the Great Council, was a different body, and the English keeps his words. `gastigare`, chastise (glossary).

**`In modo che non vi mancando l'accusatore, non vi manca il Giudice a tenere gli uomini potenti a freno`.** "In such a way that, the accuser not failing there, the judge does not fail there to hold powerful men reined in": `mancare`, fail (D206), twice; `accusatore`, accuser (D159); `tenere ... a freno`, hold reined in, as NM I.3 `tenevano la Nobiltà a freno` (D389). `freno` is the word of Guicciardini's `il freno del tribuno` (Guicciardini .01).

**`Non è adunque maraviglia (veggendo come in Roma, ordinata da sé medesima e da tanti uomini prudenti, surgevano ogni dì nuove cagioni, per le quali si aveva a far nuovi ordini in favor del vivere libero)`.** "It is no marvel, then (seeing how at Rome, ordered by itself and by so many prudent men, new causes rose every day, for which new orders had to be made in favour of free living)": `adunque`, then (D74); `ordinata da sé medesima` answers the emended `per sé medesimo si è r[e]tto` of .03; `prudenti`, prudent (glossary); `surgere`, rise (D358); `avere a` in the passive, of necessity, "had to" (D261); `vivere libero`, free living (glossary). The parenthesis is the witness's.

**`se nelle altre Città, che hanno più disordinato principio, vi surgano tali difficultà, che le non si possano riordinare mai`.** "If in the other cities, which have a more disordered beginning, such difficulties rise there that they can never be reordered": `disordinato`, disordered, and `riordinare`, reorder (glossary), the family of `ordine`; `surgere` rise again (D358). The close returns to the rubric's second case, and states it without `quasi`.
