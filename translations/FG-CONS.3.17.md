# Consideration on chapter XVII of book III

## FG-CONS.3.17.01

A prince is much more to abstain from committing himself to one [whom he] has injured, than a republic, because the [man] injured by the prince acknowledges the injury [as] wholly from him, but one injured by a republic acknowledges the injury more [as] from some particular [man] who has persecuted him or has found himself in the magistracy, than from the name of the city, and therefore, offending the city, it does not seem to him [that he] avenges himself. Afterwards, whoever seeks the ruin of the fatherland does ill to his kinsmen, to his friends, to all his things themselves, and to his own self, and with infamy of himself; which does not befall whoever acts against a prince. It is also easier to extinguish a prince than a republic; and for this, one who is injured can be more prompt to enter into this thought. Therefore I would not be easy to flee a citizen injured by his republic, and above all when the injury has not been very atrocious, in which case one could have respect to it, but that of Claudius Nero, cited in the Discourse, is a thing ridiculous to believe, that through having been calumniated in the time [he] was in Spain, and also with some reason, he should have had so much indignation that he could desire to be routed, and the words, which the Writer says that he used, were not his words, but Salinator's, who after the consulship had been condemned by the people, and having received such an ignominy, it is no marvel if he resented it more. Who, although he spoke so, either from indignation or from certain natures or fantasies that men have, it is to be believed that in fact he understood it otherwise; and his proceedings show it, first, before the choice for the consulship, that he refused it obstinately until he was almost forced by the principal citizens. Which he would have desired if he had had cupidity to avenge himself; afterwards, that, chosen consul, he did what was possible to win, and went very reluctant to give battle with Hasdrubal, even though he had said earlier that he wished to hasten it.

## Notes

**The heading and the rubric.** Canestrini's heading, CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO XVII DEL LIBRO III., with `del libro III` and a stop, as both readers saw it; the column is headed as the page is (D443, D495). His rubric is Machiavelli's sentence (Machiavelli, rubric) without the stop (FG-CONS-III17-D003). Canestrini prints no reply to chapters XXV to XXXIII of Book II, nor to chapters I to XVI of Book III, nor to XVIII; his next is to chapter XIX. His note to this reply, identifying Salinator, is not carried (D495); the person is identified below and in the commentary.

**`Molto più s'ha a astenere uno principe in non si commettere in chi ha ingiuriato, che una republica`.** "A prince is much more to abstain from committing himself to one [whom he] has injured, than a republic": the fronted comparative set in plain order (D263); `avere a`, be to, destinative (D261); `astenersi`, abstain; `in non si commettere`, literally "in not committing himself", the negative pleonastic after a verb of abstaining, not rendered; `commettersi in`, commit oneself to, entrust oneself to (D496); `chi ha ingiuriato`, one [whom he] has injured, the subject of `ingiuriato` being the prince, as the next clause shows, and supplied (D53); `ingiuriare`, injure (glossary `ingiuria`). Machiavelli's chapter speaks only of a republic (Machiavelli .01); the prince is Guicciardini's.

**`perché lo ingiuriato dal principe ricognosce la ingiuria tutta da lui`.** "Because the [man] injured by the prince acknowledges the injury [as] wholly from him": `ricognoscere ... da`, acknowledge [as] from (D283); `tutta`, wholly.

**`ricognosce più la ingiuria da qualche particulare che l'ha perseguitato o si è trovato in magistrato, che dal nome della città`.** "Acknowledges the injury more [as] from some particular [man] who has persecuted him or has found himself in the magistracy, than from the name of the city": `particulare`, particular [man] (D93), the private citizen against the city; `perseguitare`, persecute (glossary); `magistrato`, magistracy (D93). Both readers doubted `particulare` at a broken letter, and the other loci where the scan breaks a letter are read as the word they name (FG-CONS-III17-D004; D494).

**`e però offendendo la città non gli pare vendicarsi`.** "And therefore, offending the city, it does not seem to him [that he] avenges himself": `però`, therefore (D22); `vendicarsi`, avenge oneself (D480), Machiavelli's verb (Machiavelli .02); `pare`, it seems.

**`Di poi chi cerca la rovina della patria, fa male a' parenti, agli amici, a tutte le cose sue medesime, e a sé proprio`.** "Afterwards, whoever seeks the ruin of the fatherland does ill to his kinsmen, to his friends, to all his things themselves, and to his own self": `Di poi`, afterwards (D187), the second reason; `rovina`, ruin, Machiavelli's last word (Machiavelli .03); `patria`, fatherland (glossary); `male`, ill (glossary); `medesime`, postposed and intensive, "themselves" (D488); `a sé proprio`, to his own self. The comma after `patria`, the lighter of two marks the readers saw, where the 1933 edition prints none (FG-CONS-III17-D002; D494).

**`e con infamia di sé medesimo; che non interviene a chi fa contro a uno principe`.** "And with infamy of himself; which does not befall whoever acts against a prince": `infamia`, infamy (glossary); `intervenire`, befall (D298); `fare contro a`, act against.

**`È ancora più facile spegnere uno principe che una republica`.** "It is also easier to extinguish a prince than a republic": `ancora`, also, the third reason; `facile`, easy (D27); `spegnere`, extinguish (glossary), of a prince as of a republic, the man and the republic under one verb.

**`e per questo uno che sia ingiuriato, può essere più pronto a entrare in questo pensiero`.** "And for this, one who is injured can be more prompt to enter into this thought": `pronto`, prompt (D298); `pensiero`, thought, the thought of extinguishing. Neither reader prefers a stop after `pensiero`, both doubting one (one sees a faint grey mark), before the capital of `Però`; it is supplied from the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-III17-C001; D67, D494).

**`Però io non sarei facile a fuggire uno cittadino ingiuriato dalla sua republica`.** "Therefore I would not be easy to flee a citizen injured by his republic": `però`, therefore (D22), the conclusion drawn from the three reasons; `facile a`, easy to, in the older sense of disposed or inclined to (D27, D496), not "easily fled"; `fuggire`, flee (D325), here to avoid employing him. The conditional `sarei` is his own judgment, not a rule.

**`e massime quando la ingiuria non sia stata molto atroce, nel quale caso si potria avergli rispetto`.** "And above all when the injury has not been very atrocious, in which case one could have respect to it": `massime`, above all (glossary); `atroce`, atrocious; `avere rispetto a`, have respect to (glossary `rispetto`); `gli`, to it, or to him: the injury, or the citizen (the form is the masculine dative, used also for the feminine); the English "it" takes the injury, in the editor's reading. `nel quale caso` follows the not-atrocious clause, but the sense seems to require the case of an atrocious injury, which the negation implies: there, in the editor's reading, one could take the injury into account, and Machiavelli's rule holds. The English keeps "in which case" as printed and does not resolve it.

**`ma quella di Claudio Nerone allegato nel Discorso, è cosa ridicula a credere`.** "But that of Claudius Nero, cited in the Discourse, is a thing ridiculous to believe": `quella`, that [injury], feminine, taking up `la ingiuria`; the English leaves it unsupplied, "that of Claudius Nero", as the Italian does; `allegare`, cite (D431), the participle agreeing with Nero; `il Discorso`, the Discourse, the chapter (as FG II.24); `ridiculo`, ridiculous, in Guicciardini's spelling.

**`che per essere stato calunniato nel tempo era in Spagna, e anche con qualche ragione`.** "That through having been calumniated in the time [he] was in Spain, and also with some reason": `calunniare`, calumniate (D159), the private charge without proof (Machiavelli's `fu parlato inonestamente`, .02); `nel tempo era`, in the time [he] was, the relative and the subject unexpressed, as printed (D53); `e anche con qualche ragione`, and also with some reason, a concession that the charge was not baseless, in the editor's reading; it could also grant that his indignation had some reason; kept where it stands.

**`avessi avuto tanto sdegno che potessi desiderare di essere rotto`.** "He should have had so much indignation that he could desire to be routed": `sdegno`, indignation, Machiavelli's `isdegno` (Machiavelli .02); `rompere`, rout, of an army (D431, D480), as Machiavelli's `rotta` (Machiavelli .02). The reductio is Guicciardini's: on Machiavelli's account, as he reads it, Nero's indignation would have to reach a desire to be routed (Machiavelli .02 says only that, failing, Nero knew how he would avenge himself); Guicciardini finds that ridiculous to believe.

**`e le parole, che lo Scrittore dice che lui usò, non furono parole sue, ma del Salinatore`.** "And the words, which the Writer says that he used, were not his words, but Salinator's": `lo Scrittore`, the Writer (glossary), Machiavelli; `del Salinatore`, Salinator's, with the article, Marcus Livius Salinator, Nero's colleague in the consulship of 207 BC (names.md; D496). Livy puts words of like sense in Livius' mouth (27.40, the editor's reference): asked by Fabius why the haste, he would take either outstanding glory from the enemy or a joy, deserved if not honourable, from his defeated fellow citizens.

**`il quale doppo il consulato era stato condannato dal populo`.** "Who after the consulship had been condemned by the people": `doppo`, after; `consulato`, consulship (D410); `condannare`, condemn (glossary `dannare`); `populo`, people, in Guicciardini's spelling. Livy 27.34 (the editor's reference).

**`e avendo ricevuta una tale ignominia, non è meraviglia se ne risentissi più`.** "And having received such an ignominy, it is no marvel if he resented it more": `ignominia`, ignominy (D496); `meraviglia`, marvel (glossary `maraviglia`); `risentirsi di`, resent (D496); `più`, more, the comparison unexpressed: than Nero, in the editor's reading. The gerund's subject is Salinator and the main clause impersonal, as printed.

**`Il quale se bene parlassi così o per sdegno o per certe nature o fantasie che hanno gli uomini`.** "Who, although he spoke so, either from indignation or from certain natures or fantasies that men have": `Il quale`, who, Salinator, opening a new period; `se bene`, although (D31); `nature`, natures, dispositions (D496); `fantasie`, fantasies, fancies (D496). The relative is left without its verb: the period turns at `è da credere`, and the English keeps the break.

**`è da credere che in fatto la intendessi altrimenti`.** "It is to be believed that in fact he understood it otherwise": `è da credere`, it is to be believed, impersonal, not `credo`; `in fatto`, in fact, in his deeds against his words; `intendere`, understand (D374), here what he meant; `la`, it, the matter, its referent unexpressed in the Italian.

**`e lo mostrano le azioni sue prima, innanzi alla elezione del consulato`.** "And his proceedings show it, first, before the choice for the consulship": `azioni`, proceedings, narrated doings (D358); `sue`, a common doubt of both readers, read with the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-III17-D001; D494); `prima`, first, answered by `di poi` below, in the editor's reading, since `innanzi` already says "before": a sense split from D334's "earlier", which the last `prima` keeps (D496); `elezione`, choice (glossary).

**`che lo recusò ostinatamente insino non fu quasi sforzato da' principali cittadini`.** "That he refused it obstinately until he was almost forced by the principal citizens": `recusare`, refuse (D496); `ostinatamente`, obstinately; `insino non`, until, the negative pleonastic; `sforzare`, force (D496), kept apart from `costringere`, constrain (D452); `principali`, principal (glossary). Livy has the consuls bring him back to the city and the censors restore him to the Senate, and makes him complain that the white gown was forced on him (27.34, the editor's reference).

**`Il che arebbe desiderato se avessi avuto cupidità di vendicarsi`.** "Which he would have desired if he had had cupidity to avenge himself": `cupidità`, cupidity (glossary); `vendicarsi`, avenge oneself (D480). The consulship is what he would have desired; the reductio is Guicciardini's, as at `potessi desiderare`.

**`di poi che eletto consule fece il possibile per vincere, e andò molto renitente a fare la giornata con Asdrubale`.** "Afterwards, that, chosen consul, he did what was possible to win, and went very reluctant to give battle with Hasdrubal": `di poi`, afterwards (D187), the second proof; `consule`, consul (D410); `renitente`, reluctant (D496); `fare la giornata`, give battle (D49). Livy reports that many favoured deferring the battle, without naming Livius among them (27.46, the editor's reference); the reluctance is Guicciardini's account.

**`ancora che avessi detto prima volerla sollecitare`.** "Even though he had said earlier that he wished to hasten it": `ancora che`, even though (D31); `prima`, earlier (D334); `sollecitare`, hasten (D496). Canestrini's printed stop ends the reply.
