# Consideration on chapter XIX

## FG-CONS.3.19.01

Severity, naked of all humanity, or let us say pleasantness, is useless in one who governs others; humanity, or else pleasantness, not accompanied by some severity is the same; the one seasoned equally with the other would be most precious, and would be that tempered harmony which is most suave and admirable. But because this seasoning is found either seldom or never in any man; the order of nature being so, that all our things have some imperfection, indeed, it seems that each one has either more of the severe than of the pleasant, or more of the pleasant than of the severe; not without cause one doubts which is more to the purpose, either he who, partaking of the one and of the other, has more of the severe, or else he who has more of the humane, meaning therefore those who have so much of the one and of the other that where fear abounds love not fail, and where love abounds fear not fail. About which, the first distinction that occurs to me is to consider the nature of whom you govern; because some are of a mind so noble and generous that they go more willingly with pleasantness than with fear, others on the contrary full of a certain hardness, who cannot be bent with sweetness, but it is required to subdue them and break them with asperity. There is no doubt that with these such it is required to accommodate oneself according to their conditions; and to this purpose Frederick Barbarossa, a very excellent prince, and one who, born in Germany, had long conversed in Italy, used to say that the two first nations of the world, and, according to the others, full of many *virtù*, were the Germans and the Italians; but that a different art was required to govern them, because the Germans were arrogant, insolent, and of a quality that the sweetness you used with them they attributed rather to fear than to humanity; on the contrary the Italians, more tractable, more gentle, and of a nature that asperity rather made them indignant than frightened them. Therefore for these [it was] necessary to pardon at times the crimes, and to proceed with benignity; those others, to punish them severely, because otherwise they would become more insolent.

## FG-CONS.3.19.02

The other distinction that occurs to me is that a difference is to be made between one who governs as a prince and with his own authority, and one who governs as a minister and in the name of others; because I believe that a prince is to have much respect to seeking the benevolence of the peoples, since many cases can occur in which, to conserve the state, he has need of extraordinary love of the peoples. But in one who commands in the name of others I would distinguish: either in an army, and then it would be more necessary to abound in love than in fear, because, having to conduct them to actions dangerous for their city, they are conducted there much with love; but in one who governs cities or provinces in the name of others, nothing touching him but the temporary care, and he not being the supreme lord for whom the peoples are to move themselves to more than they are ordinarily held to, I believe he conducts his affairs better with somewhat more terror than ordinary princes [do]: because, the peoples knowing that the troops depend on others, and that some time hence he is not to remain in office, the benevolence that they might bear him cannot make a notable foundation for those effects for which love towards the prince is so much desired. I say therefore that, we speaking of good and legitimate governments, one can ill presuppose that where there is fear there is not also love, because the severity of justice, which is that which brings fear, cannot be that it is not loved by whoever wishes to live well; and *e converso* the love that arises from humanity, from facility of nature, and from inclination to do graces, accompanied by justice, as is to be presupposed in a good government, cannot make that it is not feared.

## Notes

**The heading and the rubric.** Canestrini's heading, CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO XIX, without `del libro III`, as both readers saw it; a pale dot after XIX, doubted by both, is not read as a stop, the 1933 printing none (D443, D110, D503). His rubric is Machiavelli's sentence (Machiavelli, rubric) with `lo ossequio` for `l'ossequio`, two forms of the article, and without the stop (FG-CONS-III19-D007). Canestrini prints no reply to chapter XVIII of Book III, and none to XX to XXIII; his next is to chapter XXIV. His note to this reply, a summary of Machiavelli's chapter quoting Tacitus' sentence, is not carried (D503).

**`La severità, nuda di ogni umanità o vogliamo dire piacevolezza, è inutile in chi regge altri`.** "Severity, naked of all humanity, or let us say pleasantness, is useless in one who governs others": `severità`, severity, Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .03); `umanità`, humanity (D504), the example of humanity among the Faliscans in Machiavelli's last sentence (Machiavelli .05); `o vogliamo dire`, or let us say, the second name offered as an equivalent; `piacevolezza`, pleasantness (D504), with `piacevole` pleasant; `inutile`, useless; `reggere`, govern (D64). Guicciardini does not take up Machiavelli's pair, `pena` and `ossequio`, penalty and compliance; he opens on severity and humanity, the qualities of the man who governs.

**`la umanità o vero piacevolezza non accompagnata da qualche severità è il medesimo`.** "Humanity, or else pleasantness, not accompanied by some severity is the same": `o vero`, or else (D504), the equivalence restated; `il medesimo`, the same, that is, useless.

**`l'una condita equalmente con l'altra sarebbe preziosissima, e sarebbe quella armonia temperata che è suavissima e ammirabile`.** "The one seasoned equally with the other would be most precious, and would be that tempered harmony which is most suave and admirable": `condito`, seasoned, and `condimento`, seasoning (D504), the figure of the kitchen kept; `equalmente`, equally (glossary `equale`); `temperato`, tempered (glossary); `suave`, suave (D504), kept apart from `dolcezza`, sweetness, below. The 1933 edition reads `farebbe`, would make, at the second verb; Canestrini's `sarebbe`, read by both readers, kept (FG-CONS-III19-D001; D38, D502). The stop after `ammirabile`, seen by neither reader, is supplied from the 1933 (FG-CONS-III19-C001; D67, D502).

**`Ma perché questo condimento o rare volte o non mai si truova in uomo alcuno; essendo così lo ordine della natura`.** "But because this seasoning is found either seldom or never in any man; the order of nature being so": `rare volte`, seldom (D97); `truovarsi`, be found; `ordine`, order (glossary); `natura`, nature (D496). The `perché` is answered only by `non sanza cagione si dubita`, after two clauses set off by semicolons, as printed.

**`che tutte le cose nostre abbino qualche imperfezione, anzi pare che ciascuno o abbia più del severo che del piacevole, o più del piacevole che del severo`.** "That all our things have some imperfection, indeed, it seems that each one has either more of the severe than of the pleasant, or more of the pleasant than of the severe": `imperfezione`, imperfection; `anzi`, indeed (D147), correcting upward after the affirmative; `pare`, it seems; `del severo`, `del piacevole`, of the severe, of the pleasant, the adjectives as nouns, as printed.

**`non sanza cagione si dubita quale sia più a proposito, o chi participando dell'uno e dell'altro ha più del severo, o vero chi ha più dello umano`.** "Not without cause one doubts which is more to the purpose, either he who, partaking of the one and of the other, has more of the severe, or else he who has more of the humane": `cagione`, cause (D64); `dubitare`, doubt; `a proposito`, to the purpose (D504); `participare di`, partake of (glossary `partecipare di`), not D28's transitive "impart"; `dello umano`, of the humane, answering `umanità`.

**`intendendo però di coloro che hanno tanto dell'uno e dell'altro, che dove abbonda il timore non manchi l'amore, e dove abbonda l'amore non manchi il timore`.** "Meaning therefore those who have so much of the one and of the other that where fear abounds love not fail, and where love abounds fear not fail": `però`, therefore (D22): the doubt is therefore about men who have enough of both; it may also be read as limiting, "meaning, however", which D22 rules out; `abbondare`, abound (D504); `timore`, fear, and `amore`, love (glossary), Guicciardini's second pair, set beside the first; `mancare`, fail (D206), the subjunctive without "should" (D348).

**`Circa a che la prima distinzione che mi occorre è considerare la natura di chi tu reggi`.** "About which, the first distinction that occurs to me is to consider the nature of whom you govern": `distinzione`, distinction (glossary), his answer to the division Machiavelli draws without the word (`o che ... o`, Machiavelli .02), which his two distinctions cross rather than adopt; `occorrere`, occur (SCOPE §3, the *currere* family); `considerare`, consider (glossary); `chi tu reggi`, whom you govern, the governed, not the governor; the second person, Machiavelli's (Machiavelli .02).

**`perché alcuni sono di ingegno sì nobile e generoso che più volentieri vanno con la piacevolezza che col timore`.** "Because some are of a mind so noble and generous that they go more willingly with pleasantness than with fear": `ingegno`, mind (D178); `nobile`, noble; `generoso`, generous, in the old sense, of high spirit; `volentieri`, willingly. The comma after `timore`, where both readers doubt a semicolon, follows the 1933 (FG-CONS-III19-D006; D110).

**`altri pel contrario pieni di una certa durezza, che non si possono piegare con la dolcezza, ma bisogna domargli e rompergli con la asperità`.** "Others on the contrary full of a certain hardness, who cannot be bent with sweetness, but it is required to subdue them and break them with asperity": `pel contrario`, on the contrary; `durezza`, hardness; `piegare`, bend; `dolcezza`, sweetness (D504); `bisogna`, it is required, the edition's rendering of the impersonal `bisognare` in every tense (D260); the reasons are in the note on `bisogni` at Machiavelli I.2; `domare`, subdue (D452); `rompere`, break (D480); `asperità`, asperity (D504), the harshness answering sweetness.

**`Non è dubio che con questi tali bisogna accommodarsi secondo le loro condizioni`.** "There is no doubt that with these such it is required to accommodate oneself according to their conditions": `non è dubio`, there is no doubt, the act of certainty; `questi tali`, these such; `accommodarsi`, accommodate oneself; `condizioni`, conditions (glossary), the natures just named.

**`e a questo proposito diceva Federico Barbarossa, principe molto eccellente, e che nato in Germania aveva lungamente conversato in Italia`.** "And to this purpose Frederick Barbarossa, a very excellent prince, and one who, born in Germany, had long conversed in Italy, used to say": Frederick I, emperor 1155-1190 (names.md); `diceva`, used to say, the imperfect of a saying repeated; `conversare`, converse, the old sense of living among and dealing with (D480). No source for the saying is traced here.

**`che le due prime nazioni del mondo, e secondo l'altre, piene di molte virtù, erano e' Germani e gl'Italiani`.** "That the two first nations of the world, and, according to the others, full of many *virtù*, were the Germans and the Italians": `nazioni`, nations; `secondo l'altre`, according to the others, either as the other nations judge or as the others go, left open; *virtù* untranslated (glossary); `e' Germani`, the Germans, `e'` the plural article, not "both" (D35), answered by `gl'`.

**`ma che bisognava diversa arte di reggergli, perché e' Tedeschi erano arroganti, insolenti`.** "But that a different art was required to govern them, because the Germans were arrogant, insolent": `bisognava`, was required (D260); `arte`, art; `reggere`, govern (D64); `Tedeschi`, Germans, the vernacular name after the learned `Germani`, one English word for both: a recorded collapse (D504); `arrogante`, arrogant (D464); `insolente`, insolent (D464), Machiavelli's word for subjects not kept in check (Machiavelli .04).

**`e di qualità che la dolcezza che tu usavi con loro, la attribuivano più presto a timore che a umanità`.** "And of a quality that the sweetness you used with them they attributed rather to fear than to humanity": `qualità`, quality (glossary), the consecutive `che` without "such"; `la dolcezza ... la attribuivano`, the object fronted and resumed by `la`, the resumption dropped in English; `più presto`, rather.

**`pel contrario gl'Italiani, più trattabili, più gentili, e di natura che la asperità più presto gli sdegnava che spaventava`.** "On the contrary the Italians, more tractable, more gentle, and of a nature that asperity rather made them indignant than frightened them": `trattabile`, tractable; `gentile`, gentle; `sdegnare`, make indignant (glossary `sdegno`, indignation); `spaventare`, frighten (D313). The clause has no verb of its own; `erano` is understood from the Germans' clause.

**`Però a questi essere necessario perdonare talvolta e' delitti, e procedere con benignità; quelli altri punirli severamente`.** "Therefore for these [it was] necessary to pardon at times the crimes, and to proceed with benignity; those others, to punish them severely": `Però`, therefore (D22); the infinitive `essere` of reported speech, still Barbarossa's, its tense supplied; `perdonare`, pardon; `delitti`, crimes (D252); `procedere`, proceed (glossary); `benignità`, benignity, with Machiavelli's `benigno`, benign (Machiavelli .01); `punire`, punish, kept apart from `pena`, penalty (D504); `quelli altri`, those others, the Germans, the farther named.

**`perché altrimenti diventerebbono più insolenti`.** "Because otherwise they would become more insolent": the stop at the paragraph's end, a split between the readers, follows the 1933 (FG-CONS-III19-D006; D110).

**`L'altra distinzione che mi occorre è che sia da fare differenza da uno che regga come principe e con autorità propria, da chi regge come ministro e in nome di altri`.** "The other distinction that occurs to me is that a difference is to be made between one who governs as a prince and with his own authority, and one who governs as a minister and in the name of others": the second paragraph, as Canestrini prints it; `essere da`, be to (D261); `autorità`, authority (glossary); `ministro`, minister (D504), one who acts for another; `in nome di altri`, in the name of others.

**`perché io credo che uno principe abbia a avere rispetto assai di cercare la benevolenza de' populi`.** "Because I believe that a prince is to have much respect to seeking the benevolence of the peoples": `credo`, I believe, the act of belief; `avere a`, be to (D261); `avere rispetto di`, have respect to (glossary `rispetto`); `assai`, much (D312); `benevolenza`, benevolence (D504); `populi`, peoples (D402), the plural kept.

**`potendo occorrere molti casi che a conservare lo Stato gli sia bisogno amore estraordinario de' populi`.** "Since many cases can occur in which, to conserve the state, he has need of extraordinary love of the peoples": `occorrere`, occur (SCOPE §3); `conservare`, conserve (glossary); `Stato`, state (glossary), the prince's state; `bisogno`, need (D260, kept apart from `bisognare`); `estraordinario`, extraordinary (D151).

**`Ma in chi comanda in nome di altri distinguerei: o in uno esercito, e allora fussi più necessario abbondare nello amore che nel timore`.** "But in one who commands in the name of others I would distinguish: either in an army, and then it would be more necessary to abound in love than in fear": `distinguere`, distinguish (D464); `comandare`, command; `esercito`, army; `abbondare`, abound (D504). The second member of `o ... o` is the `ma in chi governa` below.

**`perché avendoli a condurre a fazioni periculose per la città loro, vi si conducono assai con lo amore`.** "Because, having to conduct them to actions dangerous for their city, they are conducted there much with love": `avere a`, have to, of necessity (D261); `condurre`, conduct (D452), the soldiers, `li`, "lead" staying `menare`'s (D313); `fazioni`, actions, of military operations, the sense of D317 and D341, not the political "factions"; `periculose`, dangerous (glossary `periculo`); the form is provisional, between the two readers' `pericolose` and `periculose` (FG-CONS-III19-D003; O49); `assai`, much (D348). The 1933 edition reads `per la vita loro`, for their life; Canestrini's `per la città loro`, read by both readers, is kept, the actions dangerous for, or on behalf of, their city (FG-CONS-III19-D002; D38, D502).

**`ma in chi governa città o provincie in nome di altri, non gli toccando altro che la cura temporale, e non essendo lui il signore supremo`.** "But in one who governs cities or provinces in the name of others, nothing touching him but the temporary care, and he not being the supreme lord": `governare`, govern (D64); `provincie`, provinces (glossary); `toccare`, touch, of what falls to one's charge; `cura`, care, the charge of office, a sense split from D402's "cure", the physician's figure (D504); `temporale`, temporary, of tenure (glossary), not the temporal of rule, in the editor's reading, which the term of office below supports; `signore`, lord (glossary).

**`per il quale e' populi s'abbino a muovere a più di quello che ordinariamente sono tenuti`.** "For whom the peoples are to move themselves to more than they are ordinarily held to": `avere a`, be to (D261); `muoversi`, move oneself (D452); `ordinariamente`, ordinarily (D151); `tenuto`, held, bound.

**`credo conduca meglio le cose sue con qualche più terrore che e' principi ordinarii`.** "I believe he conducts his affairs better with somewhat more terror than ordinary princes [do]": `credo`, I believe, the second belief; `condurre le cose sue`, conduct his affairs (D452); `terrore`, terror, kept apart from `timore`, fear (D504); `principi ordinarii`, ordinary princes, those who rule in their own name (the editor's reading).

**`perché sapendo e' populi che le genti dependono da altri`.** "Because, the peoples knowing that the troops depend on others": `genti`, troops (glossary); who the troops are, and on whom they depend, is not said: the editor reads them as the forces in the governed places, which answer to the prince and not to the governor; the 1933 edition reads `le grazie`, the favours, which agrees with `fare grazie` at the close; Canestrini's `le genti`, read by both readers, is kept (FG-CONS-III19-D004; D38, D502); `dependere`, depend.

**`e che di qui a qualche tempo lui non ha a restare in ufficio`.** "And that some time hence he is not to remain in office": `di qui a qualche tempo`, some time hence, literally "from here to some time"; `avere a`, be to (D261), destinative under the negative, his tenure appointed to end, neither prohibitive (D414) nor an absence of necessity; `ufficio`, office (D56).

**`non può la benevolenza, che loro gli portassino, fare fondamento notabile a quelli effetti per e' quali si desidera tanto lo amore verso il principe`.** "The benevolence that they might bear him cannot make a notable foundation for those effects for which love towards the prince is so much desired": the fronted verb set in plain order (D263); `portassino`, might bear, the subjunctive of a supposed goodwill; `fondamento`, foundation (glossary); `notabile`, notable; `effetti`, effects (D410).

**`Dico però che parlando noi de' governi buoni e legittimi, si può male presupporre che dove è timore non sia anche amore`.** "I say therefore that, we speaking of good and legitimate governments, one can ill presuppose that where there is fear there is not also love": `però`, therefore (D22); `governo`, government (D262); `legittimo`, legitimate; `presupporre`, presuppose (glossary); `male`, ill. The comma after `amore`, where both readers doubt a semicolon, follows the 1933 (FG-CONS-III19-D006; D110).

**`perché la severità della giustizia, che è quella che reca il timore, non può essere che non sia amata da chi vuole bene vivere`.** "Because the severity of justice, which is that which brings fear, cannot be that it is not loved by whoever wishes to live well": `giustizia`, justice; `recare`, bring; `non può essere che non`, cannot be that ... not, the double negative kept; `bene vivere`, live well.

**`ed e converso lo amore che nasce da umanità, da facilità di natura, e da inclinazione a fare grazie`.** "And *e converso* the love that arises from humanity, from facility of nature, and from inclination to do graces": *e converso*, Latin, conversely, as at FG I.3 (latin.md); `nascere`, arise (D04); `facilità`, facility (glossary), Machiavelli's word for the too great facility of one who commands subjects (Machiavelli .04), here a quality of nature; `inclinazione`, inclination; `fare grazie`, do graces, grant favours (D504).

**`accompagnato dalla giustizia, come in uno governo buono s'ha a presupporre, non può fare che non sia temuto`.** "Accompanied by justice, as is to be presupposed in a good government, cannot make that it is not feared": `avere a`, be to (D261); `non può fare che non`, cannot make that ... not, the double negative kept, answering `non può essere che non` above. Canestrini's stop at the end of the reply is printed, as both readers saw it.
