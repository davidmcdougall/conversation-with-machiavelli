# Consideration on chapter XXIV

## FG-CONS.3.24.01

There is no doubt that the prorogation of commands was a great occasion to whoever wished to occupy the republic; because it was an instrument for making friends of the soldiers, and a following with kings and in foreign nations or provinces, and for the captains it increased wealth, with which they could corrupt men, as the long command of Gaul did for Caesar. But the principal foundation of the ills was the corruption of the city, which, having given itself to avarice, to delights, had degenerated from the ancient customs in such a way that there arose from it the sanguinary divisions of the city, from which in free peoples one always comes to tyrannies. From there arose the facility of corrupting the citizens, the soldiers; from here one Catiline could hope, without command and without armies, to occupy the republic; from here [a] conspiracy of several powerful [men] to divide among themselves the commands and the armies, and with these forces to hold the others low; from here the extraordinary prorogations of commands, as was that of Caesar, for whom not the utility of the republic, not the necessity of war, not the admiration of his *virtù*, but the conspiracy with Pompey and Crassus to occupy the republic, made [a] decemviral command. The command had not been prorogued to Sulla when he came to blows with Marius the first time, but the cause of it was the division between the nobility and the plebs, and, the plebs having Marius for head, the nobility was forced to seek a head for itself. Therefore I conclude that when Rome was not corrupted, the prorogations of commands and the continuation of the consulship, which in difficult times they used many times, were a useful and holy thing; but, the city corrupted, there rose the civil battles and the seeds of tyrannies, even without the prorogation of commands. And therefore one can conclude that, if there had not been the prorogations as well, there would not have failed, either to Caesar or to the others who occupied the republic, either the thought or the capacity to trouble it by another path.

## Notes

**The heading and the rubric.** Canestrini's heading, CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO XXIV, without `del libro III`, as both readers saw it; a faint trace after XXIV, doubted by both, is not read as a stop, the 1933 printing none (D443, D110, D511). His rubric is Machiavelli's sentence (Machiavelli, rubric) with `degl'imperii` for `degli Imperj`, two forms of one word, and with its printed stop (FG-CONS-III24-D008). This is the last of Guicciardini's replies: Canestrini prints none to chapters XX to XXIII of Book III and none after this one, and the next page of his volume is the half-title of the *Ricordi*, which both cold readers had as boundary evidence (sources/coldpass/COLLATION-2026-11-25-III24.md). His note to this reply, a summary of Machiavelli's two causes and two inconveniences, is not carried (D511).

**`Non è dubio che la prorogazione degli imperii fu occasione grande a chi volle occupare la republica`.** "There is no doubt that the prorogation of commands was a great occasion to whoever wished to occupy the republic": `Non è dubio`, there is no doubt, the reply's first act: assent to Machiavelli's claim, narrowed from a cause to an occasion; the cause he gives is the city's corruption; `prorogazione`, prorogation (D512), Machiavelli's second word (Machiavelli .05), not his rubric's `prolungazione`; `imperii`, commands (D124), throughout the reply; `occasione`, occasion (glossary); `a chi volle`, to whoever wished, the past (`volle`) of those who in fact wished; `occupare`, occupy (glossary).

**`perché era instrumento da farsi amici i soldati e séguito co' re, e nelle nazioni o provincie forestiere`.** "Because it was an instrument for making friends of the soldiers, and a following with kings and in foreign nations or provinces": `instrumento`, instrument (D452); `farsi amici`, make friends of (glossary `farsi amico`); `séguito`, a following, the noun, with the accent both readers saw, as `ség[u]ito` at FG I.29 .02; `forestiere`, foreign (glossary). The 1933 edition reads `e provincie`, and provinces (FG-CONS-III24-D001; D38, D510).

**`e a' capitani accresceva ricchezza con la quale potevano corrompere gli uomini, come fece a Cesare il lungo imperio di Gallia`.** "And for the captains it increased wealth, with which they could corrupt men, as the long command of Gaul did for Caesar": `a' capitani`, for the captains, the dative of advantage; `capitani`, captains (glossary); `accrescere`, increase (D69); `ricchezza`, wealth; `corrompere`, corrupt (glossary); `fece`, did, the verb standing for `accrescere ricchezza`. Caesar held the command of Gaul from 58 to 49 BC (the editor's reference). The 1933 edition reads `in Gallia`, in Gaul (FG-CONS-III24-D002; D38, D510).

**`Ma il fondamento principale de' mali fu la corruzione della città`.** "But the principal foundation of the ills was the corruption of the city": `fondamento`, foundation (glossary); `mali`, ills (glossary `male`); `corruzione`, corruption, with `corrotto`, corrupted (glossary). The claim is set against Machiavelli's: not the prorogation but the city's corruption. The word `corrotto` is not in Machiavelli's chapter.

**`la quale datasi alla avarizia, alle delizie, era in modo degenerata dagli antichi costumi, che ne nacquono le divisioni sanguinose della città`.** "Which, having given itself to avarice, to delights, had degenerated from the ancient customs in such a way that there arose from it the sanguinary divisions of the city": `avarizia`, avarice (glossary); `delizie`, delights (D480); the asyndeton kept; `in modo ... che`, in such a way that (glossary), its order set plain (D263); `era degenerata`, had degenerated, the pluperfect with `essere`; `degenerare`, degenerate (D512); `costumi`, customs (D41); `nascere`, arise (D04); `sanguinoso`, sanguinary (D512).

**`dalle quali sempre ne' populi liberi si viene alle tirannidi`.** "From which in free peoples one always comes to tyrannies": `populi`, peoples (glossary); `tirannide`, tyranny (glossary).

**`Di quivi nacque la facilità di corrompere e' cittadini e' soldati`.** "From there arose the facility of corrupting the citizens, the soldiers": `Di quivi`, from there, and `di qui`, from here, the next three times: two adverbs, kept apart; `facilità`, facility (glossary); `e' cittadini e' soldati`, the two articles in asyndeton, as the 1933 sets them (`e' cittadini, e' soldati`); Canestrini prints the first article bare, `e cittadini`, set `e'` (D35). The second `e'` could also be read as the conjunction with the article understood, "the citizens and the soldiers" (the editor's note). The series that follows runs in commas in the Italian, with one semicolon before its last member, `di qui le prorogazioni`; the English sets semicolons between the members because of the comma inside the first.

**`di qui potette sperare uno Catilina sanza imperio e sanza eserciti occupare la republica`.** "From here one Catiline could hope, without command and without armies, to occupy the republic": the fronted verb set in plain order (D263), the two `sanza` phrases kept before the infinitive; `uno Catilina`, one Catiline, the narrative indefinite kept; Catiline as names.md; `sanza imperio`, without command.

**`di qui conjurazione di più potenti di dividersi fra loro gli imperii e gli eserciti, e con queste forze tenere bassi gli altri`.** "From here [a] conspiracy of several powerful [men] to divide among themselves the commands and the armies, and with these forces to hold the others low": `conjurazione`, conspiracy (D512), with `congiurare`, conspire (D452); "[a]" and "[men]" supplied (D53); `più potenti`, several powerful, `più` before the plural pointing to the three of the next sentence; "more powerful", "the more powerful", is the other reading (the editor's note); `forze`, forces (glossary); `tenere bassi`, hold low (D512).

**`di qui le prorogazioni estraordinarie degli imperii, come fu quella di Cesare`.** "From here the extraordinary prorogations of commands, as was that of Caesar": `estraordinario`, extraordinary (glossary).

**`al quale non la utilità della republica, non la necessità della guerra, non la ammirazione della sua virtù, ma la conjurazione con Pompeo e Crasso di occupare la republica, fece imperio decemvirale`.** "For whom not the utility of the republic, not the necessity of war, not the admiration of his *virtù*, but the conspiracy with Pompey and Crassus to occupy the republic, made [a] decemviral command": `al quale`, for whom, the dative of advantage; `fece`, made, created: a command made for him, not his command altered; the three denials and the one cause kept before the verb, a catalogue (D263, D194); `utilità`, utility (D472), Machiavelli's `utilità pubblica` (Machiavelli .04); `necessità`, necessity (glossary); `ammirazione`, admiration; *virtù* untranslated (glossary). `Pompeo e Crasso`, Pompey (names.md) and Crassus (names.md, D512): both readers doubted the `e`, the shape near to `o`, and preferred it; read with the 1933 (FG-CONS-III24-D003; D510). `imperio decemvirale`, "[a] decemviral command", as both readers read Canestrini; the 1933 edition reads `decennale`, decennial, of ten years, which is what Caesar's command in Gaul became when it was extended for a further five years in 55 BC (the editor's reference). `decemvirale`, of the decemvirs, gives no sense that any source supports; it is kept provisionally as the governing witness prints it (FG-CONS-III24-D004; O50).

**`Non era stato prorogato lo imperio a Silla, quando la prima volta venne alle mani con Mario`.** "The command had not been prorogued to Sulla when he came to blows with Marius the first time": the fronted verb set in plain order (D263); `venire alle mani`, come to blows; Sulla and Marius (names.md), whose first civil war is of 88 BC (the editor's reference). Guicciardini answers Machiavelli's `Per questo Silla e Mario` (Machiavelli .05).

**`ma ne fu causa la divisione tra la nobilità e la plebe, e avendo la plebe per capo Mario, fu forzata la nobilità cercarsi uno capo`.** "But the cause of it was the division between the nobility and the plebs, and, the plebs having Marius for head, the nobility was forced to seek a head for itself": `causa`, cause (glossary, a recorded collapse with `cagione`); `nobilità`, nobility (glossary `Nobiltà`); `plebe`, plebs (glossary); `capo`, head (glossary) ×2; `forzare`, force (D512), as `sforzare` (D496); `cercarsi`, seek for itself.

**`Però conchiuggo che quando Roma non fu corrotta, le prorogazioni degli imperii e la continuazione del consulato, la quale ne' tempi difficili usorono molte volte, furono cosa utile e santa`.** "Therefore I conclude that when Rome was not corrupted, the prorogations of commands and the continuation of the consulship, which in difficult times they used many times, were a useful and holy thing": `Però`, therefore (D22), consecutive; `conchiuggo`, I conclude, the printed form of `conchiudo`, read by both; `continuazione`, continuation; `consulato`, consulship (D410); `usare`, use (D313); `santo`, holy (D313), set beside useful. The 1933 edition repeats `che` after the temporal clause (FG-CONS-III24-D005; D38, D510).

**`ma corrotta la città, sursono le battaglie civili, e i semi delle tirannidi, etiam sanza la prorogazione degli imperii`.** "But, the city corrupted, there rose the civil battles and the seeds of tyrannies, even without the prorogation of commands": `surgere`, rise (D358); `battaglie`, battles; `semi`, seeds; `etiam`, even (D453).

**`E però si può conchiudere, che se non fussino state anche le prorogazioni`.** "And therefore one can conclude that, if there had not been the prorogations as well": `però`, therefore (D22); `si può conchiudere`, one can conclude, impersonal after the first person; `anche`, as well, also.

**`non sarebbe mancato né a Cesare né agli altri che occuporono la republica, né pensiero né facultà di travagliarla per altra via`.** "There would not have failed, either to Caesar or to the others who occupied the republic, either the thought or the capacity to trouble it by another path": `mancare a`, fail to (D206), the impersonal construction kept, the subjects after the verb; the four `né` after the negative verb set as "either ... or" twice; `pensiero`, thought; `facultà`, capacity (glossary); `travagliare`, trouble (D512), transitive, of the republic, with `travagliarsi`, be troubled (D480); `via`, path (D131), `modo` staying "way". Canestrini's printed stop ends the reply (D460).
