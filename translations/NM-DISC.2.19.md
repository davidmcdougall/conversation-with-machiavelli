# Discourses II.19

## NM-DISC.2.19.01

These opinions contrary to the truth, founded upon the ill examples that have been introduced by these our corrupted centuries, make men not think of deviating from the accustomed ways. When could one have persuaded an Italian, thirty years back, that ten thousand foot could assail on a plain ten thousand horse and as many foot, and with them not only fight but vanquish them, as is seen by the example, cited by us more times, at Novara?

## NM-DISC.2.19.02

And albeit the histories are full of it, *tamen* they would not have lent faith to it; and if they had lent faith to it, they would have said that in these times one arms better, and that a squadron of men-at-arms would be apt to dash against a rock, let alone an infantry; and so with these false excuses they would have corrupted their judgment: nor would they have considered that Lucullus with few foot routed a hundred and fifty thousand horse of Tigranes, and that among those horsemen there was a sort of cavalry similar altogether to our men-at-arms; and so this fallacy has been uncovered by the example of the ultramontane troops.

## NM-DISC.2.19.03

And as it is seen by that to be true, as to infantry, that which is narrated in the histories, so they should believe all the other ancient orders to be true and useful. And when this were believed, republics and princes would err less, would be stronger to oppose an onset that came upon them, would not hope in flight, and those who had a civil living in their hands would know better how to direct it, either by the path of expanding or by the path of maintaining, and would believe that to increase their city with inhabitants, to make companions for themselves and not subjects, to send colonies to guard the acquired countries, to make capital of the spoils, to subdue the enemy with raids and with battles and not with sieges, to keep the public rich, the private poor, to maintain military exercises with supreme study, are the paths to make a republic great and to acquire empire.

## NM-DISC.2.19.04

And when this way of expanding did not please him, he would think that acquisitions by every other path are the ruin of republics, and would put a rein on every ambition, regulating his city well within with the laws and with the customs, prohibiting to it acquiring, and only thinking of defending himself, and keeping the defences well ordered, as the republics of Germany do, which in these ways live and have lived free [for] a time. Nonetheless, as I said another time, when I discoursed of the difference there was from ordering oneself to acquire to ordering oneself to maintain, it is impossible that standing quiet, and enjoying its liberty and the few borders, should succeed for a republic; because if it will not molest others, it will be molested itself; and from being molested there will arise in it the longing and the necessity of acquiring; and when it had not the enemy outside, it would find him at home, as it seems necessary [that it] befall all great cities. And if the republics of Germany can themselves live in that way, and have been able to last a time, it arises from certain conditions that are in that country, which are not elsewhere, without which they could not hold a similar way of living.

## NM-DISC.2.19.05

That part of Germany of which I speak was subject to the Roman empire, as France and Spain [were]; but, the empire having afterwards come into declination, and the title of such empire having been reduced into that province, those more powerful cities began, according to the baseness or necessity of the emperors, to make themselves free, buying themselves back from the empire with reserving to it a small annual tax; so much that little by little all those cities that were immediately of the emperor, and were not subject to any prince, have in a similar way bought themselves back.

## NM-DISC.2.19.06

It occurred in these same times that these cities were buying themselves back that certain communities subject to the Duke of Austria rebelled from him, among which was Filiburgo and the Swiss and similar [ones]; which, prospering in the beginning, took little by little so much augmentation that, not that they have returned under the yoke of Austria, they are [held] in fear by all their neighbours; and these are those who are called Swiss. This province is, then, partitioned into Swiss, republics, which they call Free Lands, princes, and emperor. And the cause that among so many diversities of living wars do not arise there, or, if they arise there, do not last long, is that sign of the emperor; who, though he has no forces, nonetheless has among them so much reputation that he is a conciliator of theirs, and, interposing himself with his authority as a mediator, extinguishes at once every scandal. And the greatest and the longest wars that there have been there are those that followed between the Swiss and the Duke of Austria; and albeit for many years now the emperor and the Duke of Austria have been one same thing, not on that account has he ever been able to overcome the audacity of the Swiss, where there has never been a way of accord, if not by force. Nor has the rest of Germany held out many aids to him, both because the communities do not know how to offend whoever wishes to live free as they [do], and because those princes in part cannot, through being poor, in part do not wish to, through having envy of his power.

## NM-DISC.2.19.07

Those communities can, then, live content with their small dominion, through not having cause, in respect of the imperial authority, to desire it greater; they can live united within their walls, through having the enemy near, and [one] that would take the occasion of occupying them whenever they were in discord. For if that province were conditioned otherwise, it would be fitting for them to seek to expand, and to break that quiet of theirs. And because elsewhere there are not such conditions, one cannot take this way of living, and it is required either to expand by paths of leagues, or to expand as the Romans.

## NM-DISC.2.19.08

And whoever governs himself otherwise seeks not his life, but his death and ruin; because in a thousand ways and for many causes acquisitions are damaging, because it goes very well together, acquiring empire and not forces; and whoever acquires empire and not forces together, it is fitting that he go to ruin. He cannot acquire forces who grows poor in wars, even though he be victorious, for he puts in more than he draws from the acquisitions; as the Venetians and the Florentines have done, who have been much weaker when the one had Lombardy and the other Tuscany than they were when the one was content with the sea, and the other with six miles of borders. Because all has arisen from having wished to acquire, and not having known how to take the way; and so much the more they merit blame, as they have less excuse, having seen the way that the Romans have held, and having been able to follow their example, when the Romans, without any example, through their prudence, knew how to find it by themselves.

## NM-DISC.2.19.09

Acquisitions, besides this, sometimes do no mediocre damage to every well-ordered republic, when one acquires a city or a province full of delights, where one can take some of those customs through the conversation that one has with them; as befell Rome, first in the acquisition of Capua, and afterwards Hannibal. And if Capua had been more far-off from the city, and the error of the soldiers had not had the remedy near, or Rome had been in any part corrupted, that acquisition would without doubt have been the ruin of the Roman republic. And Livy bears witness of this with these words: *Jam tunc minime salubris militari disciplinae Capua, instrumentum omnium voluptatum, delinitos militum animos avertit a memoria patriae.* And truly, similar cities or provinces avenge themselves against the victor without engagement and without blood, because, filling them with their wicked customs, they expose them to be vanquished by whoever assails them. And Juvenal could not better in his Satires have considered this part, saying that into Roman breasts, through the acquisitions of peregrine lands, peregrine customs had entered, and in exchange for parsimony and other most excellent *virtù*, *gula, et luxuria incubuit, victumque ulciscitur orbem.*

## NM-DISC.2.19.10

If, then, acquiring was about to be pernicious to the Romans, in the times when they proceeded with so much prudence and so much *virtù*, what will it be, then, to those who proceed at a distance from their ways? And [to those] who, besides the other errors that they make, of which enough has been discoursed above, avail themselves of soldiers either mercenary or auxiliary? Whence there often result for them those damages of which mention will be made in the following chapter.

## Notes

**The rubric.** `Che gli acquisti nelle Repubbliche non bene ordinate, e che secondo la romana virtù non procedono, sono a rovina, non a esaltazione di esse.` "That acquisitions in republics not well ordered, and that do not proceed according to Roman *virtù*, are to their ruin, not to their exaltation": `acquisto`, acquisition (glossary), with `acquistare`, acquire, the chapter's verb throughout; `ordinare`, order (glossary): the republics not well ordered; `procedere`, proceed, its subject the republics, coordinated with `non bene ordinate`, as at .10, where the Romans `procedevano` and the others `procedono`: the rubric limits which republics acquisitions ruin, not which acquisitions; *virtù* (glossary); `rovina`, ruin (glossary); `esaltazione`, exaltation; `di esse`, of them, the republics, rendered "their" where English sets the possessive before the noun. The 1554 heads the chapter with the same sentence, `procedano` for `procedono`, `ruina` for `rovina` (NM-DISC-II19-D009).

**`Queste contrarie opinioni alla verità`.** "These opinions contrary to the truth": `opinione`, opinion (D02); the chapter opens on the close of II.18, where Machiavelli argued that infantry is to be esteemed above cavalry; "these opinions" are the ones II.18 opposed (the editor's link).

**`fondate in sui mali esempj, che da questi nostri corrotti secoli sono stati introdotti`.** "Founded upon the ill examples that have been introduced by these our corrupted centuries": `in sui`, upon; `mali`, ill, the adjective kept apart from `tristo`, wicked (.09), and `cattivo`, bad (D03); `esempio`, example (glossary), with `esempj` once and the singular four times in the chapter; `corrotto`, corrupted (glossary), as at .09 (`in alcuna parte corrotta`); `secolo`, century (D422).

**`fanno che gli uomini non pensano a deviare dai consueti modi`.** "Make men not think of deviating from the accustomed ways": `fare che` with the indicative, rendered "make ... not think"; `modo`, way (glossary).

**`da trenta anni in dietro`.** "Thirty years back": before the French descent of 1494, by the editor's reckoning from a chapter written about 1517.

**`diecimila fanti ... diecimila cavalli`.** "Ten thousand foot ... ten thousand horse": the arms named as English names them; `fanteria`, infantry (.02, .03), `cavalleria`, cavalry (.02).

**`come si vede per lo esempio da noi più volte allegato a Novara`.** "As is seen by the example, cited by us more times, at Novara": `allegare`, cite (D431); `più volte`, more times (D373). The example is the Swiss foot at Novara in 1513, given in II.18 (the editor's identification).

**`E benché le Istorie ne siano piene, tamen`.** "And albeit the histories are full of it, *tamen*": `benché`, albeit (D31); `Istorie`, histories (glossary), lower case in English; `tamen`, Latin, "nonetheless", left in italic as printed (latin.md). The 1554 reads the Italian `nondimeno` (NM-DISC-II19-D001).

**`non ci arebbero prestato fede`.** "They would not have lent faith to it": `prestare fede`, lend faith; `fede`, faith (glossary); `ci`, to it, to the histories' report.

**`s'arma meglio`.** "One arms better": impersonal, of the present day's armour.

**`una squadra di uomini d'arme sarebbe atta ad urtare uno scoglio, non che una fanteria`.** "A squadron of men-at-arms would be apt to dash against a rock, let alone an infantry": `uomini d'arme`, men-at-arms, as `genti d'arme` (D313); `non che`, scalar, "let alone" (D226).

**`e così con queste false scuse corrompevano il giudicio loro`.** "And so with these false excuses they would have corrupted their judgment": the imperfect inside the chain of unreal conditionals (`arebbero detto ... né arebbero considerato`), an unreal past, as at .09 (D473); `scusa`, excuse (with `scusa` at .08); `giudicio`, judgment (D374); `corrompere`, corrupt (glossary), the root of `corrotti` (.01).

**`Lucullo con pochi fanti ruppe cento cinquantamila cavalli di Tigrane`.** "Lucullus with few foot routed a hundred and fifty thousand horse of Tigranes": `rompere`, rout (D431). The battle is Tigranocerta, 69 BC (the editor's identification); the figure is Machiavelli's and is not corrected.

**`una sorte di cavalleria`.** "A sort of cavalry": `sorte`, sort, a kind, a sense split from D452's "lot" (`per sorte o per elezione`), decided by sense and recorded (D480).

**`e così questa fallacia è stata scoperta dallo esempio delle genti oltramontane`.** "And so this fallacy has been uncovered by the example of the ultramontane troops": `fallacia`, fallacy; `scoprire`, uncover (D452, D464); `genti`, troops (glossary): the ultramontane infantries of II.18, the Swiss at Novara among them (the editor's identification); `oltramontano`, ultramontane (D422).

**`E come e' si vede per quello esser vero ... così doverebbero credere`.** "And as it is seen by that to be true ... so they should believe": `per quello`, by that [example]; `doverebbero`, conditional of `dovere`, "should", extending D348 to the conditional (D480), apart from `debbe`, must (D274); the subject is the men of .01.

**`essere veri ed utili tutti gli altri ordini antichi`.** "All the other ancient orders to be true and useful": `ordine`, order (glossary). The 1554 reads `et inutili`, "and useless", which reverses the sense (NM-DISC-II19-D002).

**`E quando questo fusse creduto`.** "And when this were believed": the subjunctive kept with "were".

**`sariano più forti ad opporsi ad uno impeto che venisse loro addosso, non spererebbero nella fuga`.** "Would be stronger to oppose an onset that came upon them, would not hope in flight": `impeto`, onset (glossary); `fuga`, flight.

**`e quelli che avessero nelle mani un vivere civile, lo saperebbero meglio indirizzare, o per la via dello ampliare, o per la via del mantenere`.** "And those who had a civil living in their hands would know better how to direct it, either by the path of expanding or by the path of maintaining": `vivere civile`, civil living (glossary); `via`, path (D131); `ampliare`, expand (D132); `mantenere`, maintain (glossary): the two ends of I.6, ordering to acquire or to maintain (the editor's link).

**`l'accrescere la Città sua d'abitatori, farsi compagni e non sudditi`.** "To increase their city with inhabitants, to make companions for themselves and not subjects": `accrescere`, increase (D69); `sua`, "their", the singular possessive for the plural `quelli`; `farsi compagni`, make companions for oneself, the reflexive kept, as II.13 (D452): Rome's allies; `sudditi`, subjects.

**`mandare colonie a guardare i paesi acquistati, far capitale delle prede`.** "To send colonies to guard the acquired countries, to make capital of the spoils": `paese`, country (glossary); `far capitale`, make capital, set by as the public's stock; `preda`, spoil.

**`domare il nimico con le scorrerie e con le Giornate, e non con le ossidioni`.** "To subdue the enemy with raids and with battles and not with sieges": `domare`, subdue (D452); `nimico`, enemy (D334); `Giornate`, battles (D49), capitalised in the 1824, not in English; `ossidione`, siege.

**`tener ricco il Pubblico, povero il privato, mantenere con sommo studio gli esercizj militari`.** "To keep the public rich, the private poor, to maintain military exercises with supreme study": `Pubblico`, the public (glossary); `studio`, study, application; `esercizio`, exercise. The 1554 reads `eſſerciti`, armies (NM-DISC-II19-D002). The list is Rome's way, set out at length in II.3-II.6 (the editor's link).

**`sono le vie a far grande una Repubblica ed acquistare imperio`.** "Are the paths to make a republic great and to acquire empire": `imperio`, empire, dominion over others (D124).

**`E quando questo modo dello ampliare non gli piacesse, penserebbe`.** "And when this way of expanding did not please him, he would think": `gli` may be the old Tuscan plural dative; the singular is certain from `penserebbe`, and "him" and "he" follow it.

**`porrebbe freno ad ogni ambizione`.** "Would put a rein on every ambition": `freno`, rein (D389); `porre`, set or put (D115).

**`regolando bene la sua Città dentro con le leggi e co' costumi, proibendogli l'acquistare`.** "Regulating his city well within with the laws and with the customs, prohibiting to it acquiring": `costume`, custom (D41); `proibire`, prohibit (glossary); `gli`, to it, the city. The 1554 has `uietandole d'acquiſtare`, the same sense in another verb (NM-DISC-II19-D003).

**`e le difese tenere ordinate bene`.** "And keeping the defences well ordered": the infinitive governed, with `difendersi`, by `pensando a`, so the English gerund follows "thinking of"; `ordinato`, ordered.

**`come fanno le Repubbliche della Magna, le quali in questi modi vivono e sono vivute libere un tempo`.** "As the republics of Germany do, which in these ways live and have lived free [for] a time": `la Magna`, Germany (names.md); `vivute`, the old participle of `vivere`; `un tempo`, a time.

**`Nondimeno, come altra volta dissi, quando discorsi la differenza ch'era da ordinarsi per acquistare, a ordinarsi per mantenere`.** "Nonetheless, as I said another time, when I discoursed of the difference there was from ordering oneself to acquire to ordering oneself to maintain": `nondimeno`, nonetheless (D45); `altra volta`, another time (glossary); `discorrere`, discourse (glossary, SCOPE §3); `da ... a`, from ... to, the two terms of the difference. The other time is Discourses I.6 (the editor's identification).

**`è impossibile che ad una Repubblica riesca lo stare quieta, e godersi la sua libertà e gli pochi confini`.** "It is impossible that standing quiet, and enjoying its liberty and the few borders, should succeed for a republic": `riuscire`, succeed; `stare quieta`, stand quiet, the adjective agreeing with the republic; `confini`, borders (glossary), with the article and no possessive, as printed.

**`perché se lei non molesterà altrui, sarà molestata ella; e dall'essere molestata gli nascerà la voglia, e la necessità dello acquistare`.** "Because if it will not molest others, it will be molested itself; and from being molested there will arise in it the longing and the necessity of acquiring": `molestare`, molest, of active harassment, apart from FG I.29's `molesto`, irksome, a split recorded (D480); `nascere`, arise (D04); `voglia`, longing (D117). The 1554 omits `e dall'essere molestata` and runs `ella le naſcera`, so that the longing arises without the cause the 1824 gives it (NM-DISC-II19-D003).

**`e quando non avesse il nimico fuora, lo troverebbe in casa, come pare necessario intervenga a tutte le grandi Cittadi`.** "And when it had not the enemy outside, it would find him at home, as it seems necessary [that it] befall all great cities": `casa`, home (D444); `intervenire`, befall (D298); `Cittadi`, cities, the old plural, as at .05. The 1554 reads `a tutti li grandi cittadini`, "all great citizens" (NM-DISC-II19-D003).

**`E se le Repubbliche della Magna possono vivere loro in quel modo`.** "And if the republics of Germany can live in that way": `loro`, the emphatic subject, "themselves"; the 1554 has `eſſe` (NM-DISC-II19-D003).

**`nasce da certe condizioni che sono in quel paese, le quali non sono altrove, senza le quali non potrebbero tenere simil modo di vivere`.** "It arises from certain conditions that are in that country, which are not elsewhere, without which they could not hold a similar way of living": `condizione`, condition, with `condizionata` (.07); `altrove`, elsewhere (glossary); `tenere`, hold.

**`Era quella parte della Magna di che io parlo, sottoposta all'Imperio romano come la Francia e la Spagna`.** "That part of Germany of which I speak was subject to the Roman empire, as France and Spain [were]": `Imperio`, empire (D124), lower case in English throughout the chapter; `sottoposto`, subject. The 1554 reads `queſta parte` (NM-DISC-II19-D004).

**`ma venuto dipoi in declinazione l'Imperio, e ridottosi il titolo di tal Imperio in quella Provincia`.** "But, the empire having afterwards come into declination, and the title of such empire having been reduced into that province": `dipoi`, afterwards (D187); `declinazione`, declination; `ridursi`, be reduced, of the title that came to rest in Germany; `provincia`, province (glossary).

**`cominciarono quelle Cittadi più potenti, secondo la viltà o necessità degli Imperatori, a farsi libere, ricomperandosi dallo Imperio con riservargli un piccolo censo annuario`.** "Those more powerful cities began, according to the baseness or necessity of the emperors, to make themselves free, buying themselves back from the empire with reserving to it a small annual tax": `viltà`, baseness (D444); `ricomperarsi`, buy oneself back; `censo`, tax; `annuario`, annual (the 1554 `annuale`, NM-DISC-II19-D004).

**`tutte quelle Cittadi, ch'erano immediate dello Imperatore`.** "All those cities that were immediately of the emperor": `immediate`, immediately (glossary), without intermediary: held of the emperor with no prince between.

**`Occorse in questi medesimi tempi`.** "It occurred in these same times": `occorrere`, occur, the *currere* family (SCOPE §3).

**`certe Comunità sottoposte al Duca d'Austria si ribellarono da lui, tra le quali fu Filiburgo e i Svizzeri e simili`.** "Certain communities subject to the Duke of Austria rebelled from him, among which was Filiburgo and the Swiss and similar [ones]": `Comunità`, communities; `ribellarsi da`, rebel from; `Filiburgo` left as printed: Fribourg is the usual identification (the editor's), and the 1554 prints `Filiborg` (NM-DISC-II19-D005); the singular `fu` agreeing with the first member, held as "was".

**`pigliarono a poco a poco tanto augumento, che non che e' sieno tornati sotto il giogo d'Austria, sono in timore a tutti i loro vicini`.** "Took little by little so much augmentation that, not that they have returned under the yoke of Austria, they are [held] in fear by all their neighbours": `pigliare`, take (glossary); `augumento`, augmentation (D69); `non che`, "not that", the clause it opens denied and surpassed: far from returning under the yoke, they are feared. A third use of `non che`, beside D410's additive "nor that" and D226's scalar "let alone", decided by sense and recorded (D480). `timore`, fear (glossary); `essere in timore a`, be [held] in fear by, the bracket supplying the passive sense the English needs (D53).

**`È adunque questa Provincia compartita in Svizzeri, Repubbliche, che chiamano Terre franche, Principi, ed Imperatore`.** "This province is, then, partitioned into Swiss, republics, which they call Free Lands, princes, and emperor": `adunque`, then (D74); `compartire`, partition; `Terre franche`, Free Lands, capitalised as a title, the free imperial cities (the editor's identification); `terra`, land.

**`E la cagione che tra tante diversità di vivere non vi nascono, o se le vi nascono, non vi durano molto le guerre`.** "And the cause that among so many diversities of living wars do not arise there, or, if they arise there, do not last long": `cagione`, cause (glossary); the subject `le guerre`, held to the end in the Italian, its pronoun `le` anticipating it, is set before its verb in the English (D263).

**`è quel segno dell'Imperatore; il quale, avvenga che non abbia forze, nondimeno ha fra loro tanta riputazione`.** "Is that sign of the emperor; who, though he has no forces, nonetheless has among them so much reputation": `segno`, sign: the emperor's name and standing as a token of the empire; `avvenga che`, though (glossary, with `avvegnaché`); `riputazione`, reputation.

**`ch'egli è uno loro conciliatore, e con l'autorità sua interponendosi come mezzano, spegne subito ogni scandalo`.** "That he is a conciliator of theirs, and, interposing himself with his authority as a mediator, extinguishes at once every scandal": `autorità`, authority (glossary); `mezzano`, mediator; `spegnere`, extinguish (glossary); `scandalo`, scandal, in the old sense of a quarrel that gives offence.

**`E le maggiori e le più lunghe guerre che vi siano state, sono quelle che furono seguite tra i Svizzeri e il Duca d'Austria`.** "And the greatest and the longest wars that there have been there are those that followed between the Swiss and the Duke of Austria": `seguire`, follow, of events that ensue. The 1554 reads `ſono ſeguite` (NM-DISC-II19-D005).

**`e benché da molti anni in qua l'Imperatore ed il Duca d'Austria sia una cosa medesima, non pertanto non ha mai potuto superare l'audacia dei Svizzeri`.** "And albeit for many years now the emperor and the Duke of Austria have been one same thing, not on that account has he ever been able to overcome the audacity of the Swiss": the emperor a Habsburg since 1438 (the editor's note); `pertanto`, on that account (D98), with the negative; `superare`, overcome (D313); `audacia`, audacity.

**`dove non è mai stato modo d'accordo, se non per forza`.** "Where there has never been a way of accord, if not by force": `modo`, way; `accordo`, accord (D452).

**`Né il resto della Magna gli ha porti molti ajuti`.** "Nor has the rest of Germany held out many aids to him": `porgere`, hold out, offer; `ajuto`, aid (glossary, as II.15).

**`sì perché le Comunità non sanno offendere chi vuole vivere libero come loro, sì perché quelli Principi, parte non possono per essere poveri, parte non vogliono per aver invidia alla potenza sua`.** "Both because the communities do not know how to offend whoever wishes to live free as they [do], and because those princes in part cannot, through being poor, in part do not wish to, through having envy of his power": `offendere`, offend, the older sense, injure (glossary); `vivere libero`, live free; `invidia`, envy (glossary); `potenza`, power (D06); `sua`, his, the emperor's. The 1554 has `uogliano`, subjunctive, and `alla potenza loro`, their power, the Swiss' (NM-DISC-II19-D005).

**`Possono vivere adunque quelle Comunità contente del piccolo loro dominio, per non aver cagione, rispetto all'autorità imperiale, di desiderarlo maggiore`.** "Those communities can, then, live content with their small dominion, through not having cause, in respect of the imperial authority, to desire it greater": `dominio`, dominion (glossary); `rispetto a`, in respect of, because of.

**`per aver il nimico propinquo, e che piglierebbe l'occasione d'occuparle, qualunque volta le discordassero`.** "Through having the enemy near, and [one] that would take the occasion of occupying them whenever they were in discord": `propinquo`, near (glossary); `occasione`, occasion (glossary); `discordare`, be in discord. The 1554 reads `uicino` (NM-DISC-II19-D006).

**`Che se quella Provincia fusse condizionata altrimenti, converrebbe loro cercare d'ampliare, e rompere quella loro quiete`.** "For if that province were conditioned otherwise, it would be fitting for them to seek to expand, and to break that quiet of theirs": `che`, for, causal; `convenire`, impersonal, be fitting (D85); `rompere`, break, of a state of things, "rout" (D431) staying for an army (.02), a sense split (D480); `quiete`, quiet.

**`non si può prendere questo modo di vivere`.** "One cannot take this way of living": `prendere`, take, with `pigliare` (D472, a recorded collapse), as at .09.

**`e bisogna o ampliare per vie di Leghe, o ampliare come i Romani`.** "And it is required either to expand by paths of leagues, or to expand as the Romans": `bisogna`, "it is required" (D260): see the note on `bisogni` at Machiavelli I.2. The two ways are those of II.4, the leagues of the Tuscans and of the Swiss, and the Roman way of companions (the editor's link).

**`E chi si governa altrimenti cerca non la sua vita, ma la sua morte e rovina`.** "And whoever governs himself otherwise seeks not his life, but his death and ruin": `governarsi`, govern oneself; `vita`, life.

**`perché in mille modi e per molte cagioni gli acquisti sono dannosi`.** "Because in a thousand ways and for many causes acquisitions are damaging": `dannoso`, damaging (D106).

**`perché egli sta molto bene insieme acquistare Imperio e non forze`.** "Because it goes very well together, acquiring empire and not forces": `stare bene insieme`, go well together, that is, the two can easily be had at once; `egli`, the impersonal subject, not rendered. The next clause draws the consequence.

**`e chi acquista imperio e non forze insieme, conviene che rovini`.** "And whoever acquires empire and not forces together, it is fitting that he go to ruin": `conviene che`, it is fitting that (D85); `rovinare`, go to ruin, the verb of `rovina`.

**`Non può acquistare forze chi impoverisce nelle guerre, ancora che sia vittorioso, che ei mette più che non trae degli acquisti`.** "He cannot acquire forces who grows poor in wars, even though he be victorious, for he puts in more than he draws from the acquisitions": the order kept, the subject delayed (D263, a landing); `impoverire`, grow poor; `ancora che`, even though (D31); `trarre`, draw (D444); `che`, for.

**`come hanno fatto i Veneziani e i Fiorentini`.** "As the Venetians and the Florentines have done": `Veneziani`, Venetians (names.md).

**`quando l'uno era contento del Mare, e l'altro di sei miglia di confini`.** "When the one was content with the sea, and the other with six miles of borders": `Mare`, the sea, capitalised in the 1824; `miglio`, mile.

**`Perché tutto è nato da avere voluto acquistare, e non avere saputo pigliare il modo`.** "Because all has arisen from having wished to acquire, and not having known how to take the way": `nascere`, arise (D04), the passato prossimo kept; `modo`, way.

**`e tanto più meritano biasimo, quanto egli hanno meno scusa`.** "And so much the more do they merit blame, inasmuch as they have less excuse": the correlative of degree `tanto più ... quanto ... meno` kept, in plain order (D263); `hanno tenuto`, "have held", the passato prossimo; `biasimo`, blame (D422); `egli`, they, the plural pronoun.

**`quando i Romani senz'alcuno esempio, per la prudenza loro, da loro medesimi lo seppero trovare`.** "When the Romans, without any example, through their prudence, knew how to find it by themselves": `prudenza`, prudence (glossary); the 1554 omits `da loro` (NM-DISC-II19-D007).

**`Fanno oltra di questo gli acquisti qualche volta non mediocre danno ad ogni bene ordinata Repubblica`.** "Acquisitions, besides this, sometimes do no mediocre damage to every well-ordered republic": `qualche volta`, sometimes (D431); `danno`, damage (D106); `bene ordinata`, well ordered, the rubric's `bene ordinate`.

**`piena di delizie, dove si può pigliare di quelli costumi per la conversazione che si ha con quelli`.** "Full of delights, where one can take some of those customs through the conversation that one has with them": `delizie`, delights; `conversazione`, conversation, in the old sense of dealings and living together.

**`come intervenne a Roma prima nell'acquisto di Capova, e dipoi ad Annibale`.** "As befell Rome, first in the acquisition of Capua, and afterwards Hannibal": `Capova`, Capua (names.md); Rome took Capua's surrender in 343 BC, and Hannibal wintered there after Cannae (the editor's dates).

**`E se Capova fusse stata più longinqua dalla Città ... era senza dubbio quell'acquisto la rovina della Repubblica romana`.** "And if Capua had been more far-off from the city ... that acquisition would without doubt have been the ruin of the Roman republic": `longinquo`, far-off (D444); `Città`, capitalised, the city of Rome; `errore`, error; the remedy near, `propinquo`, at Rome; the imperfect `era`, unreal past, rendered "would have been", as at II.15 (D473). The 1554 reads `lontana`, `uicino` (NM-DISC-II19-D008). The soldiers' error is the conspiracy of the Roman garrison at Capua in 342 BC, Livy 7.38-41 (the editor's identification).

**`E Tito Livio fa fede di questo con queste parole`.** "And Livy bears witness of this with these words": `fare fede`, bear witness (D464); `Tito Livio`, Livy (names.md).

**`Jam tunc minime salubris militari disciplinae Capua, instrumentum omnium voluptatum, delinitos militum animos avertit a memoria patriae.`** Livy 7.38.5, in the 1824's form (latin.md). The editor's English: "Capua, even then most unwholesome to military discipline, the instrument of all pleasures, turned the soldiers' beguiled spirits away from the memory of their fatherland." The 1554 adds an Italian rendering, not carried (NM-DISC-II19-D008).

**`E veramente simili Città o Provincie si vendicano contro al vincitore senza zuffa e senza sangue`.** "And truly, similar cities or provinces avenge themselves against the victor without engagement and without blood": `vendicarsi`, avenge oneself; `vincitore`, victor; `zuffa`, engagement (D49).

**`perché riempiendoli de' suoi tristi costumi, gli espongono ad esser vinti da qualunque gli assalta`.** "Because, filling them with their wicked customs, they expose them to be vanquished by whoever assails them": `tristo`, wicked (D03); `suoi`, their, the cities'; `vincere`, vanquish (D313); `assaltare`, assail (D444). The 1554 reads `de lor triſti` and `eſſalta` (NM-DISC-II19-D008).

**`E Juvenale non potrebbe meglio nelle sue Satire aver considerata questa parte`.** "And Juvenal could not better in his Satires have considered this part": `Juvenale`, Juvenal; `considerare` (SCOPE §3).

**`per gli acquisti delle Terre peregrine, erano entrati i costumi peregrini`.** "Through the acquisitions of peregrine lands, peregrine customs had entered": `peregrino`, peregrine, foreign, kept as the cognate and apart from `forestiere`, foreign (glossary); `terra`, land, as `Terre franche` (.06).

**`ed in cambio di parsimonia e d'altre eccellentissime virtù, gula, et luxuria incubuit, victumque ulciscitur orbem`.** "And in exchange for parsimony and other most excellent *virtù*": *virtù* here plural, the virtues, with *virtù* italic as everywhere (glossary). The Latin is Juvenal, Satires 6.292-293, where the poet has `saevior armis / luxuria incubuit`; Machiavelli puts `gula` in place of `saevior armis` (the editor's comparison; latin.md). The editor's English of Machiavelli's Latin: "gluttony and luxury have settled upon [us], and avenge the conquered world." The 1554 adds an Italian rendering, not carried (NM-DISC-II19-D008).

**`Se adunque l'acquistare fu per essere pernicioso ai Romani`.** "If, then, acquiring was about to be pernicious to the Romans": `essere per`, be about to (D45); `pernizioso`, pernicious (glossary).

**`che sarà adunque a quelli che discosto dai modi loro procedono`.** "What will it be, then, to those who proceed at a distance from their ways": `discosto`, at a distance (D444); `procedere`, proceed, the rubric's verb, twice in the period.

**`E che oltre agli altri errori che fanno, di che se ne è di sopra discorso assai, si vagliono dei soldati o mercenarj o ausiliarj?`** "And [to those] who, besides the other errors that they make, of which enough has been discoursed above, avail themselves of soldiers either mercenary or auxiliary?": `E che` resumes `a quelli che`, and the bracket supplies it (D53); `valersi`, avail oneself (D162); `mercenario`, mercenary (glossary); `ausiliario`, auxiliary.

**`Donde ne risulta loro spesso quei danni, di che nel seguente capitolo si farà menzione`.** "Whence there often result for them those damages of which mention will be made in the following chapter": `risultare`, result, singular with a plural subject in the Italian, the English agreeing; the following chapter is II.20, on auxiliary and mercenary arms.
