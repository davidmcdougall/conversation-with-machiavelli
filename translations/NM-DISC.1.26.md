# Discourses I.26

*A new prince, in a city or province taken by him, must make every thing new.*

## NM-DISC.1.26.01

Whoever becomes prince either of a city or of a state, and so much the more when his foundations should be weak, and does not turn, either by the path of a kingdom or of a republic, to civil life, the best remedy that he has for holding that principate is, he being a new prince, to make every thing anew in that state, as is, in the cities, to make new governments with new names, with new authority, with new men, to make the poor rich, as David did when he became king: *qui esurientes implevit bonis, et divites dimisit inanes*.

## NM-DISC.1.26.02

To build, besides this, new cities, to unmake some of the old [ones], to change the inhabitants from one place to another, and in sum to leave no thing intact in that province, and that there be neither degree, nor order, nor state, nor wealth, which he who holds it does not acknowledge [as] from you: and to take for his mark Philip of Macedon, father of Alexander, who with these ways from a small king became prince of Greece. And he who writes of him says that he used to transfer men from province to province, as herdsmen transfer their herds.

## NM-DISC.1.26.03

These ways are most cruel, and enemies of every living, not only Christian but human; and any man whatever must flee them, and wish rather to live private than king with so much ruin of men. Nonetheless he who does not wish to take that first path of the good, when he wishes to maintain himself, it is fitting that he enter into this ill. But men take certain middle paths, which are most damaging; for they do not know how to be either all good or all bad, as will be shown by example in the present chapter.

## Notes

**The rubric.** `Un Principe nuovo in una Città o Provincia presa da lui, debbe fare ogni cosa nuova`. "A new prince, in a city or province taken by him, must make every thing new": `dovere`, must (D274); `ogni cosa`, every thing, two words, as NM I.25 .03 `rinnovare ogni cosa`, which this chapter fulfils. The English supplies a comma after "prince" to set off the place, matching the one the 1824 prints after `lui`; Canestrini's setting of the rubric over Guicciardini's reply prints both. The chapter answers the last sentence of I.25: whoever would make the absolute power the authors call tyranny "must renew every thing, as will be said in the following chapter".

**`Qualunque diventa Principe o di una Città o di uno Stato`.** "Whoever becomes prince either of a city or of a state": `principe`, prince (glossary); `stato`, state (D265), here the dominion a prince holds, set beside the city and, in the rubric, the province.

**`e tanto più quando i fondamenti suoi fussero deboli`.** "And so much the more when his foundations should be weak": `fussero` is subjunctive, and "should" keeps it from reading as a past fact. The clause raises the degree of the precept that follows; it does not limit it to weak princes. `fondamento`, foundation (glossary).

**`e non si volga o per via di Regno o di Repubblica alla vita civile`.** "And does not turn, either by the path of a kingdom or of a republic, to civil life": the condition that makes the case. A prince who turns his rule into a kingdom or a republic is the one I.25 told to keep the shadow of the ancient ways; this chapter is for the one who does not. `via`, path (D131), as `per via di Repubblica, o di Regno` at NM I.25 .03. `vita civile`, civil life: the noun `vita`, where earlier chapters have the infinitive `vivere civile`, "civil living" (NM I.9).

**`il migliore rimedio ch'egli abbia a tenere quel Principato`.** "The best remedy that he has for holding that principate": `abbia` is "has", and `a tenere` says what the remedy is for; this is not `avere a` + infinitive (D261). `tenere`, hold (glossary, I.23); `Principato`, principate (glossary).

**`sendo egli nuovo Principe`.** "He being a new prince": the absolute construction kept.

**`fare ogni cosa di nuovo`.** "To make every thing anew": `di nuovo`, anew, against the rubric's `nuova`, new.

**`come è, nelle Città fare nuovi Governi`.** "As is, in the cities, to make new governments": `come è` introduces the instances, and the list runs in asyndeton through .01 (D12), infinitive after infinitive; .02 joins with `e` (`e insomma`, `e che`, `e pigliare`). The witness has only commas, and the English keeps them, so that whether making the poor rich is one of the things done `nelle Città` stays as open as in the Italian. `governo`, government (D262); `fare`, make, and `di nuovo`, `nuovo`, anew, new, carry the list.

**`con nuova autorità`.** "With new authority": `autorità`, authority (glossary). The 1554 edition reads the plural, `nuove auttorità`, "new authorities"; escalated as a word at a letter the scans break, and kept (D281; NM-DISC-I26-D003).

**`fare i poveri ricchi, come fece David quando ei diventò Re`.** "To make the poor rich, as David did when he became king": David, the shepherd who became king (1 Samuel 16-2 Samuel 5). The verse that follows is the Magnificat's (Luke 1.53), which the Vulgate prints without `qui`; Machiavelli's `qui` makes David its subject. He applies Mary's words to David's act; whether he took them for a psalm cannot be told from the text. In sense the verse echoes Psalm 107.9 (106.9 in the Vulgate). Kept as printed.

***qui esurientes implevit bonis, et divites dimisit inanes*.** Latin, left in italic (D14): "who filled the hungry with good things, and the rich he sent away empty"; the relative `qui`, absent from the Vulgate of Luke 1.53, takes David as antecedent. The 1824 prints it in italic; the 1554 flags it with marginal inverted commas.

**`Edificare oltra di questo nuove Città, disfare delle vecchie`.** "To build, besides this, new cities, to unmake some of the old ones": `edificare`, build (glossary); `disfare`, unmake, the opposite of `fare`, make, which the chapter is built on; `delle vecchie`, partitive, "some of the old ones". The 1554 edition reads `oltra di queste`, "besides these [things]", escalated and kept (D281), and `delle fatte`, "some of those made", for `delle vecchie` (NM-DISC-I26-D002).

**`cambiare gli abitatori da un luogo ad un altro`.** "To change the inhabitants from one place to another": `abitatori`, inhabitants, as NM I.1.

**`non lasciare cosa niuna intatta`.** "To leave no thing intact": `cosa niuna`, no thing, keeping the `cosa` of the rubric's `ogni cosa`.

**`né grado, né ordine, né stato, né ricchezza`.** "Neither degree, nor order, nor state, nor wealth": `grado`, degree (glossary); `ordine`, order (glossary). `stato` here is a man's standing, not a dominion; the edition keeps "state" (D265), in its older English sense of rank or condition ("a man of low state"), and the facing page shows the same word as at .01.

**`che chi la tiene non la riconosca da te`.** "Which he who holds it does not acknowledge [as] from you": `riconoscere da`, to acknowledge as received from, as NM I.11 `abbia a riconoscere`. The sentence turns to the second person here, and back to the third with `per sua mira`; the English keeps both turns. `la` takes the last of the list, `ricchezza`, and through it the whole.

**`pigliare per sua mira Filippo di Macedonia padre di Alessandro`.** "To take for his mark Philip of Macedon, father of Alexander": `mira`, the mark aimed at. Philip II of Macedon (r. 359–336 BC).

**`di piccolo Re diventò Principe di Grecia`.** "From a small king became prince of Greece": the hegemony of Greece after Chaeronea (338 BC). `Principe` keeps the edition's "prince" (glossary).

**`E chi scrive di lui, dice`.** "And he who writes of him says": Machiavelli names no author. The likeliest source is Justin's epitome of Pompeius Trogus (8.5–6), where Philip moves peoples and cities as shepherds drive their flocks now to winter, now to summer pastures.

**`tramutava ... tramutano`.** "Used to transfer ... transfer": one verb for Philip and the herdsmen, as the Italian; the imperfect is of habit. The 1554 edition has the perfect, `tramutò` (NM-DISC-I26-D004).

**`come i mandriani tramutano le mandrie loro`.** "As herdsmen transfer their herds": `mandriani`, `mandrie`, one root, kept in "herdsmen", "herds".

**`Sono questi modi crudelissimi`.** "These ways are most cruel": the verb first in the Italian, not the predicate; plain English order (D263). `modi`, ways (glossary), the same word as Philip's `questi modi` just before.

**`nimici d'ogni vivere, non solamente cristiano, ma umano`.** "Enemies of every living, not only Christian but human": `vivere`, the infinitive as a noun, living (as `vivere civile`), here any way of living together.

**`e debbegli qualunque uomo fuggire`.** "And any man whatever must flee them": `dovere`, must (D274); `gli`, them, the ways. `qualunque uomo` repeats the chapter's first word, `Qualunque`: whoever becomes prince, any man whatever.

**`e volere piuttosto vivere privato, che Re`.** "And wish rather to live private than king": `volere`, wish (D117); `privato`, private, as FG I.10 `ridursi privato` and NM I.10 `in istato privato`. The Italian sets `privato` and `Re` side by side without a noun; so does the English. `piuttosto`, rather; FG's `più presto ... che` (D135) is also "rather ... than", a recorded collapse.

**`con tanta rovina degli uomini`.** "With so much ruin of men": `rovina`, ruin (glossary).

**`Nondimeno colui che non vuole pigliare quella prima via del bene, quando si voglia mantenere, conviene che entri in questo male`.** "Nonetheless he who does not wish to take that first path of the good, when he wishes to maintain himself, it is fitting that he enter into this ill": the period opens on `colui` and turns to the impersonal `conviene` without a main verb for him; the English keeps the break. `nondimeno`, nonetheless (glossary); `conviene`, it is fitting (D85), not "he must"; `bene` / `male`, good / ill (glossary, I.2). `mantenere`, maintain (glossary): the prince maintains himself, as I.25's reformer maintains a state.

**`quella prima via del bene`.** "That first path of the good": either the path just named, living private rather than king, or the first case of .01, the turn by kingdom or republic to civil life (`per via di`). The English keeps "path" for `via` all three times (.01, here, `vie del mezzo`) and does not choose; `via`, path (D131).

**`certe vie del mezzo`.** "Certain middle paths": as NM I.6 `questa via del mezzo`, "this middle path" (D131). Guicciardini's reply recommends gaining by benefits whoever can be gained, which is close to what Machiavelli here calls a middle path; Canestrini's note to the reply (not carried) quotes this clause.

**`dannosissime`.** "Most damaging": `dannoso`, damaging (D106).

**`né tutti buoni, né tutti cattivi`.** "Either all good or all bad": `cattivo`, bad (D03). `tutti` agrees with `uomini` and is predicative: wholly good, wholly bad, not "all men". The thought is taken up in the rubric of I.27, `Sanno rarissime volte gli uomini essere al tutto tristi, o al tutto buoni`, with `tristi`, wicked, for `cattivi`.

**`come nel presente capitolo per esempio si mostrerà`.** "As will be shown by example in the present chapter": the Italian puts the place first; plain English order (D263). `per esempio` names the means of showing, "by example"; where it introduces an instance it stays "for an example" (FG I.4). The example the clause promises, Giovampagolo Baglioni, who did not dare to seize Julius II at Perugia, is given in I.27, whose rubric takes up the clause before. The 1554 edition reads `seguente`, "following", which fits the sequence of chapters; the 1824 `presente` was read by both readers and is kept as printed (D282; NM-DISC-I26-D001). The 1824 reading may be a slip, but it is Italian and construes, and the edition does not correct a reading that construes (D38).
