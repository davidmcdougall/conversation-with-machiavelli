# Consideration on chapter XXVI of Book I

*A new prince, in a city or province taken by him, must make every thing new.*

## FG-CONS.1.26.01

There are some cities or kingdoms which take little account of the changes of prince, nor are they even used to being governed so legitimately that they cannot bear a prince who dominates little politically. In those which are of this sort remedies so strong are not necessary to found the principate; and if there is some particular [man] not content with the change, a wise prince has many ways of gaining him, provided that this displeasure be founded upon respect to his own interest, for he does not lack ways to content men with advantage and with honour. But the difficulty is where the inclination of the people is wholly contrary to the new government, as are the cities used to being free, when they come under a tyrant; as [are] the kingdoms which have been very long under one lineage, which commonly love that name and that memory; albeit these one could hope to gain with good treatment, which in the end could make the memory of the past princes be forgotten. But for those who have liberty for their inclination, treating them well is not a sufficient remedy, for one cannot with any sweetness eradicate from their breast that desire not to acknowledge a superior, to govern; and therefore in like case it is required to use strong remedies, having therefore before one's eyes that the part which can be gained with benefits, to gain it; for violent remedies, if on the one hand they secure you, on the other, above all to a prince who is not founded upon his own arms, make weakness in a thousand ways. Therefore it is required that the prince have the spirit to use these extraordinary [remedies], when it be necessary, and nonetheless be so prudent that he let slip no occasion whatever that may present itself to him of establishing his affairs with humanity and with benefits, not taking thus for an absolute rule what the writer says, who was always pleased beyond measure by extraordinary and violent remedies.

## Notes

**The rubric.** Canestrini's setting of the chapter title, in lower case, with commas round the place; its stop is faint (D110). It is heading apparatus, not Guicciardini's (D32).

**`Sono alcune città o regni`.** "There are some cities or kingdoms": `regno`, kingdom (glossary). The reply opens by dividing the cases Machiavelli's chapter takes as one: `Qualunque diventa Principe o di una Città o di uno Stato` (Machiavelli .01).

**`tengono poco conto delle mutazioni del principe`.** "Take little account of the changes of prince": `mutazione`, change, the noun of `mutare` (D272); the reply has it again, singular, at `non contento della mutazione`. The 1933 edition prints `delle mutazione`.

**`né sono anche solite a essere governate sì legittimamente`.** "Nor are they even used to being governed so legitimately": `solite`, used to, as `le città solite a essere libere` below; one English phrase for both.

**`uno principe che domini poco politicamente`.** "A prince who dominates little politically": he dominates, and in a way little political; `dominare`, the verb of `dominio`, dominion (glossary), so "dominate", not "rule". `politicamente`, the adverb of `politico`, as Machiavelli's `vivere politico`, "political living" (glossary, I.6): rule by laws and orders. The cities that can bear such a prince are those not used to it.

**`non sono necessarii remedii sì forti a fondare il principato`.** "Remedies so strong are not necessary to found the principate": `remedio` / `rimedio`, remedy (glossary), Machiavelli's `il migliore rimedio` (Machiavelli .01). Both spellings occur in the reply and both are "remedy". `necessario`, necessary, kept apart from `bisogna` (D260). Canestrini's note reference stands after `sì forti`; his note, not carried, quotes the opening of Machiavelli's chapter.

**`alcuno particulare`.** "Some particular [man]": `particulare` as a noun, a private person, in the edition's word (D93); the noun is supplied in brackets (D53).

**`uno principe savio`.** "A wise prince": `savio`, wise, kept apart from `prudente`, prudent, at the end of the reply.

**`ha molti modi di guadagnarlo`.** "Has many ways of gaining him": `modi`, ways (glossary), as Machiavelli's `questi modi` (Machiavelli .02, .03): Guicciardini's ways are of another kind. `guadagnare`, gain, held through the reply (four times): the verb of winning men over, set against Machiavelli's making every thing new.

**`pure che questa displicenza sia fondata in sul rispetto dello interesse proprio`.** "Provided that this displeasure be founded upon respect to his own interest": `pure che`, provided that, a condition on the kind of discontent; `rispetto`, respect (glossary, "respect to"); `proprio`, his own. Both readers saw an a at `dallo`; read `dello` (D279; FG-CONS-I26-D001). Both doubted the last letter of `interesse` and the 1933 edition agrees with their preference (D280; FG-CONS-I26-D002).

**`a contentare gli uomini collo utile e con l'onore`.** "To content men with advantage and with honour": `utile` as a noun, advantage, the material good paired with honour; the adjective stays "useful" (`più presto dannoso che utile`, "rather damaging than useful", D135).

**`la inclinazione del populo`.** "The inclination of the people": `inclinazione`, inclination, as FG I.12; `populo`, people (glossary).

**`come sono le città solite a essere libere, quando vengono sotto uno tiranno; come e' regni`.** "As are the cities used to being free, when they come under a tyrant; as [are] the kingdoms": two instances of the contrary inclination, in parallel; the second verb is supplied in brackets (D53). The pointing after `tiranno` was doubtful to both readers; the 1933 semicolon is followed (D110).

**`che sono stati lunghissimamente sotto una progenie`.** "Which have been very long under one lineage": `lunghissimamente`, the elative of `lungamente`, "long" (D140). `progenie`, a line of descent: "lineage", since English "progeny" now means offspring. Both readers doubted a speck over the o of `sono`; read `sono` (D280; FG-CONS-I26-D002).

**`che amano comunemente quello nome e quella memoria`.** "Which commonly love that name and that memory": the antecedent of `che` is the kingdoms, that is, their peoples. `memoria` comes back in the next clause, `la memoria de' principi passati`, and the English keeps one word.

**`benché questi si potria sperare di guadagnare co' buoni trattamenti`.** "Albeit these one could hope to gain with good treatment": `benché`, albeit (D31). `si potria sperare`, one could hope: conditional, not "it can be hoped". `questi`, the kingdoms, is fronted as the object of `guadagnare`; the English keeps it first because it is set against the next sentence's `a quelli che hanno per inclinazione la libertà`: these can be gained, those cannot by good treatment (D263, contrast). `trattamenti` is plural; English "treatment" takes it as a mass noun.

**`e' quali al fine potrebbono fare dimenticare`.** "Which in the end could make ... be forgotten": `e' quali`, the good treatments.

**`Ma a quelli che hanno per inclinazione la libertà`.** "But for those who have liberty for their inclination": the cities used to being free, now named by their inclination. `a quelli` is the dative of the judgment, "for those"; `libertà`, liberty (glossary).

**`il trattarli bene`.** "Treating them well": the nominal infinitive (D109).

**`eradicare del petto loro`.** "Eradicate from their breast": both readers doubted the e of `petto`; read with the 1933 edition (D280; FG-CONS-I26-D002).

**`quello desiderio di non ricognoscere superiore di governare`.** "That desire not to acknowledge a superior, to govern": two infinitives, both objects of the one `desiderio`, standing side by side without `e`; the English keeps the asyndeton (D12) and supplies only the comma, which Canestrini does not print and the 1933 edition does (D287). `superiore di` + infinitive, "a superior in governing", is not a natural construction, and it would reduce the desire to an objection to hierarchy. The first infinitive is freedom from a superior; the second, in the context of cities used to being free, is most naturally the wish to hold the government and take part in it; the English leaves it unreflexive, as the Italian does, rather than narrow it to "govern themselves". `ricognoscere`, the older form of `riconoscere`, "acknowledge", as Machiavelli's `non la riconosca da te` (Machiavelli .02): the free refuse the acknowledgement Machiavelli's prince demands. Canestrini prints `non`; the 1933 edition brackets it as an editorial supply (FG-CONS-I26-D004).

**`e però in simile caso bisogna usare de' remedii forti`.** "And therefore in like case it is required to use strong remedies": `simile`, like (glossary); `però`, therefore (D22), consecutive. `bisogna`, "it is required": the edition's rendering of the impersonal `bisognare`, in every tense (D260); the reasons are in the note on `bisogni` at Machiavelli I.2. `de' remedii`, partitive, not rendered: "some strong remedies" would understate it.

**`avendo però innanzi agli occhi`.** "Having therefore before one's eyes": the second `però`. It is kept "therefore" (D22): the consequence drawn from the preceding judgment, that strong remedies are required for this case, is that the part which can be gained by benefits is to be gained, a consideration to be kept before one's eyes; the `perché` then gives the reason. Read as "however", it would be the adversative D22 excludes.

**`che quella parte che si può guadagnare co' beneficii, di guadagnarla`.** "That the part which can be gained with benefits, to gain it": `che` opens a clause whose subject is taken up by `-la`, and the infinitive has no governing verb; the English keeps the anacoluthon whole. `beneficii`, benefits, as at the close (`co' beneficii`). The 1933 edition reads `guadagnarli`, "to gain them", the men of that part (FG-CONS-I26-D003).

**`perché e' rimedii violenti, se da uno canto ti assicurano, dall'altro ... fanno in mille modi debolezza`.** "For violent remedies, if on the one hand they secure you, on the other ... make weakness in a thousand ways": `da uno canto ... dall'altro`, on the one hand ... on the other (D223); `assicurare`, secure (D219), Machiavelli's verb for the new prince in I.16; the second person `ti` is Guicciardini's, as Machiavelli's `da te` (Machiavelli .02). `fare debolezza`, "make weakness": `fare` kept as "make", the verb of Machiavelli's rubric. The pointing before `perché` was doubtful to both readers; the 1933 semicolon is followed (D110).

**`a uno principe che non sia fondato in sulle armi proprie`.** "To a prince who is not founded upon his own arms": `armi proprie`, own arms, a soldiery of one's own subjects (FG I.6), against hired or foreign troops. `fondato`, founded, as `fondare il principato` and `fondata in sul rispetto` above.

**`Però bisogna che il principe abbia animo a usare questi estraordinarii`.** "Therefore it is required that the prince have the spirit to use these extraordinary [remedies]": `però`, therefore (D22); `animo`, spirit (glossary); `estraordinario`, extraordinary (glossary, `straordinario`), the noun supplied from `remedii` (D53).

**`sia sì prudente, che non pretermetta qualunque occasione se gli presenti`.** "Be so prudent that he let slip no occasion whatever that may present itself to him": `prudente`, prudent (glossary); `pretermettere`, let pass, let slip; "pretermit" survives only in law. `occasione`, occasion (glossary). The negative governs `qualunque`: no occasion whatever.

**`di stabilire le cose sue con la umanità e co' beneficii`.** "Of establishing his affairs with humanity and with benefits": `stabilire`, establish; `le cose sue`, his things, his affairs; `umanità`, humanity, the quality Machiavelli's `non solamente cristiano, ma umano` (Machiavelli .03) names from the other side.

**`non pigliando così per regola assoluta quello che dice lo Scrittore`.** "Not taking thus for an absolute rule what the writer says": `pigliare`, take, Machiavelli's verb (`pigliare per sua mira`, Machiavelli .02; `pigliare quella prima via`, `pigliano certe vie`, Machiavelli .03). `regola assoluta`, absolute rule: What he denies is that it is absolute. `lo Scrittore`, Machiavelli, as `lo scrittore` at FG I.11; Canestrini capitalises it here, the English keeps lower case for both.

**`al quale sempre piacquono sopra modo e' remedii estraordinarii e violenti`.** "Who was always pleased beyond measure by extraordinary and violent remedies": the Italian delays its subject to the reply's last words, and the English passive keeps the remedies last (D18). A judgment on Machiavelli himself, not on the chapter; set it beside his own verdict on these ways, `crudelissimi`, to be fled by any man (Machiavelli .03). `sopra modo`, beyond measure: Guicciardini's charge is excess, set against his own `sì prudente`. The reply ends here; the stop is doubtful to both readers and the 1933 edition prints one (D110).
