# Discourses III.17

## NM-DISC.3.17.01

A republic must much consider not to appoint anyone to any important administration to whom any notable injury has been done by others.

## NM-DISC.3.17.02

Claudius Nero, who departed from the army that he had facing Hannibal, and with part of it went into the Marches to find the other consul, so as to fight with Hasdrubal before he joined with Hannibal, had found himself formerly in Spain facing Hasdrubal, and having shut him in a place with the army, [so] that it was required either that Hasdrubal fight to his disadvantage, or die of hunger, was craftily so entertained by Hasdrubal with certain practices of accord that he got out from under, and took from him that occasion of oppressing him. Which thing, known at Rome, gave him great charge with the Senate and with the people; and he was spoken of shamefully through all that city, not without his great dishonour and indignation. But being made consul later, and sent to meet Hannibal, he took the above-written course, which was most dangerous; so that Rome stood all doubtful and stirred up, until the news came of the rout of Hasdrubal. And Claudius, being asked later for what cause he had taken so dangerous a course, where without an extreme necessity he had gambled almost the liberty of Rome, answered that he had done it because he knew that if it succeeded for him, he would reacquire that glory which he had lost in Spain; and if it did not succeed for him, and this course of his had had a contrary end, he knew how he would avenge himself against that city and against those citizens who had so ungratefully and indiscreetly offended him.

## NM-DISC.3.17.03

And when these passions of such offences can [do] so much in a Roman citizen, and in those times when Rome was still uncorrupted, one must think how much they can [do] in a citizen of a city which is not made as that one was then. And because one cannot give a sure remedy to similar disorders that arise in republics, it follows that it is impossible to order a perpetual republic, because by a thousand unlooked-for paths is caused its ruin.

## Notes

**The rubric.** `Che non si offenda uno, e poi quel medesimo si mandi in amministrazione e governo d'importanza.` "That one not offend someone, and then send that same [one] into administration and government of importance": the subjunctives without "should", which is `dovere`'s (D348); `offendere`, offend, in the older sense, injure (glossary); `uno`, someone, and `quel medesimo`, that same [one] (D53); `amministrazione`, administration (D496); `governo`, government (D262), kept apart from `reggimento`, regimen. The rubric speaks of offending and the chapter of injury (`ingiuria`, .01) and offences (`offese`, .03): three words of one root and its neighbour, kept apart in English. The 1554 sets the same sentence as its heading, and Canestrini sets it over Guicciardini's reply (NM-DISC-III17-D006; D495).

**`Debbe una Repubblica assai considerare di non preporre alcuno ad alcuna importante amministrazione`.** "A republic must much consider not to appoint anyone to any important administration": `debbe`, must (D274); `assai` with the verb, much (D348); `considerare`, consider (glossary); `preporre`, appoint (glossary); `alcuno ... alcuna ... alcuna`, anyone, any, any, the indefinite three times under the negative, as printed.

**`al quale sia stato fatto da altri alcuna notabile ingiuria`.** "To whom any notable injury has been done by others": `ingiuria`, injury (glossary); `da altri`, by others, not by the republic itself; the subjunctive of a class of men. Guicciardini's reply turns on who the injurer is.

**`Claudio Nerone, il quale si partì dallo esercito che lui aveva a fronte ad Annibale`.** "Claudius Nero, who departed from the army that he had facing Hannibal": `a fronte a`, facing (D496); Gaius Claudius Nero, consul in 207 BC (names.md, as at II.10). The 1554 omits `lui` (NM-DISC-III17-D001; D493). The long relative that follows, through `con Annibale`, is the march to the Metaurus, told before the earlier disgrace that explains it; the order is kept.

**`n'andò nella Marca a trovare l'altro Consolo`.** "Went into the Marches to find the other consul": `la Marca`, the Marches (names.md); `Consolo`, consul (D410). The other consul, Marcus Livius Salinator, is not named; Guicciardini names him.

**`s'era trovato per l'addietro in Ispagna a fronte d'Asdrubale`.** "Had found himself formerly in Spain facing Hasdrubal": the main verb of the period, after the relative; Nero in Spain in 211 BC is Livy 26.17 (the editor's reference).

**`con lo esercito che bisognava`.** "With the army, [so] that it was required": `che` consecutive and `bisognava` impersonal, governing the two `che` clauses that follow, in the editor's reading (D497); `che` could also be relative, "the army that was required". `bisognava`, it was required, the edition's rendering of the impersonal `bisognare`, in every tense (D260); the reasons are in the note on `bisogni` at Machiavelli I.2. The 1554 has the present, `biſogna` (NM-DISC-III17-D002; D493).

**`o che Asdrubale combattesse con suo disavvantaggio, o si morisse di fame`.** "Either that Hasdrubal fight to his disadvantage, or die of hunger": `combattere`, fight (D49); `o che ... o`, either that ... or, under `bisognava`; the subjunctives without "should" (D348).

**`fu da Asdrubale astutamente tanto intrattenuto con certe pratiche d'accordo`.** "Was craftily so entertained by Hasdrubal with certain practices of accord": the subject is still Nero, from the head of the period; `tanto` with the verb, answered by `che`; `astutamente`, craftily (D52); `intrattenere`, entertain, in the older sense, hold in play (D496); `pratiche`, practices, the negotiations of a treaty (D496); `accordo`, accord (D452), here a noun after `di`, not D464's adverbial "by accord". The 1554 omits `da`, so that Hasdrubal becomes the one entertained and Nero drops out of the clause; the 1824, read without doubt by both readers, keeps Nero the one held and Hasdrubal the agent (NM-DISC-III17-D003; D493).

**`che egli uscì di sotto, e tolsegli quella occasione d'oppressarlo`.** "That he got out from under, and took from him that occasion of oppressing him": `egli` is now Hasdrubal, the subject changing without notice, as printed; `occasione`, occasion (glossary); `oppressare`, oppress, to crush (D496).

**`La qual cosa saputa a Roma gli dette carico grande appresso al Senato ed al Popolo`.** "Which thing, known at Rome, gave him great charge with the Senate and with the people": `carico`, charge, here blame laid on him (glossary); `Popolo`, people (D118).

**`e di lui fu parlato inonestamente per tutta quella Città, non senza suo grande disonore ed isdegno`.** "And he was spoken of shamefully through all that city, not without his great dishonour and indignation": the impersonal passive in plain order (D263); `inonestamente`, shamefully (D496), the root of `onesto` kept apart from `disonore`, dishonour, beside it; against the 1554's `dishoneſtamente`, the same sense (NM-DISC-III17-D004; D493); `disonore`, dishonour (glossary `disonorare`); `sdegno`, indignation (glossary), here printed `isdegno`. `suo` governs both nouns: the dishonour done him and the indignation he felt.

**`Ma sendo poi fatto Consolo, e mandato all'incontro d'Annibale, prese il soprascritto partito, il quale fu pericolosissimo`.** "But being made consul later, and sent to meet Hannibal, he took the above-written course, which was most dangerous": `poi`, later (D250); `all'incontro di`, of a march, to meet (D496), kept apart from `contro a`, against (.02); `partito`, course (D236); `soprascritto`, above-written (glossary), the march of the first sentence.

**`talmente che Roma stette tutta dubbia e sollevata`.** "So that Rome stood all doubtful and stirred up": `talmente che`, so that (glossary); `sollevato`, stirred up (D496), the city's suspense.

**`dove senza una estrema necessità egli aveva giuocata quasi la libertà di Roma`.** "Where without an extreme necessity he had gambled almost the liberty of Rome": `giuocare`, gamble, to play at hazard (D496); `libertà`, liberty (glossary); `quasi`, almost, kept on `la libertà`.

**`se gli riusciva, riacquistava quella gloria che s'aveva perduta in Ispagna`.** "If it succeeded for him, he would reacquire that glory which he had lost in Spain": `riuscire`, succeed, of a thing that comes off (D332); "Claudius, being asked", the absolute construction given its subject in plain order; `riacquistare`, reacquire (D496), the root of `acquistare`, acquire; the imperfects `riusciva`, `riacquistava`, `vendicava` stand in the reported answer for what would follow, and the English says "would" in the apodosis only.

**`sapeva come ei si vendicava contro a quella Città`.** "He knew how he would avenge himself against that city": `vendicarsi`, avenge oneself (D480). The words are put in Nero's mouth; Guicciardini answers that they were Livius Salinator's (Guicciardini .01).

**`che lo avevano tanto ingratamente e indiscretamente offeso`.** "Who had so ungratefully and indiscreetly offended him": `ingratamente`, ungratefully (glossary `ingrato`); `indiscretamente`, indiscreetly, without discretion (D496); `offendere`, offend, the rubric's verb.

**`E quando queste passioni di tali offese possono tanto in un cittadino romano`.** "And when these passions of such offences can [do] so much in a Roman citizen": `quando`, when, with the force of "if" left to the reader; `passioni`, passions (D496); `offese`, offences, the noun of the rubric's `offendere`; `potere tanto`, can so much, as `quanto elle possano`. The 1554 reads `tale offeſe` and `anchora era` (NM-DISC-III17-D005; D493).

**`e in quelli tempi che Roma era ancora incorrotta`.** "And in those times when Rome was still uncorrupted": `ancora`, still, of time, a sense split from "also" and "even" (D472, D496); `incorrotto`, uncorrupted (glossary `corrotto`).

**`E perché a simili disordini che nascono nelle Repubbliche non si può dare certo rimedio`.** "And because one cannot give a sure remedy to similar disorders that arise in republics": the fronted dative set in plain order (D263); `disordini`, disorders (glossary); `nascere`, arise (D04); `simile`, similar (glossary); `certo`, sure (D496), since "a certain remedy" would read as some particular one; `rimedio`, remedy (glossary).

**`ne seguita che gli è impossibile ordinare una Repubblica perpetua, perché per mille inopinate vie si causa la sua rovina`.** "It follows that it is impossible to order a perpetual republic, because by a thousand unlooked-for paths is caused its ruin": `ordinare`, order (glossary `ordine`); `perpetuo`, perpetual; `vie`, paths (D131); `inopinato`, unlooked-for (D496); `causare`, cause; `rovina`, ruin (glossary). The conclusion rests on the `perché` before it: no sure remedy. The ruin is the chapter's last word and its landing, and the English keeps it last (D263).
