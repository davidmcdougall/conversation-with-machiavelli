# Consideration on chapter LX of Book I

*How the consulship and any other magistracy in Rome was given without respect of age*

## FG-CONS.1.60.01

The Discourse does not remember that Scipio Africanus the younger could not be made consul if by a particular law the ban of age was not first removed from him; nor that Cicero in the . . . says that to one who is of the age of thirty-three years there lacks the time of ten years to be consul; and if in Valerius Corvinus it was otherwise, it is required to say, and so with truth, that other were the orders in the beginning of the republic, others arose in progress of time. As it was too with the time of the magistracies, because in the beginnings there was no prohibition that the consulship could not be continued; and at least he who was consul now could within a little time be chosen consul anew, but afterwards a law was made that between one consulship and the other there should be at least an interval of ten years. Which two laws, that is, of the ban of age and of time, whether they are useful to republics or not, it will be treated in another place, because in this [place] it is not our consideration, they not being treated in the Discourse

## Notes

**The rubric.** Canestrini's setting of the chapter title: Machiavelli's rubric with `consolato`, `magistrato` lower case and no stop. It is heading apparatus, not Guicciardini's (D32). Pass A read `qualunqua`, pass B `qualunque`, two forms of one word; the a-shape is the scan's barless e, and the 1933 edition, the 1824 rubric and the 1554 heading have the -e form (FG-CONS-I60-D002, D406).

**`Non si ricorda il Discorso, che Scipione Africano minore non potette essere fatto consule se per legge particulare non gli fu prima levato il divieto della età`.** "The Discourse does not remember that Scipio Africanus the younger could not be made consul if by a particular law the ban of age was not first removed from him": the fronted verb normalised (D263); `il Discorso`, the Discourse, his name for Machiavelli's chapter (FG I.58). `Scipione Africano minore`, Scipio Africanus the younger, Publius Cornelius Scipio Aemilianus (names.md). The Latin Library text of the *Periochae* of Livy, book 50 (consulted 2026-11-19), records that he was named consul by the people while standing for the aedileship, `legibus solutus`, released from the laws, for the year conventionally dated 147 BC. `minore` distinguishes him from the elder Africanus, whom names.md takes to be Machiavelli's `Scipione` (Machiavelli .03). `legge particulare`, a particular law, made for one man; `levare`, remove (D131); `divieto`, ban, the bar on holding an office, also a term of Florentine government, kept apart from `proibizione`, prohibition, below. `consule`, consul, Guicciardini's form of Machiavelli's `Consolo`.

**`non che Cicerone nel . . . dice`.** "Not to say that Cicero in the . . . says": `non che`, nor that: the opening's `non ... che` resumed, a second thing the Discourse does not remember; not the scalar `non che` of NM I.16 (D226), "not to say", "let alone". Canestrini prints a row of points here with a note, the readers counting three and four; the 1933 edition prints five. Neither names the work, and the edition prints the gap as it stands (FG-CONS-I60-D001, D407). Canestrini's note guesses Cicero's *De Legibus*, book III, which treats the magistracies. The editor's identification is *Philippics* 5.47-48: the Latin Library text (thelatinlibrary.com/cicero/phil5.shtml, consulted 2026-11-19) says that Alexander of Macedon died in his thirty-third year, an age ten years short of the consular age under Roman law. Guicciardini's `di età di trentatré anni` does not match that count exactly. The same passage names the Corvini and the elder Africanus as men made consul young, examples that tell for Machiavelli's side; whether Guicciardini had the passage in mind cannot be shown.

**`che a chi è di età di trentatré anni manca il tempo di dieci anni a essere consule`.** "That to one who is of the age of thirty-three years there lacks the time of ten years to be consul": the consular age implied, on Guicciardini's figures, is forty-three. `età`, age, as Machiavelli's rubric.

**`e se in Valerio Corvino fu altrimenti, bisogna dire, e così con verità, che altri furono gli ordini nel principio della republica, altri nacquono in progresso di tempo`.** "And if in Valerius Corvinus it was otherwise, it is required to say, and so with truth, that other were the orders in the beginning of the republic, others arose in progress of time": `bisogna`, it is required (D260; see the note on `bisogni` at Machiavelli I.2). `e così con verità`, as Canestrini prints it, an ellipsis, left unsupplied in the English as in the Italian; it may resume `dire`, said so with truth. The 1933 edition reads `e così è con verità`, with the verb (FG-CONS-I60-D002, D408). The predicate-first `altri furono` is kept because the correlative `altri ... altri` carries the contrast of beginning and progress of time (D263, a contrast). `ordini`, orders (glossary); `principio`, beginning (glossary); `nascere`, arise (D04). Guicciardini takes Machiavelli's example under `se`, neither granting nor disputing it, and answers his `mai` (Machiavelli .01) by time: the orders of the beginning were other than those that arose later. The editor reads this as allowing that early Rome may have lacked the ban.

**`Come ancora fu del tempo de' magistrati, perché ne' principii non vi era proibizione che non si potessi continuare il consulato`.** "As it was too with the time of the magistracies, because in the beginnings there was no prohibition that the consulship could not be continued": `tempo`, time, here the interval between tenures, as `del tempo` below; Machiavelli's `quella del tempo` (Machiavelli .03) is age, and Guicciardini sets `età` and `tempo` apart (`del divieto della età e del tempo`); "time" is kept in both units; `magistrati`, magistracies (D93); `continuare`, continue, hold again without a break. The `non` after `proibizione` is the pleonastic negative of a verb of forbidding; the English keeps it as the content of the prohibition, which English can bear: there was no rule that the consulship could not be continued, so nothing forbade continuing it. `chi era consule ora`, he who was consul now, the moment within the past narrated; `di nuovo`, anew (glossary); `eletto`, chosen (glossary, `eleggere`).

**`ma di poi fu fatta una legge che tra l'uno consulato e l'altro dovessi essere almanco intervallo di dieci anni`.** "But afterwards a law was made that between one consulship and the other there should be at least an interval of ten years": `di poi`, afterwards (D187); `almanco`, at least (glossary), twice in the period, the first restricting, the second a minimum. Livy records a plebiscite of the year conventionally dated 342 BC that no one hold the same magistracy again within ten years (7.42).

**`Le quali due leggi, cioè del divieto della età e del tempo, se sono utili alle republiche o no, si tratterà in altro luogo, perché in questo non è nostra considerazione, non essendo trattate nel Discorso`.** "Which two laws, that is, of the ban of age and of time, whether they are useful to republics or not, it will be treated in another place, because in this [place] it is not our consideration, they not being treated in the Discourse": `si tratterà`, impersonal: the matter deferred is whether the laws are useful, and `Le quali due leggi` is left hanging; the English keeps the break. `in altro luogo`, in another place, against `in questo`; `utili`, useful; `trattare`, treat, twice, the second of what the Discourse treats; `considerazione`, consideration (glossary), the word of his title, *Considerazioni*. Canestrini prints no stop at the end, and none is supplied (D40).
