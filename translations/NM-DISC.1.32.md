# Discourses I.32

*A republic or a prince must not defer benefiting men until their necessities.*

## NM-DISC.1.32.01

Even though it fell out happily for the Romans to be liberal to the people, the danger supervening, when Porsena came to assault Rome to restore the Tarquins, where the senate, doubting of the plebs whether it would wish rather to accept the kings than to sustain the war, to secure itself of it disburdened it of the gabelles on salt, and of every burden, saying that the poor worked enough for the public benefit if they nourished their children, and [even though] for this benefit that people exposed itself to bear siege, hunger, and war; let there not therefore be anyone who, having confided in this example, defers gaining the people for himself until the times of dangers, for what succeeded for the Romans will never succeed for him; for the generality will judge that it has that good not from you, but from your adversaries; and, having to fear that, the necessity past, you take back from them what you have forcedly given them, it will have no debt whatever with you.

## NM-DISC.1.32.02

And the cause why this course turned out well for the Romans was because the state was new, and not as yet firm, and that people had seen that laws had been made before for its benefit, such as that of the appeal to the plebs, in such a way that it could persuade itself that that good which was done to it was not so much caused by the coming of the enemies as by the disposition of the senate to benefit them; besides this, the memory of the kings was fresh, by whom they had been in many ways vilified and injured. And because like causes happen seldom, it will also occur seldom that like remedies help.

## NM-DISC.1.32.03

Therefore whoever holds state, a republic as well as a prince, must consider beforehand what contrary times can come upon him, and of what men in adverse times one can have need, and afterwards live with them in that way in which he judges [himself], whatever case supervening, to be necessitated to live. And that one who governs himself otherwise, either prince or republic, and above all a prince, and later on the spot believes, when the danger supervenes, [that he will] regain men for himself with benefits, deceives himself in it; for not only does he not secure himself of them, but he accelerates his ruin.

## Notes

**The rubric.** `Una Repubblica o uno Principe non debbe differire a beneficare gli uomini nelle loro necessitadi`. "A republic or a prince must not defer benefiting men until their necessities": `debbe`, must (D274); `differire`, defer; `beneficare`, benefit, the verb of `beneficio`, benefit, which the chapter repeats. The Italian's `nelle`, "in", names the time to which the benefiting is put off; English takes "until", as at `ne' tempi di pericoli` (.01), which is how the chapter uses it; the Italian's "in their necessities" leaves the attachment looser than the English. Guicciardini's reply carries the same rubric (Guicciardini, the rubric).

**`Ancora che ai Romani succedesse felicemente essere liberali al Popolo`.** "Even though it fell out happily for the Romans to be liberal to the people": `ancora che`, even though (glossary); `succedere`, "fall out", of an outcome, so that "succeed" stays for `riuscire`, which the same period uses twice; `liberale`, liberal, generous. `succedere` is "fall out" here, a sense split from the glossary's "succeed" (NM I.10, of succession), which goes to `riuscire` in this period (D332). The concession runs to the semicolon before `non sia però`, and the example it grants is the whole of the Porsena episode.

**`sopravvenendo il pericolo, quando Porsena venne ad assaltare Roma per rimettere i Tarquinj`.** "The danger supervening, when Porsena came to assault Rome to restore the Tarquins": `sopravvenire`, supervene, three times in the chapter (.01, .03 twice) and one English word, each time of the danger or the case that comes upon one; `rimettere`, restore, put back. Porsena, Lars Porsena of Clusium (names.md), as at I.24. Livy tells it at 2.9.

**`dove il Senato dubitando della Plebe che non volesse piuttosto accettare i Re che sostenere la guerra`.** "Where the senate, doubting of the plebs whether it would wish rather to accept the kings than to sustain the war": `dubitare che ... non`, apprehension, "doubt whether", the `non` not rendered (D205); `Plebe`, plebs (glossary); `sostenere`, sustain.

**`per assicurarsene la sgravò delle gabelle del sale, e d'ogni gravezza`.** "To secure itself of it disburdened it of the gabelles on salt, and of every burden": `assicurarsi di`, secure oneself of (D219); `sgravare` and `gravezza`, disburden and burden, one root kept; `gabelle`, gabelles, the old name, in English as in Italian, for indirect taxes and above all the tax on salt. Livy says the senate took the sale of salt into public hands and freed the plebs from port dues and tribute (2.9.6); Machiavelli gives the reliefs in Florentine terms instead, `gabelle` and `gravezza`; taking the sale of salt into public hands is not the same as remitting a salt tax.

**`dicendo, come i poveri assai operavano in beneficio pubblico, se ei nutrivano i loro figliuoli`.** "Saying that the poor worked enough for the public benefit if they nourished their children": `come`, that, after a verb of saying, as after `aveva veduto` (.02); `assai` with a verb, "enough": the `se` clause makes it a claim of sufficiency, as Livy's `satis` (2.9.6); a scoped sense, beside D254's "very" and D312's "much" (D333). `operare`, work (D83); `in beneficio`, "for the benefit", here and at .02. `figliuoli`, children (D221).

**`e che per questo beneficio quel Popolo si esponesse a sopportare ossidione, fame, e guerra`.** "And [even though] for this benefit that people exposed itself to bear siege, hunger, and war": the subjunctive `esponesse`, against the indicative `operavano` of what the senate said, most naturally resumes `Ancora che`: a second thing granted, the people's bearing the siege and hunger that followed, as Livy tells it (2.9.8). The English supplies "[even though]" (D53). It can also be read as continuing what the senate said, and the facing Italian keeps that open. `ossidione`, siege, the Latinate word (*obsidio*); the 1554 edition reads `assedio`, the common one. The list is kept with its commas.

**`non sia però alcuno che confidatosi in questo esempio differisca ne' tempi di pericoli a guadagnarsi il Popolo`.** "Let there not therefore be anyone who, having confided in this example, defers gaining the people for himself until the times of dangers": `però`, therefore (D22), as in Guicciardini's `non gli accade però` at I.30: the concession does not, on that account, license the delay. `confidarsi in`, confide in (glossary); `guadagnare`, gain (glossary). On "until" for `ne'`, see the rubric. The 1554 edition reads `de pericoli`, "of the dangers".

**`perché mai gli riuscirà quello che riuscì ai Romani`.** "For what succeeded for the Romans will never succeed for him": `riuscire`, succeed, both times; plain English order (D263, exposition). `mai` before the verb is a full negative; the 1554 edition adds `non`, the same sense.

**`perché l'universale giudicherà non aver quel bene da te, ma dagli avversarj tuoi`.** "For the generality will judge that it has that good not from you, but from your adversaries": `l'universale`, the generality (glossary, NM I.16); the second person enters here and holds to the end of the period. `avversario`, adversary, kept apart from `nimico`, enemy (.02).

**`e dovendo temere che, passata la necessità, tu ritolga loro quello che hai forzatamente loro dato`.** "And, having to fear that, the necessity past, you take back from them what you have forcedly given them": `dovendo`, having to: `dovere` (D274) as a gerund cannot take "must", and shares "having to" with `avere a` (D261), a recorded collapse (D334); `ritogliere`, take back, with `togliere`, take away (D131); `loro`, them, plural after the singular `l'universale`, kept; `forzatamente`, forcedly, given under constraint, not by force.

**`non arà teco obbligo alcuno`.** "It will have no debt whatever with you": `obbligo`, debt (D17); `alcuno` after a negative, "whatever" (D195). The period lands on the debt that the benefit does not create.

**`E la cagione perché ai Romani tornò bene questo partito`.** "And the cause why this course turned out well for the Romans": `cagione`, cause (glossary); `partito`, course (D236); `tornare bene`, turn out well.

**`fu perché lo Stato era nuovo, e non per ancora fermo`.** "Was because the state was new, and not as yet firm": `Stato`, state (D265); `fermo`, firm, as Guicciardini's `bene fermo lo Stato suo` at I.30.

**`ed aveva veduto quel Popolo, come innanzi si erano fatte leggi in beneficio suo, come quella della appellazione alla Plebe`.** "And that people had seen that laws had been made before for its benefit, such as that of the appeal to the plebs": `quel Popolo` is the subject, postposed; the English puts it first (D263, exposition). `innanzi`, before; `come`, that, as in .01; `appellazione`, appeal. Livy's law of Valerius Publicola, of the first year of the republic (2.8.2), gave the appeal `ad populum`, to the people; Machiavelli writes `alla Plebe`.

**`in modo che ei potette persuadersi che quel bene che gli era fatto, non era tanto causato dalla venuta dei nimici, quanto dalla disposizione del Senato in beneficarli`.** "In such a way that it could persuade itself that that good which was done to it was not so much caused by the coming of the enemies as by the disposition of the senate to benefit them": `in modo che`, in such a way that (glossary); `ei`, it, the people; `persuadersi`, persuade itself; `disposizione`, disposition; `nimici`, enemies. The people is "it" and "them" by turns, as the Italian has `gli` and `-li`. The good is the one the generality of .01 would credit to the adversaries; here the people credits it to the senate.

**`oltra di questo la memoria dei Re era fresca, da' quali erano stati in molti modi vilipesi e ingiuriati`.** "Besides this, the memory of the kings was fresh, by whom they had been in many ways vilified and injured": `ingiuriare`, injure, with `ingiuria`, injury (glossary). The relative is kept after "fresh", away from its noun, as the Italian has it; "they" is the people, plural again.

**`E perché simili cagioni accaggiono rade volte, occorrerà ancora rade volte, che simili rimedj giovino`.** "And because like causes happen seldom, it will also occur seldom that like remedies help": `simile`, like (glossary), twice; `accadere`, happen (D298); `occorrere`, occur, one of the *currere* family (D01); `rade volte`, seldom (D97), twice, as the Italian repeats it; `rimedio`, remedy, the word Guicciardini takes up in his last sentence (`questo remedio`); `giovare`, help, also his.

**`Però debbe qualunque tiene Stato, così Repubblica come Principe, considerare innanzi`.** "Therefore whoever holds state, a republic as well as a prince, must consider beforehand": `però`, therefore (D22), drawing the precept from the rarity of the causes; `tenere Stato`, hold state; `così ... come`, as well ... as. The rubric's pair returns, republic first.

**`quali tempi gli possono venire addosso contrarj`.** "What contrary times can come upon him": the adjective is held to the end in the Italian; English order (D263, exposition).

**`e di quali uomini nei tempi avversi si può avere di bisogno`.** "And of what men in adverse times one can have need": `bisogno`, need (glossary), the noun Guicciardini takes up in `innanzi al bisogno`.

**`e dipoi vivere con loro in quel modo che giudica, sopravvenendo qualunque caso, essere necessitato vivere`.** "And afterwards live with them in that way in which he judges [himself], whatever case supervening, to be necessitated to live": `dipoi`, afterwards (D187); `necessitato`, necessitated, as I.30. The precept is to live now as one would be forced to live then; the supplied "[himself]" is bracketed (D53).

**`E quello che altrimente si governa, o Principe o Repubblica, e massime un Principe`.** "And that one who governs himself otherwise, either prince or republic, and above all a prince": `quello`, that one, as at I.30; `governarsi`, govern oneself (D64); `massime`, above all (glossary). The 1554 edition reads `massimamente`, the same word. The prince is singled out without a reason given.

**`e poi in sul fatto crede, quando il pericolo sopravviene, coi benefizj riguadagnarsi gli uomini`.** "And later on the spot believes, when the danger supervenes, [that he will] regain men for himself with benefits": `in sul fatto`, on the spot, at the moment of the event; `poi`, later (D250); `riguadagnarsi`, regain for himself, with `guadagnarsi` of .01; `benefizj`, benefits (glossary `beneficii`). `credere` with the bare infinitive is a belief that he will regain them; English needs the supplied words, bracketed.

**`se ne inganna; perché non solamente non se ne assicura, ma accelera la sua rovina`.** "Deceives himself in it; for not only does he not secure himself of them, but he accelerates his ruin": `ingannarsi`, deceive oneself; `assicurarsi di`, secure oneself of (D219), `ne` most naturally the men; `accelerare`, accelerate; `rovina`, ruin (glossary). The chapter ends on the ruin, which the remedy brings nearer rather than averting.
