# Consideration on chapter XVI of Book I

*A people used to living under a prince, if by some accident it becomes free, maintains liberty with difficulty*

## FG-CONS.1.16.01

I make in this Discourse a very great difference between a people that has never known liberty and a people that has at some time been free but by some accident has lost liberty; for in this case the orders of liberty can be taken up again more readily, there still living whoever has seen it, and there remaining many memorials of the ancient republic. Still more kindled in the breast of men is the desire of liberty, having tried the ills of tyranny, and so much the more if it has not fallen into their hand through the line of the tyrants having failed, but because, driven by the bitterness of servitude, they have recovered it with arms. These both love liberty more than that people which has never known it, and are easier in taking up again the orders of republics, and also the matter is more disposed, for in a city that has always had a principate there is great inequality from one citizen to another, which is all contrary to the liberties, under which men are quite equal.

## FG-CONS.1.16.02

But under the principate some are very great, others small, for the prince, either from need or from conformity of spirit, has a circle of men who approach almost more to the prince, than to the private [man]. This inequality, then, is very disproportionate to liberty in a people that has always had a principate, which [inequality] cannot be in a city that has not been in a very exceedingly long servitude; for commonly whoever occupies the liberties, so as to drive the people less to despair, so as to do less violence to things, retains as much as he can the image of liberty, and according to the surface of things contrives to govern the tyranny after the use of a republic, and therefore the equality of the citizens is not altogether extinguished. Nor let the Romans be alleged to me to the contrary, who accommodated themselves well to liberty even though they had never known it, for apart from transferring the power of the kings to the consuls, they changed nothing of the orders that were under the kings; which, if they were good, it arose not so much from their prudence as from good fortune from the orders of the kingdom having been such that they served also for liberty; and the creation of the consuls is believed not to have been their invention, but learned from the Commentaries of Servius Tullius. This is shown to be true, for the other orders that were necessary to the conservation of liberty and to the quiet of the city, they made them in progress of time, pressed by necessity and taught by experience. Nor did that other goad to desire liberty fail the Romans, that is, the having tried the injuries of tyranny, for no occasion or other accident moved them, but the having felt under Tarquinius a most bitter servitude. And it is also a lesser marvel that they were inclined to liberty, for in those times almost all the neighbouring peoples were free; which examples move and inflame men much.

## FG-CONS.1.16.03

It is, then, difficult to conserve a liberty newly acquired, and much more difficult for a people that has been in continual servitude than for one that has at some time been free; nor is there a better remedy for being able to conserve it than to order a government in a tempered way, that on the one hand has vigour to oppress whoever should machinate against liberty, on the other hand is secure for those who wish to live well, and not inclined to beat down the rich and the powerful when they give no cause for it, and easy in receiving those citizens who have been friends of the tyranny, when either their bearings, or the conditions they have, give hope that they are not to be enemies of liberty. For it happens many times, and we have seen the experience of it in Florence, that when the government that succeeds to the tyranny is reasonable, well ordered and secure for everyone, that those who have been able [to do much] with the tyrants content themselves within it, above all in those cities that have the appetite of liberty as natural, for, finding themselves [with] good substance, as whoever has been favoured has most of the time, and having perhaps known more nearly than the others the vexations of servitude, willingly, when they find security and a condition equal to the other citizens, they rest and enjoy their own. And the securing of men of this sort, pacifies and unites the city, whereas the holding them in suspicion, and the troubling them, do not let it rest, neither if they are kept within nor if they are driven out.

## FG-CONS.1.16.04

Let the republic, then, be ordered in such a way that it has promptness to punish whoever machinates against the state, and in this let it be rigid and inexorable, taking up as most grave sins even those that seem light, but let it not persecute anyone on simple suspicion, nor hold as suspect so much those who have had condition under the tyrant as the men who are by nature restless, those who have fallen into poverty, or who are of such quality that they cannot hope for condition except under the tyrant. Let it beware above all things that in the city divisions do not arise, which arise every time the government is not well ordered, for in divisions those of the party that can do less, throw themselves to the tyrant, even though they had been his enemies. These were the causes that made the Medici be put back into Florence in [15]12, not by their old friends, but by many who had been enemies; and the persecuting after [15]26 bitterly without distinction of those who had been their friends has made their return desired by many, who otherwise would have abhorred it no less than the others. Let the new liberty not desire that there be sons of Brutus in it, that is, whoever may machinate against the state, so as to have cause to acquire reputation and terror with severity, for although in like cases it is necessary to put hand into blood, it would have been better not to have had the necessity, and that Brutus had not had sons, than to have them so as to have to kill them. Nor let it hold in the conception of sons of Brutus others than those who are restless by nature, rapacious, and who have not the quality to have a place in liberty; for these are those who are dangerous, not those who, accommodated with substance and with quality, can hope to feel the fruits of liberty together with the others.

## FG-CONS.1.16.05

As to a prince who has the people as enemy, since this too is touched on in the Discourse, I say that if it is his enemy through the oppressions and bitterness of servitude, it is easy to provide for it, removing the injuries and governing justly and humanely; but if the root of the enmity is the desire of liberty, as we have seen in our [people] of Florence, which desired to be free so as to partake of the honours, so as to have a hand in the government, then no sweetness, no mansuetude, no good treatment of the tyrant is apt to eradicate this desire, nor can the tyrant ever, with all the good treatments, trust it. It is indeed true that when men, besides being deprived of liberty, are also ill treated, they come into despair, and whoever is desperate does not wait for the occasions, but seeks them, and to free himself puts himself to every danger, whereas those who have no other torment than the desire of liberty, do not throw themselves headlong, but wait for the occasions; which, when they come, it does not avail the tyrant to have borne himself well and to have governed sweetly, and to have done as Clearchus; of whom it is puerile to believe that he killed the optimates to satisfy the people, for if they had been his friends he would have made little gain, but that, holding them suspect and wishing to oppress them, he gave colour of doing it to please the people. The remedy, then, that the prince has is, either to make himself partisans of such quality that they are powerful to oppress the people, or else, with beating down and annihilating the people in such sort that it cannot move, to introduce new inhabitants, and of such quality that they are not to have cause to desire liberty.

## Notes

**The rubric.** Canestrini's setting of the chapter title, without a stop: Machiavelli's rubric in lower case. It is heading apparatus, not Guicciardini's (D32).

**`Io fo in questo Discorso grandissima differenza`.** "I make in this Discourse a very great difference": `Discorso` is Machiavelli's chapter, capitalised by Canestrini, as again at .05 (`tocco nel Discorso`). The difference is Guicciardini's, and Machiavelli does not draw it: his chapter is of a people "used to living under a prince", and its example, Rome, had lived under kings. One of the two readers doubted whether the first word is `Io` or `lo`; the other read `Io`, as does the 1933 edition.

**`da uno populo ... a uno populo`.** "Between a people ... and a people": `differenza da ... a`.

**`per qualche accidente`.** "By some accident": the rubric's phrase, turned to the losing of liberty rather than its getting.

**`si possono ripigliare più facilmente gli ordini della libertà`.** "The orders of liberty can be taken up again more readily": `ripigliare`, again at .01 and, in its other sense, at .04 (`ripigliando per peccati gravissimi`, "taking up as most grave sins"), where to take up is to rebuke. One English verb for one Italian verb in two senses (D224).

**`restando molte memorie`.** "Many memorials remaining": `memorie` as Machiavelli's `memorie delle antiche Istorie`, "the memorials of the ancient histories" (Machiavelli .01). The two readers split on the final vowel (`molta`/`molte`); read `molte` with the 1933 edition (D215).

**`È ancora più acceso nel petto degli uomini il desiderio della libertà`.** "Still more kindled in the breast of men is the desire of liberty": the postposed subject is kept (D18).

**`avendo provato e' mali della tirannide`.** "Having tried the ills of tyranny": the gerund's subject is the men, not the desire. `Provare`, "try", in its older sense, to experience; again at .02 (`l'avere provato le ingiurie`). `Male`, ill (glossary).

**`per essere mancata la linea de' tiranni`.** "Through the line of the tyrants having failed": `mancare`, fail (D206), as again at .02 (`Né mancò a' Romani`).

**`acerbità`, `acerbissima`, `acerbamente`.** "Bitterness", "most bitter", "bitterly": one root, at .01, .02, .04 and .05.

**`con le armi`.** "With arms". Canestrini's note on this sentence, on the despotisms of every age, is his, and is not carried.

**`Costoro e amano ... e sono più facili`.** "These both love ... and are easier": `facile` as the glossary (D27).

**`la materia`.** "The matter": Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .02), taken up and given a cause: matter is disposed to liberty where citizens are equal.

**`alle libertà sotto le quali sono gli uomini assai equali`.** "To the liberties, under which men are quite equal": the plural, a free city's liberties, as `chi occupa le libertà` at .02.

**`per conformità di animo`.** "From conformity of spirit": `animo`, spirit (glossary).

**`uno cerchio di uomini`.** "A circle of men": `cerchio`, as Machiavelli's I.7 (D150).

**`si accostano quasi più al principe, che al privato`.** "Approach almost more to the prince, than to the private [man]": nearer the prince's condition than a private man's, or nearer the prince himself; the English leaves both. `Privato`, a man without office.

**`la quale non può essere ... in molto lunghissima servitù`.** "Which [inequality] cannot be ... in a very exceedingly long servitude": `la quale` is the inequality, which the English names in brackets so that "which" cannot attach to "principate". Such inequality is found only after very long servitude, because usurpers keep the image of liberty (`perché ... e però`). The Italian doubles the intensive (`molto` on a superlative), and so does the English.

**`chi occupa le libertà`.** "Whoever occupies the liberties": `occupare`, occupy (glossary), as Machiavelli's I.9 `occupare la tirannide`.

**`per disperare manco il populo, per violentare manco le cose`.** "So as to drive the people less to despair, so as to do less violence to things": `disperare` transitive. Its root returns at .05: men ill treated "come into despair", and "whoever is desperate" seeks the occasions.

**`secondo la superficie delle cose`.** "According to the surface of things": `superficie` as FG I.7 (D146).

**`a uso di republica`.** "After the use of a republic": `uso`, use (D41).

**`non si spegne al tutto`.** "Is not altogether extinguished": Machiavelli's Tarquins were "extinguished" (`spenti`, Machiavelli .09).

**`Né mi siano allegati in contrario e' Romani`.** "Nor let the Romans be alleged to me to the contrary": the Romans had lived under kings, so they are the case of a people that never knew liberty, and yet kept it. The objection is Guicciardini's to his own distinction, and he answers it three ways: they changed nothing but the kings' power, and if those orders were good it was fortune more than prudence; they had felt the injuries of tyranny under Tarquinius; and their neighbours were free.

**`dal transferire la potestà de' re a' consuli in fuora`.** "Apart from transferring the power of the kings to the consuls": `potestà`, power (D78). The two readers split on the apostrophe of `a'` (`a`/`a'`); read `a'` with the 1933 edition, a dropped apostrophe as D72 (D215).

**`e' quali se furono buoni, non nacque tanto da prudenza loro`.** "Which, if they were good, it arose not so much from their prudence": the relative opens and a new construction finishes, and the English keeps the break. `Nascere`, arise (D04).

**`imparata da' Commentarii di Servio Tullio`.** "Learned from the Commentaries of Servius Tullius": Livy 1.60 has the first two consuls created by the centuriate assembly "according to the commentaries of Servius Tullius". Both readers read `da'`, "from"; the 1933 edition reads `de' commentari`, "of the commentaries". `Da'` construes and is kept (D216; FG-CONS-I16-D002).

**`gli feciono in progresso di tempo stretti dalla necessità e ammaestrati dalla esperienza`.** "They made them in progress of time, pressed by necessity and taught by experience": the object is fronted and taken up by `gli`, and the English keeps both. The tribunes, the subject of Guicciardini's I.3, I.5 and I.6, are among these later orders. The 1933 edition has `ma` before `gli feciono` (FG-CONS-I16-D005).

**`quell'altro aculeo`.** "That other goad": of the two things that kindle a people once free (.01), the memory of liberty the Romans lacked; the experience of tyranny they had.

**`sotto Tarquinio`.** "Under Tarquinius": Tarquinius Superbus.

**`e' quali esempli muovono e infiammano`.** "Which examples move and inflame": `esemplo`, example (glossary).

**`È adunque difficile`.** "It is, then, difficult": both readers doubted the accent on the capital; read `È` with the 1933 edition (D215). It opens the third printed paragraph.

**`conservare`.** "Conserve" (glossary), against Machiavelli's `preservare`, "preserve", and `mantenere`, "maintain".

**`il migliore remedio`.** "A better remedy": `rimedio`, remedy, Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .04, .05). Guicciardini's remedy is an order, not a killing.

**`in modo temperato`.** "In a tempered way": `temperato` as FG I.12's `complessione temperata` (D25).

**`vivacità`.** "Vigour" (D223): the cognate "vivacity" now means liveliness of manner; the word is the government's quickness and force to put down a conspirator.

**`da uno canto ... da altro`.** "On the one hand ... on the other hand": `da altro` as the glossary (D24).

**`chi machinassi contro la libertà`.** "Whoever should machinate against liberty": `machinare`, as FG I.7 (D154), and at .04 against the state. The 1933 edition reads `contro alla libertà` (FG-CONS-I16-D003).

**`a battere e' ricchi e i potenti`.** "To beat down the rich and the powerful": Canestrini repeats the article, two classes; the 1933 edition reads `e' ricchi e potenti` (FG-CONS-I16-D004). `Battere` returns at .05, of the prince who beats the people down.

**`e' portamenti loro`.** "Their bearings": how they carry themselves.

**`che non abbino a essere inimici`.** "That they are not to be enemies": `avere a`, "be to" (D61). `Inimico`, "enemy", Guicciardini's form of Machiavelli's `nimico`.

**`e n'abbiamo visto la esperienza in Firenze`.** "And we have seen the experience of it in Florence": most likely the republic of 1494–1512, which received citizens who had served the Medici. `Esperienza` is the same word as at .02, "taught by experience".

**`che quando ..., che quelli`.** "That when ..., that those": the pleonastic `che` is kept (D136).

**`che hanno potuto co' tiranni`.** "Who have been able [to do much] with the tyrants": `potere` kept a verb, as `la parte che può manco`, "the party that can do less" (.04); "power" is `potestà`'s (.02).

**`trovandosi buone facultà`.** "Finding themselves [with] good substance": `facultà` of property, substance (D198), as at .04 (`accommodati di facultà`).

**`d'appresso`.** "More nearly": from closer at hand.

**`e' fastidii della servitù`.** "The vexations of servitude": `fastidio`, Machiavelli's word for what the great were to Clearchus (Machiavelli .07).

**`vi si contentano dentro ..., godono il suo`.** "Content themselves within it ..., enjoy their own": the reply's answer to Machiavelli's partisan enemies, who, the capacity of availing themselves taken away, "cannot live content" (Machiavelli .03). Received and secure, says Guicciardini, the tyranny's friends content themselves; Florence is his experience of it. Machiavelli's common utility of free living is "to be able to enjoy freely one's own things" (Machiavelli .03).

**`E lo assicurare`.** "And the securing": one reader doubted the capital's accent, the other read `E`, as does the 1933 edition. The conjunction construes; `È` would not. `Assicurare`, secure, Machiavelli's verb (Machiavelli .04, .05, .08, .09): here the republic secures the men it suspects, rather than securing itself against them.

**`riposare`, `si riposano`.** "Rest": the men rest and enjoy their own; suspicion does not let the city rest.

**`né se si tengono dentro, né se si cacciano fuora`.** "Neither if they are kept within nor if they are driven out".

**`ripigliando per peccati gravissimi etiam quelli che paino leggieri`.** "Taking up as most grave sins even those that seem light": `ripigliare per`, to take a fault up as. `Etiam` is Latin, as in FG I.3.

**`non perseguiti alcuno per semplice sospetto`.** "Let it not persecute anyone on simple suspicion": `perseguitare`, persecute, again at .04 of the Florentine persecuting. Machiavelli's common utility of free living is to enjoy one's own "without any suspicion" (`senza alcuno sospetto`, Machiavelli .03); `sospetto` returns at .03 (`l'avergli a sospetto`) and .05 (`avendogli sospetti`).

**`che hanno avuto condizione sotto il tiranno`.** "Who have had condition under the tyrant": standing, place. The word returns twice in the period and at .03 ("a condition equal to the other citizens").

**`inquieti`.** "Restless": twice, here and in the sons of Brutus whom Guicciardini allows.

**`Guardisi sopra tutto che nella città non nascan divisioni`.** "Let it beware above all things that in the city divisions do not arise": `nascere`, arise (D04), twice in the period. `Sopra tutto`, "above all things", kept apart from `massime`, "above all" (.03; glossary).

**`quelli della parte che può manco`.** "Those of the party that can do less": the 1933 edition reads `quella parte` (FG-CONS-I16-D006).

**`rimettere e' Medici in Firenze nel 12`.** "That made the Medici be put back into Florence in [15]12": the century is supplied (D79). The Medici returned in September 1512 behind a Spanish army, and young men of the great families forced Piero Soderini out. Canestrini's note on this sentence, on 1512 and 1530, is his, and is not carried.

**`il perseguitare doppo il 26`.** "The persecuting after [15]26": the century supplied as for `nel 12` (D79, D225); the 1933 edition prints `'26`. The persecution Guicciardini means is the last republic's, which expelled the Medici in May 1527 and proceeded against their friends; the number may be his slip. Guicciardini was himself among those the republic proceeded against. The 1933 edition has `hanno fatto` for `ha fatto` (FG-CONS-I16-D007).

**`la ritornata loro`.** "Their return": the Medici's. The Italian says the persecution has made it desired, not that it has happened.

**`Non desideri la nuova libertà che vi sian figliuoli di Bruto`.** "Let the new liberty not desire that there be sons of Brutus in it": Machiavelli's remedy (Machiavelli .04), refused as a thing to be wished for.

**`riputazione e [terrore]`.** "Reputation and terror": both readers read `tenere`, which leaves the infinitive without an object; the 1933 edition reads `terrore`. Emended under D68 (D214; FG-CONS-I16-D001). The English follows the emended text.

**`mettere mano nel sangue`.** "Put hand into blood".

**`sarebbe stato meglio ..., che averne per avergli ammazzare`.** "It would have been better ... than to have them so as to have to kill them": the preference stands under the concession `se bene in simili casi è necessario mettere mano nel sangue`, "although in like cases it is necessary to put hand into blood". Both readers doubted the last letter of `averne` (`averno`); read with the 1933 edition (D215). `Avergli ammazzare` is `avere a` without the `a`; D61's "be to" will not go after "so as", and the English has "have to". `Ammazzare`, kill (D169), Machiavelli's verb.

**`Né abbi in concetto de' figliuoli di Bruto`.** "Nor let it hold in the conception of sons of Brutus": the sons of Brutus become a class, and Guicciardini narrows it to the restless and rapacious.

**`periculosi`.** "Dangerous": `pericolo`, danger (glossary), as `periculo` at .05.

**`accommodati di facultà e di qualità`.** "Accommodated with substance and with quality": the verb of the Romans who "accommodated themselves" to liberty (.02).

**`sentire e' frutti della libertà`.** "Feel the fruits of liberty": the verb of `l'avere sentito sotto Tarquinio acerbissima servitù`, "the having felt under Tarquinius a most bitter servitude" (.02).

**`poi che questo anche è tocco nel Discorso`.** "Since this too is touched on in the Discourse": Machiavelli's prince who would gain a people that is his enemy (Machiavelli .06–.08).

**`nel nostro di Firenze`.** "In our [people] of Florence": `libero` is masculine, so the noun understood is `populo`.

**`per participare degli onori`.** "So as to partake of the honours": `participare` here takes `di` and is intransitive, the sense of `partecipare di` (glossary, "partake of"), not D28's transitive "impart".

**`nessuna dolcezza, nessuna mansuetudine, nessuno buono trattamento`.** "No sweetness, no mansuetude, no good treatment": three nouns, each with its negative. `Dolcemente`, "sweetly", returns of the tyrant's governing.

**`se ne può fidare`.** "Trust it": `ne` is the people, or its desire; the English keeps "it" open. The one reader who saw the stop after `fidare` saw it faint; supplied with the 1933 edition (D110). Both readers doubted `ne` (`ne`/`no`); read `ne` with the 1933 edition (D215).

**`È bene vero`.** "It is indeed true": as Machiavelli's `Vero è che io giudico`, "It is true that I judge" (Machiavelli .05), a concession. Both readers doubted the accent; read `È` with the 1933 edition (D215).

**`non si precipitano`.** "Do not throw themselves headlong".

**`le quali quando vengono, non giova al tiranno`.** "Which, when they come, it does not avail the tyrant": the relative opens and is not finished; the English keeps it.

**`e avere fatto come Clearco`.** "And to have done as Clearchus": Machiavelli's example (Machiavelli .07).

**`del quale è puerile credere che ammazzassi gli ottimati per satisfare al populo`.** "Of whom it is puerile to believe that he killed the optimates to satisfy the people": Machiavelli wrote that Clearchus resolved to free himself from the vexation of the great and gain the people, and cut the optimates to pieces "to the extreme satisfaction of the popular men" (Machiavelli .07). Guicciardini does not deny the killing or the satisfaction; he denies the motive.

**`ma che ... déssi colore di farlo per compiacere al populo`.** "But that ... he gave colour of doing it to please the people": the second `che` hangs on `credere`. `Colore`, a pretext. Canestrini accents `déssi`, "gave".

**`farsi partigiani di qualità che siano potenti`.** "To make himself partisans of such quality that they are powerful": `di qualità che` as twice before in the reply (.04, .05). Machiavelli's "partisan enemies and not partisan friends" (Machiavelli .03) answered in its own word, and his greatest remedy, "to make the people one's friend" (`farsi il Popolo amico`, Machiavelli .05), turned in the same reflexive verb: the prince makes himself partisans to oppress the people. `Qualità`, quality (glossary), five times in the reply.

**`annichilare`.** "Annihilating".

**`introducere nuovi abitatori`.** "To introduce new inhabitants": `abitatori` as FG I.1 (D63). The unit ends with a stop.
