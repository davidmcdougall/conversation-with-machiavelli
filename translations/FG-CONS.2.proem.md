# Consideration on the proem of Book II

## FG-CONS.2.proem.01

The conclusion is most true, that the ancient times are often praised more than is due, and the reasons are well considered by the Writer, to which one could add some other, but I pass them over. I do not accord with him in what he says, that there was always in the world as much of the good in one age as in another, albeit the places vary, because it is seen to be most true that, either by influence of the heavens or by some other occult disposition, there run at times certain ages in which, not only in one province but universally in all the world, there is more *virtù* or more vice than there has been in another age, or at least an art or a discipline flourishes more than it has flourished in any part of the world in another time. And to begin with those mechanical [arts] of which the Writer makes mention, who does not know in how great excellence painting and sculpture were in the time of the Greeks and then of the Romans, and how much afterwards they remained obscure in all the world, and how, after having been buried for many centuries, they have, in the last hundred and fifty or two hundred years, returned to light? Who does not know how much in ancient times military discipline flourished, not only among the Romans but in many provinces, of which our times and those of our fathers and grandfathers have not seen in any part of the world but small and obscure vestiges? The same can be said of letters, of religion, which without doubt in some ages have been buried everywhere, in others have been in many places excellent and of supreme price. Some age has seen the world full of wars, another has felt and enjoyed peace; from which variations of the arts, of religion, of the movements of human things, it is no marvel that the customs of men too have varied, which often take their motion from institution, from occasions, from necessity. It is then a true conclusion that the ancient times are not always to be preferred to the present, but it is not true to deny that one age is sometimes more corrupt or more virtuous than the others

## Notes

**The heading.** Canestrini's: CONSIDERAZIONE SUL PROEMIO DEL LIBRO II, in small capitals. Unlike the replies above and below it, this one has no rubric under the heading; none is supplied (D421, D32). The 1933 edition heads it NEL PROEMIO DEL SECONDO LIBRO.

**`La conclusione è verissima, che spesso e' tempi antichi sono laudati più che il debito`.** "The conclusion is most true, that the ancient times are often praised more than is due": `verissimo`, most true, the concession's word, turned in the next period to the ground of the refusal (`si vede essere verissimo`); `spesso`, often, against Machiavelli's `il più delle volte`, most times (Machiavelli .01); `laudare`, praise (glossary); `il debito`, what is owed, "is due". The stop both readers half saw after `verissima` is read as a comma with the 1933 edition, since the `che` clause states the conclusion (D110, D419). The conclusion Guicciardini grants is Machiavelli's `non sempre ragionevolmente` (Machiavelli .01), not the claim of .04.

**`e le ragioni sono bene considerate dallo Scrittore, alle quali se ne potrebbe aggiugnere qualche una altra, ma le pretermetto`.** "And the reasons are well considered by the Writer, to which one could add some other, but I pass them over": `lo Scrittore`, the Writer (glossary), Machiavelli; `considerare`, consider (glossary); `pretermettere`, pass over, as FG I.58. The reasons are Machiavelli's causes of the deceit (.02, .06). Canestrini's note here, his summary of those reasons, is his and not carried.

**`Non concordo già seco in quello che dice`.** "I do not accord with him in what he says": `concordare`, accord, kept apart from `concorrere`, concur (D01): this is not his formula of non-concurrence, and it carries no `facilmente`. `già` after the negative is left unrendered (D374), as in his last clause. It is a disagreement on the claim, stated without qualifier.

**`che sempre nel mondo fu tanto del buono in una età quanto in una altra, benché si variino e' luoghi`.** "That there was always in the world as much of the good in one age as in another, albeit the places vary": Guicciardini's restatement of Machiavelli's `tanto di buono, quanto di tristo` (Machiavelli .04), with `tristo` left out and the provinces kept in a concessive: he does not deny that the good moves among places, only that its sum is constant across ages; `benché`, albeit (D31); `età`, age (D410).

**`o per influsso de' cieli o per altra occulta disposizione corrono talvolta certe età`.** "Either by influence of the heavens or by some other occult disposition, there run at times certain ages": the two causes left open, as `o ... o`; `occulto`, occult, hidden; `correre`, run, the root of the *currere* family (D01), kept literal. `talvolta`, at times.

**`è più virtù o più vizio`.** "There is more *virtù* or more vice": `virtù` (D16) and `vizio`, vice (glossary), Machiavelli's pair (Machiavelli .07).

**`o almanco fiorisce più una arte o una disciplina`.** "Or at least an art or a discipline flourishes more": `almanco`, at least (glossary); `fiorire`, flourish, twice here and again with military discipline; the predicate-first order is exposition and goes into plain order (D263).

**`E per cominciare a quelle meccaniche di che fa menzione lo Scrittore`.** "And to begin with those mechanical [arts] of which the Writer makes mention": Machiavelli names `le arti` (Machiavelli .02) only to set them aside from the deceit, since their excellence is evident in itself; Guicciardini's first proof starts from that exception. `meccaniche` is his word for them, the arts of the hand. Both readers doubted `fa`, the scan's a/u; read with the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-IIproem-D002, D417). Canestrini's comma after `Scrittore` was doubtful to both and is read with the 1933 edition (D110, D419).

**`in quanta eccellenza fussino a tempo de' Greci e poi de' Romani la pittura e la scultura`.** "In how great excellence painting and sculpture were in the time of the Greeks and then of the Romans": Canestrini's scan shows `o la scultura`, and both readers preferred the o while naming e; the 1933 edition has `e`, and the plural verbs that follow (`restassino`, `state sepolte`, `ritornate`) take both arts together (FG-CONS-IIproem-D002, D416).

**`siano da cento cinquanta o dugento anni in qua ritornate in luce`.** "They have, in the last hundred and fifty or two hundred years, returned to light": `da ... in qua`, from then until now. Counted from the date of writing, between late 1529 and the first half of 1530 (Cutinelli-Rendina 2014), the revival falls in the fourteenth century; the longer figure reaches the age of Giotto. The identification is the editor's.

**`Chi non sa quanto a' tempi antichi fiorì non solo appresso a' Romani, ma in molte provincie la disciplina militare`.** "Who does not know how much in ancient times military discipline flourished, not only among the Romans but in many provinces": `appresso`, among; `provincia`, province (glossary). At `appresso ⟨n'|a'⟩ Romani` one reader printed the non-word `n'`; read `a'` with the other reader and the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-IIproem-D002, D417). Machiavelli's `milizia` (Machiavelli .05) is "soldiery"; `disciplina militare`, military discipline, is Guicciardini's term.

**`non hanno veduto in qualunque parte del mondo se non piccoli e oscuri vestigii`.** "Have not seen in any part of the world but small and obscure vestiges": `qualunque` after the negative, "any".

**`Il medesimo si può dire delle lettere, della religione`.** "The same can be said of letters, of religion": `si può dire`, it can be said; the two named without a conjunction, as printed.

**`in altre sono state in molti luoghi eccellenti e in sommo prezzo`.** "In others have been in many places excellent and of supreme price": `sommo`, supreme (D190); `in prezzo`, prized, held in esteem.

**`dalle quali variazioni delle arti, della religione, de' movimenti delle cose umane`.** "From which variations of the arts, of religion, of the movements of human things": `variazione`, variation (D402), Machiavelli's `la variazione de' costumi` (Machiavelli .04); `cose umane`, human things, his `le cose umane sempre in moto` (Machiavelli .03).

**`non è maraviglia siano anche variati e' costumi degli uomini, e' quali spesso pigliano il moto suo`.** "It is no marvel that the customs of men too have varied, which often take their motion": `non è maraviglia`, it is no marvel (glossary); `costumi`, customs (D41); `pigliare`, take (glossary); `moto`, motion, Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .03); `suo` for `loro`, as printed. `e' quali` may be the men or their customs; the English takes the customs, which are what is said to vary.

**`dalla instituzione, dalle occasioni, dalla necessità`.** "From institution, from occasions, from necessity": `instituzione`, institution (glossary): the word can name the ordering of a people or a man's upbringing, and the English, like the Italian, does not choose; `occasione`, occasion (glossary).

**`[È] adunque vera conclusione che non sempre e' tempi antichi sono da essere preferiti a' presenti`.** "It is then a true conclusion that the ancient times are not always to be preferred to the present": Canestrini prints `E`, leaving the period without a verb; emended from the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-IIproem-D001, D418, as D114). `adunque`, then (D74). The reply closes on its first words, `conclusione`, `vera`.

**`ma non è già vero il negare che una età sia qualche volta più corrotta o più virtuosa che l'altre`.** "But it is not true to deny that one age is sometimes more corrupt or more virtuous than the others": the words of Machiavelli's `non essere già sempre vero` (Machiavelli .03), rendered the same way, `già` unrendered (D374), turned on his claim of .04; `qualche volta`, sometimes, limits the counter-claim. The reply ends without a stop (D40, D419).
