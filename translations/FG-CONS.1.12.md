# Consideration on chapter XII

*Of how much importance it is to take account of religion, and how Italy, for
having been wanting in it through the Roman Church, is ruined*

## FG-CONS.1.12.01

One cannot speak so much ill of the Roman court that it does not deserve that
more be said, for it is an infamy, an example of all the disgraces and
opprobriums of the world. And I believe too that it is true that the greatness
of the Church, that is the authority which religion has given it, has been the
cause that Italy has not fallen into a monarchy; for on the one hand it has had
such credit that it has been able to make itself head, and to summon, when it
has been needed, external princes against whoever was about to oppress Italy; on
the other, being stripped of arms of its own, it has not had such forces as to
have been able to establish temporal dominion other than what has voluntarily
been given it by others.

## FG-CONS.1.12.02

But I do not know whether the not coming into a monarchy has been the happiness
or the unhappiness of this province, for if under a republic this could have
been glorious to the name of Italy and happiness to that city that should
dominate, it would have been calamity to all the others, for, oppressed by the
shadow of that one, they would have had no capacity of attaining to any
greatness, it being the custom of republics not to impart the fruits of its
liberty and rule to any others than its own citizens.

## FG-CONS.1.12.03

And although Italy, divided into many dominions, has at various times suffered
many calamities which perhaps in a single dominion it had not suffered, albeit
the inundations of the barbarians were rather in the time of the Roman rule than
otherwise, nonetheless in all these times it has had, on the other side, so many
flourishing cities as it would not have had under a republic, for I reckon that
a monarchy would have been more unhappy for it than happy. This reason has no
force in a kingdom, which is more common to all the subjects; and therefore we
see France and many other provinces live happy under one king; yet, whether by
some fate of Italy, or by the complexion of the men tempered in such a way that
they have wit and force, this province has never been easy to reduce under one
rule, even when the Church was not there; on the contrary, it has always by
nature desired liberty, nor do I believe there is memory of any other rule that
has possessed it whole, than that of the Romans; who subjugated it with great
*virtù* and great violence; and as the republic was extinguished and the *virtù*
of the emperors failed, they readily lost the rule of Italy. Therefore if the
Roman Church has opposed itself to monarchies, I do not readily concur that this
has been the unhappiness of this province, since it has preserved it in that way
of living which is more according to its most ancient usage and inclination

## Notes

**The rubric.** Canestrini prints a rubric over the Considerazione, in his own
wording — lower-case `religione`, a comma after `la Italia`, lower-case
`romana` — rather than reprinting the 1824 *Discorsi* heading.

**`virtù`, twice.** Left untranslated, as in the facing chapter. Guicciardini
uses it of the Romans' conquering capacity and then of what failed in the
emperors — neither moral virtue nor simply force, and the same word must carry
both.

**`facilmente`, twice, sixteen words apart.** `perderono facilmente` and `non
concorro facilmente`. The English holds one word for both — "readily" — because
the repetition is Guicciardini's and the hinge sits inside it.

**`milita`.** Guicciardini's `non milita` is the Latinate argumentative sense:
the reason has no force, does not apply. Modern English "militate" survives
almost only in "militate against", which pulls the opposite way, so the cognate
is not kept here.

**`imperio` and `imperadori`.** One Italian root, two unrelated English words:
"rule" and "emperors". The rhyme in `mancò la virtù degli imperadori ...
perderono ... lo imperio d'Italia` cannot be carried over.

**`poteva ... era ... non avevano`.** Guicciardini's hypothesis in .02 is in the
imperfect indicative throughout, the ordinary Italian idiom for a condition
contrary to fact. The English is counterfactual throughout: a single republic
ruling Italy, as none had since Rome, and what it would have done to the other
cities.

**`non ebbe patito`.** Canestrini prints the plain indicative: calamities
which under a single dominion Italy *had not* suffered — a claim about what the
one Roman dominion spared it, which the `benché` clause then qualifies.
Palmarocchi 1933 reads `non [ar]ebbe patito`, the counterfactual *would not have
suffered*, and it is the reading Guicciardini's argument seems to want. This
edition follows its governing witness even where the comparison witness reads
better, and shows the alternative here. An earlier draft of
this English was made from Palmarocchi's text under Canestrini's name and had
the counterfactual.

**`ché io reputo che`.** Canestrini accents the first `che` and leaves the second
bare: a causal conjunction and then a complementiser. The English carries both —
"for I reckon that" — where the earlier draft, following a text without the
accent, had "so that".

**`consuetudine` and `costume`.** Two words: "usage" here, "custom" at
`il costume delle republiche` in the paragraph before. Kept apart because
Guicciardini keeps them apart, and because `consuetudine` sits among his
non-deterministic causes — fate, complexion, inclination, long usage — which
are not to be modernised into national character.

**`vituperii`.** The two independent readers of Canestrini p. 27 divided on the
third letter. One read `vituperii`; the other, at high magnification, saw a
plain vertical without the crossbar and foot of this fount's *t* and read a
foul sort, `viluperii`. At the scan's resolution the question cannot be settled.
The edition prints the word, `vituperii`; the sense is the same either way.

**The close.** The witness prints no full stop after `inclinazione sua`, and
neither text here supplies one. The period ends as the page ends it. Two other
sentence-final stops in this unit, displaced in the witness by Canestrini's note
references, are supplied and declared.
