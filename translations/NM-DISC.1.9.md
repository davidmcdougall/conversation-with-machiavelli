# Discourses I.9

*How it is necessary to be alone in wishing to order a republic anew, or to reform it wholly outside its ancient orders.*

## NM-DISC.1.9.01

It will perhaps appear to someone that I have run on too far into the Roman
history, not having yet made any mention of the orderers of that republic,
nor of those orders that regarded either religion or the soldiery. And
therefore, not wishing to hold any longer in suspense the spirits of those
who might wish to understand some things about these parts, I say that many,
it may be, will judge it of bad example that a founder of a civil living,
such as Romulus was, should first have killed a brother of his, then
consented to the death of Titus Tatius the Sabine, chosen by him as companion
in the kingdom; judging from this that his citizens could, with the authority
of their prince, out of ambition and desire to command, offend those who
opposed their authority. Which opinion would be true, if one did not consider
what end had induced him to do such a homicide.

## NM-DISC.1.9.02

And this must be taken for a general rule: that never, or seldom, does it
occur that any republic or kingdom is ordered well from the beginning, or
reformed wholly anew outside the old orders, if it is not ordered by one;
indeed it is necessary that one alone be he who gives the way, and on whose
mind any like ordering depends. Therefore a prudent orderer of a republic,
and one who has this spirit, of wishing to benefit not himself but the common
good, not his own succession but the common fatherland, must contrive to have
the authority alone; nor will a wise mind ever reprove anyone for any
extraordinary action that he might use to order a kingdom or constitute a
republic. It is indeed fitting that, the deed accusing him, the effect
excuse him; and when it is good, like that of Romulus, it will always excuse
him; for he who is violent to spoil, not he who is so to mend, must be
reproved. He must indeed be so far prudent and virtuous that he does not leave
that authority which he has taken to himself hereditary to another: for, men being
readier for ill than for good, his successor could use ambitiously what by him
had been virtuously used. Besides this, if one is apt to order, the thing
ordered is not for lasting long should it remain on the shoulders of one; but
indeed when it remains in the care of many, and it rests with many to
maintain it. For just as many are not apt to order a thing, through not
knowing its good, caused by the diverse opinions that are among them, so,
once they have known it, they do not agree to leave it.

## NM-DISC.1.9.03

And that Romulus was among those who in the death of his brother and of his
companion merited excuse, and that what he did was for the common good and
not for his own ambition, is demonstrated by that man's having at once
ordered a senate, with which he might take counsel, and according to whose
opinion he might deliberate. And whoever considers well the authority that
Romulus reserved to himself will see that none other was reserved but to
command the armies when war had been deliberated, and to assemble the senate.
Which was seen afterwards, when Rome became free through the expulsion of the
Tarquins, where no order of the ancient one was innovated by the Romans,
except that in place of a perpetual king there were two annual consuls. Which
testifies that all the first orders of that city were more conformable to a
civil and free living than to an absolute and tyrannical one.

## NM-DISC.1.9.04

Infinite examples could be given in corroboration of the things above-said,
such as Moses, Lycurgus, Solon, and other founders of kingdoms and of
republics, who were able, through having attributed an authority to
themselves, to form laws to the purpose of the common good; but I wish to
leave them behind, as a thing known. I will adduce only one, not so
celebrated, but to be considered by those who might desire to be orderers of
good laws; which is, that Agis, king of Sparta, desiring to bring the
Spartans back within those bounds in which the laws of Lycurgus had enclosed
them, it seeming to him that through their having in part deviated from
them his city had lost much of that ancient *virtù*, and in consequence of forces
and of empire, was killed in his first beginnings by the Spartan ephors, as a
man who wished to occupy the tyranny. But Cleomenes succeeding him in the
kingdom, and the same desire arising in him through the records and writings
of Agis that he had found, in which could be seen what his mind and intention
were, he came to know that he could not do this good to his fatherland unless he
became alone in authority; it seeming to him that, because of the ambition of
men, he could not do what was useful to many against the longing of few: and,
a fitting occasion taken, he had all the ephors killed, and whoever else
might oppose him; then he renewed the laws of Lycurgus wholly. Which
deliberation was apt to make Sparta rise again, and to give Cleomenes that
reputation which Lycurgus had, if it had not been for the power of the
Macedonians and the weakness of the other Greek republics. For, being
assailed after such an order by the Macedonians, and finding himself by
himself inferior in forces, and not having anyone to whom to flee for refuge,
he was conquered; and that design of his remained, however just and
laudable, imperfect.

## NM-DISC.1.9.05

All these things, then, considered, I conclude that to order a republic it is
necessary to be alone, and that Romulus, for the death of Remus and of
Tatius, merits excuse, and not blame.

## Notes

**The rubric.** `Essere solo a volere ordinare`: "to be alone in wishing to
order", the founder without partners, which is the chapter's whole thesis.
`Solo` is "alone" throughout (`un solo`, "one alone"; `l'autorità solo`, "the
authority alone"; `solo di autorità`, "alone in authority"), as Guicciardini's
`uno solo`, in his opening sentence, answers it. The 1554 edition has no `o` before
`al tutto` and reads `riformata` (NM-DISC-I9-D001).

**`E' parrà`.** "It will ... appear": `E'` is the expletive pronoun (`egli`),
not the conjunction.

**`troppo trascorso`.** "Run on too far": `trascorrere`, to run past, the same
*currere* stem as `discorrere`, `occorrere`, `concorrere` (D01). The *Discorsi*
have been running ahead through Roman history; the English keeps the running.

**`ordinatori`, `ordini`, `ordinare`, `ordinazione`.** "Orderers", "orders",
"order", "ordering" (glossary): one family, held through the chapter. The
founder is an orderer first.

**`alla milizia`.** "The soldiery" (D60).

**`gli animi`.** "The spirits" (glossary, `animo`).

**`per avventura`.** "It may be": kept apart from `forse`, "perhaps", in the
first line.

**`di cattivo esempio`.** "Of bad example" (D03, `cattivo` bad). Guicciardini
answers with `di malo esemplo`, "of ill example".

**`un fondatore d'un vivere civile`.** "A founder of a civil living":
`fondatore` is the word, and "founder" is kept for it alone; `edificatore`
stays "builder" (glossary). `Vivere civile`, as `vivere libero`, `vivere
politico`: a way of living under laws. The 1554 edition reads `uno formatore`
(NM-DISC-I9-D002).

**`abbia prima morto un suo fratello`.** "Should first have killed a brother
of his": `morto` transitive, the plain word. Remus, named only in the last
sentence. The 1554 edition has the periphrasis `leuato di uita`, "taken from
life" (NM-DISC-I9-D002). `Morto` and `ammazzare` (.04) are both "killed":
recorded collapse (D169).

**`Tito Tazio Sabino`.** Titus Tatius, king of the Sabines, who after the war
over the Sabine women shared the kingdom with Romulus and was killed at
Lavinium; Livy (1.14) says Romulus took it less hard than was right.
`Eletto` is "chosen" (glossary, `eleggere`).

**`con l'autorità del loro Principe`.** "With the authority of their prince":
`Principe`, "prince" (glossary).

**`offendere`.** "Offend": injure, as in I.7.

**`che fine l'avesse indotto`.** "What end had induced him": the chapter's
test is the end, and `l'effetto` in .02.

**`non mai, o di rado`.** "Never, or seldom" (D97).

**`occorre`.** "Does it occur" (D01).

**`regola generale`.** "General rule". Canestrini quotes this sentence in a
note over Guicciardini's reply.

**`anzi è necessario`.** "Indeed it is necessary": the clause before is
formally negative (`non mai, o di rado ... se non`), but `anzi` raises
"seldom" to "necessary"; it does not reverse (D135) or correct into a
contrary (D90). D147's "indeed", extended (D168).

**`che dia il modo`.** "Who gives the way" (glossary, `modo`).

**`Però`.** "Therefore" (D22).

**`giovare`.** "Benefit", as I.8's `giovano`.

**`debbe ingegnarsi d'avere l'autorità solo`.** "Must contrive to have the
authority alone": `solo` can go with the orderer (that he alone have it) or
with the authority (only it), and the English keeps both open. `Debbe` is
"must" (D120). The 1554 edition reads `solamente`, "only", and so takes the
second (NM-DISC-I9-D003). `Ingegnarsi` and `ingegno`, two lines apart, share a
root English cannot keep.

**`uno ingegno savio`.** "A wise mind": `ingegno`, wit, a mind of judgment.

**`riprenderà`, `si debbe riprendere`.** "Will reprove", "must be reproved".
Guicciardini's noun is `reprensione`, "reproof".

**`straordinaria`.** "Extraordinary": outside the orders (glossary,
`straordinario`).

**`costituire una Repubblica`.** "Constitute a republic". Guicciardini's
`constituite le leggi` and `constituito uno senato` are the same verb.

**`Conviene bene`.** "It is indeed fitting" (D85).

**`accusandolo il fatto, l'effetto lo scusi`.** "The deed accusing him, the
effect excuse him": `accusare`, the verb of I.7 and I.8. The deed and the
effect are the chapter's two terms; `scusa`, "excuse", returns in .03 and in
the last line.

**`violento per guastare ... per racconciare`.** "Violent to spoil ... to
mend". Guicciardini takes up `racconciare`: republics should not need to be
"mended" (`racconcie`) by such a path.

**`Debbe bene intanto essere prudente e virtuoso, che`.** "He must indeed be
so far prudent and virtuous that": `intanto` read as `in tanto`, the
correlative of a consecutive `che`, degree and consequence, not time and
purpose. `Si ha presa`, "has taken to himself". The left-dislocated `quella
autorità ... la` is exposition, and goes into English order (D19). `Virtuoso`
is the adjective of *virtù* (glossary).

**`usare ambiziosamente quello, che da lui virtuosamente fusse stato
usato`.** "Use ambitiously what by him had been virtuously used": the
chiasmus is kept.

**`più pronti al male ch'al bene`.** "Readier for ill than for good" (glossary,
`male`). The 1554 edition reads `chini`, "inclined" (NM-DISC-I9-D004).

**`non è la cosa ordinata per durare molto`.** "The thing ordered is not for
lasting long": `essere per` of fitness (D116). `Quando la rimanga ... quando
la rimane`: subjunctive for the case refused, indicative for the case
granted; "should it remain ... when it remains".

**`stia il mantenerla`.** "It rests with many to maintain it" (glossary,
`mantenere`).

**`causato dalle diverse opinioni`.** "Caused by the diverse opinions":
`causato` goes, by sense, with the not-knowing rather than with `il bene`
(both are masculine), and the English is as open as the Italian.

**`lo dimostra lo avere quello subito ordinato un Senato`.** "Is demonstrated
by that man's having at once ordered a senate": the long subject clause comes
first and the verb late, and the passive keeps the demonstration last.
`Quello` is Romulus (as D165).

**`si consigliasse`, `si deliberasse`.** "Might take counsel", "might
deliberate": the first is reflexive, and the second is taken with it, Romulus
deliberating; it could also be impersonal, "it might be deliberated".

**`ragunare il Senato`.** "Assemble the senate". The 1554 edition reads
`ragionare`, which would be to reason with it (NM-DISC-I9-D005).

**`la cacciata de' Tarquinj`.** "The expulsion of the Tarquins" (names.md),
traditionally 509 BC. `Il che ... Il che`: "Which ... Which", the same
chaining relative twice.

**`non fu innovato alcun ordine dello antico`.** "No order of the ancient one
was innovated": `innovare` transitive, as in older English. The 1554 edition
reads `non fu introdotto alcun nuouo ordine`, "no new order was introduced"
(NM-DISC-I9-D005).

**`testifica`.** "Testifies". The 1554 edition reads `approua`.

**`conformi ad uno vivere civile e libero`.** "Conformable to a civil and free
living".

**`corroborazione`.** "Corroboration", the cognate. I.8's `riscontro` has the
same English word (D160); recorded collapse (D170).

**`sopraddette`.** "Above-said" (D140).

**`Moisè, Licurgo, Solone`.** Moses, Lycurgus, Solon (names.md), the founders
of I.1 and I.2.

**`a proposito del bene comune`.** "To the purpose of the common good".

**`desiderando Agide Re di Sparta`.** "Agis, king of Sparta, desiring": Agis
IV, who tried in the 240s BC to restore the Lycurgan order (cancellation of
debts, redistribution of land) and was put to death by the ephors in 241.
Machiavelli's source is Plutarch's *Agis and Cleomenes*. The 1554 edition
reads `considerando`, "considering" (NM-DISC-I9-D006).

**`ridurre ... tra quelli termini`.** "Bring back within those bounds":
`ridurre`, to lead back.

**`parendogli che per esserne in parte deviati`.** "It seeming to him that
through their having in part deviated from them": `deviati` is plural, the
Spartans. `Parendogli` is "it seeming to him" both times, of Agis and of
Cleomenes. The 1554 edition reads `usciti fuori`, "gone out", for `deviati`
(NM-DISC-I9-D006).

**`quella antica virtù`.** "That ancient *virtù*" (D16).

**`per conseguente`.** "In consequence".

**`di forze e d'imperio`.** "Of forces and of empire": `imperio` as dominion
over others (D124).

**`ne' suoi primi principj`.** "In his first beginnings": at the outset of
the attempt (glossary, `principio`).

**`occupare la tirannide`.** "Occupy the tyranny" (glossary, `occupare`).

**`Cleomene`.** Cleomenes III, king from about 235 BC. In 227 he had four of
the five ephors and some others killed, abolished the ephorate, cancelled
debts and redistributed land; defeated by Antigonus Doson of Macedon at
Sellasia in 222, he fled to Egypt and died there. Plutarch says he learnt of
Agis' plans from Agis' widow, whom he married; the "records and writings" are
Machiavelli's.

**`per li ricordi e scritti`.** "Through the records and writings": `ricordi`,
memoranda.

**`la mente e intenzione sua`.** "His mind and intention".

**`conobbe`.** "Came to know": the passato remoto of an event; `conosciuto
che l'hanno` (.02) is the same verb.

**`la voglia di pochi`.** "The longing of few" (D117).

**`presa occasione conveniente`.** "A fitting occasion taken".

**`fece ammazzare tutti gli Efori`.** "He had all the ephors killed". The 1554
edition reads `fece uenire`, "had [them] come", summoned (NM-DISC-I9-D007);
the killing is what makes Cleomenes the parallel to Romulus.

**`contrastare`.** "Oppose".

**`resuscitare Sparta`.** "Make Sparta rise again".

**`se non fusse stato la potenza de' Macedoni`.** "If it had not been for the
power of the Macedonians": `potenza`, "power" (D78).

**`dopo tale ordine`.** "After such an order": after the new ordering.

**`a chi rifuggire`.** "Anyone with whom to take refuge".

**`restò quel suo disegno, quantunque giusto e laudabile, imperfetto`.** "That
design of his remained, however just and laudable, imperfect": the last word
is the sting, and the English keeps it last (D18). `Imperfetto` is
unfinished, the older sense.

**`Considerato adunque tutte queste cose`.** "All these things, then,
considered": `adunque` "then" (D74). The participle stands invariable; the
1554 edition has `Considerate` (NM-DISC-I9-D008).

**`Remo e ... Tazio`.** Remus and Tatius: the brother and the companion of
.01, named at last.
