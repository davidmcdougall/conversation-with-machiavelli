# Consideration on chapter XIV

*The Romans interpreted the auspices according to necessity, and with prudence
made a show of observing religion when, forced, they did not observe it, and if
anyone rashly despised it, they punished him*

## FG-CONS.1.14.01

I do not hold it for certain that the captains of the armies used the authority
of the auspices and of the auguries craftily, but I believe that, especially in
the first times, their minds were occupied by this religion; nor is repugnant to
me the example of Papirius, who, having had the report from the *pullarii*
whose office it now [was], did not have to attend to what was reported to him by
third persons.

---

## Notes

**The rubric.** Canestrini sets Machiavelli's chapter title over the reply, in
his own spelling, with lower-case `religione` and no full stop.

**`di chi ora lo officio`.** The bracketed "was" is supplied. Canestrini's
`ora`, "now", leaves the clause without a verb; "was" is the word the 1933
edition's `era` would give (see Variants), so the English leans towards that
reading while keeping Canestrini's "now".

**`astutamente`.** "Craftily", not "astutely", which in modern English praises.
Guicciardini's question is whether the captains worked a religion they did not
believe in.

**`né mi repugna`.** "Repugnant" in its older sense: contrary, inconsistent.
The example of Papirius does not tell against Guicciardini's view. Machiavelli
uses the noun two chapters earlier, of his two reasons against the Church, which
"have no repugnance".

**`relazione` ... `referito`.** "Report ... reported". The *pullarii*'s report is
the one Papirius had to act on; what the soldiers told his nephew, who told him,
came from third persons. Machiavelli's `riferì`, `riferendolo`, `riferendogli`
are the same verb and take the same English.

**`attendere`.** "Attend to": Machiavelli's consul tells the nephew to attend to
his own office, and Guicciardini says the consul did not have to attend to
hearsay.
