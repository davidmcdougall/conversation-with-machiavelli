# Consideration on chapter XXX of Book I

*Which ways a prince or a republic must use to flee this vice of ingratitude; and which that captain or that citizen [must use] so as not to be oppressed by it.*

## FG-CONS.1.30.01

I praise [it] that a prince go on the expeditions personally, because they proceed with other reputation, and he is served by all his own otherwise than when he administers them through captains; and I believe that the counsel of the Discourse is perhaps necessary to a tyrant or to whoever does not have his state well firm; but of little fruit to a great and natural king. And of it we see every day the example of our princes, who, although they commonly make the wars through captains, there does not therefore happen to them, or very seldom, one of these mishaps.

---

## Notes

**The rubric.** Canestrini's setting of the chapter title, in lower case, in Machiavelli's words. After `ingratitudine` both readers preferred a colon and doubted it; the semicolon is the 1933 edition's (D110; D324). Both readers saw `a una repubblica`, a broken letter; read `o`, as the sense and the 1933 edition require (D322; FG-CONS-I30-D001). It is heading apparatus, not Guicciardini's (D32).

**`Io laudo che uno principe vadia nelle espedizioni personalmente`.** "I praise [it] that a prince go on the expeditions personally": `laudare`, praise (glossary); `vadia`, the subjunctive of `andare`, kept as an English subjunctive; `espedizioni`, `personalmente`, Machiavelli's words (Machiavelli .01). The reply opens on agreement, with the Discourse's own remedy, and takes up the prince's part only.

**`perché procedono con altra reputazione`.** "Because they proceed with other reputation": the subject is the expeditions; `altra`, other, here different and greater; `procedere`, proceed (glossary, FG I.28); `reputazione`, reputation, as Machiavelli's `riputazione` (I.29). Guicciardini gives two reasons of his own; neither is Machiavelli's, which was that the glory and the acquisition stay the prince's (Machiavelli .01).

**`e altrimenti è servito da tutti e' suoi che quando le amministra per capitani`.** "And he is served by all his own otherwise than when he administers them through captains": `e' suoi`, his own, his men, the plural article by D35; `amministrare`, administer, of the expeditions (`le`); `capitani`, captains, Machiavelli's `Capitano` (Machiavelli .02). The second reason looks to the prince's service, not to his gratitude.

**`e credo che il ricordo del Discorso sia forse necessario`.** "And I believe that the counsel of the Discourse is perhaps necessary": `credo`, I believe; `forse`, perhaps; the belief is hedged inside itself. `ricordo`, counsel: a piece of advice set down to be remembered; it is also the word that names the maxims Guicciardini collected, though nothing here points to them. It names the advice of Machiavelli's first sentence; Machiavelli, for his part, says he has no `precetto`, precept, for the prince who stays at home (Machiavelli .02). `il Discorso`, Machiavelli's chapter, as at FG I.25 and I.29.

**`a uno tiranno o a chi non abbia bene fermo lo Stato suo`.** "To a tyrant or to whoever does not have his state well firm": `tiranno`, tyrant (glossary `tirannide`); `Stato`, state (D265), here a ruler's hold on his dominion; `fermo`, firm. Guicciardini narrows where the remedy is perhaps needed: not princes in general, but the tyrant and the prince who does not hold his state well. `bene` is kept: the insecure, not the wholly insecure.

**`ma di poco frutto a uno re grande e naturale`.** "But of little fruit to a great and natural king": `frutto`, fruit, profit; `naturale`, natural, a king by birth, against the tyrant. The verb is carried over from `sia`; the English carries it too.

**`E ne vediamo tutto dì lo esemplo de' principi nostri`.** "And of it we see every day the example of our princes": `ne`, of it; `tutto dì`, every day; `de' principi nostri`, of our princes. Which princes he means he does not say; the evidence is what is seen every day, against Machiavelli's Roman emperors and the Turk.

**`e' quali se bene communemente fanno le guerre per capitani`.** "Who, although they commonly make the wars through captains": `se bene`, although (D31); `communemente`, commonly. Machiavelli's prince who stays at home was negligent or imprudent (Machiavelli .02); Guicciardini's commonly does so, and is not called either.

**`non gli accade però, o rarissime volte, uno di questi sinistri`.** "There does not therefore happen to them, or very seldom, one of these mishaps": `però`, therefore (D22): making war through captains does not, for that reason, bring the mishap; `accadere`, happen (D298), with the dative `gli`, the princes; `rarissime volte`, very seldom (D97), correcting the negation downward without replacing it: the princes are not said never to suffer these things, nor only to suffer them seldom; `sinistri`, mishaps, the noun of `sinistro`, the ill turns Machiavelli described: ingratitude to a victorious captain, or a captain's revolt. The reply ends here, with a stop, and says nothing of the captain, the republic or Rome.
