# Consideration on chapter VI of Book I

*Whether in Rome a state could be ordered that should take away the enmities
between the people and the senate*

## FG-CONS.1.6.01

I believe it to be true that the Romans wishing to employ the plebs in war, as,
through the small number of the patricians, they were necessitated, wishing to
employ their own arms, that it was necessary to keep it content, and the
patricians' not wishing to do this, was the cause of so many tumults and
seditions, for they neither wished to admit them into the government, nor
abstained from those injuries that gave the plebs cause to desire to partake
of it, for they occupied the public possessions, and were very rigid in the
exaction of the debts, and one can believe that in all the other things
justice was unequal in favour of that part which had all the authority in
hand. But I do say, that if in the beginning of liberty there had not been, as
is said in the fourth Discourse, the distinction between patricians and the
plebs; or, as was done afterwards by necessity, the honours had been
communicated from the beginning, that there would not have been among them
those tumults and seditions, which ceased as soon as the government was
communicated, up to the time of the Gracchi, in whose [time], the city being
already corrupt, the seditions arose through new humours and causes, which
were no longer of the plebs against the patricians, but of the low people
against the richer and more powerful, in which number were included many
plebeian families already ennobled through the honours. I say also that if
the patricians, without communicating the government entirely to the plebs,
had known how to set some good order to the injuries, and had opened the path
by which at certain times the principal plebeians could have been made
patricians, that perhaps there would not have been those tumults, for one sees
by experience that in the laws proposed by Publius Sextius, the plebs was
content to provide for the debts and for the goods occupied, and of the
honours it took no care; except that the principal plebeians, who had appetite
for the government, nor could enter it by another path, excluded the plebs
from hope of being able to obtain the one without the other. I do not see,
then, that it was impossible for the Romans to order the government in such a
way that between the senate and the plebs there were not to be those tumults
and seditions, on the contrary I judge it very easy, and since it could be
done, one cannot praise those defects of the government, which were the cause
that the city stayed full of tumults and seditions, and of creating the
tribunes, which magistracy, once the city was pacified, armed with so many
authorities, was rather damaging than useful

## Notes

**The rubric.** Canestrini sets Machiavelli's chapter title over the reply,
with `intra` for the 1824 `tra`, lower-case `popolo` and `senato`, `ed il` for
`e il`, and without a full stop.

**The paragraph.** Canestrini prints the reply as one paragraph, and it is
kept.

**`Io credo essere vero`.** "I believe it to be true": assent (`credo`), to
the premise only. What follows moves the cause.

**`che volendo e' Romani ... che era necessario`.** "That the Romans wishing
..., that it was necessary": the `che` is repeated after the long gerund
clause, as Guicciardini repeats it after each of his two counterfactuals. The
English keeps all three (D136).

**`necessitati`.** "Necessitated", as in I.5: compelled.

**`le armi proprie`.** "Their own arms": a citizen soldiery, not hired troops.

**`il non volere fare questo e' patrizii`.** "The patricians' not wishing to do
this": the nominal infinitive with its subject after it, as in I.4 (D109).

**`parteciparne`.** "Partake of it": of the government (glossary,
`partecipare di`).

**`le possessioni publiche`.** "The public possessions": the *ager publicus*,
public land held and worked by the rich. Below, `a' beni occupati` is "the
goods occupied": two words, two words (D139).

**`come è detto nel quarto Discorso`.** "As is said in the fourth Discourse":
Machiavelli's I.4, on the disunion of plebs and senate. `Discorso` with a
capital is Machiavelli's chapter, as in his reply to I.3 (`si dice nel
Discorso`). His own reply to I.4 put the cause of the disunion where he puts
it here, in the shutting of the plebs out of the honours.

**`comunicati gli onori`, `il governo fu comunicato`, `[comunicare]`.**
"Communicated", three times: shared. The third is an emendation: Canestrini
prints `conciare`, the 1933 edition `communicare` (D126).

**`insino al tempo de' Gracchi`.** "Up to the time of the Gracchi", as
Machiavelli's `in fino al tempo de' Gracchi` (.01). The comma before it is the
English's, so that "up to" attaches to "ceased" (D137).

**`ne' quali`.** "In whose [time]": the plural takes the Gracchi.

**`umori`.** "Humours", as in Machiavelli I.4.

**`la gente bassa`.** "The low people": the poor, against the rich, among whom
were plebeian families ennobled by office.

**`porre qualche buono ordine alle ingiurie`.** "Set some good order to the
injuries": `porre` is "set" (D115).

**`plebei principali`.** "Principal plebeians": the leading plebeian families.

**`forse`.** "Perhaps": on the second counterfactual only.

**`Publio Sestio`.** "Publius Sextius": Livy's Lucius Sextius, tribune with
Gaius Licinius Stolo, whose laws (367 BC) eased debts, limited holdings of
public land and opened the consulship to plebeians. Guicciardini's point is
Livy's (6.39): the plebs would have taken the relief and left the office, and
the tribunes would not let the laws be voted apart. The name is kept as printed.

**`appetivano`.** "Had appetite for": the verb of `appetito` (D117).

**`conseguire l'uno sanza l'altro`.** "Obtain the one without the other":
relief without office.

**`Non veggo adunque`.** "I do not see, then": not `non concorro`, "I do not
concur", but a flat denial of the impossibility Machiavelli argued.

**`non avessino a essere`.** "There were not to be" (D61).

**`anzi lo giudico molto facile`.** "On the contrary I judge it very easy":
`anzi` reverses, and the reversal is his judgment (`giudico`). Kept apart
from `più presto`, "rather", in the last line (D135).

**`defetti`.** "Defects" (glossary, `difetto`): Canestrini's spelling.

**`stessi piena`.** "Stayed full": `stare`.

**`tante autorità`.** "So many authorities": the tribunes' several powers, as
in his reply to I.3 (D138).

**`dannoso`.** "Damaging" (D106).

**No full stop.** Canestrini prints none after `utile`; the 1933 edition
does. The English ends as the Italian does (D40).
