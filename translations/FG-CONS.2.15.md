# Consideration on chapter XV

## FG-CONS.2.15.01

From two causes the ambiguities of deliberations proceed: the one from weakness of those who are to resolve, I do not say weakness of forces and of power, but weakness of prudence and of mind, and this cause can fall as well in a prince as in a republic; and I believe that when the Discourse said weak states, it understood weak in prudence, albeit weakness of forces can in part increase irresolution; because commonly the courses that weak states are to take are commonly more full of difficulties and of dangers. The other cause, which is proper to republics, is when there are several men who are to resolve, and among these the opinions are various; which can proceed either from malignity, because they have diverse ends, or else without malignity, because the judgments of men do not conform [with one another], as happens often even among prudent [men]. And it is true that these suspensions are commonly pernicious, because while you stand suspended you cannot provide for either the one case or the other; and if sometimes they are useful, as would have happened to the Lavinians, who, if they had delayed yet three or four days more in resolving themselves, would not have suffered penalty for that little journey, nevertheless this is a utility that results rather by chance than otherwise. Suspension is then to be abhorred, resolution supremely to be praised, but one has to take heed that standing neutral can also proceed from resolution, not from suspension; in the second case neutrality is reprehensible, in the first it can be both useful and pernicious, according to the quality of the cases, of which to treat is not now our matter. I say the same of deferring some other action or execution; for if the tardiness proceeds from irresolution it is always damnable, but if it is done deliberately it can be laudable.

## Notes

**The heading and the rubric.** Canestrini's heading, CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO XV, without `del libro II`, as both readers saw it; the column is headed as the page is (D443, D471). His rubric is Machiavelli's sentence (Machiavelli, rubric), `fieno` included, without the comma; the 1933 edition prints `fiano` and `diliberazioni` (FG-CONS-II15-D002). Canestrini prints no reply to chapters XVI to XVIII; his next is to chapter XIX.

**`Da due cagioni procedono le ambiguità delle deliberazioni`.** "From two causes the ambiguities of deliberations proceed": the count set first, as printed, the verb in plain order (D263); `cagione`, cause (glossary); `procedere`, proceed, Machiavelli's verb for the sources of slow deliberations (Machiavelli .03), held four times in the reply; `ambiguità`, ambiguity, plural as printed; `deliberazione`, deliberation (glossary). Guicciardini opens on the first half of the rubric, ambiguity; slowness comes in the Lavinians' case and directly at the end.

**`l'una da debolezza di quelli che hanno a risolvere`.** "The one from weakness of those who are to resolve": `avere a`, destinative, "are to" (D261), whose part it is to decide; `risolvere`, resolve (glossary), with `resolvere`, `resolversi`, `resoluzione`, `irresoluzione` below, one root in five forms.

**`non dico debolezza di forze e di potenza, ma debolezza di prudenza e di ingegno`.** "I do not say weakness of forces and of power, but weakness of prudence and of mind": `forze`, forces, and `potenza`, power (glossary, D06); `prudenza`, prudence (glossary); `ingegno`, mind (D178). The correction takes Machiavelli's own pair, `debolezza di animo e di forze` (Machiavelli .03), and moves the weakness from forces to judgment; Machiavelli's `animo`, spirit, is not Guicciardini's `ingegno`, mind.

**`e questa cagione può cadere così in uno principe come in una republica`.** "And this cause can fall as well in a prince as in a republic": `cadere`, fall, of a defect that befalls; `così ... come`, as well ... as (D334): both are open to it, not equally.

**`e credo che quando il Discorso disse gli Stati deboli, intese deboli di prudenza`.** "And I believe that when the Discourse said weak states, it understood weak in prudence": `credo`, I believe (glossary): assent to a reading of Machiavelli, not a certainty; `il Discorso`, the Discourse, the chapter, through its rubric; `intendere`, understand (D374), in the period's sense of what a sentence is taken to mean, as at FG II.10.

**`benché la debolezza delle forze può in parte accrescere la irresoluzione`.** "Albeit weakness of forces can in part increase irresolution": `benché`, albeit (D31), with the indicative, as printed; `in parte`, in part, the measure of the concession; `irresoluzione`, irresolution.

**`perché communemente e' partiti che hanno a pigliare gli Stati deboli, sono communemente più pieni di difficultà e di periculi`.** "Because commonly the courses that weak states are to take are commonly more full of difficulties and of dangers": `communemente` twice, kept twice; `partito`, course (D236); `pigliare`, take (glossary); `difficultà`, difficulty (D464), plural here with `periculi`; `periculo`, danger (glossary), in Canestrini's spelling, as at II.14 (D462). The `perché` gives the reason for the concession, not a new claim.

**`L'altra cagione, che è propria delle republiche, è quando sono più uomini che hanno a resolvere`.** "The other cause, which is proper to republics, is when there are several men who are to resolve": `proprio`, proper, belonging to republics alone; `più uomini`, several men; `resolvere`, resolve, in Guicciardini's other spelling.

**`e tra questi sono le opinioni varie`.** "And among these the opinions are various": `opinione`, opinion (D02); `vario`, various.

**`il che può procedere o da malignità, perché abbino diversi fini, o pure sanza malignità, perché e' giudicii degli uomini non si conformino`.** "Which can proceed either from malignity, because they have diverse ends, or else without malignity, because the judgments of men do not conform [with one another]": `malignità`, malignity (glossary), Machiavelli's word for those who block deliberation (Machiavelli .03), here one of two grounds of variety only; `o pure`, or else; `fine`, end; `giudicio`, judgment; `conformarsi`, conform, reflexive and reciprocal, with the bracket (D53). The two `perché` give the two grounds; the subjunctives (`abbino`, `conformino`) are rendered as indicatives, the possibility being carried by `può procedere`.

**`come accade spesso etiam tra prudenti`.** "As happens often even among prudent [men]": `accadere`, happen (D298); `etiam`, even (latin.md; glossary, D453); `prudenti`, prudent, the adjective as a noun, with "men" supplied (D53).

**`E è vero che queste sospensioni communemente sono perniziose`.** "And it is true that these suspensions are commonly pernicious": `E è`, as printed (the 1933 edition `Ed è`); `sospensione`, suspension, Guicciardini's noun for Machiavelli's `stare sospeso` (Machiavelli .04) and for both his ambiguity and his slowness: the reply's key word, spelled `sospensioni` here and `suspensione` twice below, one word; `pernizioso`, pernicious (glossary), answering Machiavelli's `nocive`, harmful (rubric, .03), in a stronger word (his `perniciosa`, .03, is of the side a popular rush turns to). `è vero che`, it is true that, opens a concession that `nondimeno` will limit.

**`perché mentre stai sospeso non puoi provedere né all'uno caso né all'altro`.** "Because while you stand suspended you cannot provide for either the one case or the other": `stare sospeso`, stand suspended, Machiavelli's phrase (Machiavelli .04) turned to the second person; `provedere`, provide (glossary), here with `a`, for; `caso`, case.

**`e se qualche volta sono utili, come sarebbe accaduto a' Lavinii`.** "And if sometimes they are useful, as would have happened to the Lavinians": `qualche volta`, sometimes (D431); `utile`, useful (the adjective, D135); `Lavinii`, the Lavinians, Machiavelli's `Lavinj` (Machiavelli .05; names.md).

**`e' quali se fussino tardati ancora tre o quattro dì più a resolversi, non arebbono patito pena di quello poco viaggio`.** "Who, if they had delayed yet three or four days more in resolving themselves, would not have suffered penalty for that little journey": `tardare`, delay, the root of `tardità`; `ancora`, yet; `resolversi`, resolve themselves, the rubric's reflexive; `patire pena`, suffer penalty; `viaggio`, journey, answering Milionius' `Questo poco della via` (Machiavelli .05). The editor's reading: three or four days more, and the news of the rout would have come before they marched.

**`nondimeno questa è una utilità che risulta più presto per caso che altrimenti`.** "Nevertheless this is a utility that results rather by chance than otherwise": `nondimeno`, nevertheless (glossary), limiting the concession; `utilità`, utility (D472), kept apart from the adjective `utile`, useful (D135), and the noun `utile`, advantage (D283); `risultare`, result; `più presto ... che`, rather ... than (D135); `per caso`, by chance, a sense split from `caso`, case (D472).

**`La suspensione è adunque da aborrire, la resoluzione sommamente da laudare`.** "Suspension is then to be abhorred, resolution supremely to be praised": `adunque`, then (D74), the conclusion drawn from what precedes; the second member without a verb, kept so; `aborrire`, abhor; `sommamente`, supremely; `laudare`, praise (D422).

**`ma s'ha da avvertire che lo stare neutrale può anche procedere per resoluzione, non per suspensione`.** "But one has to take heed that standing neutral can also proceed from resolution, not from suspension": the comma before `ma` kept, so that the qualification stays in the movement of the conclusion; `avere da`, has to (D261, the sense necessity); `avvertire`, take heed; `stare neutrale`, standing neutral, the orators' accord that the Florentines `stessero neutrali`, "stand neutral" (Machiavelli .06), with `stare` held; `per`, from, of the source.

**`nel secondo caso la neutralità è reprensibile, nel primo può essere e utile e perniziosa`.** "In the second case neutrality is reprehensible, in the first it can be both useful and pernicious": the second case is neutrality from suspension, the first neutrality from resolution: the order is reversed, as printed. `reprensibile`, reprehensible, a recorded split from D173's `riprendere`, `reprensione`, reprove, reproof: the adjective takes the cognate (D472).

**`secondo la qualità de' casi, di che trattare non è ora materia nostra`.** "According to the quality of the cases, of which to treat is not now our matter": `qualità`, quality (glossary); `trattare`, treat (glossary); `materia`, matter, as Machiavelli's `materia` (Machiavelli .01, .07). Guicciardini declines the question of neutrality here.

**`Il medesimo dico del differire qualche altra azione o esecuzione`.** "I say the same of deferring some other action or execution": plain order (D263); `differire`, defer (D334), Machiavelli's verb for the Florentines and the Lavinians; `azione`, action; `esecuzione`, execution (glossary).

**`ché se la tardità procede da irresoluzione è sempre dannabile, ma se si fa deliberatamente può essere laudabile`.** "For if the tardiness proceeds from irresolution it is always damnable, but if it is done deliberately it can be laudable": `ché`, for; `tardità`, tardiness, Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .04); `dannabile`, damnable, Machiavelli's word for the Florentine course (Machiavelli .06); `deliberatamente`, deliberately, the adverb of `deliberazione`: delay that is itself a deliberation; `laudabile`, laudable (glossary). `sempre` answers the rubric's `sempre` twice over: what is always harmful is only the tardiness of irresolution. Canestrini's note on p. 69, keyed after the last word, his summary of Machiavelli's chapter, is not carried (by precedent, D463; D470). The reply ends with Canestrini's stop.
