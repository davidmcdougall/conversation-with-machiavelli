# Discourses II.15

## NM-DISC.2.15.01

In this same matter, and in these same beginnings of war between the Latins and the Romans, one can note that in every consultation it is good to come to the individual [point] of that which is to be deliberated, and not to stand always in the ambiguous, nor upon the uncertain of the thing. Which is seen manifest in the consultation that the Latins made when they were thinking to alienate themselves from the Romans. Because, the Romans having had a presentiment of this bad humour that had entered into the Latin peoples, to certify themselves of the thing, and to see whether they could, without putting hand to arms, regain those peoples, made them understand that they send to Rome eight citizens, because they were to consult with them. The Latins, having understood this, and having conscience of many things done against the will of the Romans, made council to order who must go to Rome, and to give him commission of that which he was to say. And, being in the council on this dispute, Annius, their praetor, said these words. *Ad summam rerum nostrarum pertinere arbitror, ut cogitetis magis, quid agendum nobis, quam quid loquendum sit. Facile erit, explicatis consiliis, accomodare rebus verba.*

## NM-DISC.2.15.02

These words are without doubt most true, and must be relished by every prince and by every republic; because in the ambiguity and in the incertitude of that which one wishes to do, one does not know how to accommodate the words; but the spirit once fixed, and deliberated what is to be executed, it is an easy thing to find the words for it. I have noted this part the more willingly, inasmuch as I have many times known such ambiguity to have harmed public business, with damage and with shame to our republic. And it will always ever happen that in doubtful courses, and where spirit is required to deliberate them, there will be this ambiguity, when they are to be counselled and deliberated by weak men.

## NM-DISC.2.15.03

No less harmful also are slow and tardy deliberations than ambiguous [ones], above all those that are to be deliberated in favour of some friend; because with their slowness one aids no person, and one harms oneself. These deliberations so made proceed either from weakness of spirit and of forces, or from the malignity of those who are to deliberate; who, moved by their own passion of wishing to ruin the state, or to fulfil some desire of theirs, do not let the deliberation follow, but impede it and cross it. Because good citizens, even though they see a popular rush turning toward the pernicious side, will never impede deliberating, above all on those things that do not wait for time.

## NM-DISC.2.15.04

Hieronymus the tyrant being dead in Syracuse, and the war being great between the Carthaginians and the Romans, the Syracusans came into dispute whether they must follow the Roman friendship or the Carthaginian. And so great was the ardour of the parties that the thing stood ambiguous, nor was any course taken on it, until Apollonides, one of the first [men] in Syracuse, with an oration of his full of prudence, showed that he who held the opinion of adhering to the Romans was not to be blamed, nor those who wished to follow the Carthaginian party; but that that ambiguity and tardiness in taking the course was indeed to be detested, because he saw in such ambiguity altogether the ruin of the republic; but, the course once taken, whatever it might be, one could hope for some good. Nor could Livy show more than he does in this part the damage that standing suspended draws behind it.

## NM-DISC.2.15.05

He demonstrates it also in this case of the Latins; because the Lavinians, being sought by them for aid against the Romans, deferred so long in deliberating it that, when they had just gone out of the gate with the troops to give them succour, the news came that the Latins were routed. Whence Milionius, their praetor, said: This little of the path will cost us much with the Roman people. Because, if they had deliberated earlier either to aid or not to aid the Latins, not aiding them they would not have irritated the Romans; aiding them, the aid being in time, they could with the addition of their forces have made them win; but deferring, they came to lose in any case, as befell them.

## NM-DISC.2.15.06

And if the Florentines had noted this text, they would not have had with the French either so many damages or so many vexations as they had in the passage that King Louis XII of France made into Italy against Lodovico, Duke of Milan. Because, the King treating of such a passage, [he] sought the Florentines for accord, and the orators who were near the King came to [an] accord with him that they stand neutral, and that the King, coming into Italy, was to maintain them in the state and receive them into protection, and [he] gave the city a month's time to ratify it. Such ratification was deferred by whoever through little prudence favoured the things of Lodovico, insomuch that, the King being already upon the victory, and the Florentines later wishing to ratify, the ratification was not accepted, as [by] one who knew the Florentines to have come forced, and not voluntary, into his friendship. Which cost the city of Florence much money, and it was about to lose the state, as later, another time, befell it for a similar cause. And that course was all the more damnable because one did not serve even Duke Lodovico [by it]; who, if he had won, would have shown many more signs of enmity against the Florentines than the King did.

## NM-DISC.2.15.07

And albeit it has been discoursed above, in another chapter, of the ill that arises to republics from this weakness, nonetheless, having occasion for it anew through a new accident, I have wished to repeat it, it seeming to me above all a matter that must be noted by republics similar to ours.

## Notes

**The rubric.** "Weak states will always be ambiguous in resolving themselves, and always slow deliberations are harmful": `Stati`, states (D265); `debole`, weak (D464); `fieno`, will be, the old future, as the 1824 reads it with both readers' preference at a ligature they doubt; the 1554 reads `ſieno`, "be" (NM-DISC-II15-D007; O48, D471). The two `sempre` stand as two claims, the first after its subject, the second leading its clause, and the English keeps each in place. `ambiguo`, ambiguous (glossary); `risolversi`, resolve oneself, reflexive; `deliberazione`, deliberation (glossary); `lento`, slow; `nocivo`, harmful (D106). Canestrini sets the same sentence over Guicciardini's reply, without the comma (D471).

**`In questa medesima materia, e in questi medesimi principj di guerra`.** "In this same matter, and in these same beginnings of war": `materia`, matter, again at the close (.07); `principio`, beginning (glossary), the outbreak of the Latin war, which II.13 and II.14 have been treating. The chapter continues II.14 from Livy's eighth book.

**`si può notare, come in ogni consulta è bene venire allo individuo di quello che si ha a deliberare`.** "One can note that in every consultation it is good to come to the individual [point] of that which is to be deliberated": `come` after a verb of noting, "that" (D334), as at `mostrò, come` (.04); `individuo`, the indivisible, the particular in which a question can be settled, kept as the cognate with the supplied noun, since "the individual" alone would read as a person. The phrase is the chapter's positive term; everything after it is its opposite. `avere a` destinative, "is to be" (D261).

**`non stare sempre in ambiguo, né in su lo incerto della cosa`.** "Not to stand always in the ambiguous, nor upon the uncertain of the thing": two adjectives used as nouns, `ambiguo` and `incerto`, kept as English substantives. `stare`, stand, the verb of `la cosa stava ambigua` (.04), `lo stare sospeso` "standing suspended" (.04) and `stessero neutrali` (.06), and of Guicciardini's `stai sospeso` and `lo stare neutrale` (Guicciardini .01): one English verb for the posture throughout.

**`quando e' pensavano alienarsi da' Romani`.** "When they were thinking to alienate themselves from the Romans": `e'`, they, the old subject pronoun.

**`avendo presentito questo cattivo umore`.** "Having had a presentiment of this bad humour": `presentire`, to sense beforehand; `cattivo`, bad (D03); `umore`, humour (glossary). The participle has the Romans as its subject; the English names them first.

**`fecero loro intendere, come e' mandassero a Roma otto cittadini, perché avevano a consultare con loro`.** "Made them understand that they send to Rome eight citizens, because they were to consult with them": the reported request without "should" (D348, D445); `avere a`, were to (D261); `consultare`, consult, with `consulta`, consultation.

**`avendo coscienza di molte cose fatte contro alla voglia de' Romani`.** "Having conscience of many things done against the will of the Romans": `coscienza`, conscience, in its old sense of inward knowledge, kept as the cognate because the knowledge is of their own wrongs and the facing Italian shows the word; `voglia`, will, in this construction (D240).

**`fecero consiglio per ordinare chi dovesse ire a Roma`.** "Made council to order who must go to Rome": `fare consiglio`, make council, the Latins' council in session, D452's `Consiglio`, council, extended to the sitting; `ordinare`, order (glossary); `dovere`, must (D274), as `dovevano` (.04); `ire`, go.

**`E stando nel consiglio di questa disputa`.** "And, being in the council on this dispute": the gerund has the Latins as its subject. The 1554 reads `in questa disputa` (NM-DISC-II15-D001).

**`Annio loro Pretore`.** "Annius, their praetor": names.md, as II.13 and II.14.

**`Ad summam rerum nostrarum ... accomodare rebus verba`.** Livy 8.4.1 (the opening of Annius' speech; the editor's reference): "I judge that it pertains to the sum of our affairs that you think more what is to be done by us than what is to be said. It will be easy, counsels once unfolded, to accommodate words to things." The 1824 spells `accomodare` with one m; the 1554 follows the Latin with an Italian rendering, not carried (NM-DISC-II15-D001). Machiavelli's own sentence takes up `accomodare` (.02).

**`debbono essere da ogni Principe e da ogni Repubblica gustate`.** "Must be relished by every prince and by every republic": `dovere`, must (D274); `gustare`, relish, to taste and take to heart.

**`nella ambiguità e nella incertitudine di quello che altri voglia fare`.** "In the ambiguity and in the incertitude of that which one wishes to do": `incertitudine`, incertitude, kept apart from `incerto` (.01); `altri`, one, the indefinite pronoun: the uncertainty is of one's own purpose. `non si sanno accomodare`, "one does not know how to accommodate", `sapere` with the infinitive.

**`ma fermo una volta l'animo, e deliberato quello sia da eseguire`.** "But the spirit once fixed, and deliberated what is to be executed": two absolute participles, kept; `animo`, spirit (glossary); `eseguire`, execute (glossary).

**`Io ho notato questa parte più volentieri, quanto io ho molte volte conosciuto`.** "I have noted this part the more willingly, inasmuch as I have many times known": `quanto`, inasmuch as, after the comparative; `conoscere`, know (D374). The first person of Machiavelli's own experience, which .06 makes concrete.

**`tale ambiguità avere nociuto alle pubbliche azioni, con danno e con vergogna della Repubblica nostra`.** "Such ambiguity to have harmed public business, with damage and with shame to our republic": `azioni publiche`, public business (D389); `danno`, damage (D106); `vergogna`, shame; `Repubblica nostra`, Florence, as the close's `simili alla nostra`.

**`E sempre mai avverrà ... dove bisogni animo a deliberarli`.** "And it will always ever happen ... where spirit is required to deliberate them": `sempre mai`, always ever, the doubled adverb kept; `bisogna`, it is required (D260; see the note on `bisogni` at Machiavelli I.2), here subjunctive; `partiti dubbj`, doubtful courses (`partito`, course, D236).

**`quando abbiano ad esser consigliati e deliberati da uomini deboli`.** "When they are to be counselled and deliberated by weak men": `avere a`, are to (D261); `consigliare`, counsel, D444's `consiglio` extended to the verb; `deboli`, weak, the rubric's word, now of men.

**`Non sono meno nocive ancora le deliberazioni lente e tarde, che ambigue`.** "No less harmful also are slow and tardy deliberations than ambiguous [ones]": the fronted predicate kept, as a contrast answering the ambiguity just treated (D263); `ancora`, also; `tardo`, tardy, with `tardità`, tardiness (.04), and Guicciardini's `tardità` (Guicciardini .01); the bracket supplies the noun the comparison leaves to `che ambigue` (D53).

**`massime quelle che si hanno a deliberare in favore di alcuno amico`.** "Above all those that are to be deliberated in favour of some friend": `massime`, above all (glossary), here and twice more (.03, .07); `amico`, friend (glossary).

**`con la lentezza loro non si ajuta persona, e nuocesi a sé medesimo`.** "With their slowness one aids no person, and one harms oneself": `aiutare`, aid (D348); `persona`, no person, with the negative; `nuocere`, harm (D106). The 1554 omits the article before `lentezza` (NM-DISC-II15-D002).

**`procedono o da debolezza di animo e di forze, o da malignità di coloro che hanno a deliberare`.** "Proceed either from weakness of spirit and of forces, or from the malignity of those who are to deliberate": `debolezza`, weakness, with `debole`; `forze`, forces (glossary); `malignità`, malignity (glossary). Guicciardini takes the two sources, weakness and malignity, and redistributes them (Guicciardini .01). The 1554 reads `procedano` (NM-DISC-II15-D002).

**`di volere rovinare lo Stato, o adempire qualche suo desiderio`.** "Of wishing to ruin the state, or to fulfil some desire of theirs": `o`, or, two motives; the 1554's `e`, and, would join them (NM-DISC-II15-D002). `suo`, his, the singular after the plural `i quali`, rendered "theirs".

**`non lasciano seguire la deliberazione, ma la impediscono e l'attraversano`.** "Do not let the deliberation follow, but impede it and cross it": `seguire`, follow, of a decision taking effect; `attraversare`, cross, to set oneself across its path.

**`Perché i buoni Cittadini ... mai impediranno il deliberare`.** "Because good citizens ... will never impede deliberating": the `perché` gives the ground for calling the delayers malignant, not a further claim. `foga`, rush, the heat and onrush of the people; the 1554 reads `fuga`, flight, which hardly construes with `voltarsi` (NM-DISC-II15-D002). `pernicioso`, pernicious (glossary); `parte`, side, of a direction, a sense split from `parte`, party (D389), which the faction keeps at .04 (D472). `che non aspettano tempo`, that do not wait for time.

**`Morto che fu Girolamo Tiranno in Siracusa`.** "Hieronymus the tyrant being dead in Syracuse": names.md, as Machiavelli I.58; Hieronymus was killed in 214 BC (Livy 24.7; the debate is Livy 24.28; the editor's references). `Tiranno`, tyrant, capitalised as printed.

**`vennero i Siracusani in disputa se dovevano seguire l'amicizia romana o la cartaginese`.** "The Syracusans came into dispute whether they must follow the Roman friendship or the Carthaginian": `amicizia`, friendship, alliance in the period's word, kept; `dovere`, must (D274), in the indirect question as at `chi dovesse ire` (.01).

**`E tanto era l'ardor delle parti`.** "And so great was the ardour of the parties": the order of the correlative, which English keeps; `parte`, party (D389), the faction, and so "the Carthaginian party" below.

**`Apollonide, uno dei primi in Siracusa, con una sua Orazione piena di prudenza mostrò, come`.** "Apollonides, one of the first [men] in Syracuse, with an oration of his full of prudence, showed that": `Apollonide`, Apollonides (names.md); `Orazione`, oration, capitalised as printed; `prudenza`, prudence (glossary), the quality Guicciardini names as what the weak lack (Guicciardini .01); `mostrare, come`, show that (D334). Apollonides' argument stays in indirect speech.

**`ma che era ben da detestare quella ambiguità e tardità di pigliare il partito`.** "But that that ambiguity and tardiness in taking the course was indeed to be detested": `ben`, indeed; `che` keeps the clause in Apollonides' demonstration (the 1554 drops it, NM-DISC-II15-D003); `pigliare`, take (glossary), and `prendere` (`né se ne prendeva alcun partito`, `preso che si fusse il partito`) take too, a recorded collapse (D472).

**`perché vedeva al tutto in tale ambiguità la rovina della Repubblica`.** "Because he saw in such ambiguity altogether the ruin of the republic": `al tutto`, altogether, wholly; `rovina`, ruin (glossary).

**`ma preso che si fusse il partito, qualunque e' si fusse, si poteva sperare qualche bene`.** "But, the course once taken, whatever it might be, one could hope for some good": the whole of Apollonides' teaching, and the chapter's: any resolution over none.

**`Né potrebbe mostrare più Tito Livio ... il danno che si tira dietro lo stare sospeso`.** "Nor could Livy show more than he does in this part the damage that standing suspended draws behind it": plain order (D263); `Tito Livio`, Livy (names.md); `sospeso`, suspended, the word Guicciardini takes up in `stai sospeso` and turns into his noun `suspensione`, suspension (Guicciardini .01).

**`Dimostralo ancora in questo caso de' Latini`.** "He demonstrates it also in this case of the Latins": Livy is the subject. The story is Livy 8.11.3-4 (the editor's reference).

**`sendo i Lavinj ricerchi da loro d'ajuto`.** "The Lavinians, being sought by them for aid": `Lavinj`, the Lavinians, as printed (names.md); in modern texts of Livy the people are the Lanuvini, of Lanuvium (the editor's identification), and Guicciardini also writes `Lavinii` (Guicciardini .01). The 1554 reads `i Latini`, which has the Latins sought by themselves (NM-DISC-II15-D004). `ricercare`, seek.

**`differirono tanto a deliberarlo`.** "Deferred so long in deliberating it": `differire`, defer (D334).

**`quando eglino erano usciti appunto fuori della porta con la gente`.** "When they had just gone out of the gate with the troops": `appunto`, just; `gente`, troops (glossary), with the article and no possessive.

**`venne la nuova i Latini essere rotti`.** "The news came that the Latins were routed": `nuova`, news; the accusative with infinitive, which English cannot keep; `rotto`, routed (D431).

**`Donde Milonio loro Pretore disse: Questo poco della via ci costerà assai col Popolo romano`.** "Whence Milionius, their praetor, said: This little of the path will cost us much with the Roman people": `Milonio`, Milionius (names.md); the words unquoted, as printed; `via`, path (D131), here the short march they had made; `assai`, much. Guicciardini answers with `quello poco viaggio`, "that little journey" (Guicciardini .01).

**`Perché se si deliberavano prima ... ei non irritavano i Romani ... potevano ... farli vincere`.** "Because, if they had deliberated earlier ... they would not have irritated the Romans ... they could ... have made them win": the imperfects of the unreal past condition, in protasis and apodoses alike, which English gives as pluperfect and "would have", "could have"; `deliberarsi`, reflexive, to make up one's mind, rendered "deliberated" to hold the chapter's verb; `prima`, earlier (D334); `ei`, they; `irritare`, irritate (D464); `vincere`, win (D431).

**`con l'aggiunta delle loro forze`.** "With the addition of their forces": `aggiunta`, addition; `forze`, forces (glossary).

**`ma differendo, venivano a perdere in ogni modo, come intervenne loro`.** "But deferring, they came to lose in any case, as befell them": `in ogni modo`, in any case (D431); `intervenire`, befall (D298).

**`E se i Fiorentini avessero notato questo testo`.** "And if the Florentines had noted this text": `testo`, text, Livy's, as II.14's `questo testo`; `notare`, note, Machiavelli's verb at .02 and .07.

**`né tanti danni, né tante noje`.** "Either so many damages or so many vexations": `danno`, damage (D106), here plural; `noia`, vexation.

**`nella passata del re Luigi di Francia XII`.** "In the passage that King Louis XII of France made into Italy": `passata`, passage, the descent of 1499 (the editor's date); the numeral after `di Francia`, as printed, moved after the name in English.

**`trattando il Re tale passata, ricercò i Fiorentini d'accordo`.** "The King treating of such a passage, [he] sought the Florentines for accord": `trattare`, treat (glossary), of negotiating; the subject of `ricercò` is not repeated in the Italian, and the bracket supplies it after the absolute (D53); `ricercare d'accordo`, seek for accord, as `ricerchi ... d'ajuto` (.05), a sense split from D464's adverbial `d'accordo`, by accord (D472).

**`accordarono con lui ch'egli stessero neutrali`.** "Came to [an] accord with him that they stand neutral": `accordare`, come to [an] accord (D464); `egli`, they, the plural subject pronoun; the reported agreement without "should" (D348); `neutrale`, neutral, Guicciardini's `lo stare neutrale` (Guicciardini .01). The 1554 has the reflexive `s'accordarono` (NM-DISC-II15-D005).

**`gli avesse a mantenere nello Stato e ricevere in protezione`.** "Was to maintain them in the state and receive them into protection": `avere a`, was to (D261); `Stato`, the state, Florence's regime.

**`Fu differita tale ratificazione da chi per poca prudenza favoriva le cose di Lodovico, intantoché il Re già sendo in su la vittoria, e volendo poi i Fiorentini ratificare`.** "Such ratification was deferred by whoever through little prudence favoured the things of Lodovico, insomuch that, the King being already upon the victory, and the Florentines later wishing to ratify": `chi`, whoever, singular and indefinite, as `chi teneva` (.04), not a named faction; `cose`, things, as at .01 and .03; `poi`, later (D250); `in su`, upon, on the point of it.

**`come quello che conobbe i Fiorentini essere venuti forzati, e non volontarj nella amicizia sua`.** "As [by] one who knew the Florentines to have come forced, and not voluntary, into his friendship": the bracket supplies the agent of `non fu accettata` (D53); the two adjectives kept as adjectives.

**`Il che costò alla Città di Firenze assai danari, e fu per perdere lo Stato`.** "Which cost the city of Florence much money, and it was about to lose the state": `danari`, money (D431); `essere per`, be about to (D45).

**`come poi altra volta per simile causa le intervenne`.** "As later, another time, befell it for a similar cause": `poi`, later (D250); `causa`, cause (glossary, a recorded collapse with `cagione`, which the 1554 prints here, NM-DISC-II15-D005). The other time is very probably 1512 (the editor's identification), when the republic, which had not joined the Holy League, fell to the League's Spanish army and the Medici returned.

**`E tanto più fu dannabile quel partito`.** "And that course was all the more damnable": plain order (D263); `dannabile`, damnable, the word Guicciardini uses for irresolute tardiness (Guicciardini .01).

**`perché non si servì ancora il Duca Lodovico`.** "Because one did not serve even Duke Lodovico [by it]": `servire`, serve, impersonal; `ancora`, even, a sense split from `ancora`, also (.03, .05), recorded (D472); the delay did not even help the man it was meant to favour. "Yet" is the other possible reading of `ancora`, and "even" is the editor's.

**`arebbe mostri molti più segni di inimicizia`.** "Would have shown many more signs of enmity": `mostri`, the old participle of `mostrare`; `inimicizia`, enmity (D128). The 1554 omits `molti` (NM-DISC-II15-D005).

**`E benché del male che nasce alle Repubbliche di questa debolezza se ne sia di sopra in uno altro capitolo discorso, nondimeno`.** "And albeit it has been discoursed above, in another chapter, of the ill that arises to republics from this weakness, nonetheless": `benché`, albeit, with `nondimeno`, nonetheless (D31, D45); `male`, ill (glossary); `nascere`, arise (D04); `discorrere`, discourse (glossary, SCOPE §3); `questa debolezza`, this weakness, the weakness of the rubric. The other chapter is not named; the editor takes it to be Discourses I.38. The 1554 reads `alla Republica`, singular (NM-DISC-II15-D006).

**`ho voluto replicarne, parendomi massime materia che debba essere dalle Repubbliche simili alla nostra notata`.** "I have wished to repeat it, it seeming to me above all a matter that must be noted by republics similar to ours": `replicare`, repeat (D423); `massime`, above all, qualifying `materia`, before which it stands; `dovere`, must (D274). The 1554 reads `replicare` without `ne` (NM-DISC-II15-D006).
