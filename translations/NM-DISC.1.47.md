# Discourses I.47

*Men, even though they deceive themselves in generals, in particulars do not deceive themselves.*

## NM-DISC.1.47.01

The Roman people having, as is said above, come to loathe the consular name, and wishing that plebeian men could be made consuls, or that their authority should be limited, the nobility, so as not to debase the consular authority with either the one thing or the other, took a middle path, and was content that four tribunes should be created with consular power, who could be plebeians as well as nobles. Content with this was the plebs, it seeming to it that it extinguished the consulate, and had in this supreme degree its part. There arose from this a notable case: that, it coming to the creation of these tribunes, and it being possible to create them all plebeians, there were created by the Roman people all nobles. Whence Livy says these words: *Quorum comitiorum eventus docuit, alios animos in contentione libertatis et honoris; alios secundum deposita certamina in incorrupto judicio esse.*

## NM-DISC.1.47.02

And, examining whence this may proceed, I believe it proceeds [from this], that men in general things deceive themselves much, in particular ones not so much. It seemed generally to the plebs that it merited the consulate, through having more part in the city, through bearing more danger in the wars, through being that which with its arms maintained Rome free, and made it powerful. And this desire of its seeming to it, as has been said, reasonable, it wished to obtain this authority by every way. But when it was to make judgment of its men particularly, it knew the weakness of them, and judged that none of them merited that which, all together, it seemed to it to merit. So that, ashamed of them, it had recourse to those who merited it. At which deliberation Livy, deservedly marvelling, says these words: *Hanc modestiam, aequitatemque et altitudinem animi ubi nunc in uno inveneris, quae tunc populi universi fuit?*

## NM-DISC.1.47.03

In corroboration of this there can be adduced another notable example, which followed at Capua after Hannibal had routed the Romans at Cannae; through which rout all Italy being stirred up, Capua too was about to break into tumult, through the hatred that was between the people and the senate: and Pacuvius Calanus finding himself at that time in the supreme magistracy, and knowing the danger that city bore of breaking into tumult, he designed, of his own will, to reconcile the plebs with the nobility; and, this thought made, he had the senate gathered, and narrated to them the hatred which the people had against them, and the dangers they bore of being killed by it, and the city given to Hannibal, the things of the Romans being afflicted; afterwards he added that, if they wished to leave this thing to him to govern, he would act in such a way that they would unite together; but he wished to shut them inside the palace, and, by making it in the people's power to be able to chastise them, to save them.

## NM-DISC.1.47.04

The senators yielded to this opinion of his, and he called the people to assembly, having shut the senate in the palace, and said that the time had come to be able to tame the pride of the nobility, and to avenge itself of the injuries received from it, having shut them all in under his custody; but because he believed that they would not wish their city to remain without government, it was necessary, wishing to kill the old senators, to create new ones. And on that account he had put all the names of the senators in a purse, and would begin to draw them in their presence, and he would have those drawn die one after another, as soon as they had found the successor. And, having begun to draw one, at the name of him there was raised a very great uproar, calling him a man proud, cruel and arrogant; and Pacuvius asking that they make the replacement, the whole assembly grew quiet: and after some space one of the plebs was named, at whose name some began to hiss, some to laugh, some to speak ill of him in one way, and some in another; and so, continuing one after another, all those who were named they judged unworthy of the senatorial degree; in such a way that Pacuvius, having taken occasion upon this, said: since you judge that this city fares ill without a senate, and do not agree on making the replacements for the old senators, I think that it is well that you be reconciled together; because this fear in which the senators have been will have made them humble themselves again in such a way that that humanity which you were seeking elsewhere you will find in them. And, they having agreed to this, there followed from it the union of this order, and that deceit in which they were was discovered, when they were constrained to come to particulars.

## NM-DISC.1.47.05

Peoples, besides this, deceive themselves generally in judging things and the accidents of them, which afterwards are known particularly, they become aware of such deceit. After 1514, the princes of the city having been driven out of Florence, and there being no ordered government there, but rather a certain ambitious licence, and public things going from ill to worse, many popular [men], seeing the ruin of the city, and not understanding any other cause of it, accused for it the ambition of some powerful one nourishing the disorders, so as to be able to make a state to his purpose, and take liberty away from them: and such men as these stood about the loggias and the piazzas, speaking ill of many citizens, and threatening them that if ever they found themselves of the Signori, they would discover this deceit of theirs, and would chastise them. It occurred often that of like [men] someone ascended to the supreme magistracy, and when he had climbed to that place, and saw things from nearer at hand, he knew the disorders, whence they arose, and the dangers that hung over, and the difficulty of remedying them. And having seen that the times and not the men caused the disorder, he became at once of another spirit, and of another make, because the knowledge of particular things took away from him that deceit which, in considering generally, he had presupposed. In such a way that those who had heard him speak earlier, when he was private, and had later seen him stay quiet in the supreme magistracy, believed that it arose, not from truer knowledge of things, but because he had been got round and corrupted by the great. And, this happening to many men, and many times, there arose among them a proverb, which said: these men have one spirit in the piazza, and another in the palace.

## NM-DISC.1.47.06

Considering, then, all that has been discoursed, one sees that one can quickly make peoples open their eyes, by finding a way, seeing that a general [thing] deceives them, that they have to descend to particulars, as Pacuvius did in Capua, and the senate in Rome. I believe also that one can conclude that a prudent man must never flee the popular judgment in particular things, concerning the distributions of degrees and of dignities; because only in this does the people not deceive itself, and if it deceives itself at some time, it will be so rare that the few men who were to make like distributions will deceive themselves more times. Nor does it seem to me superfluous to show in the following chapter the order which the senate held to undeceive the people in its distributions.

## Notes

**The rubric.** `Gli uomini ancora che s'ingannino ne' generali, ne' particolari non s'ingannano`. "Men, even though they deceive themselves in generals, in particulars do not deceive themselves": `ancora che`, even though (glossary); `ingannarsi`, deceive oneself (D334), the chapter's verb, with the transitive `ingannare`, deceive (.06), the noun `inganno`, deceit (four times), and `isgannare`, undeceive, in the last line (D374). `generali` and `particolari` are adjectives used as nouns, "generals" and "particulars", as the rubric pairs them; English takes the plurals in that sense, where the singular `un generale` (.06) needs a bracket. In the body Machiavelli writes `cose generali` and `cose particolari`, general and particular things. The rubric's second clause carries no `ma`: the opposition is in the order, and the English keeps it.

**`Essendosi il Popolo romano, come di sopra si dice, recato a noja il nome Consolare`.** "The Roman people having, as is said above, come to loathe the consular name": `recarsi a noia`, to take a thing in dislike. `nome`, name: the people's quarrel is with the name, as at I.39, where the same humour rose against `il nome de' Consoli`. The period's main verbs belong to the nobility (`prese`, `fu contenta`); the people's motive is a participle.

**`per non deonestare l'autorità Consolare né con l'una né con l'altra cosa`.** "So as not to debase the consular authority with either the one thing or the other": `deonestare`, to strip of honour, here "debase", kept apart from `disonorare`, dishonour (D313); the 1554 reads `dishonestare` (form). The two things are the two demands just named, plebeian consuls and a limited authority.

**`Fu contenta a questo la Plebe, parendogli spegnere il Consolato, ed avere in questo sommo grado la parte sua`.** "Content with this was the plebs, it seeming to it that it extinguished the consulate, and had in this supreme degree its part": the fronted predicate answers `fu contenta` of the nobility in the period before, so the order is kept (D263, a contrast). `sommo grado`, supreme degree (D190); `parte`, part, the plebs' share.

**`che venendosi alla creazione di questi Tribuni, e potendosi creare tutti Plebei, furono dal Popolo romano creati tutti Nobili`.** "That, it coming to the creation of these tribunes, and it being possible to create them all plebeians, there were created by the Roman people all nobles": the delayed `tutti Nobili` is the case, and the English holds it last (D18). The 1554 reads `tutti i nobili` and, before, `nacquene di questo` (NM-DISC-I47-D007).

**`Quorum comitiorum eventus docuit, alios animos in contentione libertatis et honoris; alios secundum deposita certamina in incorrupto judicio esse.`** Left in Latin, as printed: "The outcome of which elections showed that spirits are one thing in the contest for liberty and honour, another, once the contests are laid down, in uncorrupted judgment." Livy 4.6, which the Latin Library text (thelatinlibrary.com/livy/liv.4.shtml, consulted 2026-10-22) gives as `Eventus eorum comitiorum docuit alios animos in contentione libertatis dignitatisque, alios secundum deposita certamina incorrupto iudicio esse`: Machiavelli's `honoris` for that text's `dignitatis`, and the 1824's `in` before `incorrupto`, which the 1554 does not print either (NM-DISC-I47-D004). The 1554 follows the Latin with an Italian rendering introduced by `Cioè`; the 1824 does not.

**`credo proceda che gli uomini nelle cose generali s'ingannano assai, nelle particolari non tanto`.** "I believe it proceeds [from this], that men in general things deceive themselves much, in particular ones not so much": `credo`, I believe, Machiavelli's own; `procedere`, proceed (glossary), twice; `assai` after a verb, "much" (D348). The rubric's absolute (`non s'ingannano`) is here a comparison (`non tanto`), and the close returns to the absolute within a limit (.06). The 1554 reads `ne particolari` (NM-DISC-I47-D005).

**`Pareva generalmente alla Plebe di meritare il Consolato`.** "It seemed generally to the plebs that it merited the consulate": `generalmente`, generally, the plebs judging in general, against `particolarmente` two periods on and `considerare generalmente` (.05); `tutta insieme`, all together, is the plebs as a whole. `meritare`, merit, four times in the paragraph with `meritamente`. The 1554 adds `Romana` after `Plebe` (NM-DISC-I47-D005).

**`per portare più pericolo nelle guerre, per esser quella che con le braccia sue manteneva Roma libera`.** "Through bearing more danger in the wars, through being that which with its arms maintained Rome free": `portare` of danger, bear, as twice at Capua (.03); `braccia`, the limbs, not weapons (`armi`); English "arms" carries both, a collapse recorded in the glossary (D374).

**`volse ottenere questa autorità in ogni modo`.** "It wished to obtain this authority by every way": `volse`, the old perfect of `volere`, wish (D117); `in ogni modo`, by whatever way, at any cost; `modo`, way (glossary).

**`Ma come la ebbe a fare giudizio degli uomini suoi particolarmente, conobbe la debolezza di quelli`.** "But when it was to make judgment of its men particularly, it knew the weakness of them": `avere a`, destinative, "was to" (D261); `giudizio`, judgment, with `giudicare`, judge (D374); `conoscere`, know, the chapter's verb of knowing by acquaintance, with its noun `cognizione`, knowledge (.05), kept apart from `intendere`, understand (.05) (D374); `particolarmente`, particularly, the adverb of the rubric's `particolari`. Temporal `come` is "when" throughout the unit; `come prima` (.04) is "as soon as".

**`Della qual deliberazione maravigliandosi meritamente Tito Livio`.** "At which deliberation Livy, deservedly marvelling": `Tito Livio`, Livy (names.md); `meritamente`, deservedly, in the root of the plebs' `meritare`, which English cannot hold in one word.

**`Hanc modestiam, aequitatemque et altitudinem animi ubi nunc in uno inveneris, quae tunc populi universi fuit?`** Left in Latin, as printed: "This modesty and fairness and loftiness of spirit, which was then the whole people's, where would you now find in one man?" Livy 4.6, where the same Latin Library text reads `quae tum`; the sentence is Livy's comment on the same elections. The 1554 again adds an Italian rendering (`cioè Questa modestia ...`), which the 1824 does not print.

**`In corroborazione di questo se ne può addurre un altro notabile esempio, seguito in Capova da poi che Annibale ebbe rotto i Romani a Canne`.** "In corroboration of this there can be adduced another notable example, which followed at Capua after Hannibal had routed the Romans at Cannae": `corroborazione`, corroboration (D170); `rompere` of an army, rout, so that `rotta`, rout, keeps the root in the next clause; `seguire`, follow, as `ne seguì` (.04). The 1554 reads `confermatione` and `rotti` (NM-DISC-I47-D006). The story is Livy's, 23.2-3; the Latin Library text (thelatinlibrary.com/livy/liv.23.shtml, consulted 2026-10-22) has the senate shut in the curia.

**`Capova stava ancora per tumultuare`.** "Capua too was about to break into tumult": `stare per`, be about to, as `essere per` (D45); `ancora`, too (or "still"); `tumultuare` in the root of `tumulto`, tumult (glossary), twice. `sollevata`, "stirred up", of Italy in revolt.

**`Pacuvio Calano`.** Pacuvius Calanus, Machiavelli's form; the Latin Library text of Livy 23.2 names him Pacuvius Calavius (names.md).

**`disegnò con suo grado riconciliare la Plebe con la Nobiltà`.** "He designed, of his own will, to reconcile the plebs with the nobility": `con suo grado` read as the idiom of `di suo grado`, `a suo grado`, of his own will or pleasure; the other reading, by the standing of his office, just named, is possible, and English cannot hold both (D372). `disegnare`, design (glossary).

**`farebbe in modo che si unirebbono insieme; ma li voleva serrare dentro al palazzo, e, col fare potestà al Popolo di potergli gastigare, salvarli`.** "He would act in such a way that they would unite together; but he wished to shut them inside the palace, and, by making it in the people's power to be able to chastise them, to save them": `potestà`, power (D78); `gastigare`, chastise; `salvare`, save.

**`chiamò il Popolo a concione`, `si racchetò tutta la concione`.** "He called the people to assembly", "the whole assembly grew quiet": `concione`, the people gathered to be addressed, and, at its second appearance, the gathering itself, which "harangue", the glossary's word for the speeches (NM I.4, I.5; FG I.2, I.28), cannot be. "Assembly" is a sense split, decided by sense at each occurrence (D371); so that the two gatherings do not seem to share a word, `ragunare il Senato` is "had the senate gathered" (.03).

**`e disse come egli era venuto il tempo di poter domare la superbia della Nobiltà, e vendicarsi delle ingiurie ricevute da quella, avendoli rinchiusi tutti sotto la sua custodia`.** "And said that the time had come to be able to tame the pride of the nobility, and to avenge itself of the injuries received from it, having shut them all in under his custody": `come` after a verb of saying, "that" (D334); `egli` is the pleonastic subject; `superbia`, pride (D358); `ingiuria`, injury. The subject of `vendicarsi` is the people he addresses. `sua` may be Pacuvius', the subject of `avendoli`, or the people's, given power over the senators in .03; "his" is a choice.

**`era necessario, volendo ammazzare i Senatori vecchi, crearne de' nuovi`.** "It was necessary, wishing to kill the old senators, to create new ones": `è necessario`, it is necessary (glossary), not `bisogna`; `ammazzare`, kill (D262); the English leaves the gerund as unattached as the Italian.

**`E per tanto aveva messo tutti gli nomi degli Senatori in una borsa`.** "And on that account he had put all the names of the senators in a purse": `per tanto`, on that account (D98); `borsa`, purse, the bag from which names are drawn by lot.

**`ed egli farebbe i tratti di mano in mano morire, come prima loro avessero trovato il successore`.** "And he would have those drawn die one after another, as soon as they had found the successor": `di mano in mano`, one after another, twice in the paragraph (D374); `come prima`, as soon as.

**`fu al nome di quello levato un rumor grandissimo`.** "At the name of him there was raised a very great uproar": `rumore`, uproar, public tumult (D155), where D362's "rumour" is talk with a stated content; `levare` of a cry or uproar, raise, the root sense, where D131's "remove" is `levare` with a thing taken away (D371); it pairs with `levarsi`, rise (D348).

**`e chiedendo Pacuvio, che facessero lo scambio`.** "And Pacuvius asking that they make the replacement": `scambio`, replacement, and `gli scambj` below (glossary, I.39).

**`tutti quelli che furono nominati li giudicavano indegni del grado Senatorio`.** "All those who were named they judged unworthy of the senatorial degree": a fronted object resumed by `li`, kept, since the period lands on `indegni del grado Senatorio`; `indegno`, unworthy (D374), Guicciardini's word for the people's exclusion (Guicciardini .01); `grado`, degree.

**`poiché voi giudicate ... io penso che sia bene che voi vi riconciliate insieme`.** "Since you judge ... I think that it is well that you be reconciled together": Pacuvius' words are given without quotation marks, as the witness prints them, after `disse:`.

**`perché questa paura in la quale i Senatori sono stati, gli arà fatti in modo riumiliare, che quella umanità che voi cercavate altrove, troverete in loro`.** "Because this fear in which the senators have been will have made them humble themselves again in such a way that that humanity which you were seeking elsewhere you will find in them": `paura`, fear (D313); `riumiliare`, humble again; `umanità`, humanity, as at I.40. The fronted object lands the period on `in loro` (D263, a landing).

**`ne seguì l'unione di questo ordine, e quello inganno in che egli erano, si scoperse, come e' furono costretti venire a' particolari`.** "There followed from it the union of this order, and that deceit in which they were was discovered, when they were constrained to come to particulars": `ordine`, order (glossary), which order is not said; `egli`, they, the old plural; `scoprire`, discover, as in .05; `costringere`, constrain; `venire a' particolari` answers the rubric. The 1554 reads `eßi` for `egli` (form).

**`Ingannansi oltra di questo i Popoli generalmente nel giudicare le cose e gli accidenti di esse, le quali dipoi si conoscono particolarmente, si avveggono di tale inganno`.** "Peoples, besides this, deceive themselves generally in judging things and the accidents of them, which afterwards are known particularly, they become aware of such deceit": the period does not close its construction: `le quali` opens a relative that `si avveggono`, with the peoples as subject, does not complete. The English keeps the break and supplies nothing. `accidente`, accident (glossary); `avvedersi`, become aware.

**`Dopo il 1514`.** The 1824 prints `1514`; the 1554 prints `MCCCCXCIIII`, 1494 (NM-DISC-I47-D001). The edition keeps its governing witness (D364).

**`sendo stati i Principi della Città cacciati da Firenze`.** "The princes of the city having been driven out of Florence": `Principi`, princes (D05), lower case in the English as at I.30; `cacciare`, drive out (D10).

**`andando le cose pubbliche di male in peggio`.** "Public things going from ill to worse": `male` as a noun is "ill" (glossary), so that "bad" stays `cattivo`'s (D03). The 1554 has no `certa` before `licenza` (NM-DISC-I47-D003).

**`molti Popolari veggendo la rovina della Città, e non ne intendendo altra cagione, ne accusavano l'ambizione di qualche potente che nutrisse i disordini`.** "Many popular [men], seeing the ruin of the city, and not understanding any other cause of it, accused for it the ambition of some powerful one nourishing the disorders": `Popolari`, the popular [men] (glossary); `intendere`, understand; `un potente`, a powerful one (D06). `nutrisse` is the popular men's supposition; the English gives it no "should", which D348 keeps for `dovere`.

**`e torre loro la libertà`.** "And take liberty away from them": `torre`, take away (D131). The 1554 has no `loro` (NM-DISC-I47-D003).

**`e stavano questi tali per le logge e per le piazze`.** "And such men as these stood about the loggias and the piazzas": the open places of Florence, where the proverb at the end of the paragraph puts one spirit; `palazzo`, the palace, the seat of the magistracy, where it puts the other.

**`se mai si trovassero dei Signori, scoprirebbono questo loro inganno`.** "If ever they found themselves of the Signori, they would discover this deceit of theirs": `Signori`, the members of the Signoria, Florence's chief magistracy (names.md), the `supremo Magistrato` of the next period. `loro` is the citizens': the deceit they charge is the citizens'.

**`Occorreva spesso che di simili ne ascendeva alcuno al supremo Magistrato`.** "It occurred often that of like [men] someone ascended to the supreme magistracy": `occorrere`, occur (D01), the *currere* family; `simile`, like (glossary); `ascendere`, ascend (D374), against `descendere`, descend, in .06; `salire`, climb, in the next clause.

**`conosceva i disordini donde nascevano, ed i pericoli che soprastavano, e la difficultà del rimediarvi`.** "He knew the disorders, whence they arose, and the dangers that hung over, and the difficulty of remedying them": `nascere`, arise (D04); `rimedio`, remedy (glossary), in its verb. The 1554 reads `onde naſceuono` (form).

**`E veduto come i tempi e non gli uomini causavano il disordine, diventava subito d'un altro animo, e d'un'altra fatta`.** "And having seen that the times and not the men caused the disorder, he became at once of another spirit, and of another make": `come` after a verb of seeing, "that" (D334); `subito`, at once (D177); `animo`, spirit (glossary), as in the proverb that closes the paragraph.

**`perché la cognizione delle cose particolari gli toglieva via quello inganno, che nel considerare generalmente si aveva presupposto`.** "Because the knowledge of particular things took away from him that deceit which, in considering generally, he had presupposed": `cognizione`, knowledge, the noun of `conoscere`, know, kept apart from Guicciardini's `notizia`, notice (Guicciardini .01, .02) (D373, D374); `togliere via`, take away (D131); `presupporre`, presuppose (glossary), as Appius' presupposed tyranny at I.40.

**`Dimodoché quelli che lo avevano prima, quando era privato, sentito parlare`.** "In such a way that those who had heard him speak earlier, when he was private": `di modo che`, `in modo che`, in such a way that (glossary, D374), as `in modoché` (.04); `prima`, earlier (D334).

**`ma perché fusse stato aggirato e corrotto dai Grandi`.** "But because he had been got round and corrupted by the great": `aggirare`, get round, in the root of `girare`, go round (D358); `corrompere`, corrupt (glossary); `Grandi`, the great (D118).

**`costoro hanno un animo in piazza, e uno altro in palazzo`.** The 1554 has no `altro` (NM-DISC-I47-D003).

**`si vede come e' si può fare tosto aprire gli occhi a' Popoli, trovando modo, veggendo ch'un generale gli inganna, ch'egli abbiano a descendere a' particolari`.** "One sees that one can quickly make peoples open their eyes, by finding a way, seeing that a general [thing] deceives them, that they have to descend to particulars": `discorrere`, discourse (D01), in `si è discorso` just before; `un generale`, a general [thing], the rubric's `generali` in the singular, where English "a general" would be a commander; `avere a`, "have to" (D261), with `trovando modo` as the means. The 1554 reads `ueggẽda`, which is not a form of the verb; the 1824's `veggendo` is kept (NM-DISC-I47-D002, D365).

**`che mai un uomo prudente non debbe fuggire il giudizio Popolare nelle cose particolari, circa le distribuzioni de' gradi e delle dignità`.** "That a prudent man must never flee the popular judgment in particular things, concerning the distributions of degrees and of dignities": `credo`, I believe, again, with `si possa conchiudere`, one can conclude; `dovere`, must (D274); `fuggire`, flee (D325); `grado`, degree, the fourth time in the chapter; `distribuzione`, distribution, `dignità`, dignity (D374). The conclusion is limited twice, to particular things and to distributions.

**`perché solo in questo il Popolo non s'inganna, e se s'inganna qualche volta, fia sì raro, che s'inganneranno più volte i pochi uomini che avessero a fare simili distribuzioni`.** "Because only in this does the people not deceive itself, and if it deceives itself at some time, it will be so rare that the few men who were to make like distributions will deceive themselves more times": `solo`, only (D298), the rubric's absolute restored within the limit of `in questo`; `fia`, it will be; `avere a`, destinative, "were to" (D261). The comparison with the few is what Guicciardini refuses (Guicciardini .02).

**`l'ordine che teneva il Senato per isgannare il Popolo nelle distribuzioni sue`.** "The order which the senate held to undeceive the people in its distributions": `tenere`, hold (D242); `isgannare`, undeceive, the last word of the family. `sue` may be the people's distributions or the senate's; "its" keeps both.
