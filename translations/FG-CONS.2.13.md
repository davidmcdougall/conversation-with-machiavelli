# Consideration on chapter XIII

## FG-CONS.2.13.01

If the Writer calls fraud every craft or dissimulation that is used even without guile, his conclusion can be true, that force alone, I do not say never, which is too resolute a word, but most rarely conducts men from low to great fortune. But if he calls fraud that which is properly fraud, that is, the failing of faith, or other guileful proceeding, I believe many are found who have without fraud acquired kingdoms and most great empires. Of these was Alexander the Great, of these Caesar, who from private citizen, with other arts than [those] of fraud, conducted himself to such greatness, always uncovering his ambition or the appetite for dominating. I do not now have the memory of Xenophon fresh, but I believe that he instructs Cyrus in prudence, in industry, in just simulation or dissimulation, not in fraud. Nor do I call it fraud if the Romans made such pacts with the Latins that they could patiently tolerate their empire, which was not because they did not perceive from the very beginning that, under the shadow of confederation, [what was] equal was servitude; but finding themselves impotent, and not being treated in such a way that they had cause to despair, made them wait until such time, I do not say that they had uncovered the end of the Romans, which they would have been very gross [men] if they had not known from the beginning, but that, grown in number of men, and well expert in military discipline, they had hope to be able to contend on even [terms] with the Roman people. It was also prudence, that of the Romans, not fraud, to treat the Latins well; and I believe it is most true that without like industries and prudent ways of governing oneself not only does one most rarely climb from low fortune to high, but also greatness is conserved with difficulty. But, as to fraud, it can be disputable whether it is always a good instrument for arriving at greatness; because often with deceit many fine strokes are made, often also having the name of [being] fraudulent takes away the occasion of attaining his intents

## Notes

**The heading and the rubric.** Canestrini's heading, CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO XIII, without `del libro II`, as both readers saw it; the column is headed as the page is (D443). His rubric is Machiavelli's sentence (Machiavelli, rubric), printed with broken letters, `viena` and `piu`, and a stop after `fraude` where Machiavelli has a comma: read with the 1824 rubric and the 1933 edition, with no stop after it (D451; FG-CONS-II13-D005). His note on Xenophon and on Giovanni Galeazzo and Bernabò is his commentary and is not carried.

**`Se lo Scrittore chiama fraude ogni astuzia o dissimulazione che si usa e[ti]am sanza dolo`.** "If the Writer calls fraud every craft or dissimulation that is used even without guile": `Scrittore`, the Writer (glossary), Machiavelli. `fraude`, fraud, as in Machiavelli; the reply is about what the word covers, and the English holds one word for it through both columns. `astuzia`, craft, with `astutamente`, craftily (D52): the cognate "astuteness" now praises. `dissimulazione`, dissimulation; `simulazione` (below), simulation, in its older sense of pretending. `dolo`, guile; `doloso`, guileful (below): the root held, kept apart from `inganno`, deceit (D374), and from `fraude`. `etiam`, Latin, even, as FG I.3, I.16 and II.12, and as in Machiavelli's `non essere mancati etiam della fraude` (Machiavelli .05). The two readers divide between the printed `eltam` and `etiam`; emended `e[ti]am` (D450; FG-CONS-II13-E001). "Every craft" keeps `ogni` "every"; `astuzia` is the faculty, not a trade.

**`può essere vera la conclusione sua`.** "His conclusion can be true": a concession, under the condition of the `Se`, of the conclusion of the rubric and of Machiavelli .01; `vero`, true, as Machiavelli's `verissima` (Machiavelli .01) and his own `verissimo` below.

**`che la forza sola, non dico mai, che è vocabolo troppo resoluto, ma rarissime volte conduca gli uomini da bassa a grande fortuna`.** "That force alone, I do not say never, which is too resolute a word, but most rarely conducts men from low to great fortune": `non dico mai` answers Machiavelli's `rado o non mai` and `Né credo si trovi mai` (Machiavelli .01); `vocabolo`, word; `resoluto`, resolute, the root of `resoluzione`, resolution (FG II.12). `rarissime volte`, most rarely, the superlative kept, here and below. `condurre`, conduct (D444), here and at `si condusse`, held across men and the man who conducts himself. `bassa`, low; `grande fortuna`, great fortune, Machiavelli's rubric.

**`quella che è proprio fraude, cioè il mancamento di fede, o altro procedere doloso`.** "That which is properly fraud, that is, the failing of faith, or other guileful proceeding": `proprio`, properly; `mancamento`, failing, the noun of `mancare`, fail (D206); `fede`, faith (glossary); `procedere`, proceeding.

**`credo si trovino molti ch[e] hanno sanza fraude acquistato r[e]gni e imperii grandissimi`.** "I believe many are found who have without fraud acquired kingdoms and most great empires": no "that" is supplied, here or at `credo sia verissimo`, where the Italian has no `che`; `credo`, I believe, the first of three, each an act of assent to a claim of his own, not a hedge on Machiavelli's. `si trovino` answers Machiavelli's `Né credo si trovi mai` (Machiavelli .01, .02). `acquistare`, acquire (glossary); `imperio`, empire (D124), as Machiavelli's `Imperj grandissimi`. The scan prints `cha` and `ragni`, emended `ch[e]` and `r[e]gni` (D450; FG-CONS-II13-E002, E003).

**`Di questi fu Alessandro Magno, di questi Cesare`.** "Of these was Alexander the Great, of these Caesar": Alexander, son of Machiavelli's Philip of Macedon (Machiavelli .01), the editor's observation; Caesar, Julius Caesar.

**`che di cittadino privato con altre arti che di fraude si condusse a tanta grandezza`.** "Who from private citizen, with other arts than [those] of fraud, conducted himself to such greatness": `arte`, art; `tanto`, such, as Machiavelli's `in tanto augumento` (Machiavelli .03). `grandezza`, greatness, Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .02, .03).

**`scoprendo sempre la ambizione s[u]a o lo appetito del dominare`.** "Always uncovering his ambition or the appetite for dominating": `scoprire`, uncover, here and at `ebbono scoperto il fine de' Romani`, against Machiavelli's fraud that is less vituperable `quanto è più coperta` (Machiavelli .05): Caesar's ambition was not covered. `appetito`, appetite (D117); `dominare`, dominate (glossary). The two readers divide between the printed `sna` and `sua`; emended `s[u]a` (D450; FG-CONS-II13-E004).

**`Non ho ora fresca la memoria di Zenofonte`.** "I do not now have the memory of Xenophon fresh": Machiavelli's `Senofonte` (Machiavelli .02); the reply's one admission that its ground is memory, before `credo`.

**`credo che instruisca Ciro di prudenza, d'industria, di simulazione o dissimulazione giuste, non di fraude`.** "I believe that he instructs Cyrus in prudence, in industry, in just simulation or dissimulation, not in fraud": `instruire`, instruct; `prudenza`, prudence (glossary); `industria`, industry, diligence and contrivance, with `industrie` below; `giuste`, just, plural, so that it qualifies both simulation and dissimulation. Under the second `se`, the proper sense, just simulation or dissimulation is not fraud; under the first it would be.

**`Né chiamo fraude se e' Romani fecion[o] tali patti ai Latini ch[e] potettono pazientemente tollerare lo imperio loro`.** "Nor do I call it fraud if the Romans made such pacts with the Latins that they could patiently tolerate their empire": `patto`, pact; `tollerare`, tolerate; `imperio`, empire (D124), the Romans' dominion over the Latins. `potettono` has the Latins for subject and `loro` the Romans, as the sense requires; the English keeps the pronouns. The scan prints `feciona` and `cha`, emended `fecion[o]`, `ch[e]` (D450; FG-CONS-II13-E002, E005).

**`il che non fu perché non si accorgessino insino dal principio, che, sotto ombra di confederazione, equale era servitù`.** "Which was not because they did not perceive from the very beginning that, under the shadow of confederation, [what was] equal was servitude": `insino dal principio`, from the very beginning, against Machiavelli's Latins, who `non si avvidero mai ... se non poi che` (Machiavelli .03); `accorgersi` and Machiavelli's `avvedersi`, perceive, a recorded collapse. `sotto ombra di confederazione` answers Annius' `sub umbra foederis`, which Machiavelli quotes (Machiavelli .04); `confederazione`, confederation. Canestrini's comma, which both readers read without doubt, is kept (D110), and with it `equale` stands as the subject, [what was] equal, and `servitù` as the predicate; the 1933 edition, without the comma, reads `confederazione equale`, an equal confederation, Livy's `foederis aequi`.

**`ma il trovarsi impotenti, né essere trattati in modo che non avessino causa di desperarsi, gli fece aspettare insino a t[a]nto`.** "But finding themselves impotent, and not being treated in such a way that they had cause to despair, made them wait until such time": the two infinitives are the subject of `fece`. `né` after an affirmative member is `e non`, and the `non` of `che non avessino` is concord with it and idle; the English renders `né` "and not" and drops the `non` (as D412): the Latins were not treated so ill as to despair. `desperarsi`, despair; `impotente`, impotent, without power. The scan prints `tonto`, emended `t[a]nto` (D450; FG-CONS-II13-E006).

**`insino a t[a]nto, non dico che ebbono scoperto il fine de' Romani`; `ma che ... ebbono speranza`.** "I do not say that they had uncovered the end of the Romans ... but that ... they had hope": the second `non dico` of the reply, correcting what `insino a tanto` might be taken to mean; `fine`, end, aim.

**`il quale sarebbono stati bene grossi se non avessino cognosciuto da principio`.** "Which they would have been very gross [men] if they had not known from the beginning": `grosso`, gross, dull of wit; `bene` an intensifier, "very" here, "well" at `bene esperti`. `cognoscere`, know (D374). He has just said that they perceived it `insino dal principio`.

**`cresciuti di numero di uomini, e bene esperti di disciplina militare`.** "Grown in number of men, and well expert in military discipline": `esperto`, expert (D444, `espertissimo`); `disciplina militare`, military discipline (FG II.proem).

**`speranza poter[e] contendere del pari col populo romano`.** "Hope to be able to contend on even [terms] with the Roman people": `del pari`, on even [terms], `pari` even (D262); `populo`, people. The scan prints `potera`, emended `poter[e]` (D450; FG-CONS-II13-E007).

**`Fu anche prudenza quella de' Romani, non fraude, a trattare bene e' Latini`.** "It was also prudence, that of the Romans, not fraud, to treat the Latins well": `anche`, also; the 1933 edition reads `adunche`, then (D74), which would make it a conclusion from the Latins' case; `anche` kept (D38, D450; FG-CONS-II13-D003).

**`credo sia verissimo`.** "I believe it is most true": the third `credo`, assent to a claim of his own, in Machiavelli's superlative (Machiavelli .01 `cosa verissima`).

**`sanza simili industrie e prudenti modi di governarsi`.** "Without like industries and prudent ways of governing oneself": `simile`, like (glossary); `modo`, way (glossary); `governarsi`, govern oneself.

**`non solo rarissime volte si salga da bassa fortuna a alta, ma ancora difficilmente si conservi la grandezza`.** "Not only does one most rarely climb from low fortune to high, but also greatness is conserved with difficulty": `salire`, climb, Machiavelli's verb (Machiavelli .05 `a sublimi gradi salire`); `conservare`, conserve (glossary), the question Machiavelli does not ask; `difficilmente`, with difficulty, kept apart from `facilmente`, readily (D27).

**`Ma, quanto alla fraude, può essere disputabile se sia sempre buono instrumento di pervenire alla grandezza`.** "But, as to fraud, it can be disputable whether it is always a good instrument for arriving at greatness": not a judgment but a question left open; `sempre` answers Machiavelli's `fu sempre necessaria` (Machiavelli .05); `pervenire`, arrive at (Machiavelli .01, .02); `instrumento`, instrument.

**`perché spesso con lo inganno si fanno di molti belli tratti, spesso anche l'avere nome di fraudolent[o] toglie occasione di conseguire gli intenti suoi`.** "Because often with deceit many fine strokes are made, often also having the name of [being] fraudulent takes away the occasion of attaining his intents": `inganno`, deceit (D374), Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .02, .03, .04); `tratto`, stroke, a move well played; `togliere`, take away (D131, D313); `occasione`, occasion (glossary); `conseguire`, attain (glossary); `intento`, intent. The two `spesso` are set side by side without a conjunction, and the English keeps the asyndeton. The scan prints `fraudolenta`, which agrees with nothing; emended `fraudolent[o]` (D456; FG-CONS-II13-E008, FG-CONS-II13-D002). The reply ends without a stop, and so does the English (D40).
