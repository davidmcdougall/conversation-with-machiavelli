# Consideration on chapter XIX

## FG-CONS.2.19.01

Who doubts that the city of Florence, that the republic of Venice would be weaker and of less power if they had shut in their territory within small borders than they are? Having subdued the neighbouring cities, and enlarged their jurisdiction, it is not easy for every neighbour to assail them; they are not troubled by every weak accident; they hold, if a great motion does not come, the enemy outside the yolk of their state; war does not readily approach their walls; having many subjects makes the public revenues greater in many ways, makes the dominant city richer in private. With which means, although they are not armed with soldiers of their own, they conduct strangers, by whom to be defended is better than not to be defended by anyone. I confess that a republic that has arms of its own is more powerful and makes more capital of acquisitions, but I will not confess that a disarmed republic becomes weaker the more it acquires, nor that Venice, which now fears neither kings nor emperors, if without dominion on land and at sea, would be more secure than it is at present. Which, if it were true, I do not know why the Discourse restricts itself only to republics, because for the same causes a prince who did not have arms of his own would derive from acquisitions and from the expansion of dominion weakness and not power, which both the reasons and the experience show at large to be most false

## Notes

**The heading and the rubric.** Canestrini's heading, CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO XIX, without `del libro II`, as both readers saw it; the column is headed as the page is (D443, D479). His rubric is Machiavelli's sentence (Machiavelli, rubric), `repubbliche` in lower case; the 1933 edition prints `procedano`, `ruina`, `ad esaltazione` (FG-CONS-II19-D002). Canestrini prints no reply to chapters XVI to XVIII, nor to XX to XXIII; his next is to chapter XXIV.

**`Chi dubita che la città di Firenze, che la republica di Vinegia sarebbono più deboli e di minore potenza`.** "Who doubts that the city of Florence, that the republic of Venice would be weaker and of less power": a question that expects no answer: `più deboli` answers Machiavelli's `molto più deboli` of Venice and Florence (Machiavelli .08); `dubitare`, doubt (D205); the two subjects set side by side with `che ... che`, without a conjunction, as printed; `Vinegia`, Venice (names.md); `debole`, weak (D464); `potenza`, power (D06).

**`se avessino rinchiuso il territorio loro tra piccoli confini che non sono`.** "If they had shut in their territory within small borders than they are": `rinchiudere`, shut in; `confini`, borders (glossary), Machiavelli's word for Florence's six miles (Machiavelli .08); `che non sono`, than they are, the second term of `più deboli e di minore potenza`, held back past the `se` clause, with the pleonastic `non` of the comparative, as `più sicura che non è di presente` below.

**`Avendo domato le città vicine, e allargato la loro jurisdizione`.** "Having subdued the neighbouring cities, and enlarged their jurisdiction": `domare`, subdue (D452), Machiavelli's verb for the enemy (Machiavelli .03); `allargare`, enlarge, apart from `ampliare`, expand (D132); `jurisdizione`, jurisdiction, in Canestrini's spelling. The subject of the gerunds is the two republics; the next clause turns to their neighbours.

**`non è facile a ogni vicino assaltarle`.** "It is not easy for every neighbour to assail them": `assaltare`, assail (D444).

**`non per ogni debole accidente si travagliano`.** "They are not troubled by every weak accident": `travagliarsi`, be troubled; `accidente`, accident (glossary); `debole`, weak, here of the accident, not of states. The fronted phrase normalised (D263).

**`tengono, se non viene moto grande, lo inimico fuora del tuorlo del suo Stato`.** "They hold, if a great motion does not come, the enemy outside the yolk of their state": `tenere`, hold; `moto`, motion, a great upheaval; `inimico`, enemy (D334); `tuorlo`, yolk, the core of the state, Guicciardini's image, kept; `suo`, their, the singular possessive for the plural; `Stato`, state (glossary). The parenthesis kept where it stands.

**`non si accosta facilmente la guerra alle loro mura`.** "War does not readily approach their walls": `accostarsi`, approach; `facilmente`, readily (D27); the inversion normalised (D263).

**`lo avere molti sudditi fa in molti modi le entrate publiche maggiori, fa la città dominante in privato più ricca`.** "Having many subjects makes the public revenues greater in many ways, makes the dominant city richer in private": `sudditi`, subjects, Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .03); `entrate`, revenues (D444); `dominante`, dominant, the ruling city over its subject territory; `in privato`, in private, its citizens' wealth in the editor's reading, against `publiche`, public. Machiavelli's `tener ricco il Pubblico, povero il privato` (Machiavelli .03) stands behind the pair, in the editor's reading. The comma after `maggiori` is the 1933 edition's (D110, FG-CONS-II19-D001).

**`Co' quali mezzi se bene non sono armate di soldati proprii, conducono de' forestieri`.** "With which means, although they are not armed with soldiers of their own, they conduct strangers": `mezzi`, means, the resources just listed: D84's "intermediaries" scoped to persons (D480); `se bene`, although (D31); `soldati proprii`, soldiers of one's own (D431), in Canestrini's spelling; `condurre`, conduct (D444), to take into pay; `forestieri`, strangers (D57). Machiavelli's closing question calls such soldiers `o mercenarj o ausiliarj`, either mercenary or auxiliary (Machiavelli .10).

**`da' quali essere difeso è meglio che non essere difeso da alcuno`.** "By whom to be defended is better than not to be defended by anyone": `difendere`, defend; the infinitives kept as subject and complement.

**`Confesso che una republica che ha armi propri[e], è più potente e fa più capitale degli acquisti`.** "I confess that a republic that has arms of its own is more powerful and makes more capital of acquisitions": `Confesso`, I confess (D431), a narrow concession; `armi proprie`, arms of one's own (D431); `fare capitale`, make capital, Machiavelli's phrase for the spoils (Machiavelli .03), turned to the acquisitions themselves, in the editor's reading. Both readers read `proprio`, with the vowel doubted, a masculine singular that agrees with nothing; emended `propri[e]`, as the reply itself has `armi proprie` in its last period and as the 1933 edition reads (D478, FG-CONS-II19-E001).

**`ma non confesserò già che una republica disarmata diventi più debole, quanto più acquista`.** "But I will not confess that a disarmed republic becomes weaker the more it acquires": `già` after a negative, not rendered (D374, D423); `disarmato`, disarmed; `quanto più`, the more. Guicciardini's general restatement of Machiavelli's Venice and Florence (Machiavelli .08), in the editor's reading: not granted, not refuted. Machiavelli does not say "disarmed".

**`né che Vinegia che ora non teme de' re né degli imperatori`.** "Nor that Venice, which now fears neither kings nor emperors": `temere`, fear (glossary); the kings and emperors are the powers of the League of Cambrai and after, in the editor's reading.

**`se sanza dominio in terra e in mare, fussi più sicura che non è di presente`.** "If without dominion on land and at sea, would be more secure than it is at present": the protasis without a verb, as Canestrini prints it; the 1933 edition reads `se fussi sanza dominio`, "if it were without dominion" (FG-CONS-II19-D003; not taken, D38, D478); `dominio`, dominion (glossary); `in terra e in mare`, on land and at sea, answering Machiavelli's Venice content with the sea (Machiavelli .08) in the editor's reading, and going beyond it: Machiavelli never proposes Venice without the sea; `sicuro`, secure (glossary).

**`Il che se fussi vero, non so perché il Discorso si ristringa solo alle republiche`.** "Which, if it were true, I do not know why the Discourse restricts itself only to republics": the relative `Il che` left hanging, as printed, the main clause taking another subject; `non so perché`, I do not know why: under the condition `se fussi vero`, the opening of a reductio, not the suspension of `non so ... se`; the consequence is called `falsissimo` at the close; `il Discorso`, the Discourse, the chapter (as FG II.15); `ristringersi a`, restrict oneself to, scoped apart from D58's "draw together" (D480); `solo`, only (D298). The rubric names republics only.

**`perché per le medesime cagioni uno principe che non avessi armi proprie, caverebbe degli acquisti e dell'ampliazione del dominio debolezza e non potenza`.** "Because for the same causes a prince who did not have arms of his own would derive from acquisitions and from the expansion of dominion weakness and not power": `cagione`, cause (glossary); `cavare`, derive, kept apart from `trarre`, draw (D444), which is Machiavelli's (Machiavelli .08); `ampliazione`, expansion, the noun of Machiavelli's `ampliare` (D132); `debolezza`, weakness (D472); `debolezza e non potenza` answers Machiavelli's `imperio e non forze` (Machiavelli .08), in the editor's reading.

**`il che essere falsissimo mostrano largamente e le ragioni e la esperienza`.** "Which both the reasons and the experience show at large to be most false": `largamente`, at large (D464); `ragione`, reason, plural as printed; `esperienza`, experience; `e ... e`, both ... and. What is most false is the consequence for the prince, and through it, in the editor's reading, the claim for republics. The order normalised (D263); the Italian ends on its subjects, `e le ragioni e la esperienza`. The reply ends without a stop: the faint trace after `esperienza` is not supplied at a unit's end (D40, D478).
