# Consideration on chapter XXIV

## FG-CONS.2.24.01

One must not praise antiquity so much that a man blame all the modern orders that were not in use among the Romans; because experience has uncovered many things that were not considered by the ancients, and, through the foundations' being besides different, [things] are fitting or are necessary to one of the things which were not fitting, or were not necessary, to the others. Therefore, if the Romans did not use to build fortresses in the subject cities, it is not for this that he err who builds them there nowadays; because many cases happen in which it is very useful to have fortresses, both to a prince or else a tyrant with the citizens themselves, and to a lord with his subjects, and to a potentate with strangers. The reasons seem to me so manifest that I marvel that this opinion has contradictors, because, principally, if, such as the rules are, such the subjects always were, that is, that when they are well treated they loved their prince, I confess that as to them fortresses would be useless to every prince that governed well; because, to defend him from his citizens and subjects, the love of the peoples would be enough. But considering how many times the peoples, even well treated, are often little reasonable, how desirous of new things, how much the memory of the old prince can be worth in them if they are now under a new rule, how much the appetite for liberty, if they are used to having it, and how often, for this and for other respects, a prince or tyrant is forced to govern his citizens or subjects with some injury, I say that both for those who can have the peoples [as] friends and for those who cannot hope to attain this benevolence, it is necessary to make some foundation upon force, upon holding their peoples in some terror; otherwise he would be too often a prey to the levity, to the malignity, to the just hatred of his subjects. And that reason which is adduced in the Discourse, that fortresses give princes spirit to be insolent and to make ill carriages, is very frivolous, because if one were to consider this, a prince would have to stand without guard, without arms, without armies, so as to have so much the more to seek to live in such a way that he were pleasing to the peoples, the more he found himself exposed to their discretion. Afterwards, things that in themselves are useful must not be fled, although the security that you draw from them may give you spirit to be bad: *verbi gratia*, is one to blame medicine because men, under trust of it, can guard themselves less from disorders and from the causes that make [one] fall sick? This is not a good reason, nor [one] to make the good be refused, when the ill that can follow from it is in your power, whether it follow or not.

## FG-CONS.2.24.02

And to come to the particulars according to the order of the Discourse, I say that to a tyrant of a city, or to every prince, fortresses in that city are most useful, because neither the people nor the particular enemies, seeing the prince secure in his fortress, can make a movement on every light occasion; because it is difficult to do it in such a way that the prince is killed with all his lineage; not easy to have the forces and the succours prepared in such a way that the fortress can be shut in or taken so quickly that the prince has not time to take up the town again with new troops introduced through the fortress. I say the same of a subject city, which, through the rein of the fortress, cannot think of rebellion unless it sees a foreign army, enemy to the prince, in that province. Nor are they good, the examples of Milan and the others that he cites, which, albeit they had the fortresses, lost the states, because [they lost them] not through rebellion of the peoples alone, but through occasion of powerful war; and it could be said that, if they had not had them, they would have lost them perhaps much earlier, even in the times of peace. And if by *virtù* of the fortress the lost town is not always recovered, one has also seen, sometimes, some recovered, as befell Monsignor de Foix at Brescia, who, even though he found himself with a powerful army, if he had not been introduced through the fortress, was not enough to recover Brescia. And when by way of the fortress the town is not recovered, the fear of the fortress is enough to keep the enemies engaged, without doing other offence there, until they have acquired it; which interval of time can be the cause of great benefit to whoever finds himself assailed.

## FG-CONS.2.24.03

And as to the example of the Romans that is cited, setting aside the example of Duke Guido, of Ottaviano and of the others, whose authority is not enough to confound the authority of so many others who have built fortresses, I say that, if the Romans did not use fortresses, the causes could have been two: the one, that, as the Author has said elsewhere, in the beginnings of their empire they did not use to bring cities into express servitude, but to keep them under a shadow of liberty and of equal confederation, which institute did not bear the building of fortresses there; the other, that, finding themselves always with ordered and most powerful armies, and in many places with colonies, they judged they had less need of fortresses, above all since they were accustomed sooner to destroy the cities which they deemed most inimical; and nonetheless, if they had judged them useless, they would have destroyed that of Tarentum and the others that they found built, because a fortress built by others would be as useless as one that were built by you. I confess, then, that in many cases and in many times fortresses do not help; that for the security of your state there are other remedies, perhaps sometimes more useful and harder than fortresses; but that fortresses are often useful to whoever holds them, to secure himself from conspiracies, to flee rebellions, and to recover lost towns. Therefore it is not without cause that our times employ them; they were in use among the ancients, and the Romans at Tarentum, and in the other places where they found them made, did not unwall them.

## Notes

**The heading and the rubric.** Canestrini's heading, CONSIDERAZIONE SUL CAPITOLO XXIV, without `del libro II`, as both readers saw it; the column is headed as the page is (D443, D487). His rubric is Machiavelli's sentence (Machiavelli, rubric), `fortezze` in lower case, with no stop (FG-CONS-II24-D003). Canestrini prints no reply to chapters XX to XXIII, nor to XXV to XXXIII; his next is to chapter XVII of Book III. His two notes to this reply, on Brescia and on Guidobaldo and Ottaviano Fregoso, are not carried (D487); the persons are identified in the commentary.

**`Non si debbe laudare tanto la antiquità, che l'uomo biasimi tutti gli ordini moderni`.** "One must not praise antiquity so much that a man blame all the modern orders": `dovere`, must (D274); `laudare` / `biasimare`, praise / blame (D422); `l'uomo`, a man, the impersonal subject; `ordini`, orders (glossary), Machiavelli's word for Rome's institutions (Machiavelli .01).

**`perché la esperienza ha scoperte molte cose che non furono considerate dagli antichi`.** "Because experience has uncovered many things that were not considered by the ancients": `scoprire`, uncover (D452, D464); `considerare`, consider (glossary), Machiavelli's `non bene considerata` (Machiavelli .01) turned back on him, in the editor's reading.

**`e, per essere inoltre e' fondamenti diversi, convengono o sono necessarie a una delle cose che non convenivano, o non erano necessarie all'altre`.** "And, through the foundations' being besides different, [things] are fitting or are necessary to one of the things which were not fitting, or were not necessary, to the others": causal `per` with the infinitive; the subject of `convengono` left unexpressed, as printed (D53); `convenire`, be fitting (D85), here personal, of things suited to a case. The period is compressed and is left so.

**`Però se e' Romani nella città suddite non usorono di edificare fortezze`.** "Therefore, if the Romans did not use to build fortresses in the subject cities": `però`, therefore (D22); `usare di`, use to, as `non usorono ridurre` (.03); `suddito`, subject (glossary); `nella` with the plural `suddite` as printed, read as plural by sense; the phrase set after its verb in the plain English order.

**`non è per questo che erri chi oggidì ve le edifica`.** "It is not for this that he err who builds them there nowadays": the subjunctive `erri` kept, the consequence denied, not its reason; `errare`, err (glossary `errore`).

**`perché accaggiono molti casi`.** "Because many cases happen": `accadere`, happen (D298).

**`e a uno principe o vero tiranno co' cittadini medesimi, e a uno signore co' sudditi suoi, e a uno potentato co' forestieri`.** "Both to a prince or else a tyrant with the citizens themselves, and to a lord with his subjects, and to a potentate with strangers": `o vero`, or else, here perhaps one case under two names (as D453 left `ovvero` open); `medesimi` postposed, intensive, "themselves" (D488); `tiranno`, tyrant (glossary); `signore`, lord (glossary `Signore`); `forestieri`, strangers (D57). Three uses, as Machiavelli's three: the prince's own city (Machiavelli .04), the city held as subject (Machiavelli .09), and enemies outside (Machiavelli .13); Guicciardini names the third and does not argue it.

**`Le ragioni mi paiono sì manifeste, che io mi maraviglio che questa opinione abbia contradittori`.** "The reasons seem to me so manifest that I marvel that this opinion has contradictors": `parere`, seem (D264's "it seems" for `pare`); `maravigliarsi`, marvel (glossary `maraviglia`); `opinione`, opinion (D02); `contradittori`, contradictors.

**`se, quali sono gli imperii, tali fussino sempre e' sudditi`.** "If, such as the rules are, such the subjects always were": `imperio`, rule, sovereignty in general (D124), the second sense; the plural the ruling of each prince, good or ill, as `cioè ... bene trattati` glosses, not regulations; `quali ... tali`, such as ... such.

**`io confesso che quanto a loro sarebbono a ogni principe che governassi bene inutili le fortezze`.** "I confess that as to them fortresses would be useless to every prince that governed well": `confesso`, I confess (D431), the first of two; the concession is conditional on `se ... tali fussino sempre e' sudditi`, a condition he denies at once. The inversion normalised (D263).

**`lo amore de' populi`.** "The love of the peoples": `populi`, peoples, in Guicciardini's spelling; `populo` (.02) the people. The delayed subject kept last, as a landing (D263): the love answers Machiavelli's benevolence (Machiavelli .04), and the next period refuses it.

**`Ma considerato quanto molte volte e' populi, eziamdio bene trattati, sono spesso poco ragionevoli`.** "But considering how many times the peoples, even well treated, are often little reasonable": `eziamdio`, even (D453, D488); `bene trattati`, well treated, answering Machiavelli's `mali trattamenti` (Machiavelli .03); `molte volte ... spesso`, both kept.

**`quanto possi valere in loro la memoria dello antico principe`.** "How much the memory of the old prince can be worth in them": `valere`, be worth, apart from `valersi`, avail (D162) (D488); `antico`, old, of a former ruler, kept apart from `antichi`, the ancients (D488).

**`quanto lo appetito della libertà se sono usati a averla`.** "How much the appetite for liberty, if they are used to having it": the verb `possi valere` understood from the clause before; `appetito`, appetite (D117); `libertà`, liberty (glossary).

**`è sforzato governare e' cittadini o sudditi suoi con qualche ingiuria`.** "Is forced to govern his citizens or subjects with some injury": `sforzare`, force, Machiavelli's verb (Machiavelli .03 `poterli sforzare`); `ingiuria`, injury (glossary).

**`dico che e a quelli che possono avere e' populi amici, e a quelli che non possono sperare di conseguire questa benevolenza`.** "I say that both for those who can have the peoples [as] friends and for those who cannot hope to attain this benevolence": `e ... e`, both ... and; `conseguire`, attain (glossary); `benevolenza`, benevolence (D431), Machiavelli's `benivolenza` (Machiavelli .04, .07) in Guicciardini's spelling.

**`è necessario fare qualche fondamento in sulla forza, in sul tenere [e]' populi suoi in qualche terrore`.** "It is necessary to make some foundation upon force, upon holding their peoples in some terror": `fondamento`, foundation (glossary), against Machiavelli's `in su la benivolenza degli uomini si fondino` (Machiavelli .04), in the editor's reading; `forza`, force (D452). Both readers read `a populi`, doubting the letter; the preposition construes nothing, and the plural article, which Canestrini prints `e` throughout the reply, is read with the 1933 edition (`e' popoli`): emended `[e]'` (FG-CONS-II24-E001; D486). `suoi`, their: the antecedent is `quelli ... quelli`; the singular returns at `sarebbe`.

**`altrimenti sarebbe troppo spesso in preda della leggerezza, della malignità, del giusto odio de' sudditi suoi`.** "Otherwise he would be too often a prey to the levity, to the malignity, to the just hatred of his subjects": `leggerezza`, levity (D358); `malignità`, malignity (glossary); `giusto odio`, just hatred, the hatred of Machiavelli's .03 granted its justice, in the editor's reading.

**`E quella ragione che si adduce nel Discorso, che le fortezze danno animo a' principi a essere insolenti e fare mali portamenti, è molto frivola`.** "And that reason which is adduced in the Discourse, that fortresses give princes spirit to be insolent and to make ill carriages, is very frivolous": Machiavelli's `ti danno più animo a far loro male` and `mali portamenti` (Machiavelli .03); `animo`, spirit (glossary); `insolente`, insolent (D464); `portamenti`, carriages (D488); `il Discorso`, the Discourse, the chapter (as FG II.15, II.19).

**`perché se s'avessi a considerare questo, arebbe uno principe a stare sanza guardia, sanza armi, sanza eserciti`.** "Because if one were to consider this, a prince would have to stand without guard, without arms, without armies": `avere a`, "be to" in the protasis, "have to" in the apodosis, where the sense is necessity (D261); `guardia`, guard (glossary). A reductio: the conclusion is the absurdity.

**`che fussi grato a' populi quanto più si trovassi esposto alla loro discrezione`.** "That he were pleasing to the peoples, the more he found himself exposed to their discretion": `grato`, pleasing, the old sense; `discrezione`, discretion, a recorded collapse with `arbitrio`, discretion (glossary) (D488).

**`Di poi le cose che in sé sono utili non si debbono fuggire`.** "Afterwards, things that in themselves are useful must not be fled": `Di poi`, afterwards (D187); `fuggire`, flee (D325).

**`se bene la sicurtà che tu trai di loro ti possa dare animo a essere cattivo`.** "Although the security that you draw from them may give you spirit to be bad": `se bene`, although (D31); `sicurtà`, security (glossary), Machiavelli's word (Machiavelli .03); `trarre`, draw (D444); `cattivo`, bad (D03).

**`verbi gratia, hassi egli a biasimare la medicina`.** "*Verbi gratia*, is one to blame medicine": the Latin kept (latin.md); `avere a`, be to (D261); `medicina`, medicine.

**`sotto la fidanza di quella si possono guardare manco da' disordini`.** "Under trust of it, can guard themselves less from disorders": `fidanza`, trust (D488); `guardarsi`, guard oneself; `disordini`, disorders (glossary), here of the body.

**`Non è questa buona ragione né da fare rifiutare il bene, quando il male che ne può seguire è in potestà tua se seguiti o no`.** "This is not a good reason, nor [one] to make the good be refused, when the ill that can follow from it is in your power, whether it follow or not": `bene` / `male`, good / ill (glossary); `potestà`, power (D78).

**`E per venire a' particulari secondo l'ordine del Discorso`.** "And to come to the particulars according to the order of the Discourse": `particulari`, particulars (D374); `ordine`, order (glossary): the order is Machiavelli's two cases, the prince's own city (Machiavelli .04) and the city occupied (Machiavelli .09).

**`dico; che a uno tiranno di una città, o a ogni principe sono utilissime le fortezze in quella città`.** "I say that to a tyrant of a city, or to every prince, fortresses in that city are most useful": the stop after `dico`, doubted by both readers, as the lighter of the marks read (D110), not carried into the English; `o a ogni principe`, read by both without doubt, kept against the 1933 `ed a ogni principe` (FG-CONS-II24-D001; D486); the inversion normalised (D263).

**`perché né il populo né gli inimici particulari ... non possono per ogni leggiera occasione fare movimento`.** "Because neither the people nor the particular enemies ... can make a movement on every light occasion": the doubled negative given once; `inimico`, enemy (D334); `particulare`, particular (D93); `movimento`, movement, a rising.

**`perché è difficile farlo in modo che si ammazzi il principe con tutta la sua progenie`.** "Because it is difficult to do it in such a way that the prince is killed with all his lineage": `ammazzare`, kill (D262); `progenie`, lineage (D283).

**`non facile avere le forze e i soccorsi preparati`.** "Not easy to have the forces and the succours prepared": `soccorso`, succour (glossary).

**`che il principe non abbia tempo a ripigliare la terra con genti nuove introdotte per la fortezza`.** "That the prince has not time to take up the town again with new troops introduced through the fortress": `ripigliare`, take up again (D224); `terra`, town (D488); `genti`, troops (glossary); `introdurre`, introduce (glossary).

**`Il medesimo dico di una città suddita`.** "I say the same of a subject city": the fronted object normalised (D263, as FG II.15). Machiavelli's second case (Machiavelli .09).

**`per il freno della fortezza`.** "Through the rein of the fortress": `freno`, rein (D389), Machiavelli's figure (Machiavelli .01, .04).

**`se non vede esercito forestiere inimico del principe in quella provincia`.** "Unless it sees a foreign army, enemy to the prince, in that province": `forestiere` (adjective), foreign (glossary); `provincia`, province (glossary).

**`Né sono buoni gli esempli di Milano e gli altri che lui allega`.** "Nor are they good, the examples of Milan and the others that he cites": the order kept, so that the relative `che ... perderono` follows its antecedent, Milan and the others; `benché`, albeit (D31); Machiavelli .05-.07; `allegare`, cite (D431); `lui`, he, Machiavelli.

**`perché non per rebellione de' populi soli, ma per occasione di guerra potente`.** "Because [they lost them] not through rebellion of the peoples alone, but through occasion of powerful war": the verb understood (D53); `rebellione`, rebellion, in Guicciardini's spelling.

**`e si potria dire che se non l'avessino avute, l'arebbono perdute forse molto prima etiamdio ne' tempi della pace`.** "And it could be said that, if they had not had them, they would have lost them perhaps much earlier, even in the times of peace": `si potria dire`, it could be said, the conditional of D264's `si può dire`, a possibility offered, not his judgment; `forse`, perhaps, limiting `molto prima`, not the loss; `prima`, earlier (D334); `etiamdio`, even (D488). Machiavelli's counterfactual (Machiavelli .05: without the fortress the Sforza would have seen the danger sooner) turned the other way, in the editor's reading.

**`E se per virtù della fortezza non si recupera sempre la terra persa`.** "And if by *virtù* of the fortress the lost town is not always recovered": *virtù* (D16), as Machiavelli's `la virtù delle Fortezze` (Machiavelli .09), ironic in the editor's reading; `recuperare`, recover (glossary).

**`si è anche visto qualche volta recuperarne`.** "One has also seen, sometimes, some recovered": impersonal; `qualche volta`, sometimes (D431); `-ne`, some of them, lost towns.

**`come intervenne a monsignore di Fois a Brescia`.** "As befell Monsignor de Foix at Brescia": `intervenire`, befall (D298); the objector's modern example, which Machiavelli answers (Machiavelli .11-.12): there the fortress needed Foix's army; here the army needed the fortress, in the editor's reading; Gaston de Foix, February 1512 (names.md); `ancora che`, even though (D31).

**`non era bastante a recuperare Brescia`.** "Was not enough to recover Brescia": `bastante`, enough, with `bastare` (D205); "sufficient" stays `sufficiente`'s.

**`è il timore della fortezza bastante a tenere impegnati li inimici sanza farci altra offesa insino l'abbino acquistata`.** "The fear of the fortress is enough to keep the enemies engaged, without doing other offence there, until they have acquired it": `timore`, fear (glossary); `impegnare`, engage; `offesa`, offence (glossary `offendere`); `ci`, there, in the editor's reading; "to us" is possible.

**`il quale intervallo di tempo può essere causa di gran beneficio a chi si truova assaltato`.** "Which interval of time can be the cause of great benefit to whoever finds himself assailed": `causa`, cause (glossary, a recorded collapse with `cagione`); `beneficio`, benefit (D334); `assaltare`, assail (D444).

**`E quanto allo esemplo che si allega de' Romani, posposto lo esemplo del duca Guido, di Ottaviano e degli altri`.** "And as to the example of the Romans that is cited, setting aside the example of Duke Guido, of Ottaviano and of the others": `posporre`, set aside (glossary); Guidobaldo of Urbino and Ottaviano Fregoso (Machiavelli .07, .08; names.md); `esemplo`, example (glossary).

**`la autorità de' quali non basta a confondere la autorità di tanti altri`.** "Whose authority is not enough to confound the authority of so many others": `autorità`, authority (glossary), Machiavelli's `l'autorità de' Romani` (Machiavelli .10) answered with the authority of numbers, in the editor's reading.

**`due potettono essere le cause`.** "The causes could have been two": the past of possibility; the order normalised (D263).

**`come altrove ha detto lo Autore`.** "As the Author has said elsewhere": `Autore`, Author, capitalised as Canestrini prints it, beside `Scrittore`, the Writer (glossary). The place is Discourses II.4, on Rome's companions, in the editor's reading.

**`ne' principii dello imperio loro non usorono ridurre le città in espressa servitù, ma tenerle sotto ombra di libertà e di confederazione equale`.** "In the beginnings of their empire they did not use to bring cities into express servitude, but to keep them under a shadow of liberty and of equal confederation": `principio`, beginning (glossary); `imperio`, empire (D124), the first sense; `ridurre`, bring (D314); `servitù`, servitude (glossary); `ombra`, shadow (glossary); `confederazione`, confederation (D452); `equale`, equal (D262).

**`il quale instituto non comportava lo edificarvi fortezze`.** "Which institute did not bear the building of fortresses there": `instituto`, institute, kept apart from `instituzione`, institution (FG I.10) (D488); `comportare`, bear (glossary).

**`trovandosi sempre con gli eserciti ordinati e potentissimi, e in molti luoghi con le colonie`.** "Finding themselves always with ordered and most powerful armies, and in many places with colonies": `ordinato`, ordered (glossary); `colonie`, colonies.

**`massime che erano consueti distruggere più presto le città, le quali reputavano inimicissime`.** "Above all since they were accustomed sooner to destroy the cities which they deemed most inimical": `massime`, above all (glossary); `più presto` without `che`, sooner (D464); `reputare`, deem (D259); `inimicissimo`, most inimical, of `inimico`; the comma before `le quali` is Canestrini's (D110), the relative read as restrictive by sense.

**`perché così sarebbe inutile una fortezza edificata da altri, come quella che fussi edificata da te`.** "Because a fortress built by others would be as useless as one that were built by you": `così ... come`, as ... as (D334); the order normalised (D263).

**`Confesso adunque che in molti casi e in molti tempi le fortezze non giovano`.** "I confess, then, that in many cases and in many times fortresses do not help": the second `Confesso` (D431), a real concession; `adunque`, then (D74); `giovare`, help (D334), Machiavelli's `le quali non ti giovano in alcuna parte` (Machiavelli .05). Both readers doubted the conjunction against `o`, both preferring `e`; read `e`, as the 1933 edition's `ed` (D486).

**`che alla sicurtà dello Stato tuo sono degli altri remedii, forse qualche volta più utili e più gagliardi che le fortezze`.** "That for the security of your state there are other remedies, perhaps sometimes more useful and harder than fortresses": `remedio`, remedy (glossary); `gagliardo`, hard (D313), of force, not "more difficult"; `forse` and `qualche volta` both kept.

**`per assicurarsi dalle congiure, per fuggire le rebellioni, e per recuperare le terre perdute`.** "To secure himself from conspiracies, to flee rebellions, and to recover lost towns": `assicurarsi`, secure oneself (D219), Machiavelli's verb (Machiavelli .01, .14); `congiure`, conspiracies (D452's `congiurare`); `fuggire`, flee (D325); `terre`, towns (D488).

**`Però non sanza cagione e' tempi nostri le adoperano`.** "Therefore it is not without cause that our times employ them": `però`, therefore (D22); `adoperare`, employ (glossary); the fronted litotes kept as a landing (D263), answering the opening's `non è per questo che erri`. Both readers' split at `le` (A) and `la` (B) read `le`, the fortresses, with the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-II24-D002; D486).

**`furono in uso apresso agli antichi, e i Romani a Taranto, e negli altri luoghi che le trovorono fatte, non le smurorono`.** "They were in use among the ancients, and the Romans at Tarentum, and in the other places where they found them made, did not unwall them": `in uso`, in use, the reply's opening phrase (`non erano in uso`) returned; `Taranto`, Tarentum (names.md); `smurare`, unwall, Machiavelli's `smuravano e non muravano` (Machiavelli .10) answered in his own verb. The reply ends with Canestrini's printed stop.
