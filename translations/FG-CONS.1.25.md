# Consideration on chapter XXV of Book I

*Whoever wishes to reform an ancient state in a free city, let him retain at least the shadow of the ancient ways.*

## FG-CONS.1.25.01

The conclusion of the Discourse is more necessary to whoever does not change the species of government but reforms it, *verbi gratia* to whoever wishes to introduce new orders in a free city, than to whoever changes the species of government, because if out of a kingdom I introduce a liberty, as the Romans did, it being already in the opinion of men that that living is not good, there is no call to conserve the ancient orders so exactly. And the example of the lictors and of the king *sacrificulus* are not of much moment, because in the one respect was had to the superstition which men could have in religion; in the other it would not have been tolerable that, changing the royal power as too much, the consuls should be armed with insignia of greater power.

## Notes

**The rubric.** Canestrini's setting of the chapter title, in lower case, with `stato` and `città` uncapitalised; the stop is faint. It is heading apparatus, not Guicciardini's (D32).

**`La c[o]nclusione del Discorso`.** "The conclusion of the Discourse": `il Discorso`, Machiavelli's chapter, as at FG I.23 and I.24. Canestrini prints `canclusione`, which both cold readers read and which is not a word; emended from the 1933 edition (D267; FG-CONS-I25-D001).

**`è più necessaria a chi non muta spezie di governo, ma lo riforma ... che a chi muta spezie di governo`.** "Is more necessary to whoever does not change the species of government but reforms it ... than to whoever changes the species of government": a comparative. Guicciardini does not deny Machiavelli's conclusion; he ranks the cases it fits. Machiavelli applies the rule to exactly the case Guicciardini ranks lower, `tutti coloro che vogliono scancellare uno antico vivere in una Città, e ridurla ad uno vivere nuovo e libero` (Machiavelli .03), and keeps the renewal of every thing for tyranny alone; Guicciardini's `in una città libera` takes the rubric's own case as the one the conclusion fits. `spezie`, species (glossary); `riformare`, reform, the rubric's verb, now set against `mutare`, change. The readers split on the letter before `chi muta` (A `a`, B `n`); ruled `a` (D268; FG-CONS-I25-D002).

**`verbi gratia`.** Latin, "for example", kept in italic, as FG I.23.

**`introdurre nuovi ordini`.** "To introduce new orders": `ordine`, order (glossary), as Machiavelli's `ordini nuovi` (Machiavelli .01).

**`se di uno regno io introduco una libertà`.** "If out of a kingdom I introduce a liberty": the first person is Guicciardini's hypothetical founder, not a claim about himself. `libertà`, liberty (glossary). The readers split on `uno` (B `ano`); ruled `uno` (D268; FG-CONS-I25-D003).

**`come feciono e' Romani`.** "As the Romans did": the expulsion of the kings, Machiavelli's own example, which Guicciardini reclassifies as a change of species, not a reform.

**`essendo già nella opinione degli uomini che quello vivere non sia buono`.** "It being already in the opinion of men that that living is not good": `opinione`, opinion (D02); `vivere`, living, as Machiavelli's `vivere libero` (.02) and `uno antico vivere` (.03). The subjunctive `sia` is that of the reported opinion.

**`non accade conservare sì esattamente gli ordini antichi`.** "There is no call to conserve the ancient orders so exactly": `non accade`, impersonal; `accadere` is "happen" elsewhere (FG I.23), but "it does not happen to conserve" is not English, and "occasion" is `occasione`'s. `conservare`, conserve (D220). The claim is of degree, `sì esattamente`, not that the ancient orders may be thrown away.

**`E lo esemplo de' littori e del re Sacrificulo non sono di molto momento`.** "And the example of the lictors and of the king *sacrificulus* are not of much moment": the singular `esemplo` takes the plural `sono`, two examples counted as one; the English keeps the disagreement rather than correct it: kept, not a misprint. `di molto momento`, of much moment (glossary, I.8). `re Sacrificulo`, as Machiavelli's `Re sacrificolo` (Machiavelli .02), in Guicciardini's Latin form.

**`nell'uno s'ebbe rispetto alla superstizione`.** "In the one respect was had to the superstition": `l'uno` is the second-named, the king of sacrifices; `l'altro` the lictors. The order is crossed against the order of naming. `avere rispetto a`, have respect to (glossary). Guicciardini gives the king of sacrifices a religious reason, where Machiavelli gave a political one (the people should not miss the kings); he calls it superstition, Machiavelli did not.

**`che potevano avere gli uomini nella religione`.** "Which men could have in religion": `religione`, religion (glossary).

**`nell'altro non sarebbe stato tollerabile`.** "In the other it would not have been tolerable": the lictors. Machiavelli too says the number was held `per non passare` that of the kings (Machiavelli .02); Guicciardini reads the same limit differently: not as keeping the shadow of the old, but because, the kingship having been changed as too great, insignia of greater power would not have been borne.

**`mutando la potestà regia come troppa`.** "Changing the royal power as too much": the gerund has no expressed subject, and the English leaves it so; the Romans changed the kingship because its power was too great. `mutare`, change, the reply's verb (`muta` twice in the first period). `potestà`, power (D78). Machiavelli's one `potestà` is the tyrant's, set against `potenza`, ordered power (Machiavelli .03); Guicciardini uses `potestà` twice, of royal and of consular power. The collapse in English hides that; the collapse is kept by ruling (D278).

**`si armassino e' consuli con insegne di maggiore potestà`.** "The consuls should be armed with insignia of greater power": `si` passive. Both readers doubted whether the letter before `consuli` carries an accent; `è` does not construe; read `e'`, the article (D269; FG-CONS-I25-D004). `insegne`, insignia: the lictors with their fasces were the visible sign of the consuls' *imperium*. The reply ends here, with a faint stop.
