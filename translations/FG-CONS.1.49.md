# Consideration on chapter XLIX of Book I

*If those cities that have had a free beginning, like Rome, have difficulty in finding laws that may maintain them, those that have it immediately servile have almost an impossibility of it.*

## FG-CONS.1.49.01

And this Discourse and many others show that which I, against the opinion of the Writer, have said in another place, that, military discipline set aside, the Roman government was in many parts defective; because, what more absurd thing than that it should be in the power of one man alone to stop the public business, or not to let a deliberation of the city have effect, as those consuls did? To whom, although there was the rein of the tribune, nonetheless to the tribune, when he wished to make like disorder, there was no remedy whatever. It was also an error that it should be in the power of the two censors to deprive of the senate, for so good a work, Mamercus Aemilius, a most honoured citizen and so well deserving of the republic; indeed it was in the power of one alone. Nor do I believe that he had other remedy for it than either a law of the people that he should be restored to the senate, which one does not read was made, or that the following censors, when they chose the senate, should restore him; which too I am not certain they could do, albeit I believe it

## Notes

**The rubric.** Canestrini's setting of the chapter title: Machiavelli's rubric with `città` lower case, `mantenghino` for `mantengano`, and a comma for the semicolon. `servo`, "servile", as in Machiavelli's rubric (D388). It is heading apparatus, not Guicciardini's (D32). Both readers doubted whether `ne` carries an accent; `ne hanno`, "have ... of it", is the pronoun, as the 1933 edition and the 1824 rubric read (FG-CONS-I49-D004, D383).

**`E questo Discorso e molti altri mostrano quello che io, contro alla opinione dello Scrittore, ho detto in altro luogo`.** "And this Discourse and many others show that which I, against the opinion of the Writer, have said in another place": `il Discorso`, the Discourse, his name for Machiavelli's chapter; `lo Scrittore`, the Writer, Machiavelli, capitalised in Canestrini, as at FG I.29; `opinione`, opinion (D02). `io` was doubtful to both readers (`lo`, `to`); the pronoun, as the 1933 edition reads (FG-CONS-I49-D004, D383). The other place is not named. The nearest in this edition is his reply to I.4, where military discipline was `eccellentissima, e la virtù sua sostenne tutti gli altri difetti del governo`; whether he means that reply or another text, the note does not settle.

**`che posposta la disciplina militare, il governo romano era in molte parti defettivo`.** "That, military discipline set aside, the Roman government was in many parts defective": `posporre`, set after, set aside: military discipline is excepted from the judgment, not judged; `governo`, government (D262); `defettivo`, defective, Machiavelli's `difettivo` of the historian (Machiavelli .02), turned on the Roman orders, Machiavelli's other alternative.

**`perché, che più assurda cosa che fussi in potestà di uno uomo solo fermare le azioni publiche, o non lasciare ch[e] una deliberazione della città abbia effetto, come feciono quelli consuli?`** "Because, what more absurd thing than that it should be in the power of one man alone to stop the public business, or not to let a deliberation of the city have effect, as those consuls did?": the question is rhetorical and has no main verb, and the English supplies none. `potestà`, power (D78); `uno uomo solo`, one man alone (D172); `azioni publiche`, public business, what the state does, a third sense beside `azioni` of an office, "functions" (D348), and of narrated doings, "proceedings" (D358) (D389); `deliberazione`, deliberation (glossary). Canestrini prints `cha`, read by both readers with `che` as the alternative; the 1933 edition reads `che`: emended (FG-CONS-I49-D001, D381). `quelli consuli` are the consuls of the next chapter, I.50, Titus Quinctius Cincinnatus and Gnaeus Julius Mento, who `sendo disuniti avevano ferme tutte le azioni di quella Repubblica` and agreed only in refusing to create a dictator; I.50's rubric is `Non debbe uno Consiglio o uno Magistrato potere fermare le azioni della Città`. Guicciardini has no heading for I.50; his reply to I.49 takes it in, and the edition's treatment of I.50 is D390.

**`A' quali se bene vi fu il freno del tribuno, nondimanco al tribuno, quando voleva fare simile disordine, non vi era remedio alcuno`.** "To whom, although there was the rein of the tribune, nonetheless to the tribune, when he wished to make like disorder, there was no remedy whatever": `se bene`, although (D31); `freno`, rein, as Machiavelli's `tenere ... a freno` (Machiavelli .05) and NM I.3 (D389); in I.50 the senate had recourse to the tribunes, whose office was useful `a frenare l'ambizione`. `nondimanco`, nonetheless, the form of `nondimeno` (D45); `simile`, like (glossary); `disordine`, disorder (glossary); `remedio`, remedy (glossary), Machiavelli's `senza alcun rimedio` (Machiavelli .02); `alcuno` after its noun, "whatever" (D349). The tribune's veto is the case; Guicciardini names no tribune.

**`Fu anche [e]rrore che in potestà de' dua censori fussi privare del senato per sì buona opera Mamerco Emilio cittadino onoratissimo e tanto benemerito della republica`.** "It was also an error that it should be in the power of the two censors to deprive of the senate, for so good a work, Mamercus Aemilius, a most honoured citizen and so well deserving of the republic": `errore`, error, Machiavelli's word for the censorship's first form (Machiavelli .02), here turned on the censors' power. Canestrini prints `arrore`, read by both readers with `errore` as the alternative; the 1933 edition reads `errore`: emended (FG-CONS-I49-D001, D381). `dua`, two; `opera`, work (D253), his law; `sì`, so, on which the readers split (A `sì`, B `si`), read with the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-I49-D002, D382). `benemerito`, well deserving, as `meritamente` is "deservedly" (D374). Machiavelli's `privarono Mamerco del Senato` is taken as given.

**`anzi era in potestà di uno solo`.** "Indeed it was in the power of one alone": `anzi` correcting upward, "indeed" (D147): not two censors but one could do it. Canestrini's comma before it is a semicolon in the English, which cannot run the clauses together (D65). `uno solo`, one alone (D172), answering `uno uomo solo` above. The readers split at the stop after `solo`; it is supplied from the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-I49-C001, D384).

**`Né credo che lui vi avessi altro remedio, che o una legge del populo che fussi restituito al senato, la quale non si legge che fussi fatta`.** "Nor do I believe that he had other remedy for it than either a law of the people that he should be restored to the senate, which one does not read was made": `credo`, I believe (glossary), negated, and governing both remedies; `vi`, for it, against the deprivation; `populo`, people; `restituire`, restore; `non si legge`, one does not read, the histories' silence, as Machiavelli's `la Istoria non mostra` (Machiavelli .02).

**`o che e' seguenti censori, quando eleggevano il senato, lo restituissino`.** "Or that the following censors, when they chose the senate, should restore him": `e'`, the plural article (D35), read where both readers doubted the letter (`è`, `i`), as the 1933 edition reads (FG-CONS-I49-D004, D383); `eleggere`, choose (glossary), here the censors' revision of the senate's roll.

**`il che anche non sono certo potessino fare, benché lo credo`.** "Which too I am not certain they could do, albeit I believe it": `anche` adds this doubt to the unrecorded law; `non sono certo`, I am not certain, a suspension, set against `non è dubio`, `certo` ("has no doubt", register); `benché`, albeit (D31); `lo credo`, I believe it. The readers doubted `il` (`el`) and `lo` (`io`), both read with the 1933 edition (FG-CONS-I49-D004, D383); with `lo` the belief is in their power to restore him, with `io` it would be the bare act. The reply ends without a stop, and so does the English (D40, D384): one reader saw a faint trace, the other none.
