# Project review, 2026-11-25

A review of the edition through two lenses, a professional scholar's and an
interested amateur's, followed by David McDougall's decisions on it. The fixes
run in seven passes, one per session. **Each pass reads this file first, ticks
its own row in the plan below, and ends by giving David the prompt for the
next pass.**

Decisions are recorded in `decisions/DEC-013-review-2026-11-25.md`, which
also amends DEC-012 (v1.0).

## Findings

### The professional's view

- **Witness choice.** The 1824 Milan *Discorsi* governs, but neither SCOPE
  nor the source notes say why a nineteenth-century reprint was preferred to
  the 1531 printings.
- **Bausi.** SCOPE promised to note where the text differs from modern
  critical consensus, but treated Bausi 2001 as something that could not be
  opened. Those two positions cannot both hold.
- **Completeness.** "He did not stop early" and "the set is closed" claim
  more than the evidence allows. The conventional reading is that the
  *Considerazioni* are unrevised working notes and the 39 are what survives.
- **Date.** The edition gave the date of composition three ways ("1529–30",
  "about 1530", "usually dated about 1530") and cited no source for any of
  them.
- **No scholarship.** There is no secondary literature in the notes, no
  bibliography and no further reading.
- **Unbacked claims in SCOPE.** `qc/source-integrity/` was cited as evidence
  but was never in the repository. The pairing map carried a provenance
  note that had already been discharged. SCOPE's header date was two months
  old. Two word counts for Machiavelli disagreed (≈38,000 and ≈36,000).
- **Citation.** `CITATION.cff` called this a "critical edition", which SCOPE
  §1 disclaims. It gave no preferred citation, and it listed three licences
  without saying what each covers.
- **Releases.** There were no per-version tags or archived snapshots short of
  the v1.0 DOI.
- **Rights.** UK law on computer-generated works (CDPA s.9(3)) is missing.
  The Palmarocchi analysis can be made more precise. The hosted witness
  extracts serve no reader. There is no machine-readable licence marking.
  The rights assessment was written by Claude and cited as the project's.
- **Apparatus.** Variants are written as prose rather than as a conventional
  apparatus. Process (passes, collator, `WIT-` IDs, D-numbers) shows on
  reader pages.
- **Argument notes** paraphrase the text beside them.

### The amateur's view

1. The site's main promise isn't kept. Guicciardini's reply sits thousands of
   pixels below Machiavelli's chapter and its apparatus, so the two are not
   on one screen.
2. The site has no way in: no introduction, no start-here list, no search,
   no themes. The index rows are rubrics and dates.
3. The notes cite a glossary, names, Latin and the decision log, and none of
   these is published.
4. The notes are too dense and in low-contrast type. The editor's summary is
   too long.

### The thesis

The title argument (the 1513 letter, and Guicciardini denied the answer
Machiavelli claimed) is sound and correctly framed as a quotation. The
introduction draft needs revising (pass 7).

## Stop doing

Anything that bears on one of these is a defect in every pass:

- Putting process (passes, collator, witness IDs, D-numbers) on reader pages.
- Writing Argument notes that paraphrase the text beside them.
- Making claims in SCOPE that the repository can't back.
- Treating Bausi as untouchable.
- Dating rows on the index.

## David's decisions (DEC-013)

1. The 1824 *Discorsi* governs for now, because a complete scan is freely
   available and legible. A future edition may rely on the 1531 Blado
   printing.
2. Bausi 2001 is treated exactly as Palmarocchi 1933. Its readings may be
   consulted and reported as facts at loci where we emend or where our
   witnesses split. Its expression is never copied.
3. Completeness: the conventional reading. The *Considerazioni* are
   unrevised working notes, the 39 units are the set that survives, and
   whether the work is complete is an open question.
4. Date of composition: one source decides it and one wording is used
   everywhere, citing it. Ada Palmer has published nothing on the dating, so
   the source is a current Guicciardini specialist, approved by David before
   it was applied.
5. v1.0 also requires secondary literature in the annotations, a
   bibliography and a further-reading list. A person checks every citation
   against a library catalogue.
6. The rights assessment stays open for a second reader.

### Pass 2 — 2026-11-26

- RIGHTS §6 is marked as awaiting a second reader. Item 5 adds UK CDPA
  s. 9(3) and s. 12(7), with the state of reform checked 2026-11-26. The
  March 2026 report proposes removing s. 9(3) with no timetable; the
  government's 15 May 2026 response says it has no current plans to amend;
  the provision is in force.
- Palmarocchi's death in 1956 is now checked against authority records.
  His textual choices fall under art. 85-*quater* (twenty years, so expired
  1953, if the right ever arose). His prose enters the Italian public domain
  on 1 January 2027 and the US public domain on 1 January 2029.
- The 1933 Nota (pp. 339–342, read on Wikisource) says the edition follows
  the single autograph (Filza IX), that the bracketed rubrics are his own,
  and names twenty-one Canestrini readings as errors against the autograph.
  This is recorded per record in
  `sources/comparisons/FG-CONS/WIT-FG-CONS-1933-basis.yml`. One Nota entry
  (II.10 `siano`/`furno`) is the reverse of both books as printed; it is
  logged as an anomaly and not resolved. O50 gains the Nota's evidence for
  `decennale`.
- Source notes link to the page images at the Internet Archive (`/page/nN/`,
  with N being the recorded zero-based PDF index). The mapping was checked
  for the 1824 against IA's page-number file and for Canestrini by eye. No
  new witness extracts will be added (SCOPE §4, RIGHTS §5).
- Licence marking: `rel="license"` links in the column heads (the Italian
  CC0, the English CC BY 4.0); the JSON-LD on each pair page describes each
  column (`WebPageElement` by `cssSelector`, with `license` and
  `inLanguage`); every footer carries a licence line.
- **Raised with David, not decided:** (a) The Nota reports that the
  autograph lays out a further 20 chapter numbers with blank pages after
  them. That is direct evidence the work is unfinished, firmer than "whether
  it is complete is open" (DEC-013 point 3). (b) Of the twenty-one Canestrini
  readings the Nota calls errors against the autograph, most stand in our
  text under D38, with the 1933 reading in a variant note. D38 was written
  before the edition knew the 1933 readings were the autograph's.
