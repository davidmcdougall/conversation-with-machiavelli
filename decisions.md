# Decision log

One line per non-obvious choice: the source phrase, the ruling, the reason.
This log becomes the translator's preface.

## Settled

| # | Source | Ruling | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D01 | `discorrere` / `occorrere` / `concorrere` | discourse / occur / concur | SCOPE §3. Three *currere* compounds survive into English; most translators sever them, and severing them loses Guicciardini's answer to Machiavelli. |
| D02 | `opinione` (3×) | opinion | Draft had belief/belief/opinion. "The belief in miracles" is the stock English phrase; the Italian says opinion, and pairs it with `credulità` as something princes cultivate. |
| D03 | `rei` / `cattivi` / `tristi` | evil / bad / wicked | Draft collapsed all three into "wicked". `cattivi` especially: it stands two clauses after `ogni bene`, and the good/bad opposition is erased if it becomes "wicked". |
| D04 | `nascere` (4×) | arise | Was born/arise/arise/has arisen. One verb. |
| D05 | `Principi` (.02, .03) | princes, both times | Draft had "the other leading men of the city" at .03 — a glossary violation, and it cut the noun half of the .02→.03 echo. Also the conventional English for Latin *principes*, i.e. exactly where a remembered translation would intrude. |
| D06 | `un potente` | a powerful one | Not "a power", which reads as an abstract state-actor and breaks the `potente` chain running through .06. |
| D07 | `più propinqui` / `meno` | nearer / less | Comparatives, not superlatives. The claim is a correlation — the nearer, the less — not a statement about the extreme case. |
| D08 | `o la rovina o il flagello` | either the ruin or the scourge | Draft supplied possessives ("its ruin"). The Italian does not say whose, and .05's turn to Italy depends on that staying open. |
| D09 | `favorirle ed accrescerle` → `favorita e accresciuta` | favour and augment, both times, and .03 keeps the Italian's clause-final position | The echo is the demonstration: Rome did what the chapter recommends. |
| D10 | `ne cacciò` (2×) | drove ... out of it | The locative clitic was dropped twice. Italy-as-territory is the chapter's subject. |
| D11 | `la Italia` | it, not she | The draft had Italy as "she" and the Church as "it", inconsistently, inside one sentence. Gendered Italy is the hallmark of the older English tradition; register.md refuses costume. |
| D12 | asyndeton (`cerimonie, sacrifizj, riti`; `dipoi ne cacciò`) | not supplied with "and" | The draft normalised two bare Italian lists into English series. |
| D13 | `come che le giudicassero false` | even though they should judge them false, kept interposed | Draft had "however false they may judge them" at the end of the clause — smoother than the Italian, and it turns a concessive subjunctive into a scalar. This sits in the chapter's most quoted sentence. |
| D14 | `vis venire Romam?` | left in Latin, italic, glossed in a note | |
| D15 | em-dashes at .03 `il che dimostra T. Livio` | supplied, once, recorded | The only supplied punctuation of its kind in the unit. The English participial absolute genuinely strains without it. Recorded rather than silent. |

| D16 | `virtù` (.06) | left as *virtù*, italic, with a note | **O01, David 2026-09-18.** Capacity, not moral virtue, and not simply power — distinguished from `potente` in the same clause. A facing-page edition can carry the loan-word; every English option loses something. Sets the pattern for contested terms across the corpus. |
| D17 | `obbligo` (.05, .07) | debt | **O02, David 2026-09-18.** Ironic in both places. "Obligation" is the cognate but reads neutral-legal where the Italian is sardonic. |
| D18 | Postposed subjects | passive delay, not Italian order | **O03, David 2026-09-18. The register boundary for the whole edition.** Hold the landing without breaking English: the chapter closes "more disorder would be made in that province by the wicked customs of that court", delaying the sting without forcing a reread. Full Italian order is not English; plain SVO loses the sting. The facing Italian shows the reader the original order. |
| D19 | Left-dislocation (.02) | plain English order | **O04, David 2026-09-18.** `.02` is exposition, not a rhetorical landing, so nothing is lost by smoothing. Not in tension with D18: that rule protects landings, and this is not one. |
| D20 | `hanno meno religione` (.04) | source corrected; note written | **O05, settled on the page image 2026-09-18.** The witness prints lower case; the same page capitalises `Religione` four times. Pass A had regularised the exception away. English cannot carry the shift, so the note does. Corpus corrected to v0.2.1 (NM-DISC-I12-C001). |
| D21 | `nelle Religioni, eziandio false` | comma restored; English follows | Same page check. The comma is printed. English moves from "even in false religions" to "in religions, even false ones", which is what the pointed Italian says. |

| D22 | `però` ×2 (FG) | therefore | Consecutive (*per ciò*), not adversative "however". The profile flags it as the likeliest error in the whole *Considerazioni*; ruled explicitly so it cannot recur. |
| D23 | `milita` | has no force | "Militate" survives in modern English almost only in "militate against", which pulls the opposite way and cannot take "in". The cognate does not still work. |
| D24 | `al rincontro` | on the other side | An adversative redirector, not reciprocity — the draft's "in return" read as though the flourishing cities were payment for the calamities. "On the other hand" is spoken for by `dall'altra parte` and `da altro`, so this takes a third formula. |
| D25 | `complessione` | complexion | Humoral, and `temperata` in the next word confirms it. "Constitution" both cut the humoral pairing and invited a political reading in a text about `republica`, `regno` and `monarchia`. |
| D26 | `vituperii` | disgraces | The period sense is the state of disgrace, not abusive speech. "Vituperations" made the Roman court an example of the world's name-calling rather than its shames — a weaker charge, in the unit's most quotable line. |
| D27 | `facilmente` ×2 | readily, both | Glossary reservation enforced: `perderono facilmente` and `non concorro facilmente` are eleven words apart in the same period, and the hinge sits inside the repetition. |
| D28 | `participare ... a` | impart ... to | Transitive: the republic withholds a share. "Share ... with" made it mutual. |
| D29 | `viversi felici` | live happy | Predicate of civic condition, not an adverb of manner — and it answers NM .06 `unita o felice` → "united or happy". |
| D30 | `sotto uno imperio` | under one rule | "A single rule" imported `solo` from four lines earlier and broke the numeral chain answering NM's "one republic or one prince" and "one head". |
| D31 | `se bene` / `benché` | although / albeit | Two concessives doing different jobs: `se bene` admits the calamities, `benché` supplies the counterweight that licenses `nondimeno`. The draft had both as "although" and demoted the second into an em-dash parenthesis — the one clause the profile says must not be made parenthetical. |
| D32 | FG rubric | restored from Canestrini, marked as his | The first draft imported the 1824 Machiavelli rubric, unattributed, because the source omitted one. Canestrini prints his own (FG-CONS-I12-C001). |

| D33 | Accent direction — `Né`/`Nè`, `perché`/`perchè` | modern acute on closed final *e*; grave where modern Italian sets grave | **N01, ruled 2026-09-21. DEC-009 Class A.** Orthographic: no Italian word pair is distinguished by accent *direction* on the same vowel, so the parse cannot move. Both witnesses print grave (dual-attested: pass B reads `Perchè`, `nè` in the 1824 too), but this edition disclaimed being a text of record, and grave on `perchè` is period costume, which register.md refuses (D11). The question as filed described only NM-DISC, which is grave except four acute `Né`; FG-CONS.1.12 is acute throughout against a grave witness. The rule corrects both. |
| D34 | Space after an elided apostrophe | apocope spaced, elision solid | **N02, ruled 2026-09-21. DEC-009 Class A.** `de' Romani`, `a' suoi`, `e' quali` / `l'ha`, `d'Italia`, `all'altre`. Justification variance is a red herring — the spaced apocope is the modern convention whatever the line does, and p. 30 prints it spaced anyway. Two cases, one rule, no page. |
| D35 | The apostrophe on the Florentine plural article (`e'` / `e`) | supplied throughout, including where the witness omits it | **Ruled 2026-09-21. DEC-009 Class A.** Filed as a reading (collation B4) and reclassified: Canestrini prints bare `e` at `e vituperii`, `e frutti`, `e sudditi`, `e quali` — confirmed at magnification on pp. 27, 29, 30 — but the syntax fixes the parse regardless (`di tutti e vituperii` cannot be the conjunction). So the apostrophe establishes nothing; it *signals* what the syntax already determined, which is what a reading edition owes a modern reader. Pass A's four unrecorded supplies are retrospectively correct, and now declared. |
| D36 | Euphonic *d* | modern usage — `ed`/`ad`/`od` before the same vowel only; never page-checked | **N03, ruled 2026-09-21. DEC-009 Class A.** Filed as "needs one look at the Canestrini page"; the look was taken and does not settle the rule. p. 30 prints `consuetudine e inclinazione` bare, so pass A supplied in all three places — but a per-instance page rule is unaffordable across 78 units on a scan where a missing `d` and a dropped `d` are indistinguishable, and it buys nothing a reader can use. `ed` is a sandhi variant of the same conjunction, not a different word; setting it by rule establishes no reading. |
| D37 | Punctuation weight — semicolon vs comma (collation §A4) | the witness as printed; neither lightened nor strengthened | **Ruled 2026-09-21. DEC-009 Class B.** Not previously deferred, settled or opened — it fell through. It is also the only one of the collation's four "systematic conventions" that is potentially substantive: where the stops fall decides which clause `nondimeno` answers, which is what D31 already turns on. Checked on the page, and the collation's guess is wrong. p. 29 prints clean commas at `a tutti e sudditi,` and `non ci era la Chiesa, anzi` with no upper dot; p. 30 prints unmistakable semicolons at `de' Romani;` and `violenza;` at the same type size on the same scan. The scan is not losing dots — **pass A pointed the text more heavily than Canestrini does.** See the open item below for the corpus consequence. |
